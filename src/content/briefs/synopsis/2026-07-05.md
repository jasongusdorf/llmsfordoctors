## 2. Benchmarks & Medical LLMs background, Jun 12

A **Nature Medicine** Brief Communication (Jun 12, PMID 42286322) found frontier general-purpose models, **GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6**, outperformed specialized clinical tools (**OpenEvidence, UpToDate Expert AI**) across knowledge, expert alignment, and real-world clinical evaluation:

| Benchmark | Best general LLM | OpenEvidence | UpToDate Expert AI |
|----|----|----|----|
| MedQA | 97.4% (Gemini 3.1 Pro; 95% CI 95.6–98.5) | 89.6% | 88.4% |
| HealthBench | 88.0 (GPT-5.2) | 62.6 | 61.3 |
| Real Clinical Queries | 3.62 (Gemini) | 3.24 | 3.17 |

**Caveats that matter:** (1) **OpenEvidence disputes the methodology**, alleged MedQA contamination, stylistic scoring, and the Real-Clinical-Queries set added post-review. That's a vendor rebuttal, not a refutation, but weigh it. (2) The authors themselves flag a benchmark-vs-bedside applicability gap. (3) **Dating:** Jun 12 is ~2 weeks before the window, recent context, not past-week. \[Confidence: high · 3-0\]

## 4. Safety, hallucination, equity background

- **Automation bias is real even in AI-trained doctors.** Single-blind RCT (NCT06963957, 44 physicians, Pakistan, all with 20-hr AI-literacy training): flawed LLM advice cut top-diagnosis accuracy **90.5% → 76.1%** (adj −18.3 pp, P\<.0001) and reasoning 84.9% → 73.3%. Human oversight is not a sufficient safeguard by itself. \[3-0\]
- **Note error rates (GPT-4, npj Dig Med 2025):** 1.47% hallucination, 3.45% omission across 12,999 annotated sentences, *omissions outnumber hallucinations.* The follow-on claim that prompt tuning pushes errors below human baseline was **refuted (0-3).** \[2-1\]
- **Equity:** underlying speech recognition shows higher word-error rates for Black vs White speakers (corroborated by Koenecke 2020 PNAS, Zolnoori 2024 JAMIA Open); scribes largely escape FDA oversight as "administrative" tools. As of early 2026 the FDA had authorized ~1,350 AI-enabled devices but **no generative-AI device.** \[3-0\]
- **Clinicians self-correct toward caution** (arXiv 2606.00018, UC Health, 62,811 note pairs): editors added hedging/uncertainty more often than they removed it (McNemar P\<0.001). A reassuring human-in-the-loop signal, but a preprint, and outside the window.

## What this means for a hospitalist

- **Nothing this week changes your practice.** No new inpatient-grade evidence, no clearance you'd act on. If a vendor cites "new" Ambience inpatient capability, it's a product announcement, ask for validation data before believing throughput or safety claims.
- **The scribe wave is moving inpatient ahead of its evidence.** The RCT base is ambulatory and low-acuity; the Mar-2026 Perspective is explicit that inpatient multi-speaker attribution is unsolved. Reasonable to pilot, unreasonable to trust unsupervised on a busy ward.
- **Automation bias applies to you, not just trainees.** The Pakistan RCT degraded accuracy in *AI-literate* physicians. Read the chart before the AI summary when it matters.
- **"FDA-cleared" ≠ validated.** Both the UpDoc clearance and the PLOS "illusion of safety" audit are reminders that 510(k) clearance can sit alongside thin clinical-benefit evidence.
