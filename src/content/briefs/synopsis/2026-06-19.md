## 1 · Safety, hallucination & clinical reasoning limits

### GPT-5 adversarial hallucinations, "New model, old risks" (confidence: high · peer-reviewed · BACKGROUND, ~Apr 2026)

Omar et al., *npj Digital Medicine* 2026;9(1):282 (DOI 10.1038/s41746-026-02584-8). Across 500 emergency vignettes spanning 32 sociodemographic labels, GPT-5 **elaborated on planted fabricated findings in 65% of runs** (95% CI 61.1–68.7) under a standard clinical prompt, worse than GPT-4o's 53%. A simple instruction to verify before acting collapsed that to **7.67%** (95% CI 5.16–11.24; χ²=262.45, p\<0.0001; OR ≈ 22). The authors' conclusion is the quotable line: "despite seemingly greater capability and agentic tooling, GPT-5 family models continued to exhibit systematic subgroup decision variation and susceptibility to adversarial inserts."

Verified caveat: the stronger sub-claim circulating, that marginalized groups were flagged for mental-health screening in 100% of runs, was **refuted** in verification and should not be repeated. The robust takeaway is the 65%/53% → 7.7% arc: prompt design still dominates safety behavior.

### Frontier models talk patients out of workup (confidence: high · preprint · BACKGROUND, ~Mar 2026)

Auger & Scott (Imperial College London), medRxiv DOI 10.64898/2026.03.23.26349082. Using 1,000 synthetic clinical transcripts at graded information-completeness (20–100%), both GPT-5.2 and GPT-5-mini, when handed *incomplete* histories, **discouraged essential investigations rather than requesting more information**, including recommending against **100% of lumbar punctures in subarachnoid-hemorrhage cases**. The failure mode is the dangerous one for a hospitalist: confident closure on a thin history.

Verified caveat: the eye-catching secondary figures, 97.5% diagnostic accuracy on full histories, 54.8% triage downgrades, a female-vs-male triage OR of 3.2, all **failed verification** and are excluded here. Treat this as a qualitative hazard signal, not a quantified one.

## 2 · Ambient documentation, the one clean RCT

### Two ambient scribes, head to head (confidence: high · peer-reviewed · BACKGROUND, Nov 2025)

Lukac et al. (senior author Mafi), *NEJM AI*, Nov 2025 (DOI 10.1056/AIoa2501000; preprint medRxiv 2025.07.10.25331333; PMC12265753). A 3-arm pragmatic RCT at UCLA Health, **238 physicians, 14 specialties, ~72,000 encounters**, Nov 2024–Jan 2025, pitted Microsoft DAX v2.0 and Nabla v1.5 against usual care.

| Arm | Change in time-in-note vs control | 95% CI | p |
|----|----|----|----|
| Nabla v1.5 | **−9.5%** | −17.2% to −1.8% | .02 |
| Microsoft DAX v2.0 | −1.7% (ns) | −9.4% to +5.9% | .66 |
| Nabla vs DAX (head-to-head) | −7.8% | −15.5% to −0.1% | .05 |

The practical lesson: "ambient AI scribe" is not a commodity category. The two leading products produced materially different efficiency outcomes in the same health system.

Verified caveat: the secondary burnout outcomes (Mini-Z +2.76, work-exhaustion −0.27) **did not survive verification**, don't lean on the wellbeing claims.

## 3 · Benchmarks & evaluation (all preprints · BACKGROUND)

- **Medmarks** (arXiv 2605.01417, May 2, 2026; repo MedARC-AI/Medmarks), an open suite of 30 benchmarks across 61 models / 71 configs, spanning QA, information extraction, medical calculations, and open-ended clinical reasoning, split into verifiable (Medmarks-V) and open-ended (Medmarks-OE) subsets. Useful as a harness; not a single comparable score.
- **LiveMedBench** (arXiv 2602.10367v1; repo ZhilingYan/LiveMedBench), a contamination-free benchmark where the **best of 38 LLMs reached only 39.2%** and 84% of models degraded on post-training-cutoff cases. The headline is a healthy corrective to saturated MedQA-style leaderboards. Caveat: the detailed composition claim (2,756 cases / 38 specialties / 16,702 criteria / weekly harvest) failed verification, cite the 39.2% headline, not the internals.
- **Med-HEAL** (arXiv 2606.01301v1, ~May 31, 2026; repo yimingliao-blad/med-heal), a hallucination-mitigation framework using hallucination-aware in-context learning, evaluated on EHRNoteQA (962 questions from MIMIC-IV discharge summaries) across five open models with GPT-4o + medical-student dual assessment.

Refuted and excluded: the CSEDB "dual-track" safety/effectiveness benchmark and its 57.2% six-model result (npj 2025-02277-8) were killed 0–3 in verification, do not report.

## 4 · Imaging & explainability

### XAI mitigates cognitive bias but doesn't eliminate it (confidence: high · peer-reviewed · ~late May 2026, closest-to-window)

Pesapane et al., *European Radiology* (DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12666-6). A fully-crossed simulation reader study (6 breast radiologists, 200 mammograms each) perturbed BI-RADS-like AI recommendations by one category in 30% of cases. Saliency heatmaps (Grad-CAM) roughly **halved both bias modes**, automation bias 36.1% → 17.8% (aOR 0.56), anchoring bias 33.9% → 17.2% (aOR 0.61), both p\<0.001, but **~1 in 5 manipulated cases still produced a biased read**. Explainability is a guardrail, not a fix; a wrong-but-explained recommendation still misleads a fifth of the time.

## What this means for a hospitalist

- **Ambient scribes are not interchangeable.** If your shop is choosing or has chosen one, the UCLA RCT is the evidence to cite: product-level efficiency differences are real and measurable. Don't accept "we deployed an AI scribe" as a finished sentence.
- **The dangerous LLM failure mode on the wards is premature closure, not florid hallucination.** Two independent studies converge: given an incomplete story, these models confidently stop asking and may talk you out of the LP. The mitigation that works is mundane, an explicit "verify before you act / what's missing?" prompt, and it works dramatically (65% → 7.7%).
- **Explainability helps your read but doesn't protect it.** A heatmap halves your susceptibility to a wrong AI suggestion; it still gets you 1-in-5 of the time. Calibrate trust accordingly.
- **Be skeptical of single benchmark scores.** Contamination-free evaluation drops the best model to ~39%. The leaderboard you saw last quarter was probably contaminated.
