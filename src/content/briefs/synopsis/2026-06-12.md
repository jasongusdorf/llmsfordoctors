## 2. Clinical research: diagnosis & decision support

Google AMIE, first real-patient feasibility study BACKGROUND · confidence: high (3-0) · Mar 2026

Single-arm feasibility study, 100 adults at an academic medical center (Beth Israel Deaconess, your old shop), patients text-chatting with AMIE up to 5 days pre-visit. AMIE's differential contained the chart-confirmed final diagnosis in **90%** of cases (75% top-3). Blinded review: DDx and management quality statistically similar to PCPs (DDx p=0.6, safety p=1.0), but **PCPs beat AMIE on plan practicality (p=0.003) and cost-effectiveness (p=0.004)**. Read it for the failure mode, not the headline: the model diagnoses well but plans expensively and impractically. Rigor caveats, vendor-authored (Google), N=100, single-site, single-arm, not comparative-effectiveness. Pre-registered NCT06911398. [\[9\]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08448) [\[10\]](https://research.google/blog/exploring-the-feasibility-of-conversational-diagnostic-ai-in-a-real-world-clinical-study/)

## 3. Ambient documentation / AI scribes

This is where the verified evidence actually clusters this cycle. The honest synthesis: **modest, vendor- and site-specific time savings; no demonstrated outcome benefit.**

| Study / source | Design | Documentation-time effect |
|----|----|----|
| **NEJM AI RCT** (UCLA) \[11\] | 3-arm pragmatic RCT, 238 physicians, 14 specialties, ~72k encounters; DAX vs Nabla vs usual care | Nabla **−9.5%** time-in-note (p=0.02); DAX **−1.7%, NS** (p=0.66) |
| **JMIR Med Inform** time-motion \[12\] | Prospective, 9 clinicians, 169 consults (Singapore) | **−15%** per-consult doc time (5.3→4.5 min, p=0.04); **no** throughput gain |
| **npj Digit Med** real-world \[13\] | Perspective aggregating deployments | MGB median **−5.6 min**; Permanente (~2.5M uses) **~18 sec**; Intermountain **NS** |

**The "no outcome evidence" point matters.** The npj Digital Medicine perspective (Mar 2026) states there is still *no* evidence ambient scribes improve clinical outcomes, reduce errors, or reduce harm, and flags EDs, ICUs, ORs, pre-hospital/EMS, and low-resource settings as essentially unstudied. Adversarial flag: a circulating claim that scribes "consistently reduce burnout, with 84% of clinicians / 56% of patients reporting positive impact" was **refuted 0-3** in verification, do not cite those figures. [\[11\]](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/AIoa2501000) [\[12\]](https://medinform.jmir.org/2026/1/e85580) [\[13\]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02554-0)

## 4. Safety, bias & equity

Geographic bias: LLMs lean high-income-country epidemiology BACKGROUND · confidence: medium

medRxiv preprint (Mar 3, 2026): four LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini-2.0-flash, Copilot) on respiratory vignettes matched physician diagnoses **32%** of the time in LMIC settings vs **50%** in the UK (p=0.028). Bias persisted even when querying via VPN from the target country or stating the country in the prompt. Treat as hypothesis-grade: non-peer-reviewed, only 5 vignettes, wide CIs, single source. [\[14\]](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.02.26347405v1)

Proxy-variable bias in LLM ED triage BACKGROUND · confidence: medium

arXiv preprint (Jan 2026) on MIMIC-IV-ED with gpt-4o-mini predicting ESI: ~**3 of 4** tested proxy variables produced a statistically significant net shift in predicted acuity. The effect was *polarity-independent*, the mere presence of proxy-associated tokens shifted ESI in the same direction regardless of positive/negative framing, suggesting the model keys on non-causal signal rather than true acuity. Caveats: single preprint, demo-scale subset, not peer-reviewed. Directionally corroborated by a second arXiv bias study. [\[15\]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15306) [\[16\]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16273)

## What this means for a hospitalist

- **On scribes:** if Cornell/NYP is evaluating ambient documentation, the vendor choice is not interchangeable, the head-to-head RCT shows one tool meaningfully cutting note time and another doing essentially nothing. Demand *your* specialty's data, not the aggregate pitch deck. And set expectations: the honest sell is documentation time, not throughput, burnout, or outcomes, none of those are proven.
- **On diagnostic LLMs:** AMIE's pattern, strong differential, weak *practicality and cost*, is the clinically relevant failure mode. As you supervise residents using these tools, the diagnosis line may be sound while the plan is over-resourced. Review the plan, not just the DDx.
- **On bias:** the geographic and proxy-variable findings are preprint-grade but directionally consistent with your own equity work. The actionable read: model triage/acuity outputs can shift on non-clinical tokens. For any deployed acuity tool, that's a validation question worth asking the vendor directly.
