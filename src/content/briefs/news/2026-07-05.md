## International

**Iran mourns Khamenei, and shows its cracks.** The funeral for assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, killed in February in US–Israeli strikes, entered a second day, with mourners chanting “revenge.” The *Times* paired the spectacle with reporting on “glimpses of a changing Tehran,” and on-site pieces framing the site as a symbol of his failures as much as his authority. Beneath the choreography is a real succession struggle: son **Mojtaba Khamenei** was installed as supreme leader in March, but hardliners loyal to him are resisting diplomatic concessions, state TV abruptly cut off parliament speaker (and chief US negotiator) Ghalibaf mid-broadcast on June 30, and pension protests have spread across several provinces. NYT (read via Chrome); CBS News. Regime-stability framing partly sourced to opposition-linked outlets, treat succession-crisis claims as analysis, not settled fact.

**Ukraine and Russia escalate.** A Russian missile-and-drone barrage on Kyiv on July 2–3 killed at least 22, among the deadliest strikes in weeks, hours after Zelensky publicly warned one was coming. Ukraine hit back hard: a strike on **Belgorod** knocked out power and water across the city, and fires were reported at a ferry terminal and airfield in occupied **Crimea**. Overnight July 4, air defenses downed 115 of 129 drones. In a TV interview, Putin reaffirmed he intends to fully control all four annexed regions (Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson, Zaporizhia), rejecting a reported Ukrainian de-escalation proposal. Al Jazeera; Ukrinform.

**Elsewhere.** Hundreds of firefighters are battling wildfires across Portugal, Spain, and Greece as an early, brutal heat season bites; in China, heavy rains killed five in the north while a tropical storm submerged cars in the south. Wire services.

## US

**A storm-soaked semiquincentennial.** The nation’s 250th birthday was disrupted by severe weather up and down the East Coast, cancellations in Hartford, Harrisburg, and Wilkes-Barre, and a thunderstorm that delayed President Trump’s National Mall address by nearly two hours. When he did speak, Trump delivered a campaign-style address that mixed veneration of veterans with pointed attacks on the progressive left, and pressed his **SAVE America Act**, requiring photo ID and proof of citizenship to vote and sharply limiting mail-in ballots. He also complained he “wasn’t treated that well” despite Americans’ right to equal justice. The bill faces a steep climb in the Senate. CNBC; NPR; ABC News; The Hill.

**DOGE reaches its sunset.** The Department of Government Efficiency hit the July 4, 2026 expiration written into Trump’s January 2025 executive order. After a year that saw thousands of federal employees exit and billions in contracts voided, debate now centers on how, and how cleanly, it actually winds down. Wire roundups; exact wind-down mechanics still developing.

**Also on the domestic ledger:** the *Times* reports federal agencies are dropping discrimination cases at Trump’s direction and rolling back dozens of gun regulations, while the White House publicly criticized the Smithsonian for “extreme political activism.” In Michigan, state Sen. **Mallory McMorrow suspended her Senate bid**, unable to gain traction between the party’s left and establishment wings. NYT.

## Israel

**Ceasefire on a knife’s edge.** Trump said on July 1 that Israel had agreed to “the necessary conditions” to finalize a **60-day Gaza ceasefire**, and Hamas signaled a “positive” response to the Qatar-brokered framework. But on Saturday, Netanyahu’s office said Hamas had requested “unacceptable” changes, leaving the deal, and a possible Trump trip to the region, in limbo. CBS News; NBC News; Times of Israel. Fast-moving and single-thread on the “unacceptable changes” characterization, sourced to the PMO.

**The war grinds on under the “ceasefire.”** Israeli fire killed three Palestinians in Gaza on July 1 despite the truce; the humanitarian picture remains dire, with nearly all of Gaza’s 2.1 million people displaced and Israel controlling roughly 70% of the Strip per Netanyahu’s May directive. Separately, the IDF detained settler activists who tried to breach the border with **Syria**. A lighter note from the liveblog: former hostages Sasha Troufanov and Sapir Cohen married. Al Jazeera; Times of Israel liveblog.

## Economic & Markets

**The jobs number that changed the conversation.** June payrolls came in at just **57,000**, roughly half of forecasts, with April and May revised down by a combined ~74,000. Unemployment ticked to 4.2%. Counterintuitively, stocks rose on the print (the Dow up ~246 points intraday) as a cooling labor market takes pressure off the Fed. CNBC; U.S. News; Yahoo Finance.

**The Warsh Fed stays put, and hawkish.** Under new Chair **Kevin Warsh**, the Fed held its benchmark at **3.50–3.75%** at the June meeting, with inflation still running at 4.2% annually. It scrapped forward guidance for pure data-dependence, raising uncertainty. After the soft jobs data, traders took a September hike off the table; the next decision comes July 28–29. Meanwhile **JPMorgan lifted its 2026 S&P 500 target to 7,800** (from 7,200), implying ~5% further upside. US markets were closed Friday July 3 for the holiday and reopen Monday. Kiplinger; CNBC; Schwab.

## Also notable

**World Cup 2026.** On home-ish soil, France edged Paraguay 1–0 as **Mbappé drew level with Messi at seven goals**; Morocco knocked out a co-host to reach the quarterfinals. In a very 2026 twist, Trump personally asked FIFA to review the World Cup suspension of US top scorer Folarin Balogun, the latest chapter in FIFA president Infantino’s long courtship of the president. Mexico–England is the marquee last-16 tie ahead. NYT; The Athletic.

**“The Revenge of the Philosophy Majors” (NYT, ~11 min).** AI labs are quietly hiring contrarian, chin-stroking humanities types to stress-test their models’ reasoning. A sharp, slightly gleeful piece on how the “useless” degree became a hot hire, and one that should land for anyone thinking about where judgment sits in an AI stack.
