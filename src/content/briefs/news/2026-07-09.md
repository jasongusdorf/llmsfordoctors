**TL;DR.** The April Iran ceasefire has collapsed: US and Iran are into a second day of open strikes, with Tehran hitting US bases across the Gulf and oil spiking as Hormuz traffic thins. At home, the Maine Senate race blew up overnight, progressive nominee Graham Platner suspended his campaign after a rape accusation. And the IMF cut global growth to 3%, pinning the drag squarely on the Iran energy shock.

## International

### US and Iran sink into a violent cycle

The headline story everywhere this morning. President Trump, capping the NATO summit in Ankara, declared the April ceasefire with Iran “over” and warned of more attacks. US Central Command says it has now completed a **second consecutive day of strikes**, reporting hits on roughly **90 targets** along Iran’s coastline, Sirik, Qeshm Island, Bandar Abbas, Kharg Island, framed as protecting freedom of navigation after Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran’s IRGC says it retaliated against US bases in **Kuwait and Bahrain**, and the regular army claimed a strike on a US site in **Qatar**; air-raid sirens sounded across Gulf states. Trump also says he is reimposing a naval blockade, while claiming (uncorroborated) that Tehran “called” to negotiate. Developing and fast-moving, casualty and damage figures are still unconfirmed, and some analysts (per FT) argue the tanker attacks risk “overplaying Iran’s hand.” Sources: NYT live coverage, FT “US carries out second day of strikes,” CNN, NPR, WaPo, cross-confirmed.

### Khamenei’s state funeral concludes

The week-long, remarkably large state funeral for Supreme Leader **Ayatollah Ali Khamenei**, who was killed in US-Israeli strikes back on Feb 28 at the war’s outset, reached its final stage today with burial in Mashhad, though NYT reports the last rites were delayed. Iran anticipated up to 15 million mourners; foreign delegations included Russia’s Medvedev and a Hamas contingent. FT’s analysis (“Iran’s new order takes shape”) flags the succession question: son **Mojtaba Khamenei**, about whom little is known publicly, is now positioned to inherit a regime under enormous strain. Sources: NPR, CNN, FT, Wikipedia (State funeral of Ali Khamenei).

### NATO summit: better than feared

The Ankara summit that many expected to fracture instead ended, in FT’s phrase, with “free guns and ‘love in the room.’” Trump granted Ukraine a **license to domestically produce Patriot PAC-3 interceptors**, a longstanding Kyiv demand, and met Zelensky. NYT’s “Tale of Two Summits” analysis is more pointed: while Trump insulted allies and demanded loyalty, the alliance quietly moved toward Europeans shouldering more of their own defense. Germany’s Merz also announced a deal to buy US Tomahawks. Sideshow: Trump left Turkey on an older Air Force One for security reasons. Sources: NYT, FT (multiple).

### Russia’s fuel crunch; Le Pen’s comeback

FT reports **Russia has banned diesel exports** as Ukrainian drone strikes on refineries create petrol and diesel shortages inside Russia, long queues, Cossacks deployed to break up fights, a rare instance of the war biting ordinary Russians directly. Separately, France’s **Marine Le Pen** confirmed a 2027 presidential run despite her embezzlement conviction and a court-ordered electronic monitor, styling herself (per FT) as a modern Joan of Arc against the establishment. Sources: FT.

## US

### The Platner implosion

Overnight the biggest domestic story: **Graham Platner suspended his Maine Senate campaign** after a rape accusation, which he denies. Platner, a progressive insurgent who had energized the Democratic base, leaves a race both parties saw as pivotal to Senate control. NYT’s reconstruction (“A Slow-Rolling Disaster”) describes a campaign that was messy and disorganized well before the allegation, doomed by a steady drip of scandal. The New Yorker’s Jon Allsop frames it as a “point of no return” with stakes for the broader direction of the party. Sources: NYT (multiple), New Yorker.

### Manhattan building evacuation

A Times exclusive: the firm that inspected the Manhattan high-rise whose **interior columns buckled**, a former Pfizer HQ mid-conversion to residential, had been repeatedly cited for missing problems at other sites. A “frozen zone” and evacuations remain in place. Source: NYT.

### Trump’s disclosure

The New Yorker’s David Kirkpatrick digs into Trump’s latest financial disclosure, reporting he earned in the **billions last year, largely via crypto token sales**, while the investors who bought in fared far worse. A useful long-form companion to the day’s wire coverage of Trump’s finances. Source: New Yorker.

## Israel

### Fallout from the renewed Iran war

Defense Secretary **Pete Hegseth canceled a planned Israel visit** as fighting with Iran resumed; he’d been due to discuss Israel’s objections to Trump’s plan to sell **F-35s to Turkey** with Netanyahu and Defense Minister Katz. Source: Times of Israel.

### Israel-Lebanon framework

The Rubio-brokered **trilateral framework agreement**, mutual sovereignty recognition (a first since 1983) and a phased IDF withdrawal from south Lebanon contingent on the Lebanese army disarming Hezbollah, continues to reverberate. Hezbollah’s Naim Qassem has declared it “null and void” and “a surrender of sovereignty,” and critics warn it could either transform the region or seed the next war. Contested / still developing. Sources: Al Jazeera, INSS, Washington Institute, Soufan Center.

### Gaza and domestic unrest

An IDF strike on a Gaza tent **killed four, including a 10-year-old**; Israel also said it is holding a Gazan man’s body as a bargaining chip. At home, fresh **clashes between Haredi protesters and soldiers** broke out outside the Neve Tzedek military prison amid the ongoing ultra-Orthodox draft standoff. Source: Times of Israel liveblog.

## Economic & Markets

### Oil, the IMF, and stocks

The Iran escalation is the market story. **Crude jumped ~5%** Wednesday, WTI to about \$74, Brent briefly touching ~\$80, on Hormuz supply fears, with FT noting shipping traffic through the strait is already thinning. The **IMF cut its 2026 global growth forecast to 3%** (from 3.5% in 2025 and its own 3.1% April call), blaming the energy shock; it now sees oil up ~32% and global consumer prices up 4.7% on the year, i.e. stalled disinflation. US equities were mixed-to-lower: **Dow -1.1%, S&P -0.3%, Nasdaq +0.2%.** In China, factory-gate (PPI) prices rose a fourth straight month as the Hormuz disruption feeds through supply chains. Sources: TheStreet, Fortune, OilPrice/IMF, FT.

One contrarian note worth holding onto: FT’s Alan Beattie argues the “Iran global inflation crisis” stubbornly refuses to fully materialize, neither this Gulf war nor the Covid-Ukraine spike has returned us to 1970s stagflation. And Robert Armstrong (Unhedged) asks why the Fed *isn’t* raising rates despite all this. Source: FT Opinion.

And in sport: the World Cup quarterfinals continue, Morocco faces France and Mbappé today; Coco Gauff plays a Wimbledon semifinal. Sources: NYT, The Athletic.
