## International

### A Russian drone hit a munitions warehouse near Kyiv, and the secondary explosions killed at least 27

A drone struck a warehouse in Myla, in the Bucha district about 35 km west of Kyiv, on Friday evening, setting off detonations of stored explosive material. Kyiv regional governor Tymur Tkachenko said Saturday the toll had reached at least 27 dead and 42 injured, with 381 people evacuated and roughly 50 residential buildings damaged; Dmytrivka community head Taras Didych was among the dead. The uncomfortable part is not the strike but what was in the building. Zelenskyy pointed straight at a domestic failure: "There are regulations in place - ammunition cannot be stored near homes or other residential areas." The Prosecutor General's Office has opened an investigation into whether the storage was licensed at all, noting a similar incident at a Vyshneve depot in July. Russia's defense ministry said it struck a drone-production arms depot. Russia launched at least 267 drones and two missiles over the same period; a 14-year-old girl was killed in Boryspil. Al Jazeera, CNN, Kyiv Post · Aug 28-29 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/29/russian-attack-on-warehouse-near-ukraines-kyiv-kills-at-least-27)

### The Himalayan flood is now one of the worst disasters in Nepal's history, and it may not be over

A glacier collapsed at roughly 5,200 metres and fell about 1,200 metres, gathering rock and debris before slamming into the Lende River on Wednesday. The surge raised the Trishuli by nine metres in thirty minutes and tore through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading. As of Saturday the confirmed toll stood at 633 - 626 in Nepal, at least seven in Tibet - with close to 3,000 still missing, including 517 foreigners on the Nepalese side. The State Department has said roughly 90 Americans are unaccounted for. Rescue work resumed after a pause driven by the newly formed barrier lake upstream; glacial hazards scientist Alton Byers described the dam holding it as "highly unstable, made up of loose debris," with "not much holding it." Nepal has asked India and China for specialist tunnel-rescue teams. Dozens of bridges and about 25 miles of road are gone. Figures have moved fast and disagree between sources - treat every number as provisional. Al Jazeera, NPR, CNN · Aug 26-29 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/29/nepal-and-china-resume-flood-rescue-as-death-toll-nears-600)

### Six months into the Hormuz war, Qatar is trying to broker a corridor Iran says it still controls

Qatari PM and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Tehran on Thursday, the first high-level Qatari visit since the war began February 28. The talks build on a phased Iran-Oman framework for a temporary shipping corridor and joint mine-clearing; Oman's foreign minister is due in Tehran Tuesday. Then the IRGC Navy undercut it, declaring Friday that it retains "full control" of the strait and that it stays closed to ships that do not coordinate with Tehran - a direct contradiction of Trump's claim days earlier that "that sucker is open." Pezeshkian said Oman had accepted a framework conditioned on Washington lifting the blockade and sanctions. As of Aug 27 US forces had turned back 75 commercial vessels, disabled three and boarded two; roughly 6,000 sailors are stranded aboard ships in the Gulf and 19 seafarers have been killed since February. Washington says it is not currently talking to Iran and prefers what Treasury Secretary Bessent has called "economic asphyxiation." ### King Harald V of Norway died at 89

Harald died Friday morning at Rikshospitalet in Oslo, where he had been hospitalized since Aug 17 with haemolytic anaemia and a subsequent bloodstream infection. He reigned 35 years and was Europe's oldest sitting monarch. As a child he fled the Nazi invasion and spent part of the war sheltering at the White House - a biographical detail that shaped a reign widely credited with making the Norwegian monarchy modern and unpretentious. His son, 53, is now King Haakon VIII and takes the oath before the Storting on Tuesday, Sept 3. Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store: "We will remember King Harald for his love for his people, for his humour and warmth and for his unwavering faith in us." NPR, Al Jazeera, NBC · Aug 28 · [link](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/28/nx-s1-5947778/norways-king-harald-v-dies-king-haakon-viii)

## United States

### Trump announces a 100-year Venezuelan oil deal, with almost nothing to verify

Friday evening Trump posted that the US "has just entered into an Agreement with the Country of Venezuela on, THE BIGGEST OIL DEAL IN WORLD HISTORY!" - covering 17 fields with proven potential of 65 billion barrels, negotiated by Rubio and Hegseth with Venezuelan acting president Delcy Rodriguez. A US official told AP the structure is a new private joint venture between the US government and an unnamed Venezuelan operator, granted 100-year development rights, with the US holding 55% of effective output plus the right to buy oil at cost. That entity would be the second-largest corporate holder of proven reserves after Saudi Aramco. Caracas says the deal could draw \$100bn in investment and yield \$209bn in taxes. Rubio called it "a huge win for both the American and Venezuelan people."

**Flag it.** No official text has been released, one administration source told AP it was finalized while another said it was not wrapped up as of Friday night, and the counterparty is an unelected interim government installed after US forces captured Maduro. Treat every number here as a claim, not a fact.

### Hellerstein blocks the hush-money removal a third time, in unusually blunt language

Judge Alvin Hellerstein (SDNY) denied Trump's renewed motion to move his New York conviction to federal court and dismiss it on immunity grounds, finding the arguments "neither new nor legally sufficient." On the merits: "paying hush money to an adult film star or engaging in a cover-up of an embarrassment are not subsumed in presidential immunity." He also swatted the suggestion that his role was merely to certify the record upward - "Federal district courts are not certifying clerks" - and called the motion a bid for "a 'second bite at the apple,' a result the law disfavors." Trump's team noticed an appeal within hours, calling the ruling "baseless and lawless." The 34-count conviction stands; a separate state appellate challenge is still pending. AP, Courthouse News · Aug 28 · [link](https://apnews.com/article/trump-hush-money-conviction-8ef34a954c61b7e4261827b8a6a99567)

### Mail ballots go out in a week, and the law governing them changed twice this week

Judge Indira Talwani in Boston imposed a fresh 14-day hold Thursday blocking USPS from implementing regulations under Trump's March executive order restricting mail voting - the order would have limited ballot mailing to a list of eligible voters and imposed a uniform envelope format. The timing is the story: first ballots for the Nov 3 midterms ship in roughly a week. This came days after the Supreme Court's conservative majority lifted a separate Talwani injunction on Aug 24 as unripe. She is overseeing both cases; the new freeze arises from the League of Women Voters suit. Expect a fast appeal and a plausible return trip to the Supreme Court. AP, NBC News, Votebeat · Aug 27 · [link](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/judge-blocks-trumps-postal-service-regulation-mail-ballots-rcna594692)

### Health desk: three stories worth your attention

**The Samoa documents.** AP and The Guardian, using records obtained through a lawsuit against the State Department, published a Jan 20, 2019 letter on Children's Health Defense letterhead addressed to Samoa's then-prime minister, in which RFK Jr.'s team proposed a "detailed health informatics assessment" of the country's MMR use following two infant deaths he called "temporally associated with MMR vaccines." The letter uses "vaccine" or "vaccination" eight times. Kennedy told two 2025 congressional hearings the trip had "nothing to do with vaccines." Samoa subsequently suspended all vaccination for about ten months; the measles outbreak that followed sickened more than 5,700 and killed 83 in three and a half months. HHS maintains the visit was unrelated to vaccines ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/rfk-jr-kennedy-samoa-vaccines-mmr-ba6ad3ffadca48599b1c178a392fb37b)).

**Pennsylvania's measles deaths.** The state reported its first measles-related deaths in decades - two unvaccinated residents, one reportedly an infant. Governor Shapiro said vaccination would have prevented them. Kennedy responded on social media that the deaths "may even have been altogether fabricated." Amesh Adalja of Johns Hopkins: "Now it's turned political. It's no longer about getting answers and figuring this out." This lands amid what AP describes as the worst national measles surge in more than three decades ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/measles-deaths-pennsylvania-politics-health-f102d57041e1bd4757142c9ab927b123)).

**Cyclospora, and it is enormous.** FDA added Georgia, Tennessee and Texas, bringing the outbreak to 20 states and 11,458 illnesses - the largest cyclosporiasis outbreak on record in the US, up from about 10,900 a week earlier. Source traced to iceberg lettuce from a Taylor Farms de Mexico operation, recalled July 17. CDC separately counts roughly 29,000 confirmed or suspected cases nationally in 2026 with 922 hospitalizations, against about 4,700 in the previous worst year, 2019. Worth keeping in the differential: it will not show on routine stool culture, it needs TMP-SMX, and the presentation is prolonged watery diarrhea rather than an acute self-limited course ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/cyclosporiasis/outbreaks/07-26/investigation.html), [AP](https://apnews.com/article/lettuce-cyclospora-fda-cdc-outbreak-mexico-19899950075127fb664282dd73cccfc9)).

## Israel & Middle East

### Israel strikes Jenin from the air for the first time since early 2025, and Katz declares the camp occupied

A drone strike near Jenin on Friday evening killed three Palestinians, including Qais Bitawi, described by the IDF and Shin Bet as a senior Hamas operative and former Jenin cell leader; Palestinian Islamic Jihad separately claimed him as a senior figure in its armed wing. ## Economic & Markets

### Warsh's first Jackson Hole moves the September meeting from cut-or-hold to hike-or-hold

The new Fed chair said PCE inflation is running at 3.7% year-over-year and 4.1% annualized over six months, and that better-than-expected summer prints "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved." He argued the labour market is effectively at full employment and that financial conditions may not actually be restraining the economy - and pointedly declined to offer forward guidance or a reaction function. Markets did the rest: CME FedWatch odds of a September *hike* jumped to about 59% from 35% on Thursday. Polymarket went as high as 69% for a 2026 hike; Kalshi showed 48% for 25bp. Worth noting the read is genuinely contested - US News headlined the same speech as providing "little guidance on rates," and Warsh explicitly said it is not yet clear the Fed must act urgently. The Washington Post's framing, that the bank "has more work to do," is probably the fairest summary. ### The jobs data got quietly worse

BLS published its preliminary annual benchmark revision Friday morning, cutting total nonfarm employment for March 2026 by 79,000 (-0.1%). Private payrolls were marked down considerably more, 178,000, offset by an upward revision to government. The median Bloomberg forecast had been *+*183,000, so this was a wide miss in the wrong direction. Weakness concentrated in retail trade, education and health services, manufacturing and business services; transportation and warehousing, information, financial activities and construction were revised up. The final benchmark lands in January 2027. Put next to Warsh's "full employment" framing, this is the tension the September meeting has to resolve. Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance · Aug 28 · [link](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/bls-revises-u-job-growth-172153070.html)

### Corporate: a \$50bn deal dies, and the AI trade gets pickier

Advent International and Stripe walked away from a bid of more than \$50bn for **PayPal**, which would have been among the largest LBOs ever. PayPal's board had reportedly judged the offer too low and held out for more; the stock fell as much as 16-17% premarket and closed down roughly 12-13%, the S&P's worst performer. The buyers could return ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/acquiring/2026/advent-stripe-abandon-50-billion-pursuit-of-paypal), Bloomberg).

Meanwhile the AI trade is showing something more interesting than a selloff: it is getting selective. **Nvidia** reported fiscal Q2 revenue of \$96.22bn and adjusted EPS of \$2.22 against consensus of \$92.37bn and \$2.10 - revenue up 106% year-over-year, a fifth straight quarter of acceleration - and guided Q3 to roughly \$108bn. **Marvell** then beat on both lines, guided above consensus, and fell more than 7% after hours and about 6% Friday anyway. The stock was up 184% year-to-date. When a beat-and-raise gets sold, the market is repricing the multiple, not the quarter ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/27/marvells-stock-sinks-despite-earnings-beat-and-strong-guidance/), CNBC).

## Also Notable

**A federal judge found the Pentagon illegally retaliated against Anthropic.** Judge Rita Lin (N.D. Cal.) issued a 59-page order holding that the Defense Department's designation of Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" violated the First Amendment, the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause and the APA, and ordered the label removed. She wrote that the government acted "based on a desire to make a public example out of Anthropic for its 'arrogance' in criticizing the government, not based on any articulable basis to believe that Anthropic would actually sabotage its model," and that the national-security rationale was assembled "after the fact to justify the foreordained conclusion." The line the coverage keeps quoting: "The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics." The dispute began in February when Hegseth pressed the company to allow unrestricted military use and Dario Amodei refused over autonomous-weapons and mass-surveillance concerns. An appeal is expected. *Disclosure, since it is the polite thing to do: Anthropic makes the model writing this briefing.* ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-pentagon-lawsuit-supply-chain-risk-f15e3c30186385e73e72bee82d85b05c), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/28/pentagon-blacklisted-anthropic-over-claude-powers-it-didnt-have/5293266))

**FDA cleared the 2026-27 COVID vaccines, on much narrower terms.** Moderna's Spikevax and mNexspike, Pfizer-BioNTech, and Novavax-Sanofi's Nuvaxovid, all reformulated against XFG, a recombinant omicron variant running at roughly 40% of US cases in July. Unlike prior years there is no universal recommendation: Spikevax for 6 months-64 with a high-risk condition plus all 65+, Pfizer for 5-64 with a condition plus 65+, mNexspike and Nuvaxovid for 12-64 with a condition plus 65+. ACIP has not voted, which leaves insurance coverage obligations genuinely ambiguous heading into the season - worth knowing before your first clinic conversation about it. It is flying about nine months ahead of its committed schedule, which almost never happens. ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/roman/2026/08/28/nasas-roman-space-telescope-go-for-launch/))

## Worth Reading

### Rachel Aviv, "The Scientist Who Lived with Dozens of Children from the South Pacific", The New Yorker

The one to read this weekend. Aviv reconstructs Carleton Gajdusek, who won the 1976 Nobel for identifying kuru among the Fore of Papua New Guinea, brought dozens of Melanesian and Micronesian children back to Maryland, educated them, and sexually abused some of them. She reports from both ends - the surviving men he raised, and the American scientific establishment that heard the allegations and either rationalized them as cultural relativism or declined to believe them. It is less a portrait of a monster than an anatomy of how institutional prestige, the ethical fog of fieldwork, and the romance of the heroic investigator combine to make certain harms literally unspeakable. For a physician it is the sharpest recent case study in how research medicine metabolizes its own misconduct, and how the people used as evidence live afterward. About 13,000 words and worth every one. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/24/the-scientist-who-lived-with-dozens-of-children-from-the-south-pacific)

### Andrew Marantz, "How Far Will the Trump Administration Go to Deport Mahmoud Khalil?", The New Yorker

Marantz embeds with Khalil and his family in the legal limbo following his detention, tracing the Columbia protest movement, his upbringing in a Palestinian family in Syria, and the machinery of a deportation case built on speech rather than conduct. The real subject underneath is how far executive power can stretch immigration law to punish political expression, and what precedent that sets. It has drawn a genuinely polarized reaction, which is itself an argument for reading it firsthand rather than through summary. The most serious critique from sympathetic readers is that Marantz stays so close to his subject that the analytic frame goes thin - fair, and worth holding as you read. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/24/how-far-will-the-trump-administration-go-to-deport-mahmoud-khalil)

### Christopher Cox, "What if America Went Completely Dark?", NYT Magazine

A reported feature on grid fragility: aging infrastructure, a multi-year transformer shortage, and demand growth driven substantially by AI data centres. Cox enters through the 2013 sniper attack on a Silicon Valley substation and then walks the cascade of a true national blackout - water and food systems degrading within days, communications gone, hospitals running on finite diesel. The hospital-medicine implications are unavoidable and rarely spelled out this concretely. If you have ever wondered what your institution's actual failure mode looks like, it is in here. [Read](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/18/magazine/national-blackout-power-electricity-outage.html)

The magazine gave her the Sept 7 cover. Probably the right register for a Saturday.
