## International

### The Iran war widens into the Gulf and the Mediterranean

Iran's regular army and the IRGC announced drone strikes early Friday on US military assets at Kuwait's Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base and Sheikh Isa Air Base in southern Bahrain, framing both as retaliation for a heavy overnight wave of American strikes on IRGC targets across Iran. Kuwait's military said it destroyed Iranian drones in its airspace and reported material damage but no casualties, which directly contradicts Iranian claims of hits on hangars and satellite communications. One worker was reported killed at a Chinese company's building in Kuwait. damage claims unverified Every battle-damage figure in this war currently comes from either IRNA/Fars or CENTCOM; treat all of it as claimed, not established. Iranian state media also said US strikes killed a couple and their two-year-old child in Chah Tangu on Qeshm Island. Al Jazeera, Times of Israel liveblog, CNBC, July 31. [Al Jazeera →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/irgc-strikes-us-targets-in-kuwait-a-day-after-us-hits-iran-latest-events)

The more consequential development may be geographic. A drone struck the US-owned LNG carrier *Energos Winter* at Damietta on Egypt's Mediterranean coast, setting a fire that spread to the Greek-owned *GasLog Salem*. No casualties; the port is back at capacity. **No group has claimed it and the Houthis explicitly denied involvement**, do not assign blame to anyone yet. El-Sissi warned of regional escalation, and analysts immediately flagged what the strike implies for the Suez corridor, currently the last comparatively safe export route for Saudi crude. Iran continues to insist the Strait of Hormuz is closed, and the IRGC claimed Friday to have struck or blocked two tankers transiting under US air escort, a single-source claim with no vessel names or crew status. CNBC, UPI, The War Zone, July 30. [The War Zone →](https://www.twz.com/news-features/drone-strike-on-egyptian-port-on-mediterranean-stokes-concerns-iran-conflict-will-widen)

### Saudi Arabia goes public, and builds a coalition

Joint US–Saudi strikes on Iran-backed militia logistics in Iraq marked Riyadh's first public acknowledgment of direct participation, after earlier covert action and Houthi drone attacks on Saudi targets including Abha Airport. Notably, Tehran declined to name Saudi Arabia in its response statement even after Saudi officials owned the role, a signal that both sides may want to keep the Saudi front deniable. Separately, Riyadh hosted military chiefs from 43 countries on Thursday and got 14 to sign a declaration on a Saudi-led, Saudi-headquartered maritime defence alliance for the Red Sea, Bab al-Mandab and Gulf of Aden: Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Pakistan, Türkiye, Egypt, Jordan, Yemen, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Sudan, Djibouti and Somalia. It is a framework, not a force: no operational date, contributions or command arrangements have been published. FT, Al Jazeera, AP, Foreign Policy, July 29–30. ### Ceuta: 49,000 in one day

This is the number to sit with. Spain's national security department said "more than 49,000" people entered the enclave of Ceuta on Thursday, more than half the local population of about 83,000, and nearly five times the daily peak of the entire 2015 migration crisis. Most swam around a breakwater to reach a beach. Security forces recovered 19 bodies from the water, per a statement posted at 8am Friday and then taken down; earlier official counts said at least 9 dead, so the toll is still moving. Spain deployed the army, declined Ceuta's request for a national emergency declaration, and blamed smuggling networks exploiting a Spanish Supreme Court ruling this month restricting immediate removal of sea arrivals. Analysts read Rabat's hand in it: the enclaves are Morocco's standing leverage over Madrid, as in 2021 over Western Sahara. Thousands began returning voluntarily once told they would not be allowed to stay; Italy has called for EU travel restrictions. FT (Barney Jopson, updated 05:59 July 31), AP, CBC. [FT →](https://www.ft.com/content/34917d6f-c8ec-4e13-890a-4d8475806bb7)

### A Russian missile lands in Poland

During an overnight barrage that reached as far west as Lviv, a projectile that Donald Tusk said "all indications suggest" was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile flew roughly 90km into eastern Poland and exploded near Tarnawa-Kolonia, leaving a large crater and no injuries. NATO scrambled fighters, tankers and AWACS; Mark Rutte called it "yet another reckless act." Tusk was careful: a very serious incident, but "absolutely no reason to believe Poland was the target," and Poland was prepared to shoot it down had it continued. In Ukraine the toll climbed through the day, at least 8 in early reporting, later counts of 10 to 13, including six in Kryvyi Rih among them girls aged 5 and 12. toll still developing Russia also targeted hundreds of Ukrainian petrol stations, per an FT exclusive. Meanwhile Trump said he is "not sure" he will let Ukraine manufacture Patriot missiles after earlier saying he would, a live problem for Kyiv's air defence pipeline. ### Two disasters in Pakistan

A methane explosion in the Sorange coalfield outside Quetta, Balochistan, during a shift change killed at least 34 miners; 32 bodies were recovered by Friday with two remaining. Families were offered 500,000 rupees (about \$1,800) each. Balochistan's mines are chronically lethal, poor ventilation, weak gas monitoring, minimal rescue equipment. Separately, an avalanche struck a summit team at about 6,600m on Broad Peak on Thursday morning, leaving ten climbers missing including **Nirmal "Nimsdai" Purja**, 43, the former Royal Navy SBS commando who in 2024 became the fastest person to climb all fourteen eight-thousanders without supplemental oxygen. Four bodies have been recovered; two are identified, and neither is Purja. Purja is missing, not confirmed dead Weather has grounded helicopter searches. AP/WaPo, Reuters, CNN, Al Jazeera, Kathmandu Post, July 30–31. [Al Jazeera →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/nepali-climber-purja-9-others-missing-in-pakistan-after-avalanche)

## Israel

### A deal announced that neither party has accepted

Trump announced on social media a "HISTORIC agreement for the COMPLETE DISARMAMENT of Hamas," brokered by the US-led Board of Peace: Hamas surrenders weapons and destroys tunnels while Israel withdraws in phases as disarmament is verified. Reported terms include a 14-day window to finalise timelines, full disarmament in roughly 200–350 days, a 5,000-strong International Stabilization Force, a technocratic National Committee for the Administration of Gaza replacing Hamas governance, a new Palestinian police force, and \$16bn in Gulf reconstruction pledges. Hamas negotiators reportedly signed a disarmament framework, a first.

Then the floor gave way. **Netanyahu's office said Israel cannot accept the proposal**, arguing the Board of Peace's 15-point plan fails Israel's demand for complete demilitarisation *prior* to withdrawal; the reported sticking point is that Hamas weapons would be stored under Palestinian rather than Israeli control. A senior US official warned Trump would be "very disappointed" if Israel obstructs it, adding: "We're not asking Israel to do anything that isn't in the 20-point plan they agreed to initially." Hamas's Ghazi Hamad mirrored the Israeli position from the other side: "We will not take a single step unless we are certain that Israel will also carry out its commitments," and Hamas will not disarm before withdrawal. do not call this a concluded deal No sequencing mechanism, verification body, enforcement provision or ISF troop contributors have been named, and outlets are using "15-point" and "20-point" for two different documents while giving disarmament timelines of both 6–8 months and 200–350 days. Israeli airstrikes wounded Palestinians in Gaza within hours of the announcement. FT, NPR, NBC, CNN, Times of Israel, July 30–31. ### A mutiny at Sde Teiman, and an election in ninety days

About 100 soldiers from the Givati Brigade's Tzabar Battalion walked off their Sde Teiman base Thursday after a dispute with a commander who had removed signage the soldiers considered insulting to new troops. ## Economic & Markets

### A slow headline hiding strong demand

BEA's advance estimate had Q2 real GDP at 1.5 percent annualised, down from 2.1 percent and below a roughly 2.1 percent consensus. But the composition cuts the other way: consumer spending accelerated to 3.2 percent from 0.5 percent, and real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 3.9 percent from 1.7 percent. The drag was imports and lower government spending. The inflation lines in the same release were uncomfortable, gross domestic purchases prices up 5.7 percent, PCE 5.1 percent, core PCE 3.4 percent annualised. Then June PCE, out the same morning, came in softer: headline minus 0.1 percent on the month and 3.7 percent year over year (from 4.1), core plus 0.1 percent and 3.3 percent, with goods prices down 0.6 percent, the biggest drop since November 2023, and services inflation slowing to 0.1 percent. Cooling, still well above target. BEA, July 30. [BEA →](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-2nd-quarter-2026)

### The bond market's verdict on Kevin Warsh

This is the story with the longest tail. The FOMC held at 3.5 percent on July 29 with **three governors dissenting in favour of higher rates**. Markets did not take the press conference well: critics said Warsh failed to explain why the Fed wasn't hiking with inflation above 4 percent on the then-latest May data and five years above target, and objected to his characterising weakening indicators as "strong." The S&P fell 1.52 percent and the 30-year yield jumped to 5.23 percent, its highest since 2007. Bank of America's Mark Cabana called it "a classic central-bank credibility shock"; Jon Hilsenrath's version was blunter, "the bond market puked on him." The FT editorial board wrote on Warsh's "concerning communication style," and investors told the paper his stripped-back communications approach is "already backfiring" by eroding the Fed's influence over the Treasury market. repricing characterised differently across outlets CME data showed roughly 65 percent odds of a September *hike* per Fortune, while CNBC reported investors lowering hike odds even as the long end sold off; pricing moved intraday, so don't over-read either. One sequencing note if you see the numbers quoted: the 4.1 percent PCE figure in the Warsh criticism is May data, the latest available at the meeting. June's 3.7 percent landed the next morning. FT, CNBC, Bloomberg, Fortune, July 29–30. [CNBC →](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/kevin-warsh-fed-treasury-yields-inflation-credibility-interest-rates.html)
