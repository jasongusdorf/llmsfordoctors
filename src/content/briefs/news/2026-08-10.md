## International

### Ukraine strikes 1,000 km deep; Russia rejects a freeze on the eve of US envoy visits

Ukrainian long-range drones hit Nizhnekamsk in Russia's Tatarstan region overnight, roughly 1,000 to 1,200 km from Ukrainian territory, killing 13 people including a child and wounding about 75 according to regional authorities. Ukraine's General Staff confirmed striking the TANECO refinery, one of Russia's principal refining complexes, and drones reportedly also hit the Nizhnekamskneftekhim petrochemical plant. Russia's Defense Ministry claimed it downed 456 drones across 16 regions, a figure that cannot be independently verified. The strike caps a roughly 40-day intensified deep-strike campaign that has produced fuel shortages inside Russia. Diplomatically, deputy foreign minister Mikhail Galuzin dismissed the idea of freezing the front, saying "no freeze is possible without addressing the root causes of this crisis" and that Zelensky "has lost all touch with reality," language landing just before Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected in both capitals. NPR, NBC News, Kyiv Independent, Aug 10. [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-rules-out-freezing-war-in-ukraine-ahead-of-us-envoy-visits-to-kyiv-moscow/)

### Hormuz: Trump says the US is "only semi-negotiating"

Trump said Washington is "only semi-negotiating" with Tehran and will let economic pressure build rather than escalate militarily, having earlier canceled planned strikes. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Oman-mediated talks are "approaching the final stages" but cautioned this "is not a sign of the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz." Iran's six conditions are expansive: an end to US attacks on Iran and its allies, lifting the port blockade, withdrawal of US forces from the region, sanctions relief, unfreezing of assets, and war-damage compensation. Washington's stated ask is unrestricted commercial navigation with no Iranian tolls or approvals, a gap that is not obviously bridgeable. Despite the talks, Iran reportedly targeted an ADNOC-owned vessel in the strait on Saturday. Al Jazeera liveblog, CBS, CNN, Aug 9-10. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/10/iran-war-live-us-semi-negotiating-with-tehran-israel-rejects-gaza-plan)

### Houthis hit Aramco's Jazan refinery days after the Mecca defence pact

A Houthi drone started a fire at Aramco's 400,000 bpd Jazan refinery on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast early Sunday. Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed the blaze was extinguished with no casualties, and Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said recent attacks have caused some production interruption but no material operational or financial impact. The Houthis framed it as retaliation for Saudi drone incursions over Saada and Hajjah, and separately claimed an operation against Saudi-aligned forces at the Red Sea port of Mokha; Houthi strikes inside Yemen killed at least 11 that day, though early figures ranged from seven. The timing matters: it came two days after the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement signed August 7 by Mohammed bin Salman, Erdogan and Shehbaz Sharif, a trilateral mutual-defence pact extending the September 2025 Saudi-Pakistan arrangement to include Turkey. Analysts read it as a hedge against a diminished US security guarantee in the Gulf, and Tehran cited it Monday as evidence of a regional "change in perception" toward Washington. Reuters, Al Jazeera, NBC, NPR, Aug 9. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/saudi-arabia-says-fire-extinguished-at-aramco-facility-in-jizan)

### Iran elevates Mohsen Rezaei to its top security job

President Masoud Pezeshkian appointed Mohsen Rezaei, 71, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, with the Supreme Leader issuing a concurrent decree naming him his personal representative on the body, a dual mandate that makes the appointment unusually weighty. Rezaei commanded the IRGC from 1981 to 1997, through the Iran-Iraq war, and has argued that control of Hormuz is worth more than "dozens of atomic bombs." He replaces Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr; the previous secretary, Ali Larijani, was reported killed in a US-Israeli strike in March. Analysts split on whether this elevates military judgment for a longer war or provides hardline cover for an eventual deal. Given the Hormuz negotiations, this is the personnel move to watch this week. Al Jazeera, Aug 10. ### Storm Dolphin batters eastern China; Europe logs its hottest June-July

Dolphin made landfall late Sunday at Yuhuan in Zhejiang with maximum sustained winds of 151 km/h, arriving as a typhoon before weakening. China activated a red alert, its highest typhoon warning. More than a million people were evacuated, including over 900,000 in Wenzhou, nearly 100,000 in Shanghai and 99,000 in Fujian; Shanghai's two airports cancelled 943 flights, cutting capacity by roughly 40 percent. Parts of Zhejiang were forecast for 250 to 500 mm of rain, with the system tracking toward Hubei and Henan. Separately, the EU's Copernicus service reported Monday that Western Europe just had its hottest June-July on record, averaging 21.62C, 2.79C above normal and beating 2022. Non-polar sea surface temperatures set a July record at 20.96C. The consequences are already tangible: France's Gironde megafire burned nearly 42,000 hectares and displaced more than 220,000 people, Spain's Avila fire burned roughly 44,000, the Seine, Rhine and Danube hit critically low levels, and Hungary and Slovenia cut nuclear output for want of cooling water. Al Jazeera, Euronews, Copernicus, Aug 10. [Copernicus report](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/10/june-and-july-hottest-on-record-in-western-europe-monitor-says)

### Kashmir votes under lockdown

The final phase of Azad Jammu and Kashmir's 53-seat assembly election went ahead Monday in only four of eleven scheduled constituencies, in Bagh and Haveli districts, with about 460,000 registered voters; seven constituencies were postponed on security grounds. The unrest stems from Joint Awami Action Committee protests over twelve seats reserved for displaced Kashmiris. Residents and rights groups say more than 50 people have been killed; the government says at least seven police and paramilitary personnel died. JAAC was banned in June under anti-terrorism laws and an internet shutdown across Poonch division has run more than 50 days. Turnout was 46 percent in phase one and 53 percent in phase two, well below the 60-plus of 2016 and 2021, and international journalists were ordered out. Al Jazeera, Aug 10. ## United States

### Blanche confirmed Attorney General 50-49 in a pre-dawn Saturday vote

The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche, Trump's former personal criminal defense lawyer, as attorney general at 4:31 a.m. Saturday, just before leaving for a five-week recess. Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski voted no; Bill Cassidy announced Friday he would supply the deciding vote, saying he preferred a confirmed AG to an acting one despite what he called Blanche's "poor judgment." Mitch McConnell, absent since a June fall, missed the vote. Blanche had been confirmed deputy attorney general in March without GOP opposition and became acting AG in April when Pam Bondi departed. Collins said "the Department of Justice has become increasingly political"; Murkowski said she doubted he would "check the worst impulses of this administration." The objections centered on a \$1.8 billion DOJ anti-weaponization fund rescinded the previous weekend, handling of the Epstein files, and reported tax-audit immunity for the president and his family. Roll Call, PBS NewsHour, NPR, Washington Post, Aug 8. [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/08/08/blanche-senate-confirmation-vote-attorney-general/)

### ICE promises universal body cameras, keeps discretion over the footage

ICE said Saturday it expects every field officer and agent to be equipped with a body-worn camera by the end of August, a rapid expansion that would create an unprecedented visual record of federal immigration enforcement. Per AP, the catch is that release remains largely at the agency's discretion, with provisions letting leadership withhold or indefinitely delay footage even after serious injuries or deaths in custody. ICE publicly disputed AP's characterization of the release policy. The announcement follows two fatal shootings by ICE officers in July, a 25-year-old motorist in Maine shot by an officer with a documented history of violent behavior and a Houston homebuilder shot on his way to work. Neither was recorded on body camera. AP, carried by PBS, CNN, CNBC, Boston Globe, Aug 8-9. [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/ice-says-it-expects-to-equip-every-field-officer-with-body-cameras-by-the-end-of-august)

### DC Circuit blocks the White House ballroom; SCOTUS appeal imminent

A 2-1 panel of the DC Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction halting construction of a \$400 million, 90,000-square-foot ballroom on the site of the demolished White House East Wing. "Each President is a temporary tenant, not the owner, of the White House," the panel wrote, and cannot fundamentally reshape it without congressional authorization. The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued after the East Wing was torn down and construction began without congressional approval. The court stayed its own ruling for 14 days, expiring around August 21, to allow an emergency appeal, and Trump said he will go to the Supreme Court immediately, arguing the ruling "jeopardizes lives of future presidents." SCOTUSblog flags this as one of the administration's next likely emergency-docket asks. NBC News, CNBC, SCOTUSblog, ruling Aug 7. [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/white-house/appeals-court-blocks-trump-ballroom-construction-rcna591376)

### HHS moves to deregulate Head Start

HHS proposed a rule stripping a wide range of federal health and education mandates from Head Start, including requirements on class size, expulsion policy and health screening, shifting authority to states and local grantees. The department projects the change would free \$2.2 billion and let the program serve up to 268,000 additional children, and proposes cutting the administrative cost cap from 15 percent to 5 percent. Providers and early-childhood researchers told the Washington Post it would strip out precisely the comprehensive health, nutrition and family-services model that distinguishes Head Start from ordinary preschool. The rule enters a 60-day comment period. NPR, Washington Post, K-12 Dive, Aug 6-9. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/nx-s1-5920386/head-start-regulations-trump)

## Israel

### Netanyahu rejects the 15-point plan, then reverses a pullout he had approved

At Sunday's cabinet meeting Netanyahu said flatly, "Israel rejects the 15-point document," adding that "the IDF will not make any withdrawal until Hamas is truly disarmed" and specifying that disarmament must cover "heavy weaponry, lighter weaponry, all weaponry," and be "genuine, not fictitious." The framework, announced July 30 by the US-led Board of Peace, would begin with removal of Hamas heavy weapons, production sites, storage and tunnels, paired with phased IDF pullbacks, eventually placing Gaza under a technocratic Palestinian committee. Hamas says it remains committed to full implementation. The sharper detail came Sunday night: Israeli security officials told Israeli media that Netanyahu had personally approved a pilot IDF withdrawal from three small areas near Rafah, discussed with Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir and cleared with Washington, then reversed himself within roughly 24 hours, with officials reportedly learning of the reversal from the press. Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov said Monday that only "full, verified implementation" guarantees "this tragedy will not occur again." Washington Post, CNN, CBS, Bloomberg, Al Jazeera, Times of Israel, Haaretz, Aug 9-10. [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/08/09/netanyahu-rejects-trump-backed-plan-hamas-disarm-israel-leave-gaza/)

The Rafah reversal rests on unnamed Israeli security officials via Channel 12 and Times of Israel; credible but single-track. Channel 12 also reports the White House is not alarmed, reading the rejection as election politics, and notes Israel has not struck Gaza for five days. The White House had not formally responded as of Monday morning. Gaza casualty totals cited by Al Jazeera come from Gaza health authorities and are disputed by Israel.

### Lebanon talks hit a wall over the pilot zones

Lebanese and Israeli negotiators are unlikely to reconvene before the end of summer following the seventh US-brokered round in Rome, August 4 to 7, which was described as fruitful technically but produced no movement on the two core disputes: expanding the pilot zones and Hezbollah's withdrawal from the Ali al-Taher heights. The pilot scheme covers three villages in its first stage, Zawtar al-Gharbiyeh, Srifa and Froun, where the IDF pulls back and the Lebanese Armed Forces move in to verify disarmament. Lebanon wants to move immediately to new areas; Israel insists on verifying the first two zones first. The Rome round was itself suspended mid-week over events on the ground before resuming. ### Likud edges back ahead; Arab parties fail to unify

A Kan poll published Sunday puts Likud back on top at 24 seats, with Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar at 23 and Naftali Bennett's Together at 13. The coalition bloc gained a seat to 53; the opposition bloc sits at 56, still short of 61 without the Arab parties. That reverses the August 6 poll, which had Yashar ahead. On Saturday the Arab parties failed to agree a joint slate after Ta'al rejected a reconciliation proposal, a meaningful blow to the anti-Netanyahu bloc's path to a majority. ### West Bank settler violence up sharply

Data reported Monday shows 660 settler-attack incidents in the first half of 2026 against 405 in the same period of 2025, a 63 percent rise, with six Palestinians killed and about 140 wounded. The IDF closed the Christian village of Taybeh, east of Ramallah, to Israeli nonresidents in response to settler violence, with reports of settlers massing near Al-Mughayyir ahead of Palestinian agricultural activity and further attacks in Beita. Human Rights Watch warned in late July that the surge "threatens mass atrocities," and UN officials have described settler violence at an all-time high. Note that Al Jazeera's own July tracking uses a different methodology and yields a 24 percent rise, so the two figures should be attributed separately rather than blended. Also Monday: the cabinet moved to approve an additional NIS 2.6 billion, about \$700 million, for urgent classified Defense Ministry procurement, and a new program will offer roughly 20,000 discounted apartments or land parcels to IDF reservists over 2026-27. ## Also notable

### Rachel Naomi Remen, MD, dies at 87

Remen was a pediatrician who became the most influential humanist voice in American medical education. She wrote *Kitchen Table Wisdom* and *My Grandfather's Blessings*, co-founded the Commonweal Cancer Help Program, and created The Healer's Art, a curriculum on meaning, grief and loss in medicine now taught at a majority of US medical schools and completed by more than 30,000 students. She died at home on hospice of breast cancer, which she had survived nearly a decade, having lived for decades with Crohn's disease diagnosed at 15. That patient experience grounded everything she taught physicians. Of everything in this brief, this is the one most likely to matter to your working life: she is a large part of why a generation of doctors has language for what the job costs them. Reported via Wikipedia's Deaths in 2026 listing and a tribute by Lissa Rankin, MD, Aug 8. [Tribute](https://lissarankinmd.substack.com/p/the-most-influential-person-in-my)

Not yet confirmed by a major obituary desk as of this morning; sourcing is Wikipedia plus a first-person tribute. Treat as very likely but not wire-confirmed.

### Don Nelson dies at 86; Blue Origin finds its valve; two nutrition studies

Don Nelson, who retired as the second-winningest coach in NBA history, a three-time Coach of the Year and a five-time champion as a Celtics player, died Sunday. He invented the point forward and pushed the small-ball, pace-and-space style the modern league now takes for granted, first at Milwaukee and then at Golden State and Dallas, and pushed the Warriors to draft Curry in 2009. In space, Blue Origin traced the May 28 static-fire explosion that destroyed a New Glenn first stage and damaged Launch Complex 36 to the main oxygen valve on a BE-4 engine; the fix is a small retrofittable modification, but because ULA's Vulcan uses the same engine the fault propagates across two national launch programs. On the medical side, a 12-week randomized study found adults eating three daily servings of full-fat dairy showed no meaningful increase in weight, body fat or cholesterol, short and small enough not to move guidelines but useful at the bedside, and a 340,000-adult cohort found heavy drinking raises mortality regardless of beverage type. A separate finding this week: frequent cannabis users show abnormally high waking cortisol, alongside data on slower cognitive growth in adolescent users. ESPN, CNN, SpaceNews, ScienceDaily, Aug 5-9. ## Worth reading (New Yorker & long-form)

### "The Future, Made in China", Evan Osnos

Letter from Beijing, August 10 issue. Osnos reports from inside China on the contest with the US over semiconductors and AI, arguing it is not a trade dispute but a wager about which political system can better convert science into national capability. His method is to route macro-strategy through individual engineers, officials and entrepreneurs, so it reads as reportage rather than think-tank analysis. The most useful counterweight available to the US-centric AI coverage everyone is swimming in. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china)

### "The Mentor: How Roy Cohn Taught Donald Trump Everything", Kai Bird and Susan Goldmark

American Chronicles, August 10 issue, excerpted from their forthcoming *American Scoundrel*. The argument is that Trump's political method is inherited rather than improvised, a specific curriculum Cohn taught across the 1970s and 80s: never defend, always counter-attack; delay litigation indefinitely; avoid a paper trail; reframe every loss as a win; build networks on personal loyalty rather than institutional rules. Cohn's operative maxim was that winning was not enough, people had to be told you won, which makes narrative dominance the actual objective in every conflict. Bird, who co-wrote *American Prometheus*, writes biography as intellectual genealogy, and the payoff is that "Where's my Roy Cohn?" stops sounding like a quip and starts sounding like a man missing his instructor. [Read](https://messenger.rsn.org/focus-kai-bird-and-susan-goldmark-the-mentor/)

### "The Man Who Was Kidnapped Twice", Alexis Okeowo

Our Far-Flung Correspondents, August 10 issue. Okeowo on Nigeria's kidnapping-for-ransom economy, in which bandit groups hold thousands captive as routine business rather than aberration. The narrative spine follows a man abducted twice, an Abuja lecturer and politician taken from his car. The interest is structural: how ransom became a functioning parallel market, what it does to families forced to negotiate, and what the state's absence from that market means. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-man-who-was-kidnapped-twice)

Also in the issue: Anthony Lane on books, fiction by Rachel Yoder. The prior August 3 issue carried a profile of Gadi Eisenkot shaped by the loss of his son in Gaza, which given his current polling position is newly relevant.
