## International

### Trump calls off the Iran strike and claims a deal; Tehran says no talks are scheduled

Al Jazeera, Bloomberg, NPR, CNBC, Aug 2-3 · [read →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/trump-says-new-iran-negotiations-starting-what-we-know)

Trump announced on Truth Social that he had cancelled an imminent attack on Iran because “the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to,” including the immediate and total reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear program, and told reporters talks would begin Monday. He named no venue and no participants. On Monday morning Tehran contradicted him on the record: foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said there is no immediate plan for direct talks with Washington, and that Iran's only live negotiation is with Oman over a temporary safe-passage corridor through the Strait, which Tehran calls close to final. Flag: these two accounts are directly incompatible and neither is independently verified. Trump has announced strike halts before that did not hold, and Iran's Mehr agency called his negotiation claims “a new lie.” Treat the whole thread as unresolved.

### Israel says it found out about the cancelled strike from social media

Times of Israel citing Channel 12, Iran International, Aug 2 · [read →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-2-2026/)

A senior US official confirmed Israel was slated to join the aborted operation against Iranian energy and infrastructure targets, planning for which began after Netanyahu's White House visit. Israeli officials say they were not notified of the stand-down. A senior Israeli official told Channel 12: “For hours, we were in real uncertainty. President Trump left us in the fog. Senior officers were updated about what was happening from Trump's social media.” Trump says he stood down at the request of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Iran; Israeli reporting attributes it more specifically to Qatari pressure and an MBS warning about escalation against Gulf oil facilities. The motive attribution is thinner than the core fact.

### The icebreaker story: \$9bn in no-bid contracts traced to a golf game

New York Times, Aug 1 · [read →](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/01/climate/trump-arctic-ships.html)

The Times traces Trump's fixation on Arctic vessels to a chance encounter in Helsinki thirty years ago and, more proximately, to a 2025 golf tournament he won alongside Finnish President Alexander Stubb, after which he announced an icebreaker agreement. The order grew to 11 ships, roughly twice what the Coast Guard said it needed, at nearly \$7bn, and about \$9bn in Arctic Security Cutter contracts were awarded without competitive bidding under a rarely used public-interest exemption. Building the first hulls in Finland required waiving a statute barring overseas construction of national-security vessels. Contract awards and shipyard splits (Bollinger in Louisiana, Davie in Helsinki and Texas) are confirmed in trade press; the no-bid framing is NYT-only.

### Head Start to be deregulated rather than defunded

New York Times, The Bulwark, Jul 31 · [read →](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/nation-politics/trump-administration-plans-to-upend-head-start-by-deregulating-it/)

A rule expected as soon as this week would strike the bulk of Head Start's 133 pages of federal performance standards: class-size limits, teacher-child ratios, curriculum requirements, medical and developmental screenings, home visits. Funding for the program, which serves more than 700,000 low-income children, would be preserved, with authority shifted to states and local providers. New mandates would be added, including English-only instruction and 30 minutes of daily physical activity. Earlier draft reporting from The Bulwark described an enrollment restriction that would exclude some US-citizen children whose parents lack qualifying status. The text is under OMB review and remains pre-decisional.

ICE took more than 46,000 people into custody in July, up from about 43,000 in June and a record for the second term, averaging roughly 1,500 a day against a White House target of 2,000. DHS departure and deportation totals are administration claims and not independently verified.

### The mail-voting circuit split lands at the Supreme Court today

The Hill, SCOTUSblog, CBS News, briefs due Aug 3 · [read →](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/07/trump-administration-asks-supreme-court-to-clear-the-way-for-new-mail-in-voting-restrictions/)

Two appeals courts have ruled in opposite directions on Trump's executive order restricting vote-by-mail and directing USPS and DHS to compile federal voter lists. The First Circuit upheld an injunction blocking it across 23 suing states; days later a D.C. Circuit panel allowed implementation. Justice Jackson set today as the deadline for briefs on whether the Court should take the case. With the midterms roughly three months out, states need settled rules to print ballots and set mail deadlines, which makes this among the most consequential election-law questions of the cycle.

Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post, Ynet, Aug 2-3 · [read →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/katz-announces-hes-replacing-top-west-bank-general-idf-says-hell-stay-in-his-role/)
