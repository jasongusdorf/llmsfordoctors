**TL;DR.** The US–Iran war is moving toward a pause: Trump and Vance virtually signed a 60-day framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with a formal Geneva ceremony slated for Friday, markets ripped to records and oil sank. Attention now shifts to Ukraine as G7 leaders gather in Évian. The catch: Israel is unhappy, and Lebanon’s truce is more rumor than reality on the ground.

## International

### US and Iran virtually sign a framework to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz

The headline development of the cycle: on Sunday, June 15, President Trump and Vice President Vance digitally signed a memorandum of understanding with Iran, with Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf signing for Tehran. The deal lifts the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, reopens the Strait of Hormuz to all traffic, and freezes fighting on all fronts for 60 days while nuclear negotiations proceed toward a permanent settlement. A formal signing ceremony is scheduled for Geneva on Friday. The framing is deliberately ambiguous so both sides can claim victory; Iran's negotiators have publicly insisted they will retain control over Hormuz and have lashed out at fresh EU sanctions. Still developing: this is a framework/MOU, not a finalized treaty, and the 60-day window leaves the core nuclear and regional questions open. Sources: CNN live (Jun 15), NPR (Jun 15), NBC, ABC News, cross-confirmed.

### G7 opens in Évian, pivoting to Ukraine

The 52nd G7 summit runs June 15–17 in Évian-les-Bains, hosted by Macron. With the Iran deal taking some heat out of the Middle East file, Trump signaled the US wants to refocus on ending Russia's war in Ukraine. Zelensky is attending at France's invitation; leaders of Egypt, Qatar and the UAE are joining a dedicated Middle East stability session. France also invited Brazil, Egypt, India, Kenya and South Korea. Other agenda items: macroeconomic imbalances, critical-mineral supply chains, Chinese competition, and AI/child-safety online. A communiqué is expected when the summit closes Wednesday. Sources: NPR (Jun 16), Euronews live, Consilium/Élysée briefings.

### UK seizes a Russian “shadow fleet” tanker in the Channel

British forces seized a sanctions-evading, Russian-linked oil tanker (the *Smyrtos*) transiting the English Channel on Sunday. Keir Starmer called it a significant setback for Moscow's war financing; the Kremlin's Dmitry Peskov branded it illegal and “bordering on piracy.” It follows France's Atlantic seizure of the *Tagor* earlier in June, part of a coordinated Western campaign to throttle the network of off-the-books tankers Russia uses to export oil under sanctions. Sources: Al Jazeera (Jun 16), FT, ITV.

Also moving internationally: the French military, with UK Royal Navy support, intercepted a Russian shadow-fleet vessel in the Atlantic; the Philippines and Vietnam upgraded ties to an “enhanced strategic partnership” during Tô Lâm's Manila visit; and health authorities are responding to a new **Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo's Ituri province**, straining an already fragile system. Sources: Wikipedia current-events, Euronews, wires.

## US

### Fed opens its meeting, Warsh's debut as chair

The FOMC begins a two-day meeting today, June 16, the first chaired by Kevin Warsh, with the rate decision Wednesday. Despite Trump's pressure for cuts, he declared on June 9 that he “loved the inflation,” effectively giving Warsh cover to hold, markets widely expect rates unchanged, as still-elevated energy prices keep inflation hot. The wrinkle: if the Iran deal durably lowers oil, this quarter may mark the inflation peak, opening room to ease later in the year. The FT's editorial board framed it bluntly: Warsh will be judged on his integrity and independence. Sources: FT (FT View, Markets), Kiplinger, Barchart.

### Sam Bankman-Fried loses his appeal

The disgraced FTX founder lost his bid to overturn his fraud conviction, with the appeals court upholding both the verdict and sentence. Separately, former star bond investor Ken Leech pleaded guilty to obstructing a federal probe. Source: FT (US News).

On immigration, the Trump administration's restrictive posture continues to bite, NYT ran a wrenching feature on an Afghan family shattered by the visa ban now covering 39 countries, though there was no single breaking court ruling in the window. The New Yorker also previews the administration marking the nation's 250th birthday by tightening the path to citizenship. Sources: NYT, New Yorker.

## Israel

### Broad Israeli discontent over the emerging Iran deal

NYT's Isabel Kershner reports that Israelis across the political spectrum view the US–Iran framework as leaving Iran's fundamental security threats unaddressed, a rare cross-ideological unease. The mood underscores how far Trump's diplomacy has drifted from Netanyahu's maximalist aims after months of war. Steven Erlanger's analysis adds that Iran's newer, more militaristic leadership has “survived the worst” the US and Israel could deliver and is more willing to absorb risk, a harder negotiating partner than the pre-war regime. Sources: NYT Middle East (Kershner, Erlanger).

### Lebanon: a truce on paper, strikes on the ground

Lebanon woke Monday to reports, via Iranian and Pakistani officials, that the US–Iran ceasefire includes Lebanon. Reality is murkier: Hezbollah has not carried out operations since the deal was announced, and some displaced Lebanese began returning south, but Israeli officials reacted with defiance, vowing to keep striking Hezbollah if they deem it necessary, and a Lebanese drone strike on a car reportedly killed the driver. Single-thread / developing: whether the southern-Lebanon war is actually over remains unconfirmed. Netanyahu has said Israel halted attacks on Iran while stopping short of acknowledging a ceasefire. Sources: Al Jazeera (Jun 15–16), Reuters, NYT.

In domestic Israeli politics, Netanyahu media adviser **Jonatan Urich** was charged with endangering state security over leaking classified intelligence on Hamas negotiations to a German newspaper. Source: NYT (Odenheimer).

## Economic & Markets

### Records on Wall Street as the Iran deal lands

US equities surged on the ceasefire news June 15: the Dow rose 0.9% to a record 51,671, the S&P 500 gained 1.7% to 7,554, and the Nasdaq jumped 3.1% to 26,683. Oil fell on Trump's declaration that Hormuz would reopen shortly, the single most important macro variable here, since the war-driven oil spike has been the chief inflation risk. Strategists cautioned the rally hinges on the deal actually holding. Sources: TheStreet (Jun 15), FT Markets.

### SpaceX's blockbuster IPO and the first trillionaire

SpaceX debuted on US markets Friday, June 12, opening at \$150 (up from a \$135 IPO price) and closing up ~18% near \$159, for a valuation around \$2 trillion after a record ~\$75bn raise. The listing vaulted Elon Musk past \$1 trillion in net worth, the world's first trillionaire, worth roughly twice Bezos and Arnault combined. The FT frames it as a display of Musk's market dominance and the broader AI/space capital frenzy. Note: the debut was Friday, just outside the 24-hour window, but it remains the dominant markets story and led the FT front page through the weekend. Sources: FT, Bloomberg, Al Jazeera (Jun 12).

Other threads worth a glance: foreign buyers are piling into UK companies (deal value more than tripled year-on-year to a two-decade high); China is teeing up a digital-payments system to challenge the dollar; and investors are pouring into the \$100bn race for Venezuelan oil even as Venezuelans sour on Trump. Source: FT (Companies, Markets, World).
