TL;DR

The US–Iran pause is over. Tehran fired ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan Wednesday; Washington answered overnight with a heavy wave of strikes on dozens of IRGC targets, and US and Saudi jets hit Iran-backed militias in Iraq. Markets took it badly on top of a hawkish Fed: the Dow fell 1,153 points, the 30-year Treasury yield hit a 19-year high, and Brent jumped nearly 8% to about \$91. ## International

**The US–Iran war reignites after a nine-day lull.** Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles Wednesday at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base and a CENTCOM facility in Jordan; Jordanian air defenses said they intercepted five. Trump, meeting Netanyahu at the White House, told Fox News "we'll be hitting them hard," and by early Thursday CENTCOM said it had completed a "heavy wave of strikes" against dozens of IRGC targets inside Iran, command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance sites. Iranian state media reported at least three deaths; those figures are not independently verified. This ends the pause Trump declared after 13 days of intensive bombing, during which he had claimed "good talks" with Tehran. Oman's proposal for joint management of the Strait of Hormuz was rejected by Iran. *(Washington Post, AP/NPR, Al Jazeera, CNN, July 29–30; well corroborated.)* [Washington Post →](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/07/29/us-strikes-iran-response-surprise-ballistic-missile-launch/)

**The conflict is spilling across the region.** US and Saudi aircraft jointly struck Iran-backed militia targets in Iraq, killing at least 20 fighters, after CENTCOM said the militias launched more than 30 drones at American troops and Saudi oil facilities in three days; Iraq's presidency condemned the strikes on its soil. The IRGC also hit three tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, the Houthis attacked Saudi facilities and shipping near Bab al-Mandeb, and a drone struck a US-owned tanker at Egypt's Damietta port. CENTCOM says its naval blockade has now redirected 20 commercial vessels and boarded or disabled four. *(NPR/AP, Gulf News, ABC, July 29.)* [NPR →](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/29/g-s1-136063/us-saudi-arabia-iran-iraq)

**Russia's largest barrage in weeks kills at least eight in Ukraine and pulls in Poland.** More than 70 missiles and roughly 280 drones hit Kyiv, Lviv, Poltava and Kryvyi Rih overnight, a day after Zelenskyy warned an attack was coming. At least eight people were killed, among them two girls aged 5 and 12 in Dnipropetrovsk; 34 were wounded in Lviv alone, including three children. A Russian Kh-101 cruise missile crossed into Polish airspace, prompting Warsaw to scramble fighter jets; Prime Minister Tusk said it came down in an uninhabited area. Moscow says it targeted air bases and arms plants. The EU disbursed €3.47bn in fresh military aid. Casualty figures were still rising at press time. *(Al Jazeera, NPR, Meduza, July 30.)* [Al Jazeera →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/30/russia-ballistic-missile-strikes-on-kyiv-come-after-zelenskyys-warning)

**Japan's Kumamoto quake toll reaches 25.** Two days after the M7.1 struck Kyushu, at least 25 are confirmed dead with nine further deaths under investigation and 86 injured, five seriously. Rescuers are still working the partially collapsed shopping mall in Kashima Town where employees were trapped. The tsunami advisory has been lifted and bullet-train service is resuming. *(NPR, Al Jazeera, Washington Post.)* [NPR →](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/30/nx-s1-5913050/japan-earthquake-updates)

**The DR Congo Ebola outbreak is now the fastest-growing on record.** WHO's Africa office reports 3,262 confirmed Bundibugyo virus cases and 1,437 deaths as of July 26, 62 new cases and 32 new deaths in a single reporting day, with 723 patients hospitalized in isolation. Ituri province accounts for 2,901 cases and 1,207 deaths across 28 of 36 health zones. Bundibugyo is the strain with no licensed vaccine or approved therapeutic, which is a large part of why the case fatality rate is sitting north of 40%. The linked Ugandan outbreak appears to be ending. Declared a PHEIC in May. *(WHO AFRO Situation Report 11, Al Jazeera, UN News.)* [WHO AFRO →](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/democratic-republic-of-congo/publication/ebola-bundibugyo-virus-disease-outbreak-3)

**Washington bans new Chinese robots on security grounds.** The FCC barred imports of new-model foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots and power inverters, citing critical-infrastructure risk and naming Unitree, Huawei and Sungrow. Already-authorized models are untouched. China's embassy called it "smearing" and promised countermeasures. China holds roughly 85% of the global humanoid-robot market, and the timing, weeks before a planned September Trump–Xi meeting, is pointed. *(Reuters via Al Jazeera, CNN Business, July 29.)* [Al Jazeera →](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/29/us-bans-imports-of-new-chinese-robots-over-security-concerns)

**Iberian and French wildfires meet a fourth heatwave.** Fires that began in Gironde have burned roughly 104,000 acres in France and 190,000 in Spain, with close to 300,000 people evacuated. Many are now contained, but Spain has entered its fourth heatwave of the summer with temperatures forecast above 40°C and about ten fires still active. Copernicus data puts France at 3.4 times its annual average fire activity this year. *(NPR, CNN, AP.)* [NPR →](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/29/g-s1-136057/france-wildfires)

## United States

**Todd Blanche's attorney general nomination is suddenly in real trouble.** The Senate Judiciary Committee pulled Thursday's scheduled vote Wednesday night after John Cornyn said he was "not prepared to vote yes." The Texas Republican's objection is specific: a provision in the settlement of Trump's lawsuit against the IRS that gives Trump and others effective immunity from audits and enforcement on returns filed before the settlement. A meeting between Cornyn and Blanche was cancelled. With the Senate about to break for August recess, the nomination could stall until fall. Thom Tillis has also signaled reservations. *(CNN, The Hill, CNBC, Texas Tribune, July 29; well corroborated.)* [CNN →](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/29/politics/todd-blanche-nomination-cornyn)

**The FTC sued Hims & Hers over patient privacy, the one to actually read.** The commission, joined by Utah and Los Angeles County, alleges the telehealth company shared sensitive patient health information with Meta and Snap advertising platforms while promising privacy, billed customers for prescriptions *before* a clinician had reviewed the intake form, and engineered cancellation to be difficult. Shares fell as much as 15%; the company says it will fight. The billing allegation is the striking one clinically, it describes a business charging for a prescription in advance of the medical decision that justifies it. This is also a marker for where enforcement against direct-to-consumer telehealth is heading, since the FTC Act reaches platforms that sit outside HIPAA's covered-entity perimeter. *(Washington Post, CBS, CNBC, STAT, FTC release, July 29.)* [Washington Post →](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2026/07/29/ftc-sues-hims-hers-alleging-it-breached-patient-privacy/)

**Fauci invoked the Fifth Amendment before the Senate Homeland Security Committee.** Under subpoena from Chairman Rand Paul, Anthony Fauci declined to answer more than 100 questions about COVID-19's origins, after Paul released redacted excerpts of Fauci's personal 2019–22 diaries ahead of the hearing. Fauci said Paul's "obsession" and the diary release were meant to intimidate him. Paul rejected the Fifth Amendment claim, pointing to Biden's preemptive pardon, and said the committee will vote next week on holding Fauci in contempt of Congress. *(NPR, CNN, US News, Al Jazeera, July 29.)* [US News →](https://www.usnews.com/news/national-news/articles/2026-07-29/rand-paul-promises-repercussions-after-anthony-fauci-invokes-the-fifth-amendment-at-covid-19-hearing)

**Hadi Matar convicted of transnational terrorism for stabbing Salman Rushdie.** A federal jury in New York found Matar, 28, guilty on all counts Wednesday over the August 2022 attack at the Chautauqua Institution that cost Rushdie the sight in his right eye. Prosecutors pointed to Hezbollah material found in his New Jersey bedroom and on his computer. He is already serving 25 years on a state attempted-murder conviction; the federal counts carry a potential life sentence. *(CBS, NBC, AP, Al Jazeera, July 29.)* [CBS News →](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/salman-rushdie-convicted-federal-terrorism-2022-stabbing/)

**Washington buried Lindsey Graham.** The South Carolina senator, who died unexpectedly on July 11 at 71, was eulogized at the National Cathedral on Tuesday. Trump gave a 14-minute address calling him "a true American original" and, in a characteristically double-edged line, noting "he was extremely hawkish, he never saw a war that he didn't like." Netanyahu and Zelenskyy both attended, each having met Trump separately at the White House the same day. *(NBC, ABC, CBS, Time, July 28.)* [NBC News →](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/washington-says-goodbye-sen-lindsey-graham-rcna589475)

**The administration wants the Supreme Court to revive its mail-in voting order before the midterms.** A Justice Department emergency application filed July 27 asks the justices to pause lower-court rulings blocking key parts of Trump's election order across 23 states and the District of Columbia. The First Circuit had upheld Judge Indira Talwani's injunction, warning the order "would sow confusion and threaten disenfranchisement" if implemented before the fall elections. Solicitor General D. John Sauer filed the application; the Court has ordered the states to respond by **August 3**, with a ruling expected shortly after. If granted, agencies could proceed with new citizenship lists, voter data requirements and ballot-tracking rules while litigation continues. *(SCOTUSblog, Bloomberg, CBS, The Hill.)* [SCOTUSblog →](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/07/trump-administration-asks-supreme-court-to-clear-the-way-for-new-mail-in-voting-restrictions/)

**And a quieter retreat: the birthright-citizenship rehearing never came.** Trump vowed on July 8 to "immediately" seek rehearing of the Supreme Court's June 30 ruling striking down his birthright-citizenship executive order. The Justice Department let the 25-day deadline lapse on Monday without filing. The Court has not granted rehearing of an argued case in over fifty years, so this was always a long shot, but the practical effect is that the ruling stands, and Republicans are now pivoting to legislation (bills from Rep. John McGuire and Sen. Jim Banks) to codify the restriction instead. *(NBC News, SCOTUSblog, Newsweek, The Hill, reported July 29.)* [NBC News →](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/supreme-court/trump-hasnt-asked-supreme-court-rehear-birthright-citizenship-case-vow-rcna589061)

## Israel

**Netanyahu and Trump met in the Oval Office, their first face-to-face since the Iran war began in February.** Netanyahu travelled quietly to Washington, reportedly amid an Iranian security threat, for a 90-minute closed-door session. He called it "one of the best we've ever had"; the White House called it "productive and positive." No agreements were announced. The agenda: Iran policy, the US–Israel–Lebanon ceasefire framework, expanding the Abraham Accords, and Israel's posture in Syria. An Israeli official said Netanyahu did not press Trump to strike Iran this time but made clear Israel would not wait for a green light if attacked directly, and, separately, that he raised a desire to wean Israel off US aid. He stayed on for Graham's funeral. *(Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post, July 28.)* [Times of Israel →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-says-white-house-meeting-with-trump-was-one-of-the-best-weve-ever-had/)

**Gaza: strikes continue, and a disarmament trial balloon.** The Gaza health ministry reported six killed and 34 wounded in 24 hours, including at least two children. The IDF said it struck a Hamas sniper-cell commander in the Nuseirat Battalion, an operative it links to the October 7 attack, and a Gaza City mosque it says stored weapons. Separately, Times of Israel reported, citing unnamed officials, that Hamas is near agreement on a phased handover of weapons to a new postwar security force, beginning in southern Gaza and including tunnel destruction, while resisting full disarmament and seeking to retain internal-security arms. Israeli security officials are skeptical. **Treat the disarmament report as developing and unconfirmed**; casualty figures are ministry-sourced and not independently verified. *(Times of Israel, Haaretz, July 29–30.)* [Times of Israel →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/hamas-given-proposal-for-gradual-weapon-handover-in-months-long-process-officials/)

**The West Bank remains volatile after the al-Tal killings.** Following the July 24 incident near Nablus, in which settlers from the Havat Gilad outpost attacked Palestinian homes, a Palestinian man seized a settler's rifle and killed him before being shot by soldiers, and four members of the Ramadan family were killed, Netanyahu ordered a large-scale operation involving roughly 26 battalions, home demolitions, revoked work permits and accelerated outpost regularization. Nightly raids have extended to Jenin, Tubas, Ramallah, Hebron, Bethlehem, Tulkarem and East Jerusalem, with hundreds detained, alongside settler reprisal attacks. HRW and the UN human rights office both report settler violence at an all-time high: roughly 190 attacks a month in 2026 against 153 in 2025. *(Al Jazeera, HRW, UN News, July 24–29. Casualty framing varies by source.)* [Al Jazeera →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/palestine-weekly-west-bank-in-flames-after-tal-killings)

**Lebanon ceasefire friction ahead of Rome.** The IDF says a Hezbollah drone struck an Israeli military bulldozer inside the southern Lebanon buffer zone, calling it a blatant violation of the ceasefire, days before talks in Rome on implementing the framework. **This was single-sourced to Times of Israel's liveblog as of writing**, consistent with the established pattern of intermittent violations, but worth a Reuters or AP check before treating it as settled. Roughly 640,000 displaced Lebanese have returned home since the ceasefire took hold. *(Times of Israel, July 30.)* [Times of Israel →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-30-2026/)

**The haredi draft law keeps eroding the coalition.** The Knesset's July 14 law freezing enforcement against haredi draft evaders until November 30 passed 58–54 over the objections of the IDF chief of staff and the Knesset's own legal advisers; Likud MKs Yuli Edelstein and Dan Illouz left the party over it. Netanyahu has since criticized his haredi partners publicly, apparently positioning ahead of elections while planning to reconcile after. New this morning: Israel says it has closed the loophole that let draft evaders under exit bans board flights out of the country, with enforcement starting before Rosh Hashanah. *(Haaretz, Times of Israel, Ynet.)* [Ynet →](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1ezxnoebe)

## Economic & Markets

**A genuinely divided Fed held rates, and the bond market called the bluff.** The FOMC voted 9–3 to keep the funds rate at 3.50–3.75%, a fifth consecutive hold. Beth Hammack (Cleveland), Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis) and Lorie Logan (Dallas) all dissented in favor of a 25bp *hike*, the first time since 2016 that three policymakers have dissented in the same direction. The statement acknowledged inflation "remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal," blaming supply disruptions in energy among other sectors, while describing solid expansion and steady employment. Chair Kevin Warsh called the hold "especially prudent" given Middle East uncertainty, adding that "five-plus years of inflation above target cannot be cured in nine weeks." *(Federal Reserve statement, primary source; CNBC, Bloomberg.)* [FOMC statement →](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm)

**Then long bonds sold off hard.** The 30-year yield rose more than 9bp to roughly 5.19–5.23% intraday, its highest since 2007, while the 10-year climbed about 5bp to around 4.66%. Short yields fell as long yields spiked, producing one of the sharpest post-FOMC curve steepenings since the mid-1990s. Strategists read it as the market saying Warsh's inflation rhetoric is not yet backed by action, tough talk without a hike. *(Bloomberg, Reuters.)* [Bloomberg →](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/bond-rout-sends-warning-to-warsh-that-tough-talk-is-not-enough)

**Equities had their worst day in over a year.** The Dow fell 1,153 points, or 2.19%, to 51,594, its worst session since April 2025, with the S&P 500 off about 1.5% and the Nasdaq about 1.7%. The hawkish hold, the yield spike, the oil surge and weak SK Hynix earnings dragging AI-adjacent chip names all compounded. *(CNBC, NPR, Yahoo Finance, TheStreet, CNN.)* [Yahoo Finance →](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-july-29-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-082009165.html)

**Oil snapped back violently.** Brent gained 7.9% to settle at \$90.74 and WTI rose 6.6% to \$84.46, reversing part of a 16% three-session slide that had been the sharpest since 2020. The whipsaw is entirely a function of the diplomacy-versus-escalation cycle: prices collapsed on Trump's "good talks," then spiked when the missiles flew. Hedge-fund net length in Brent had already rebounded to a two-month high above 192 million barrels. *(CNBC, Bloomberg.)* [CNBC →](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-iran-us-hormuz.html)

**Microsoft and Meta split the AI trade.** Microsoft posted fiscal Q4 revenue of \$90.01bn against roughly \$87.6bn expected, with Azure up 43% in constant currency and Azure's annual revenue topping \$100bn for the first time; shares rose. Meta reported revenue of \$60.8bn (up 28%) but diluted EPS of \$6.18, down from \$7.14 a year earlier and well short of the roughly \$7.20 consensus. Q2 capex hit \$31.08bn, nearly double a year ago, and full-year capex guidance was raised to \$130–145bn; Q3 revenue guidance came in light. Shares fell roughly 9–10% after hours. ## Worth Reading

**Rivka Galchen, "What Would It Mean to See a New Color?"**, *The New Yorker*, July 27. Galchen builds an essay around "olo," a hyper-saturated green that Berkeley's Ren Ng and Austin Roorda produced by using laser stimulation to fire only the M cones in a subject's retina, a color no pigment can mix, no camera can capture, and no screen can display. She threads it through Newton and Itten on color theory, Dalton on color blindness, tetrachromacy, and finally Hume on whether a mind can generate a shade it has never encountered. It is the rare science essay that gets more interesting as it gets more philosophical, and it will land well with anyone who thinks about perception as a clinical phenomenon rather than an abstract one. [Read →](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/03/what-would-it-mean-to-see-a-new-color)

**Mike Spies, "A Woman Lost Two Partners to Guns, Then the N.R.A. Came to Collect"**, *The New Yorker*, issue of August 3. A decades-spanning American Chronicles piece following a Maryland woman whose first husband died by suicide with a gun in 1991 and whose later partner died the same way, and how the N.R.A.'s political machinery, including its long campaign against firearm-suicide research, shaped the country she and her son had to grieve in. Spies is the best reporter working this beat, and the piece is a clean argument for why gun suicide is a medical story that has been kept from being studied as one. [Read →](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/03/a-woman-lost-two-partners-to-guns-then-the-nra-came-to-collect)
