**TL;DR.** The US–Iran fight is now a sustained air campaign, not a flare-up: a fifth consecutive day of strikes, a reinstated naval blockade, Iranian retaliation reaching Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan, and Trump publicly threatening Iran's power grid and bridges next week. Markets are strikingly calm about it, oil at only one-month highs, equities slightly green, which is the tell worth watching. Meanwhile Israel and Lebanon quietly agreed to pilot withdrawal zones, the most concrete de-escalation step in months.

## International

**The Iran campaign enters its fifth day.** US forces launched two further waves of strikes Wednesday, the second announced around 3 p.m. ET, targeting what CENTCOM described as Iranian capabilities "used to threaten vessels freely transiting through the Strait of Hormuz." Overnight, a fourth round ran roughly seven hours, with US aircraft, drones and naval vessels hitting missile and drone sites, coastal defenses and naval assets. Iranian authorities said a strike on an army barracks killed at least seven troops and wounded 260, and that US strikes have killed more than 30 civilians in recent days. Those casualty figures are Iranian government claims and have not been independently verified. The US also reinstated its naval blockade of Iranian ports on July 14 and reportedly struck a tanker attempting to skirt it. *([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/15/nx-s1-5894582/us-iran-updates), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/15/world/live-news/iran-war-trump), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/us-iran-war-trump-ceasefire-attacks-strait-of-hormuz/))*

**Trump threatens the grid.** In a Fox News interview Tuesday night, Trump said: "We're going to knock out all their power plants. We're gonna knock out their bridges unless they get to the table and negotiate," adding that "next week comes the power plants." He separately said Iran wants to meet. The blockade had been lifted under a temporary ceasefire struck last month; Trump has declared that truce over. Striking power generation and bridges would mark a shift from military-and-shipping targets toward dual-use civilian infrastructure, a meaningful legal and humanitarian escalation, and worth watching as a distinct threshold rather than more of the same. *([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/15/trump-iran-hormuz-strikes-power-plants-targeted.html), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/15/iran-launches-strikes-on-neighbours-as-us-resumes-blockade-and-trump-threatens-to-hit-powe))*

**Iran widens the map.** Tehran said it targeted US military infrastructure in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan, taking the war onto the territory of three US partners rather than confining it to the Gulf waterway. The IRGC threatened to close "all other export corridors" benefiting US allies, which analysts read as a hint at Bab el-Mandeb. Separately, Iranian cyber operations reportedly exploited outdated network vulnerabilities to track US military personnel's phones in the region, and Washington is backing an Iraq–Syria effort to restore the Kirkuk–Baniyas crude pipeline as a Hormuz workaround. *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/15/world/live-news/iran-war-trump))*

**Black Sea: two blockades at once.** Russia hit Odesa for a fifth straight day, a missile striking a multi-story apartment building overnight and killing three with at least six wounded, per city military administrator Serhiy Lysak. Moscow's MoD said it targeted fuel storage and drone-assembly infrastructure at Odesa and Chernomorsk ports. Ukraine's drone commander Robert Brovdi claimed Kyiv struck 20 Russian vessels overnight, 17 oil tankers, two gas tankers, a tugboat, and 116 vessels over nine days. Lavrov called the ship attacks "terrorism." Note a discrepancy worth flagging: the Kyiv Independent reports 12 killed and 90 injured nationwide, versus the three-in-Odesa figure carried by Al Jazeera and Moscow Times; these are different scopes, and neither is independently confirmed. The mutual port strangulation is quietly a grain-market story as much as a war story. *([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/15/russian-attack-on-odesa-kills-three-as-ukraine-targets-vessels-in-black-sea), [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-attacks-kill-12-injure-90-across-ukraine-as-odesa-oblast-hit-with-missiles-for-fifth-straight-day/))*

**ICC prosecutor's suspension upheld.** The UK's Bar Standards Board upheld the suspension of ICC Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan over sexual misconduct allegations, barring him from practising law in the UK while proceedings continue. The ICC's oversight body votes later this month on removal. *Single-source in this sweep (Just Security); worth a second read before relying on the detail.* *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

**Mediterranean.** At least 50 migrants, including women and children, are feared lost after a wooden boat capsized off eastern Libya; 10 survived. *Developing; casualty counts in these events typically move.*

## US

**Blanche in the chair.** Todd Blanche, Trump's nominee for attorney general, faced the Senate Judiciary Committee and acknowledged errors in the vetting of the Epstein files released to the public, a notable concession given how much of the hearing's political energy ran through that release. He was also pressed on how he would handle "weaponization" investigations amid pressure from Trump allies. *([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/15/politics/live-news/todd-blanche-confirmation-hearing))*

**Democrats block the defense bill.** Senate Democrats defeated the motion to proceed to the \$1.15 trillion annual defense authorization 50–46, strictly along party lines, citing the absence of congressional authorization for the Iran campaign. This is the war-powers fight finding a procedural vehicle: with no AUMF debate on offer, the NDAA becomes the leverage point. *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

**Immigration enforcement under scrutiny.** ICE will require body-worn cameras on all arrest teams following fatal shootings that went unrecorded, and officers temporarily halted most vehicle stops. A migrant died during a Florida operation, and Mexico has filed criminal complaints over migrant deaths in US custody. *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

**Also on the domestic ledger.** Justice Kagan said threats against justices are expected to rise 40% this year, with \$18.9 million in security funding sought. Defense Secretary Hegseth blocked seven senior Navy officer promotions, five of them women or people of color. And Trump paid E. Jean Carroll over \$5 million in sexual abuse damages. *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

## Israel

**Rome produces something concrete.** Israeli and Lebanese officials concluded a US-mediated round in Rome and agreed to implement "pilot zones" in southern Lebanon, areas where Israeli forces withdraw and hand control to the Lebanese army. Trump has urged Netanyahu to redeploy forces out of Syria and Lebanon. Against a regional backdrop this bad, an actual agreed mechanism for territorial handover is the most substantive de-escalation item on the board, and the pilot framing gives both sides a cheap exit if it fails. *([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260715-middle-east-live-tehran-and-us-exchange-strikes-as-blockade-of-iranian-ports-resumes), [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-15-2026/))*

**Gaza.** An Israeli strike on a residential building in Deir al-Balah killed at least one person, with the body brought to Al-Aqsa Hospital; a military official said the target was a Hamas operative. Israeli airstrikes have killed at least a dozen people in Gaza over the past two days, including a six-year-old child and seven police officers, with the military characterizing targets as Hamas militants. *([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-15-2026/), [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

**West Bank.** Israel approved a \$434 million budget for 34 new West Bank settlements, moving in the opposite direction from the Lebanon track, and a reminder that the two files run on separate political logics. *([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/147315/early-edition-july-15-2026/))*

**The influence-campaign story.** Vice President Vance reacted sharply, "go to hell", to a TIME report describing an alleged Israeli-funded influencer and digital media campaign aimed at undermining US negotiations with Iran, discussing it in an appearance with Joe Rogan. *Still developing; the underlying TIME reporting is the thing to read rather than the reaction to it.* *([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-15-2026/))*

**Espionage sentence.** A military court sentenced an active-duty Israeli soldier to five years for sending videos of missile interceptions to an Iranian agent during last June's 12-day war. *([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-15-2026/))*

## Economic & Markets

**Oil is up, but not panicking.** WTI for August delivery added 26 cents to settle at \$79.60; September Brent rose 22 cents to \$84.95, a third consecutive session higher and one-month highs. Consider the gap between headline and tape: roughly 20% of the world's oil and gas normally moves through Hormuz, the US is blockading Iranian ports, and Iran is threatening to close further corridors, yet crude is up cents, not dollars. The market is pricing a contained campaign in which flows continue. If that assumption breaks, a genuine closure attempt, or the threatened strikes on Iranian infrastructure triggering a wider response, the repricing would be violent. This is the asymmetry to watch. *([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/15/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-blockade.html))*

**Equities shrug.** The S&P 500 rose 0.38% and the Dow gained 0.29% to 52,658.64, supported by tech and inflation data. Morgan Stanley climbed 0.97% after beating on Q2, posting \$3.46 per share on \$21.35 billion in revenue. SpaceX dipped below its IPO price for the first time. Note earlier in the week the pattern ran the other way, stocks ended lower July 12 as oil surged on Hormuz tension, so the equity market's tolerance here is recent, not settled. *([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-july-15-2026), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html))*

**The toll that wasn't.** Trump dropped plans to collect a 20% fee on ships transiting Hormuz, saying Gulf allies asked him to and that forgone revenue would be more than offset by future Gulf investment in the US. The fee had been a genuine tail risk for shipping and insurance costs; its removal is quietly one of the more market-relevant items of the day. *([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-trump-toll-iran.html))*

## Also notable

- **Apple v. OpenAI.** Apple's trade-secret suit alleges OpenAI and two former Apple employees took confidential hardware-design and product-development material, a live front in the fight over consumer-facing AI devices.
- **New York's data-center moratorium.** A one-year ban on hyperscaler data centers drawing 50+ megawatts. Grid constraints are becoming the binding limit on AI buildout, and states are starting to say so out loud.
- **An AI watchdog proposal.** Google DeepMind's CEO called for a body to screen advanced models before release. The Trump administration separately launched "Gold Eagle," an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse for finding and patching vulnerabilities.
- **Sudan.** The WFP warned of deepening hunger driven by the civil war and compounded by Iran-war supply disruption; the EU banned Sudanese gold imports to choke off war financing. Chronically under-covered relative to its scale.
- **Cuba.** The national grid collapsed again, reportedly the third major blackout in nine days, attributed to the US oil blockade. *Single-source (Democracy Now) in this sweep; treat the framing and the count as unconfirmed.*
