## International

**The Iran ceasefire framework expires Monday, and the two sides do not agree it exists.** The US and Israel have been at war with Iran since February 28; a Pakistan-brokered agreement signed June 17 in Islamabad was meant to end hostilities within 60 days, a clock that runs out tomorrow. Pakistani sources told Anadolu both sides had agreed in principle to extend, but Foreign Minister Araghchi said Friday the memorandum "was not a ceasefire" and "cannot be extended," with no decision made on resuming talks. That followed Trump's remark in New York that "after we finish defeating Iran … pretty soon I'll be declaring the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States," which drew a reply from deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi that the strait "cannot be seized with a tweet, nor with an aircraft carrier." Qatar and Pakistan are relaying messages. Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global oil, so Monday is a market event as much as a diplomatic one. *Treat the reported extension as unresolved; it is thinly sourced and contradicted by Tehran.* Al Jazeera, NBC, Anadolu · Aug 14–16 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/trump-says-he-will-declare-strait-of-hormuz-a-us-territory)

**Ukraine sent roughly 600 drones at Moscow overnight, the largest such attack of the war.** Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 600 drones were detected heading for the capital and roughly 201 were destroyed over the Moscow region; the Defence Ministry claimed 822 downed nationwide, a figure that is an official claim rather than independently verified. An 83-year-old man was killed when a drone struck a home in the Moscow region and five more died across three towns in Rostov, per the two regional governors. Ukraine's military said it hit a solid rocket propellant plant in Rostov region; a fire broke out at a Wildberries warehouse in Podolsk. Russia struck back at Kyiv and Kryvyi Rih with ballistic missiles and drones, killing a woman. The analytic point is that Ukraine now appears able to saturate the densest air defences in Russia. CNN, NPR, CBS, France 24 · overnight Aug 15–16 · [link](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/15/europe/russia-moscow-ukraine-air-attack-intl)

**A NATO fighter shot down a drone over Romania early Sunday.** Romania's defence ministry said an aerial object crossed from Moldova around 04:44 local, roughly 24 km north of Galati. A Spanish Air and Space Force F-18 on combat air patrol out of Mihail Kogalniceanu made radar contact, received authorisation, and destroyed it at 05:01 over uninhabited terrain. No injuries. NATO spokesperson Martin O'Donnell said the drone "appears to be Russian." This is the fourth drone Romania has downed in 2026; after a July 24 Shahed-type incursion Bucharest expelled a Russian diplomat and recalled its ambassador. Watch for Article 4 consultations and further eastern-flank air-defence deployments. CNN, Euronews, Al Jazeera · Aug 16 · [link](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/15/europe/drone-romania-airspace-nato-hnk-intl)

**At least 51 are dead after a magnitude 7.7 earthquake off Indonesia's Flores Island.** The quake struck Saturday at 05:58 local, 10 km deep, epicentre about 68 km north-northwest of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara. The toll has been climbing all weekend and outlets differ, NPR reported 51 Sunday, Indonesia's disaster agency has cited at least 53, so read it as at least 51 and rising. More than 230 aftershocks followed, the strongest 6.2, and a separate 6.9 hit Sumatra without casualties. Sub-metre tsunami waves were recorded before the warning lifted. Over 1,300 homes were damaged, more than two-thirds severely, along with health facilities and schools; roughly 2,000 villagers are in temporary shelters in Nagekeo regency, where landslides have blocked roads and cut communications. NPR, Al Jazeera, WaPo · Aug 15–16 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/at-least-two-killed-as-magnitude-7-7-quake-hits-indonesia)

**South Korea's president proposed talks to formally end the Korean War.** Speaking at Liberation Day ceremonies Saturday, Lee Jae-myung said "let us stop making threats to each other and start talking about ending this decades-long war," and proposed discussing a halt to the advancement of North Korea's nuclear capabilities plus a multi-layered defence arrangement along the DMZ. The two Koreas remain technically at war because 1950–53 ended in an armistice. The overture lands as Pyongyang has ignored Seoul's reconciliation efforts while deepening ties with Moscow, and there was no North Korean response as of Sunday morning, which is itself the likeliest outcome. France 24, RTÉ · Aug 15 · [link](https://www.rte.ie/news/world/2026/0815/1588005-korea-war/)

**Belgium is fighting the largest wildfire in its recorded history.** The blaze in the High Fens nature reserve in Liege province has consumed more than 27 sq km, nearly double the previous 2011 record of 14 sq km. Around 600 residents of Waimes and Butgenbach were evacuated Saturday; German residents in three Monschau districts received advisories. More than 250 personnel from four countries are deployed, with the EU civil protection mechanism supplying three helicopters and two water bombers. No homes damaged as of Sunday morning, but flames were within about 3 km of the German border. It started Friday afternoon amid 37C heat. Al Jazeera, WaPo, France 24 · Aug 14–16 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/belgiums-largest-wildfire-on-record-spreads-towards-german-border)

**Twelve Polish travellers died when a coach overturned in Hungary.** A Polish-registered bus carrying 57 passengers and two drivers from Serbia to Poland left the eastbound M3 motorway near Mezokeresztes, about 140 km east of Budapest, and overturned in a ditch around 1 a.m. Sunday. Eleven died at the scene and a twelfth in hospital; at least 10 were seriously injured and 37 sustained minor injuries. Hungarian police said the driver likely fell asleep and is in custody. It is Hungary's deadliest road accident since 2003. AP, Al Jazeera, RTÉ · Aug 16 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/at-least-12-people-killed-in-hungary-bus-crash)

## US

**Hurricane Lala hit Hawaii's Big Island hard without technically making landfall.** Lala tracked past as a Category 1 Saturday and weakened to a tropical storm early Sunday local time. One person died in a storm-related car crash. Reporting indicates at least 19 buildings lost roofs and tens of thousands lost power, outage counts ranged from 60,000 to about 124,000 across outlets overnight, with homes swept away at Naalehu. Rain fell at 3 to 5 inches per hour; Nahuku logged 20.74 inches and Mauna Kea's summit saw 140 mph gusts, with isolated stations reporting up to 35 inches over 36 hours. Gov. Green declared an emergency and deployed 180 National Guard troops. Life-threatening flooding and mudslide risk continued Sunday over the Big Island and Maui. Hawaii has had no direct hurricane landfall since Iniki in 1992. *Figures were still moving overnight.* NBC, AP/PBS, CNN, CBS · Aug 15–16 · [link](https://www.nbcnews.com/weather/hurricanes/hawaiis-big-island-lashed-rain-wind-tropical-storm-lala-rcna592682)

**The FBI reported the largest violent crime drop on record, and the politics around it are messier than the number.** The 2025 Uniform Crime Report showed violent crime down 9.3% and murder down 18.1% year over year, the steepest declines since FBI estimation began in 1936; Director Patel noted the 4.1 per 100,000 murder rate ties 1955–56 for the lowest ever. Trump called it "the single largest reduction in violent crime in American history" and tied it to immigration enforcement. The statistic is real; the causation is contested. Analyst Jeff Asher notes this is the third consecutive year of record murder declines, a trend that predates this term, and the Council on Criminal Justice's Adam Gelb said the driver is "about as clear as a glass of milk." Worth flagging: the same report shows assaults on law enforcement up 13.8% to a ten-year high, which directly contradicts the claim of falling attacks on officers. NBC, CBS, UPI, FBI release · Aug 14–16 · [link](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/fbi-reports-record-decrease-violent-crime-2025-rcna592593)

**The administration asked the Supreme Court to let the White House ballroom go back up.** DOJ filed an emergency stay application Friday seeking to pause a D.C. Circuit ruling blocking aboveground work on the roughly \$400 million, 90,000-square-foot ballroom on the site where the East Wing was demolished last fall. Two lower courts held the president lacked unilateral authority to build without congressional authorization or appropriation. The August 7 appellate decision takes effect August 21, which is what makes this urgent. Chief Justice Roberts, as circuit justice, ordered the National Trust for Historic Preservation and co-challengers to respond by noon Tuesday. A separation-of-powers and appropriations test landing months before the midterms; an order could come this week. NBC, PBS, WaPo, Bloomberg · Aug 14 · [link](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-asks-supreme-court-to-let-white-house-resume-ballroom-construction-during-appeal)

**Health policy, and the most clinically actionable item here: there are still no federal fall respiratory vaccine recommendations.** FDA did its part, flu strain selection in March, COVID composition in May, but ACIP has held neither its March nor its June 2026 meeting, so no federal recommendations govern fall vaccine use, including age bands and immunocompromised dosing. IDSA, AAP, ACOG and AAFP have each issued independent guidance to fill the gap. The load-bearing legal fact: a March 2026 ruling in *AAP v. Kennedy* (D. Mass., Judge Murphy) stayed the January schedule cuts and the ACIP appointments, so the operative CDC schedule reverts to the pre-June-2025 version, which is what most physicians are following. Insurers have pledged no-cost-sharing coverage of recommended vaccines through end of 2027. Separately, AAP President Andrew Racine published direct guidance rebutting the August 10 executive order's call to split MMR into three injections, saying there is no medical rationale and it delays protection by years. No lawsuit against that EO had been filed as of Sunday morning; a California judge hears a motion to dismiss in the 15-state suit September 3. MedPage Today, STAT · Aug 14 · [link](https://www.medpagetoday.com/publichealthpolicy/publichealth/122617)

*Two more worth your attention:* CMS will not renew Arkansas's Medicaid 1115 waiver covering roughly 200,000 enrollees, citing new budget-neutrality rules effective January under the 2025 reconciliation law, a dozen states have waivers expiring December 31 facing the same test ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/medicaid/trump-cms-medicaid-expansion-1115-waivers-budget-neutrality-arkansas/)). **Two mass shootings and an immigration split rounded out the domestic weekend.** At Virginia State University, five people were shot outside dormitories just after 1:30 a.m. Saturday as students returned for the school year; Camron Harris, 19, was arrested that afternoon hiding in a Seward Hall closet and faces eight felony warrants, held without bond. Neither he nor four of the five victims were students. In Missaukee County, Michigan, Chad Hickman, 39, killed five people at two homes Friday morning before being found dead in a wooded area; a 13-year-old girl was critically wounded and is stable. Separately, the DNC passed two competing non-binding ICE resolutions Saturday by voice vote, one to abolish and replace the agency, one calling only for reform, exposing an establishment-versus-left split, while Judge Allison Burroughs lifted her own stay and cleared the way to end Somalia's TPS designation for roughly 1,100 people, holding she was bound by the Supreme Court's June *Mullin v. Doe* decision. AP, CNN, Reuters, WaPo · Aug 14–16 · [link](https://apnews.com/article/virginia-state-university-shooting-campus-lockdown-783d5190d7fe0818fc27a98123311dc3)

## Israel

**The deadliest Israeli strikes in south Lebanon since the June truce killed eleven.** The Lebanese health ministry says 11 were killed and 19 wounded, seven in Ansar including three children, four dead and 17 wounded in Deir al-Zahrani. The IDF says it struck Hezbollah infrastructure near Ali al-Taher and Ansar in response to an attack on its troops: an explosive drone launched around 2:30 a.m. Saturday that seriously wounded an officer and two soldiers of the Yahalom engineering unit. The IDF later said it killed Ali Hassan, whom it identified as a Radwan Force battalion commander. President Joseph Aoun called the strikes "a clear message to the negotiation track," Speaker Berri called it a massacre, and PM Nawaf Salam rejected Israel's justification. Hezbollah warned of an "appropriate response." A June 26 framework ties Israeli withdrawal to Hezbollah disarmament; whether it survives is the question. AP/PBS, Al Jazeera, Times of Israel, RTÉ · Aug 15–16 · [link](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/israeli-airstrikes-on-southern-lebanon-kill-11-people-health-ministry-says)

**The Gaza ceasefire took two hits Saturday.** The IDF says a tank operating near the Yellow Line in southern Gaza drove over an explosive device it attributes to Hamas, with no injuries, and separately that Paratroopers Brigade soldiers killed two operatives who crossed the line and approached troops threateningly. The military called both violations viewed "with the utmost severity." Hamas has not publicly claimed the tank attack. Palestinian sources reported injuries from subsequent Israeli strikes in Khan Younis and Nuseirat, and Haaretz's live blog reported seven wounded in drone strikes on tents in Muwasi. Al Jazeera cites Gaza health ministry figures of more than 1,250 Palestinians killed since the October 2025 ceasefire took effect, a ministry attribution, not an independently verified number. Watch whether Israel mounts a larger retaliatory wave as it has after earlier violations. This is the sharpest open US-Israel policy split in months, and the Kushner-Netanyahu meeting is the thing to watch. Al Jazeera, Times of Israel, MEMO · Aug 14–16 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/hamas-leader-heads-to-cairo-for-gaza-talks-ahead-of-kushner-visit)

**Washington is paying the most for 30-year money since 2001, and AI is part of why.** Thursday's \$25 billion 30-year auction cleared at 5.216%, the steepest since 2001, capping a historic long-end selloff and stirring speculation Treasury will tilt issuance shorter. The squeeze is three-way: years of deficits, waning demand from traditional long-duration buyers, and an unprecedented corporate borrowing wave funding AI infrastructure. Hyperscalers and AI-adjacent issuers have sold roughly \$220–244 billion of bonds in 2026, double the \$108 billion full-year 2025 total, with S&P Global projecting about \$400 billion for the year. US 30-year real yields have reached about 3%, near 18-year highs; UK and German 10-year real yields sit at decade highs. BlackRock's Vivek Paul called it "a contest for capital with few recent parallels." The 30-year fixed mortgage is at 6.69%, highest since July 2025. Bloomberg, Fortune, FT · Aug 13–14 · [link](https://fortune.com/2026/08/14/us-bond-sale-25-year-high/)

**Four soft prints in eight days halved September hike odds.** Worth keeping the frame straight: this cycle's debate is hike-versus-hold, not cut-versus-hold. July CPI rose just 0.1% month over month and 3.4% year over year; core rose 0.2% and 2.5%. ## Worth reading

**[Can Extreme Morning Sickness Be Cured?](https://www.newyorker.com/news/essay/can-extreme-morning-sickness-be-cured)**, Anna Russell, The New Yorker\
Russell traces hyperemesis gravidarum from cultural punchline to biological disease. She opens with Kate Middleton and Amy Schumer, then pivots to Marlena Fejzo, the USC geneticist who developed H.G. herself, was told by her own physician it was a psychological rejection of motherhood, and spent two decades, NIH rejections, saliva samples stored in a UCLA freezer on weekends, proving otherwise. Her 2018, 2022 and 2023 papers pinned the condition to GDF15, opening real prevention and treatment pathways. It is a clean case study in how diagnostic dismissal of women's symptoms becomes a research-funding failure becomes a clinical one: H.G. is the second-leading cause of hospitalization in pregnancy after preterm birth, with untreated fetal loss reported up to 34%, and it barely appears on federal lists of pregnancy complications. *Note: the page carries no visible dateline; publication inferred from section ordering as within the last several days.*

**[Inside Russia's New Gulag](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/17/inside-russias-new-gulag)**, Joshua Yaffa, The New Yorker, Aug 10\
Human-rights groups estimate more than ten thousand Ukrainian civilians have vanished into the Russian penal system. Yaffa narrows that abstraction to a single cell in SIZO-1, the Catherine-the-Great-era pretrial jail in Rostov near the Southern Military District headquarters where the show trials are staged. His central figure is Liliia Prutian, a fifty-something Aidar Battalion medic and mother of three, concussed and partly deafened early in the war, raped on the battlefield the day of her capture. Around her: Azov Brigade cooks charged with armed seizure of power ("Making borscht isn't some kind of coup"), a sixty-four-year-old English teacher on terrorism charges, a woman held since 2019 whose daughter was four when she disappeared. The grim bureaucratic tell is that conditions *improve* near trial, because torture is harder to hide in court. The best kind of foreign reporting: one room, rendered completely.
