## International

### US–Iran: strikes widen, blockade back, strait in the crosshairs

The dominant story of the last 36 hours is the sharp escalation between Washington and Tehran. US Central Command said it carried out another wave of strikes late Tuesday (US time), a roughly seven-hour operation using fighter aircraft, drones and naval vessels against dozens of Iranian missile, drone, naval and coastal-defense assets near the Strait of Hormuz and along Iran's coastline, framed as degrading Iran's ability to threaten commercial shipping. Simultaneously, the US reinstated its naval blockade of Iranian ports. (CNBC, Reuters, July 15.)

Politically, Trump walked back his weekend threat to charge a 20% "toll" on all cargo transiting Hormuz, after markets and allies balked, while keeping the blockade and declaring the US the "guardian of the Strait." Iran's parliament has begun drafting legislation on the strait, and President Pezeshkian vowed Iran would defend "every inch" of its territory; Iranian outlets reported strikes on Bushehr on the Gulf. The June 17 memorandum of understanding that briefly paused the war is effectively dead. (FDD Overnight Brief, Yahoo Finance, Times of Israel.) Developing; strike counts and casualty figures come largely from each side's military and are not independently verified.

### Ukraine: Black Sea drone blitz, a new PM, and an EU defense pact

Kyiv released footage of a large-scale drone operation striking 20 Russian vessels in the Black Sea, 17 oil tankers, two gas tankers and a tugboat, part of a campaign it says has hit 136 ships tied to Russia's "shadow fleet" in ten days, a pivot toward choking Russia's seaborne fuel exports. The same day, Ursula von der Leyen and Volodymyr Zelensky signed a major EU–Ukraine defense deal in Kyiv covering joint weapons production and storage of Ukrainian drones on EU soil. Naftogaz chief Serhii Koretskyi is set to become Ukraine's next prime minister. (Al Jazeera, TVP World, July 15.)

### France legalizes assisted dying

One for your world as a physician: France's National Assembly gave final approval to a right-to-assisted-dying bill in a 291–241 vote, ending years of debate and fulfilling a Macron campaign promise. Eligibility is limited to adult French citizens or legal residents with an incurable, advanced or terminal illness, with layered safeguards (physician disclosure of prognosis, palliative-care and psychological options, and the right to withdraw the request). The conservative-led Senate opposed it, but the Assembly has the final word; the Constitutional Council may still review it. (CNN, France 24, Al Jazeera, July 15.)

## US

### Trump's prime-time election address

Trump is set to address the nation at 9pm Thursday on what he says will include "elections and voting machines," an unusual use of a rare prime-time slot that most expect to revisit his unproven 2020 fraud claims and pressure Republicans to pass the SAVE America Act. Politico reports GOP lawmakers are privately nervous ("scared s–tless") less about the prepared text than about what Trump might ad-lib. The speech lands with the US–Iran fight pushing fuel prices up ahead of a midterm cycle strategists still expect to hinge on cost of living. (AP via US News, Politico, USA Today, July 15–16.)

## Israel

Israel is threading a difficult needle. Even under the fragile Gaza truce, it is stepping up targeted operations to hunt and kill everyone it links to the October 7 attack, and Israeli strikes continue to hit Gazan police and civil officials, a strike on Jabalia this week killed a police-station director and several officers. Critics, quoted by Al Jazeera, argue the campaign is designed to prevent Palestinian governance from re-forming; the UN warns that the expansion of Israeli-controlled territory (now roughly 65% of Gaza, up from 54% at the ceasefire) is endangering civilians and choking aid. (Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, UN News.)

On the northern and eastern fronts, Netanyahu warned Tehran that Israel's response to any strike on its territory would be "far more powerful" than before, and is reportedly planning a Washington visit next week; Israeli and Lebanese officials held another round of US-mediated talks. The subtler story, per the NYT, is that Israel now regards a US–Iran *deal* as something to fear, officials reportedly see a return to open war as preferable to an agreement that leaves Iran's threats to Israel intact, leaving Jerusalem in an uneasy limbo on the sidelines. A domestic storm over the ultra-Orthodox draft law adds to Netanyahu's pressures. (NYT, Times of Israel, FDD.)

## Economic & Markets

**Inflation.** June CPI came in notably cooler than expected: headline prices fell 0.4% on the month (consensus was −0.2%), dropping the annual rate to 3.5% from a hotter prior read, led by a ~5.7% drop in energy and ~9.7% fall in gasoline. Core CPI was flat on the month, 2.6% year over year. The soft print reinforced expectations the Fed holds its benchmark at 3.50–3.75% at the July meeting. (CNBC, Zacks, July 14–15.)

**Equities.** Megacap tech did the heavy lifting midweek: the Nasdaq led, Apple jumped ~4% toward a \$5 trillion market cap, and BlackRock rallied ~6.6% after reporting assets under management above \$15 trillion for the first time. Semiconductors were the soft spot, the PHLX Semiconductor index fell ~2.1% with Micron, Marvell, Intel and AMD lower, even as Dutch equipment maker ASML posted record orders and raised guidance. Earlier in the week IBM sank ~25% on a profit warning. BofA reiterated a cautious year-end S&P target of 7,100, warning speculation is at "extreme levels." (WSJ, CNBC, Fortune.)

**Oil.** Crude is the cleanest read on the Iran risk: Brent has hovered around \$77–83 and WTI near \$78, roughly one-month highs, as US strikes and the reinstated blockade cut Hormuz traffic "to a trickle." Prices were little changed Wednesday, suggesting markets have partly priced the standoff, but any Houthi move against Saudi or Red Sea flows is the tail risk traders are watching. (Reuters, CNBC, NYT.)
