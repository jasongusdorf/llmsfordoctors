## International

### Magnitude 7.1 earthquake devastates Kumamoto; at least 13 dead

NPR, Al Jazeera, CNN, Japan Times · struck Tue July 28 ~4:30 pm JST, toll updated Wed

A powerful quake struck Kumamoto Prefecture on Kyushu, killing at least 13 with rescue operations still underway. Between 260,000 and 300,000 residents were told to evacuate. The Aeon Mall Kumamoto in Kashima partially collapsed, trapping shoppers; the Nippon Paper Yatsushiro Mill is the other major rescue site. Sections of the Kyushu Expressway split apart, part of Kumamoto Castle's wall collapsed, and more than 36,000 homes lost power. Toyota and Honda operations were disrupted. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi called it "a race against time." A tsunami advisory for the Ariake and Yatsushiro Seas has been lifted, and no abnormalities were reported at the Sendai nuclear plant. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/29/nx-s1-5911613/japan-earthquake-updates)

**Still developing.** Outlets differ on magnitude (7.1 as reported by Japanese authorities vs. USGS listing 6.8, epicenter near Uto) and on the evacuation figure. The death toll is likely to move.

NPR, CNN, Al Jazeera, Euronews · ongoing, July 28–29 updates

### CMS sunsets the Part D premium stabilization demonstration

CMS fact sheet, Bloomberg Law, AP, WSJ · Tue July 28

CMS will end the Medicare Part D Premium Stabilization Demonstration at the close of CY2026, returning Part D to what it calls "traditional market conditions" in 2027. The demonstration applied a \$15 direct subsidy to the base beneficiary premium and, more consequentially, capped year-over-year standalone PDP premium increases at \$35. CMS set the 2027 base beneficiary premium at \$41.33, up about 6% from \$38.99, but that number understates real-world exposure because individual plan premiums no longer have the guardrail. GAO estimated the demonstration cost roughly \$9.8 billion across 2025 and 2026; CMS justified the wind-down by arguing plan sponsors now have enough experience with the IRA-redesigned benefit to price accurately. Actual plan-level premiums land in late October and early November. [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-part-d-2027-national-average-monthly-bid-amount-information)

Practical read for you: expect 2027 medication-adherence and cost-of-care conversations to get materially harder, particularly for lower-income and dual-eligible-adjacent patients on standalone drug plans. This is the kind of thing that shows up on the wards as a nonadherence problem six months after it happens as a policy announcement.

### Gideon Lewis-Kraus, "The Rise of the 'Scenario'"

The New Yorker, Weekend Essay · July 25

This is the pick of the week for you. Lewis-Kraus uses Annie Jacobsen's *Biological War: A Scenario*, a minute-by-minute fictional timeline of an engineered pathogen escaping a Russian facility, as a way into the broader vogue for scenario-based forecasting among futurists, biosecurity people and AI-risk circles. His sharpest move is a paradox: the meticulous realism that makes Jacobsen's book credible is also what domesticates the catastrophe, rendering extinction-scale risk oddly procedural. He then turns the knife on the skeptics, arguing that reflexive dismissal of apocalyptic warnings is less an epistemic position than a sociological one, a way of signalling sophistication. Same author as "What Is Claude? Anthropic Doesn't Know, Either," now applying that skepticism to pandemic modelling.
