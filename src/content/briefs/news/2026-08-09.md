## TL;DR

Iran and Oman have all but settled how ships would transit Hormuz, but Tehran's security council spent the weekend attaching maximal conditions to actually reopening it, and Houthi drones set fire to Aramco's Jizan refinery overnight. In Washington, the Senate cleared a pre-dawn triple: Todd Blanche confirmed attorney general 50–49, a stopgap to December 11 passed 90–6, and the Graham-named Russia/Iran sanctions bill passed 86–11. Markets closed Friday at a record after July payrolls unexpectedly *shrank* by 23,000, gutting September Fed-hike odds and setting up Wednesday's CPI as the week's hinge.

## International

**Hormuz: lanes agreed, reopening not.** Tehran and Muscat have settled route coordinates for the Strait of Hormuz, inbound through a northern lane in Iranian waters, outbound through a southern lane in Omani waters. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the Oman deal "very close" but said Sunday that Iran will not enter direct talks with Washington while the US remains in breach of the June memorandum of understanding. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi went further, insisting the reopening "has its own specific mechanism and has nothing to do with the negotiations between Iran and Oman." The Supreme National Security Council's secretary, Mohammad Baqer Zolghadr, then published six conditions: an end to US threats, permanent cessation of hostilities, US naval and air withdrawal from Iran's periphery, war reparations, sanctions relief, and release of frozen assets. A 60-day interim term, mine clearance of the central corridor, and a transit-fee mechanism (Iran wants 5–7% of shipment value, Oman proposes ~3%, Washington insists on zero) are all reported but unconfirmed by Muscat or the US, and VP JD Vance says Iran told the US it has "no plans" to toll the strait at all, which contradicts Tehran's own statements. *Confirmed on the lanes; the terms are developing and largely Iranian-sourced.* [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/iran-deliberates-hormuz-arrangement-amid-uncertain-prospects-with-us)

**Shipping keeps getting hit anyway.** Overnight Aug 8–9, UKMTO reported a vessel struck by a projectile about 18 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman; the fire was extinguished and the crew is safe. On Aug 8 an ADNOC tanker was targeted by a missile in the strait, and ADNOC says three of its transiting vessels have now been attacked. Oman has warned publicly that continued attacks could derail the near-final navigation agreement. The pattern extends the Aug 3–4 strike on the Greek-owned bulker *Minoan Pioneer*, whose third engineer is still missing. *Incidents confirmed; attribution to Iran is not officially established in most cases.*

**Houthi drone hits Aramco's Jizan refinery.** Early Sunday, Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed that Aramco firefighting teams extinguished a blaze at the Jizan refinery on the Red Sea coast, with no casualties, and did not state a cause. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed a "precise" drone strike, framing it as retaliation for Saudi airspace violations over Saada and Hajjah. Jizan processes roughly 400,000 barrels a day. Separately, explosions and fire were reported at gas infrastructure in the Jubail industrial region, with NASA FIRMS satellite data showing a ~125 MW thermal anomaly at the Berri Gas Plant, *that one rests on satellite data plus Israeli media reporting with no Saudi confirmation; treat as unverified.* [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/09/nx-s1-5926387/yemens-houthis-claim-attack-on-aramco-oil-facility-in-saudi-arabia-and-other-middle-east-news)

**A "Sunni NATO," on paper.** The trilateral Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, signed Aug 7, treats an armed attack on Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, or Turkey as an attack on all three, extending the September 2025 Saudi–Pakistan pact to Ankara. Turkey's foreign minister explicitly likened the clause to NATO's Article 5. It is the first time three of the largest militaries in the Muslim world have been bound by a single defensive formula since the Baghdad Pact era, and it follows months of Iranian missile and drone attacks on Gulf states. Egypt was excluded despite having joined the preceding four-nation consultations. The Atlantic Council reads it as a hedge against doubts about US guarantees; Asia Times argues it is "more strategic hedge than mutual defense revolution" given the absence of integrated command. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/7/trump-says-war-cant-go-much-longer-hormuz-deal-close)

**Ukraine's grid, and a notable diplomatic first.** Zelensky told reporters in Belgrade that repeated Russian strikes have left Ukraine with "virtually no intact thermal power plants" heading into winter. Overnight Russian strikes in the Kyiv region killed four, including a three-year-old; Ukrainian officials counted 13 dead across frontline communities in 24 hours. The setting matters as much as the warning: this was Zelensky's first visit to Serbia since 2019, a real breach of Belgrade's traditional closeness to Moscow. He and Vucic agreed to cooperate on EU accession, to finalize a free trade agreement by year-end, and Serbia pledged humanitarian aid plus energy equipment. [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20260808-ukraine-braces-for-winter-power-crisis-after-russian-strikes-zelensky-says)

**Schengen stress test.** Spain imposed retaliatory checks on travellers arriving from Italy at midnight Saturday, running through Sept 7, answering Italy's Aug 1 reintroduction of controls on arrivals from Spain. The trigger was the arrival of roughly 72,000 migrants from Morocco at Spain's Ceuta enclave in late July, after Spain's Supreme Court ruled that migrants intercepted at sea en route to Ceuta or Melilla cannot be returned to Morocco. Of those still in the enclave, at least 1,342 are children, many unaccompanied; Ceuta says 1,100 children need assistance against a theoretical capacity of 90 places. Two EU founding members suspending free movement against each other is not a small thing. [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/08/08/spain-italy-ceuta-migrants-schengen-border-controls/58b374bc-9328-11f1-9fdc-0a725c989a7b_story.html)

## US

**Blanche confirmed AG, 50–49, at 4:31 a.m.** Trump's former personal criminal defense lawyer is now the country's top law enforcement officer, in one of the narrowest AG confirmations on record. Every Democrat opposed, joined by Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski; Bill Cassidy supplied the decisive vote after publicly wavering, arguing the choice was "not between perfection and Mr. Blanche" but between Blanche and another acting AG. To close it, Cornyn and Tillis extracted written assurances that the contested \$1.8 billion "Anti-Weaponization Fund" would be rescinded and that tax-audit protections from a Trump–IRS settlement would apply only retroactively to Trump, two of his sons, and the Trump Organization. Legal experts quoted by NPR doubt those side assurances are enforceable. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/09/g-s1-137631/senate-confirms-todd-blanche-attorney-general)

**Shutdown punted to December.** The Senate cleared a continuing resolution 90–6 funding the government through December 11, six weeks past the Sept 30 deadline, after days of intra-GOP wrangling over a White House-backed delay of a hemp-derived THC ban. The bill holds current levels, closes a Border Patrol funding-transfer loophole, and temporarily blocks a White House proposal letting political appointees review federal grants, Collins's provision, and the one most likely to cost Republican votes in the House. The House does not return until Aug 31 and Speaker Johnson has not said whether he will take up the Senate text; the House already passed its own GOP-only CR. [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-funding-bill-continuing-resolution-approved/)

**Ballroom blocked; Trump goes to the Supreme Court.** A 2–1 D.C. Circuit panel affirmed Judge Richard Leon's injunction, ordering a stop to above-ground work on the ballroom rising where the East Wing stood. "Each President is a temporary tenant, not the owner, of the White House," the panel wrote, holding that Congress controls funding for White House development and never authorized the roughly 90,000-square-foot, 1,000-guest project. Below-ground work Trump calls security-related may continue, and the court stayed its own order 14 days for a Supreme Court appeal. Trump called the ruling a "National Security Threat" and said he would appeal immediately. Cost estimates conflict badly: \$200M projected by the White House, ~\$400M in most reporting, \$600M+ implied by invoices the Washington Post obtained in June. *Whether the emergency application has actually been docketed, as opposed to announced, is the live uncertainty.* [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5925123/white-house-ballroom-appeals-court-congress)

**Hunter Biden on his father's cancer.** In a BBC Newsnight interview reported Saturday, Hunter Biden said the former president's prostate cancer "has spread, is metastasized into his bones and further," calling it "very painful" and "very debilitating in many respects," and adding, "I wish he would complain more, because it's not good." Biden, 83, was diagnosed in May 2025 with an aggressive prostate cancer already spread to bone; this is the most specific public update since. *Single family interview, no statement from the former president's office or physicians as of early Sunday.* [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/hunter-biden-joe-biden-prostate-cancer-spreading-rcna591499)

**Second attempt on Lisa Cook.** Deputy chief of staff Dan Scavino sent the Fed governor a letter dated Aug 5 reviving allegations, originating with FHFA Director Bill Pulte, that she misrepresented her primary residence on mortgage applications, conduct he characterized as showing "gross negligence in financial transactions" incompatible with Fed service whether or not it is criminal. Cook has until Aug 26 to respond. In June the Supreme Court held that the Fed has special constitutional standing and governors may be removed only "for cause," remanding to determine whether these allegations clear that bar. Cook's attorney Abbe Lowell calls the claims "as baseless now as they were a year ago." *Developing; no removal has occurred.* [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5925167/trump-lisa-cook-federal-reserve)

**Russia/Iran sanctions pass 86–11.** The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 sanctions senior Russian officials and authorizes tariffs of up to 100% on major buyers of Russian oil and gas, aimed at China and India. The long-stalled bill gained momentum after Graham's death in July, shortly after he returned from Kyiv announcing a White House deal on updated text. It gives Trump substantial implementation discretion, the concession that had blocked earlier versions. Johnson has signaled support; the House is out until Aug 31. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924914/senate-passes-russia-sanctions-bill-that-was-long-championed-by-lindsey-graham)

**Also from the Senate's last night, and the week's residue.** Trump's demand that senators stay to pass the SAVE America Act, documentary proof-of-citizenship voting, pushed by Mike Lee, collapsed; a narrower photo-ID substitute failed 52–46 on a procedural vote needing 60, and senators left for a five-week recess. Republicans discussed folding elements into a September budget package that would also fund the Iran war, an approach with its own Byrd-rule problems (*reporting on private conversations; single-sourced*). In Hawaii's Saturday primary, Gov. Josh Green took ~83%, Ed Case beat state Sen. Jarrett Keohokalole 57–34, and Kauai Mayor Derek Kawakami won the LG nomination after Lt. Gov. Sylvia Luke withdrew amid a bribery investigation. And around Spokane, three fires have burned ~10,300 acres, damaged or destroyed ~921 structures, and displaced some 65,000 people; containment is now established on all three.

## Israel

**Qatargate widens into an Egypt intelligence leak.** An Israeli security official told Israel Hayom that three Netanyahu advisers, Jonatan Urich, Yisrael Einhorn, and Eli Feldstein, passed classified Israeli material to Qatar while allegedly on Doha's payroll. The material reportedly came from March–May 2024 meetings involving Netanyahu, the National Security Council, the IDF, and the Shin Bet, and centered on Israeli assessments that Egypt was violating the 1979 peace treaty by moving forces into Sinai. Qatar allegedly fed it to Israeli journalists to discredit Cairo and position Doha as lead hostage mediator; defense officials feared it endangered the treaty and cut Egyptian cooperation. All three are existing Qatargate suspects: Urich and Feldstein have been arrested and questioned, Einhorn questioned in Belgrade after refusing to return. Yair Golan called it treason; Gadi Eisenkot demanded a state commission of inquiry. The PMO declined to comment. *The Qatargate legal facts are multiply sourced; the leak allegation itself rests on one unnamed official via a single outlet. Developing and contested.* [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/netanyahu-aides-accused-of-leaking-qatar-classified-intel-for-anti-egypt-push/)

**Gaza: mutual preconditions, no movement.** Netanyahu has said publicly that Israel "didn't agree" to the Board of Peace's plan for disarming Hamas and that the IDF will not withdraw "from our current lines" until Hamas is "completely disarmed." The Board of Peace's framing is that withdrawal beyond the Yellow Line follows completed decommissioning; Hamas says it implements nothing until Israel meets withdrawal obligations. On the ground Saturday, medical sources told Anadolu that one man was killed by Israeli fire in Deir al-Balah and nine wounded including four children, seven of them at Nasser Hospital after tank fire near al-Shakoush. Israeli forces placed new concrete barriers marking the Yellow Line on Salah al-Din Street near Shujayea. Gaza's Health Ministry puts the post-ceasefire toll at 1,257 killed and 4,131 wounded since Oct 10, 2025, and says only about 35% of expected aid trucks have entered. *Diplomatic positions are on-record; casualty figures are Gaza Health Ministry and Palestinian medical sources, not independently verified, with no IDF comment located on the Aug 8 incidents.* [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms)

**Lebanon pilot zone incursion.** Lebanon's NNA and the local mayor said an Israeli force advanced into Zawtar al-Gharbiya shortly after midnight Friday and erected an earthen barrier near the town square; an AFP correspondent independently saw a barrier at the village's eastern entrance. Zawtar al-Gharbiya is one of the first designated "pilot zones" under the US-brokered framework, where the Lebanese Armed Forces deployed July 21 and roughly 50 families have since returned. A Lebanese military source called it a violation; the IDF told AFP it was "not aware of such an event in this area." It lands days after the seventh round of US-mediated talks in Rome, where Lebanon pushed to expand the pilot-zone scheme and Israel insisted on verifying compliance in the first zones first. *The barrier is confirmed by an AFP eyewitness; who built it rests on Lebanese sources.* [Arab News/AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2653878)

**The October election is scrambling the right.** Likud attacked Yuli Edelstein and Gilad Erdan as "embittered fake right-wingers" after they launched a new party built around opposition to ultra-Orthodox draft exemptions; Erdan replied that Likud perpetuates "draft evasion and abandons families of soldiers." Channel 12 reported that Naftali Bennett and Avigdor Liberman have discussed a post-election merger specifically to block Eisenkot from the premiership (*single-source, unconfirmed*). Polling is volatile: a Midgam survey had Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar tied at 23 seats with the Jewish opposition bloc one seat short of a majority, while an Aug 6 Direct Polls survey showed Eisenkot leading Netanyahu for the first time. MK Yoav Segalovitz formally resigned from the Knesset. And the parents of slain hostage Itay Chen urged voters to back only parties committing to a state commission of inquiry into Oct 7, asking "who knew, who warned, who ignored", a demand Eisenkot echoed Sunday over the Qatar affair. [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-09-2026/)

**Briefly:** the IDF says troop conduct in Idna is under review after footage showed a soldier beating a Palestinian alongside two Israeli civilians, and Palestinian sources report six injured in a settler attack at Wadi Rahim in the southern Hebron Hills. CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper visited Israel Aug 8, meeting IDF chief Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir. Roughly half of Gaza's 36 hospitals are only partially functional, with the Health Ministry reporting 61% shortfalls in cancer and blood-disorder medicines.

**Payrolls fell.** July nonfarm payrolls **dropped 23,000** against a +83,000 consensus, the first outright decline in months, with May revised down 66,000 (to +63,000) and June down 37,000 (to +20,000), 103,000 lower combined. The counterintuitive part: because this Fed is weighing a *hike*, not a cut, weak data read as risk-positive. **Warsh's actual problem.** Chair Kevin Warsh goes into the Sept 15–16 meeting with genuine stagflationary tension: June CPI at 3.5% y/y against a labor market that just contracted. July's FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% on a **9–3 split**, the fifth consecutive hold, with three members preferring a 25 bp increase; Kashkari said in early August it was time to raise. Warsh has deliberately cut forward guidance, and the standing complaint from strategists is that he has never specified what would trigger a hike, which makes the reaction function, not the data, the main source of rate volatility. Politically it is combustible: Trump has pressed publicly for cuts, and NEC director Kevin Hassett said Friday the two speak "all the time." [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/kevin-warsh-fed-inflation-rate-hike-markets.html)

The fund had been up 439% in the first half of 2026. *The collapse mechanics are well corroborated; how much of the rebound to attribute to the unwind versus the jobs print is CNBC's framing.* [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-fund-fire-sale.html)

## Worth reading

**Evan Osnos, "The Future, Made in China"**, The New Yorker, Aug 3 online / Aug 10 print. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china)\
Osnos, who lived in Beijing from 2005 to 2013, returns and argues America's self-inflicted retreat has arrived precisely as decades of Chinese industrial policy pay off, and he grounds it in specifics rather than declinist mood. China now generates more than twice the electricity of the US at far lower prices, ran a record \$1.2 trillion trade surplus after the tariffs backfired, and in 2024 deployed nine industrial robots for every one installed in America. The section that will land hardest for you is the science reversal: a Princeton/Harvard/MIT study counted nearly 20,000 scientists of Chinese descent leaving the US between 2010 and 2021; China now runs more clinical trials than the US, at under half the recruitment time and roughly 40% cheaper, generates nearly a third of the world's new drug candidates, and in 2024 Akeso's experimental cancer drug outperformed Keytruda. Peking University's Yi Rao is bracingly self-critical, he dismisses 30% of Chinese biology papers as "garbage" and says China excels at "two to a hundred," not "zero to one", but tells Osnos that if the US defunds cancer research, "it would now be natural that China might invent a cancer vaccine before the U.S." Then Osnos undercuts the triumphalism with China's own engineering hubris: the one-child policy, designed by a rocket scientist on false-precision extrapolations, and a fertility rate now at one birth per woman with 90 million empty homes. His conclusion is that the successor to the American order is not a Chinese order but "un-order."

**Kai Bird & Susan Goldmark, "The Mentor: How Roy Cohn Taught Donald Trump Everything"**, The New Yorker, Aug 3 online / Aug 10 print. [Read](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/roy-cohn-profile)\
Bird, co-author of *American Prometheus*, reconstructs the Cohn–Trump relationship from its 1973 origin at Le Club, and they interviewed Trump himself last year, so his self-serving version runs against the documentary record. The organizing case is the 1973 DOJ housing-discrimination suit: the government would have accepted a no-fine, no-admission consent decree, but Cohn told Trump to reject it and countersue for \$100 million in defamation, a filing a federal judge threw out as frivolous. Trump signed essentially the same decree in 1975 and spent decades claiming he had won, the thesis in miniature: Cohn's real lesson was that strident counterclaims can drown out facts, and controlling the narrative beats winning the law. They follow the favor bank through the Commodore Hotel abatement, Trump Tower's concrete poured by a firm jointly owned by the Genovese and Gambino bosses straight through the 1982 strike, and Cohn's use of Reagan contacts to put Trump's sister on the federal bench. It ends with Cohn dying of AIDS in 1986, invited to one last Mar-a-Lago dinner, after which staff were reportedly told to "fumigate" the cutlery.
