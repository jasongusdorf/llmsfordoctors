## International

### Canada and the U.S. go to open trade war

Negotiations collapsed late Friday, minutes before a deadline that ushered in 50% American tariffs on a broad range of Canadian goods worth roughly \$20 billion by the administration's own count. Mark Carney said he suspended talks because Washington introduced last-minute terms that were "unfair, uneconomic, and called into question the reliability of any deal," and pledged to retaliate "dollar for dollar." USTR Jamieson Greer told reporters Canada had walked away and had made "new demands and walk backs of other commitments." Negotiators had been locked in the USTR's office for up to ten hours a day; the sticking points were autos, steel, aluminum and softwood lumber on the Canadian side, and provincial boycotts of American wine and spirits plus dairy access on the American side. Carney has domestic cover: a recent Léger poll found 56% of Canadians opposed to further concessions, and Doug Ford endorsed the walkout within the hour. *NYT and FT, Aug 21–22.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/21/world/canada/trump-tariffs-trade-no-deal-carney-canada.html) · [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/24f9c08e-e23f-434e-b0f4-fc1eb55a7b04)

### Double-tap strike on Kryvyi Rih; Kyiv hit again overnight

Russian drones struck a crowded shopping complex in Zelensky's hometown in a daytime attack Friday, with a second wave arriving roughly thirty minutes later while rescue crews were already working the site, the signature of a deliberate double-tap. The Kyiv Independent reported at least 16 dead and about 130 injured including 23 children; other outlets have carried figures from 14 to 19, so treat the toll as unstable and rising. Overnight into Saturday, ballistic missiles hit Kyiv shortly after 1:15 a.m.; Ukraine's air force claimed 182 drones and nine missiles downed or suppressed, with at least seven killed nationwide. Ukrainian drones in turn hit an Ozon logistics warehouse in Samara Oblast and set the Perm refinery alight, though those deep-strike claims are Ukrainian-sourced and unverified. On the diplomatic track, Sergey Ryabkov said Moscow is "ready to listen to any reasonable proposals" that match Putin's goals, and the Kyiv Independent reports, single-source, that Washington and Kyiv are floating a "ceasefire in the skies" as a first step. Putin's stated preconditions have not moved. *Kyiv Independent, Al Jazeera, FT, Aug 21–22. ### Iran's president breaks ranks on ending the war

Masoud Pezeshkian said publicly Friday it would be "better to end the war today while we are in a position of strength and dignity," defending the June memorandum with Washington as "dignified." He runs the economy but does not set wartime strategy, and the split is now visible: the Revolutionary Guards insist Washington cannot be trusted and Tehran can absorb more pain, while Speaker Ghalibaf, in Iraq, warned that "no matter how strong we are militarily, if the people are hungry... we will not endure." ### The DRC Ebola outbreak is still accelerating, and the vaccine may not be the right one

WHO figures as of Aug 20 put the outbreak at 5,290 confirmed cases and 2,516 deaths, with 81 new cases and 40 new deaths since the previous report. Ituri accounts for 4,447 cases and 1,984 deaths across 28 of 36 health zones; Bas-Ualé became the sixth affected province on Aug 13. More than 16,000 doses of Ervebo arrived at N'djili airport in Kinshasa late Friday, the first tranche of a pledged 70,000. The caveat you will care about: this outbreak is the *Bundibugyo* species, and Ervebo is licensed against Zaire ebolavirus. WHO's vaccine advisory group only recommended a Phase 3 trial of Ervebo against Bundibugyo on Aug 7, so the doses arriving are effectively an unproven intervention deployed under emergency conditions. The UN this week described the spread as "exponential." *WHO, AP, Al Jazeera, Aug 20–22.* [WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/situations/ebola-outbreak---drc-2026)

## United States

### Federal judge voids the 75-country visa ban

Judge Jeannette A. Vargas of the Southern District of New York issued a 61-page ruling Friday striking down the administration's ban on issuing immigrant visas to nationals of 75 countries, finding it "contrary to law" and beyond Marco Rubio's statutory authority. The policy was billed as a "pause" premised on applicants becoming a public charge; Vargas found that consular officers were in fact instructed to deny visas *even when applicants provided evidence overcoming the public-charge finding*, in violation of the statutory requirement for individualized assessment. An internal State Department cable entered as evidence carried that instruction in plain language. The ruling also reverses any denial based solely on the policy, which took effect in January, potentially forcing review of thousands of applications. Plaintiffs included six U.S. citizens with relatives blocked in Ghana, Jamaica, Guatemala and Ethiopia, plus five Colombian professionals including a Harvard-trained endocrinologist. The 2018 Supreme Court decision upholding the third travel ban addressed entry, not visa issuance, a distinction Vargas leaned on. Appeal to the Second Circuit is likely. *NYT, Aug 21.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/21/us/politics/judge-trump-75-country-visa-ban.html)

### The Postal Service publishes its mail-ballot rule

A 95-page final rule published Friday spells out how USPS would implement the March executive order restricting mail voting. Under it, the Postal Service would deliver mail ballots only in states that share voter data with the agency, would inspect every ballot envelope for compliance before accepting it into the system, and would refuse delivery entirely to non-complying states. The order was largely blocked by federal courts this summer; USPS says it will not implement for the 2026 election "unless and until the government obtains relief from those injunctions," and DOJ has told the Supreme Court the same. The Court is reviewing and could rule at any time, roughly ten weeks from the midterms. Worth remembering the president's own stated rationale: in March he told Republican lawmakers that cracking down on mail ballots, which Democrats now use more than Republicans, would "guarantee the midterms." He voted by mail earlier this week. *NYT, Aug 21.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/21/us/politics/postal-service-rule-restrict-mail-ballots.html)

### Pentagon fires the top editors of Stars and Stripes

The Defense Department on Friday terminated editor in chief Erik Slavin, publisher Max Lederer (who had already announced his retirement, citing conflict with Pentagon leadership) and Middle East correspondent Lara Korte. The firings came roughly ten days after the paper reported on hardships aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, whose deployment was extended to nine months during the Iran war. Slavin said his termination notice cited insubordination and an unauthorized media appearance, a CBS News interview in July about editorial interference at the paper. "I am being fired for stating in a CBS interview that censorship of news for service members would constitute a red line," he said. Capt. William Urban, installed as military deputy to the publisher earlier this month, wrote Friday that his priority is "the highest quality of editorially independent reporting." Stars and Stripes' editorial independence is required by statute. *NYT, Aug 21.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/21/us/politics/pentagon-fires-stars-stripes-editor.html)

### Alex Jones award slashed on a technicality of Texas law

The Third Court of Appeals in Austin cut the \$49 million Sandy Hook judgment against Jones to roughly \$6 million, preserving the \$4 million in compensatory damages but slashing the \$45 million punitive award to fit the state's statutory cap. The court held that the claims by Scarlett Lewis and Neil Heslin, parents of six-year-old Jesse Lewis, did not warrant lifting the cap, and that the trial judge improperly allowed an amended petition after the verdict. Jones called it a victory. It does not touch the \$1 billion-plus he owes the Connecticut families, a verdict that has survived multiple appeals. No family has collected anything; the money is tied up in bankruptcy court and the realistic recovery is a small fraction of any award. *NYT, Aug 21.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/21/us/politics/alex-jones-sandy-hook.html)

### Roberts pauses the ballroom injunction

Chief Justice Roberts issued an administrative stay Friday allowing construction of the roughly \$400 million ballroom on the site of the demolished East Wing to continue. The D.C. Circuit had ruled 2–1 for the National Trust for Historic Preservation that the project cannot proceed without congressional authorization; two courts have now found it unlawful on that ground. Because this was a solo administrative order there is no reasoning, vote count, or noted dissent, and Roberts is expected to refer the application to the full Court. Procedural, not a merits ruling, but the practical effect is that the concrete keeps pouring while the Court decides. *NPR, PBS, CBS, Aug 21.* [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/21/nx-s1-5935417/supreme-court-allows-trumps-ballroom-construction-to-continue-for-now)

### Removals to Haiti resume; the Liberia pact goes live

Two enforcement milestones landed Thursday. A flight from Alexandria, Louisiana carrying 161 people landed in Cap-Haïtien, the first removal flight to Haiti since TPS lapsed for more than 300,000 Haitian nationals, and the eighth flight to Haiti this year. Port-au-Prince was avoided because gangs control the capital; twice-weekly flights are planned, and the UN has warned Haiti cannot safely absorb mass returns. Separately, twenty deportees, mostly Venezuelan, Cuban and Colombian, were flown to Liberia, the first removals under an arrangement for Liberia to accept up to 1,200 third-country nationals over a year in exchange for \$5 million in "migration management" funding. The Liberia pact faces multiple lawsuits. Closer to home, NYT reports Haitians with TPS in Springfield, Ohio are now being fitted with ankle monitors. *Bloomberg, Washington Post, NBC, NYT, Aug 20–22.* [WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/immigration/2026/08/20/ice-begins-deporting-haitians-who-lost-tps-back-homeland/) · [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/22/us/haiti-tps-springfield-ankle-monitors.html)

### Beef, water, power, and the midterm map

Trump announced a 90-day suspension of out-of-quota tariffs on up to 300,000 metric tons of imported ground beef, framed as relief from record prices before the midterms, claiming "a commitment that this beef will be sold at 25% below current market prices", with no mechanism disclosed and no obligation on retailers to pass anything through. ## Israel & the region

### A U.S. envoy publicly contradicts Netanyahu on the Syria strike

Tom Barrack said Saturday that Israel's Aug 17–18 strike on the Abu al-Duhur airbase near Aleppo risked direct conflict with Turkey, that Israeli jets went in without notification, and that Israel was "baiting the Turks" given Ankara's history of downing aircraft in similar circumstances. Critically, he said U.S. intelligence found no evidence of Turkish military movement to the site, directly contradicting Netanyahu's office, which had justified the strike as pre-empting a Turkish deployment that would breach the Israel–Syria status quo. Turkey and Syria both deny any Turkish presence there. Barrack also floated a domestic Israeli electoral motive, which is his characterization rather than established fact. Separately, Syrian media reported one person wounded Saturday in an Israeli drone strike on a truck near Mazraat Beit Jinn, about six miles from the Israeli border; no Israeli comment. *Haaretz, Times of Israel, Aug 22.* [ToI liveblog](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-22-2026/)

### Turkey seeks an Interpol red notice for Netanyahu

Justice Minister Akin Gurlek announced Friday that Turkey had issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and an Israeli named Afek Moskovitch over the May interception of a Gaza-bound flotilla carrying roughly 430 people, citing genocide, crimes against humanity, torture and unlawful seizure of a vessel, and had requested Interpol red notices. Netanyahu's office called it "a pathetic attempt to intimidate the leaders and soldiers of Israel" and said Erdogan "now seeks to extend his aggression against Israel into Syria." Interpol screens red notice requests and has not, as reported, acted; practical enforcement is close to nil. The symbolism, layered on the airbase strike, is the point. *Times of Israel, Haaretz, Aug 21.* [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/turkey-issues-fresh-genocide-arrest-warrant-for-netanyahu-amid-growing-syria-tensions/)

On Friday settlers from Metzad entered Area B near the village of Sa'ir in the southern West Bank on what the IDF itself called an "unauthorized excursion." ### Lebanon: everyone claims the last word, nobody has it

Christina Goldbaum's analysis from Beirut is the sharpest thing written on Lebanon this week. Four months and seven rounds after the first direct Israeli–Lebanese talks in decades, there is nothing to show. President Joseph Aoun took office with a mandate to reclaim the state's monopoly on arms; his government did real work stripping Hezbollah's influence from the airport, the port and the banks, and then Hezbollah fired on Israel in March in solidarity with Iran and dragged the country into war anyway, which is precisely the demonstration that the project failed. The two sides agreed in principle to a phased Israeli withdrawal matched by Lebanese Army deployment, but the rollout has stalled, with Israel refusing to expand it. Lebanon's National News Agency reported another night of Israeli strikes and demolitions across the south. Paul Salem: "They are at complete loggerheads." The World Bank projects a 6.4% contraction this year. *NYT, Aug 22.* [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/22/world/middleeast/lebanon-hezbollah-israel-diplomacy.html)

### Gaza and the WCK file

The IDF announced Aug 19 that it will not bring criminal charges over the April 2024 drone strike on a marked World Central Kitchen convoy that killed seven aid workers. On Friday the UK, Canada and Australia issued a joint statement calling the closure "shameful"; WCK called it "inconsistent with the full truth and deeply offensive." Several officers were dismissed after the 2024 internal review, but no one has faced charges in more than two years. ## Worth reading

### Rachel Aviv, "The Children a Scientist Took Home", The New Yorker

This one is squarely yours. Carleton Gajdusek won the 1976 Nobel for showing that kuru, the fatal spongiform encephalopathy of the Fore people in the eastern highlands of Papua New Guinea, was transmissible, work that opened the door to prions. Aviv's piece is about what he did alongside the science: he ran an NIH lab focused on child development in societies "wherein civilization and the major religions of mankind have not yet set their pattern of imprint," collected blood and urine from children he gave lollipops to, kept 600,000 pages of journals, and brought dozens of boys back to the United States. The story is told largely through Luwi Ikabala, now 57, who hems sleeves at a market in Goroka, reads Dostoyevsky, has kept a journal for forty years at Gajdusek's urging, and calls him "Father of America." Gajdusek was later convicted of child molestation. Aviv does what she always does, refuses to let the reader settle into either the hagiography or the monster story. For anyone who thinks about research ethics, consent, and what field science extracts from the people it studies, this is the essay of the month. [Read it](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/24/the-scientist-who-lived-with-dozens-of-children-from-the-south-pacific)

### Eric Klinenberg, "The End of the European Summer", The New Yorker

Klinenberg wrote the definitive sociology of the 1995 Chicago heat wave, so he is the right person to look at European cities in 2026 and ask what happens when extreme heat overwhelms places physically built for a climate that no longer exists, stone, no air conditioning, dense elderly populations living alone. Also in the same issue: Andrew Marantz on how far the administration will go to deport Mahmoud Khalil, and Adam Gopnik on a nineteenth-century sex scandal and what our appetite for public shaming costs the truth.
