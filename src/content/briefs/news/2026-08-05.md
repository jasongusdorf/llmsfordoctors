## International

### Russia's heaviest Kyiv barrage in weeks kills at least 17

Overnight Tuesday into Wednesday, Russia fired roughly 29 missiles (24 ballistic, four cruise) and about 115 drones at Kyiv and surrounding towns including Brovary and Bucha. At least 17 people were killed. The targets were overwhelmingly civilian logistics: warehouses, a brewery, construction-materials storage, an Epicentr retail store, a railway station. Cluster munitions were reportedly used. The story underneath the story is arithmetic: Ukraine's Patriot interceptor stocks are depleted, and Zelensky said plainly that "ballistic missile interceptors could have saved the lives of those killed today." Washington is weighing Ukrainian component manufacture with final assembly in Germany, or a cheaper interceptor variant, but neither arrives in time for this month. A 40-day retaliatory air campaign has just expired. FT, BBC, Al Jazeera · Aug 5 · toll ranged 15-17 in early reports, so treat the exact figure as still firming. [Open →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/russian-attacks-kill-17-exploiting-ukraines-lack-of-missile-interceptors)

### Hormuz: a deal reportedly close, on terms Washington doesn't love

Regional officials said Tuesday that Iran and Oman have converged on a framework to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, largely closed since Iranian forces declared it shut in March following the February 28 US and Israeli strikes. Ships would enter the Gulf through an Iranian-controlled lane and exit through an Omani-controlled one, with service fees levied for security and environmental protection. Rubio said there has been "progress made in those talks, but not finality yet"; Bessent suggested a deal was imminent, which is what moved markets. The sticking points are real: the US objects to Iranian tolls and wants international-waterway status restored, and Tehran ties any deal to lifting the US blockade of its ports. Note the sequencing dispute in the public record too. Trump has described talks with Tehran as resuming; Iran's foreign ministry says it is talking only to Oman, not to Washington. AP, FT, CNN, Bloomberg · Aug 4-5 · Developing. No signed agreement. [Open →](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/iran-and-oman-make-progress-on-deal-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz-officials-say)

## US

### El-Sayed wins Michigan, and the Democratic Party's argument gets louder

Abdul El-Sayed, the progressive former Detroit health director, defeated Rep. Haley Stevens in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary. NBC projected the race Wednesday morning with El-Sayed up roughly 19,400 votes out of more than 1.5 million cast, turnout that broke the record for any non-presidential Democratic primary in Michigan going back to at least 1998. He did it against roughly \$65m in outside spending, much of it AIPAC-aligned money for Stevens. He now faces former Rep. Mike Rogers, who was unopposed, in a race that could decide Senate control.

For a physician-reader this one has an extra layer: El-Sayed is an MD and epidemiologist, a former county health director, and he ran explicitly on health policy. Read alongside Edward Luce's FT column this morning on the Democrats' widening civil war over Israel, the result reads less as an upset than as a measurement. NBC, NYT, WaPo, CNN · called ~5-7am ET Aug 5 · margin still narrowing as counting completes. ## Israel

### The Gaza framework's sequencing problem breaks into the open

After Board of Peace Gaza envoy Nickolay Mladenov met Netanyahu in Jerusalem on Monday, the US-led board issued a statement saying withdrawal of the IDF beyond the Yellow Line "will take place only once decommissioning is complete." That inverts the October 2025 roadmap, under which Hamas disarmament and Israeli pullback were to proceed in tandem. Netanyahu then said publicly that Israel "didn't agree" to the board's terms and would not withdraw until Hamas is completely disarmed, while his office said the published text "does not reflect Israel's positions." Smotrich called the agreement "completely different" from what cabinet approved. Hamas insists Israel must implement its obligations first. This sequencing fight is now the central obstacle to phase two, and the NYT's analysis frames the escalating strikes as actively undermining the plan. Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, NYT, i24 · Aug 3-4. [Open →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-appears-to-shift-terms-for-idf-pullback-after-meeting-pm-gaza-airstrike-reported/)

### IDF pushes strike approval up to the chief of staff

The IDF has centralized authority for targeted killings in Gaza: most now require personal approval from Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir, rather than Southern Command chief Maj. Gen. Yaniv Asor or division commanders. Troops facing immediate ground threats are exempt. The change followed a political-echelon decision to impose stricter oversight, and reporting suggests it will substantially reduce strike volume. Read against the withdrawal dispute, it looks like a deliberate de-escalation lever. JPost, Times of Israel, Ynet, Haaretz · Aug 4. [Open →](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/defense-news/article-904571)

### Tariffs: \$100bn back out the door

US customs officials have refunded roughly \$100bn in "liberation day" tariffs, about 60% of the levies struck down by the Supreme Court in *Learning Resources Inc. v. Trump* in February. The policy has already been rebuilt on new footing: the temporary 10% global Section 122 surcharge expired July 24 and was replaced the same day by Section 301 duties of 10-12.5% covering 60-80 trading partners and about 99.4% of US imports, tied to forced-labor investigations. Because Section 301 carries no statutory expiration, the wall is structurally more permanent than what it replaced. The average effective US tariff rate has run near 17%, the highest since the early 1930s, and NY Fed research finds roughly 90% of the cost is borne by US firms and consumers. FT, Thomson Reuters, PwC, Brookings · Aug 5. [Open →](https://www.ft.com/world)

### Chips, and a hawkish Fed

AMD reported Q2 revenue of \$11.54bn, up 50.1%, with non-GAAP EPS of \$1.66 and data center revenue doubling to \$6.7bn, now 58% of sales. Q3 guidance came in about 2.9% above the Street.
