**TL;DR.** The US-Iran framework deal is the story of the week, with an MOU reportedly slated for signing June 19 in Geneva even as Israel's campaign against Hizbollah in Lebanon threatens to derail it and Trump publicly scolds both sides. At home, the Fed delivers its first decision under Chair Kevin Warsh at 2pm ET today, with a hold near-certain but the tone in focus. And after 53 years, the Knicks are NBA champions.

## International

**US-Iran framework deal edges toward signing.** The biggest thread running through the week is the emerging agreement to wind down the war that began in February. US and Iranian officials have described a framework built around a 60-day ceasefire and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, with a memorandum of understanding reportedly slated for signing on June 19 in Geneva and Pakistan acting as mediator. The MOU is meant to open, not close, the hard questions: Iran's nuclear and ballistic programs, sanctions relief and reconstruction, freedom of navigation, and a durable peace. Both Washington and Tehran are eager to frame it as a win. The NYT's news analysis (Steven Erlanger, June 13) cautions that Iran's newer, more militaristic leadership has survived the worst the US and Israel could deliver and now seems readier to absorb risk -- a sobering read on how much leverage the US actually holds. Developing / partly single-thread: exact terms and the signing date are still reported secondhand; treat specifics as provisional. Sources: NYT Middle East, Al Jazeera, CSIS.

**G7 summit in Evian-les-Bains.** France hosted the leaders June 15-17, with the Iran war and China's economic challenge topping the formal agenda. The subtext was Ukraine: European leaders, led by Macron, spent the summit trying to push the war back up Trump's priority list after more than four years of fighting and a US pullback on aid that has left France and its allies as Kyiv's largest backers. Reporting frames the gathering as allies quietly insuring themselves against US disengagement rather than securing firm new commitments. Sources: NPR, CFR, Newsweek.

**Trump leans on Israel and Hizbollah.** Per the FT's lead, the president has publicly told both Israel and Hizbollah to "stand down" to protect the Iran deal, tying regional de-escalation directly to the Hormuz reopening he expects. It is an unusually direct intervention in Israel's Lebanon operations -- see the Israel section. Source: Financial Times (read via Chrome).

**Other international threads.** UN Secretary-General Guterres visited Port-au-Prince as Haiti's gang crisis grinds on (roughly 2,300 killed there this year). And the US military struck a boat it accused of drug smuggling in the eastern Pacific, killing one and leaving two survivors -- part of a pattern of maritime interdiction worth watching. Single-source items; lightly confirmed. Sources: NPR World, wires.

## US

**DOJ scrutiny of Trump's opponents widens.** California Governor Gavin Newsom said on June 15 that the Justice Department is investigating him and his wife, and accused the administration of targeting him politically. It lands amid a documented pattern -- indictments and investigations touching James Comey, Letitia James, Lisa Cook and others, an election-fraud prosecutor installed with nationwide authority, and the Public Integrity Section reportedly gutted from 36 attorneys to two. Former DOJ lawyers have gone public about how the department has been reshaped. This is the throughline of US political coverage right now and the one I'd watch most closely. Sources: NBC News, ABC News, Slate, Protect Democracy tracker.

**World Cup is here.** The tournament is underway across the US, Canada and Mexico, with several first-round upsets already. After a strangely muted buildup and a rough stretch for the US men's team, the games themselves are starting to pull in attention. Sources: PBS NewsHour; New Yorker (Louisa Thomas).

**Worth a footnote.** A University of Hawaii study reports that tectonic stress on Southern California's San Andreas and San Jacinto systems is at its highest in ~1,000 years -- though the authors stress there's no sign of imminent rupture. Useful context, not cause for alarm. Source: regional news, study writeup.

## Israel

**Lebanon is the flashpoint.** Israel's escalating campaign against Hizbollah -- including strikes on Beirut's outskirts -- is the complication sitting on top of the Iran deal. Trump said plainly he is "not happy with the way Israel has handled themselves with Lebanon and with Hizbollah," while Netanyahu insists troops will remain in southern Lebanon "for as long as necessary." Iran's military command has threatened retaliation if the southern Lebanon offensive continues. The result is a delicate, contradictory moment: cautious optimism about a regional deal layered over a hot war that hasn't stopped. Sources: Times of Israel liveblog (June 17), SBS, NYT.

**Discontent at home over the deal.** The NYT's Isabel Kershner reports broad unease across the Israeli political spectrum that the emerging US-Iran framework leaves fundamental security threats unaddressed -- Netanyahu has publicly said Israel is not a party to the deal Trump announced. Separately, the NYT reports Israel is building an army base in Jenin in apparent breach of 1990s accords, and domestic politics churn on (a Knesset immunity fight over MK Tally Gotliv; Haredi protests over draft enforcement near Bnei Brak). Sources: NYT Middle East, Times of Israel.

## Economic & Markets

**The Fed, today.** The main event is the FOMC decision at 2:00pm ET, the first under new Chair Kevin Warsh, followed by his debut press conference at 2:30pm. Markets price a hold at 3.50%-3.75% as near-certain (~97% odds), so the story is the tone, the dot plot, and how Warsh frames a path where cuts are no longer the baseline -- May CPI ran hot at 4.2% year over year on energy-driven inflation, and a December hike is on the table in some pricing. Note: as of this morning the decision has not yet been announced; coverage projecting a "hawkish hold" is anticipatory. Sources: StockTitan, IndexBox, Kiplinger, FT.

**Markets and oil.** The Dow notched a record high on June 16, while the Nasdaq and Russell 2000 backslid into the close ahead of the Fed. Treasury yields fell and oil slid another ~5% on hopes that a Hormuz reopening normalizes traffic -- a direct market read-through from the diplomacy above. Source: TheStreet.

**Two FT threads worth your eye.** Wall Street is digesting a record fundraising haul as the AI capital race intensifies (the FT's framing: "AI is revolutionising the stock market"). And China is teeing up a digital payments system pitched explicitly as a competitor to the dollar -- a slower-burn but strategically meaningful story for anyone tracking dollar dominance. Source: Financial Times (read via Chrome).

## Also notable

**Knicks win it all.** New York beat the San Antonio Spurs 94-90 in Game 5 to take the Finals 4-1 -- their first title since 1973. Jalen Brunson took Finals MVP with a Knicks Finals-record 45 points; the team erased double-digit deficits in all five games, including a 29-point Game 4 hole. Brunson, Mikal Bridges and Josh Hart became the first trio to win both an NCAA title and an NBA championship together. Sources: ESPN, NBA.com.

**Quick hits.** Swiss voters rejected a referendum to cap the population at 10 million (FT). Norway's Marius Borg Hoiby was sentenced to four years, convicted on two of four rape charges (wires). And the New World screwworm -- a flesh-eating parasite contained for decades -- has broken back through US defenses into Texas cattle after a containment program began failing in 2023 (FT). Sources: FT, wires.

## Worth reading (New Yorker & long-form)

**"How Did American Christianity End Up Like This?" -- Michael Luo.** A sharp essay arguing that the separation of church and state turned American religion into a competitive marketplace, and that an anti-intellectual, fiercely political strand of Protestantism became its biggest winner. Given your own work on Judaism and identity, this is the standout of the week's long-form. New Yorker.

**"Iran Can Hold America Hostage in Either War or Peace" -- Robin Wright.** A clear-eyed companion to the top story: Wright argues that seizing the Strait of Hormuz is an adaptation of Iran's long-running strategy of grabbing things of value to extract concessions -- meaning the leverage doesn't vanish just because a deal gets signed. New Yorker.
