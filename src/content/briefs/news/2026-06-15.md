**TL;DR , ** The 2026 Iran war is winding toward a close: the US and Iran have reached a framework deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and lift the US naval blockade, with a signing ceremony slated for Friday in Switzerland, though Israelis are deeply uneasy and Lebanon's Hezbollah front is still hot. Oil has cratered on the news while markets cheer SpaceX's record IPO that just made Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire. Twelve died in a Missouri skydiving-plane crash, and the Supreme Court enters a make-or-break final month.

## International

### US–Iran reach a framework deal; Strait of Hormuz to reopen

The dominant story of the weekend: President Trump announced that the United States and Iran have agreed the text of a framework deal to end the 2026 war, with a formal signing ceremony scheduled for **Friday, June 19, in Switzerland**. In its current form the agreement establishes a 60-day ceasefire, a memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran, the immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping, removal of the US naval blockade, and provisions to dismantle Iran's nuclear program and remove its enriched uranium. A senior administration official put the odds of signing at roughly 80% and stressed the two sides were “not quite at the finish line yet.” The deal is well cross-confirmed across CNN, AP, NBC, PBS, Axios, the FT and NYT, but remains a framework, not a signed agreement, treat the final terms as still-developing. (CNN, AP/NBC, PBS, FT, June 14)

### G7 summit opens in Evian-les-Bains

The 52nd G7 convenes in the French spa town of Evian (June 14–17), with the Iran deal overtaking the agenda. European leaders arrive carrying grievances over US tariffs, Trump's ambiguous NATO posture, and the economic fallout of the Hormuz closure. Volodymyr Zelensky is set to join a Tuesday working session on European security but, tellingly, will not get a one-on-one with Trump, a marker of Kyiv's diminished standing in Washington. Thousands marched in nearby Geneva under a “No-G7” banner. (NPR, Al Jazeera, FT, June 14)

### Lebanon front still burning

Even as regional optimism builds, Israel struck the outskirts of Beirut on Saturday as fighting with Hezbollah escalated, and Lebanon's government warned displaced residents against rushing home. The NYT framed it bluntly: “Lebanon's war grinds on,” out of sync with the cautious calm taking hold elsewhere. (NYT, June 12–14)

### Briefly

Swiss voters **rejected** a referendum proposal to cap the country's population at 10 million. In the UK, far-right activist Tommy Robinson was detained under terrorism legislation, and Keir Starmer is preparing to announce an Australia-style social-media ban for teenagers. (FT, June 14)

## US

### Twelve killed in Missouri skydiving-plane crash

A single-engine Pacific Aerospace P750 turboprop operated by Skydive Kansas City crashed Sunday morning shortly after takeoff near Butler Memorial Airport, about 60 miles south of Kansas City, killing all 12 aboard, 11 skydivers and the pilot. Witnesses described the plane failing to gain altitude, banking sharply left, and going down near Business I-49, possibly during an attempted emergency landing. It ranks among the deadliest US skydiving incidents in decades; the FAA and NTSB are investigating. (WaPo, CNN, NPR, June 14)

### Supreme Court's explosive final month

The Court is racing to clear roughly two dozen cases before its summer recess, several central to Trump's agenda: a final ruling on his **birthright-citizenship** order, the bid to end **Temporary Protected Status** for certain Haitian and Syrian migrants, plus executive-power, mail-ballot and Second Amendment disputes. The justices recently sided with the administration in a fight over speech restrictions on immigration judges. Expect a cluster of consequential decisions over the next two weeks. (CNN, PBS, Reuters)

### Briefly

Sam Bankman-Fried lost his appeal of his FTX fraud conviction, and former star bond investor Ken Leech pleaded guilty to obstructing a federal probe. (FT, June 14)

## Israel

### Broad discontent over the emerging Iran deal

Inside Israel the mood is sour rather than celebratory. NYT's Isabel Kershner reports that Israelis across the political spectrum fear the US framework leaves the fundamental security threats posed by Iran unaddressed, trading a temporary ceasefire for an Iran that, in the view of NYT analysis, has grown *more* willing to absorb pressure under its newer, more militaristic leadership. Former PM Naftali Bennett charged that the government has failed to convert Israel's battlefield gains into durable security. (NYT, June 13–14)

### Civil-defense gaps exposed

The State Comptroller warned that “the lack of protective infrastructure endangers lives,” finding that roughly **one-third of Israel's residents** are not adequately protected against missile attack, a stark admission after months of war. The IDF Home Front Command rolled out an updated alert app (red/yellow/green prompts). Separately, US refueling aircraft stationed at Ben-Gurion are expected to be partially relocated to ease pressure on the airport. (Times of Israel, June 15)

### Briefly

Netanyahu media adviser Jonatan Urich was charged with endangering state security over an alleged leak of classified Hamas-negotiation intelligence to a German newspaper. The PA's Mahmoud Abbas said presidential elections would be held in 2027. (NYT, Times of Israel, June 11–14)

## Economic & Markets

### SpaceX's record IPO mints the first trillionaire

SpaceX priced and debuted the **largest IPO in history**, 555.5 million shares at \$135, raising about \$75bn and valuing the company near \$1.77 trillion, eclipsing Saudi Aramco's 2019 record. Shares jumped roughly 19% on day one, vaulting SpaceX into the ranks of the seven most-valuable US companies (ahead of Tesla). Because Musk retains close to half the equity and 82% of the voting power, the surge makes him the world's first trillionaire. The FT framed the debut as both a display of Musk's dominance and a marquee event in an AI-fueled fundraising boom. (CNBC, NPR, FT, June 11–12)

### Oil cracks on peace hopes

Crude has fallen sharply as the Iran deal nears: Brent dropped below \$84 and WTI below \$81, with futures off about 4% as markets reopened and down roughly 12% from mid-last-week, a long way from the ~\$126 peak touched during the war. A reopened Strait of Hormuz would restore a major share of seaborne crude flows; watch for follow-through at the pump if the signing holds. (NPR, CNBC, June 12–14)

### Briefly

Markets are digesting a record fundraising haul as the AI race intensifies. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces his first policy meeting with headline inflation back in focus. China is teeing up a digital-payments system aimed at challenging the dollar, and foreign buyers are piling into UK companies at a two-decade high. (FT, June 14)
