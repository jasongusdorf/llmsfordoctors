**TL;DR.** The US-Iran war is ending: Trump personally signed a 14-point memorandum at Versailles to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, lift sanctions and the naval blockade, and defer the hardest nuclear questions to later talks. Markets sold off not on the Middle East but on a hawkish Fed: Kevin Warsh's first meeting stripped out the bias toward cuts and now pencils in a 2026 hike. ## International

Trump signs the Iran deal at Versailles, ending a four-month war

NPR / NBC News / CBC · signed Wed June 17, announced June 18

The headline event of the cycle, and a genuinely large one. President Trump signed a 14-point "memorandum of understanding" with Iran over dinner with Emmanuel Macron at the Palace of Versailles. (A US official told NBC the document was first signed digitally on Sunday by VP JD Vance and Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, with Trump as witness, then formalized Wednesday.) The deal calls for an immediate, permanent end to military operations in the war that began in February. Iran agrees to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, safe, free passage for commercial vessels for 60 days, followed by Oman-brokered talks on the strait's "future administration." Roughly 20% of the world's oil moved through Hormuz before the war. In exchange the US will terminate sanctions, unfreeze Iranian assets, and begin lifting its naval blockade. On the nuclear file, Iran "reaffirms it shall not procure or develop nuclear weapons," with the mechanism for handling its enriched-uranium stockpile left to be "mutually agreed" over a roughly two-month negotiating window. Cross-checked across NPR, NBC, CBC, CBS and PBS.

The early verdict: a lifeline for Tehran, hard parts deferred

NYT analysis · June 18

The NYT's read is skeptical: Iran gets a "major economic lifeline for minimal concessions," with the most difficult steps, above all verifiable limits on enrichment, pushed into later talks that may or may not happen. A companion analysis frames it as a reversal of Trump's own opening demand for Iran's "unconditional surrender." This is the central interpretive fight to watch: whether the MOU is a durable settlement or a pause that locks in Iranian gains. Treat the nuclear and Lebanon provisions as still-developing.

Versailles signing scrambles the G7; Trump rules out the \$300bn fund

NYT / FT · June 17–18

The summit swung from "a day of harmony" to Trump upending proceedings, per the NYT. Notably, Trump said the US will *not* invest in a proposed \$300bn reconstruction fund for Iran, and quipped that Washington had taken "a lot" of Iranian money it would now have to return. Macron-Trump optics were the sub-plot the FT flagged as a "bruised bromance."

## US

Fed holds, but kills the dovish tilt in Warsh's first meeting

CNBC / Federal Reserve / TheStreet · June 17

The FOMC unanimously held the funds rate at 3.5–3.75%, as expected, but the story is the guidance. In Kevin Warsh's debut as chair, the statement was sharply shortened and stripped of language signaling a bias toward cuts. The median dot for end-2026 rose to 3.8% from 3.4% in March, implying the committee now sees at least one *hike* this year to lean on inflation. Traders moved to price a possible October hike. This is a real hawkish pivot and the proximate cause of the day's market drop.

The Iran deal splits the GOP

NYT · June 18

Parts of Trump's own party are openly skeptical the agreement extracted adequate concessions from Tehran, opening new intra-Republican rifts. From the other side, Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called the war "one of the biggest American disasters." Expect the confirmation-and-oversight machinery (see also the contested Todd Blanche AG fight) to become the venue for this.

## Israel

Israel is cut out of the deal, and the Trump-Netanyahu rift goes public

NBC News / PBS / Times of Israel · June 18

This is the most consequential second-order story. Israel was not a signatory and, per NBC, was not even shown the MOU before it was finalized. Netanyahu declared the "struggle is not over" and vowed Israeli forces will hold "security zones" in southern Lebanon "for as long as necessary"; the IDF published a map of a zone reaching up to 10km into Lebanese territory. Trump, for his part, has been publicly deriding Netanyahu, reportedly calling him "crazy" and venting that the parallel Hezbollah fight was complicating his Iran deal, while telling reporters Bibi "gets a little excited sometimes." Israelis across the spectrum are angry the deal leaves Iran's core threat unaddressed, and some are lashing out at Netanyahu (PBS).

Iran says Israeli boots in Lebanon would nullify the agreement

Times of Israel / CBS · June 18

Iran's foreign minister states that any remaining Israeli forces in southern Lebanon, or any Israeli strikes, would constitute a violation of the US-Iran deal. A senior US official reportedly told Israel it won't be a signatory but "every action you take will have to be coordinated with the US." This is the fault line most likely to crack the ceasefire; treat as developing. Separately, FM Gideon Sa'ar cut contact with UN Secretary-General Guterres after Israel's security forces were added to a UN blacklist of entities credibly accused of conflict-related sexual violence.

## Economic & Markets

Hawkish Fed, not the Middle East, drives the tape

TheStreet / CNBC · June 17 close

Stocks fell and yields surged after the Fed's shift: S&P 500 -1.21% to 7,420.10; Nasdaq -1.34% to 26,021.66; the 2-year Treasury yield jumped more than 16bp to 4.216%. The FT separately notes investors piling into bullish dollar bets as the "US exceptionalism" trade returns, while corporate-bond/Treasury desks largely "shrugged at the ceasefire."

Oil eases, pump prices dip below \$4

NYT · June 18

The prospect of a reopened Hormuz pulled crude lower and, per the NYT, the average US gasoline price fell below \$4 for the first time in months, though the Times cautions airfares may not follow quickly. China, meanwhile, is teeing up a cross-border digital-payments system explicitly aimed at competing with dollar rails (FT).

## Worth reading (New Yorker & long-form)

Michael Luo, "How Did American Christianity End Up Like This?"

The standout this week. Luo argues that the separation of church and state turned American faith into a competitive marketplace, and that a particular anti-intellectual, fiercely political strand of Protestantism won the competition. A long, sourced cultural history with obvious resonance for the present moment, the kind of piece worth a quiet evening.

Robin Wright, "Iran Can Hold America Hostage in Either War or Peace"

The best companion to today's news: Wright reads the seizure of Hormuz as an adaptation of Iran's decades-old strategy of grabbing things of value to extract concessions, a useful frame for judging whether the Versailles deal actually changes Tehran's playbook.
