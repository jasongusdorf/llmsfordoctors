## TL;DR

The Iran war looks closer to an off-ramp than it has in months: Tehran and Muscat say a Hormuz shipping framework is in its final stage, though the strait would not automatically reopen and Iran is simultaneously drafting a law barring US and Israeli vessels. Oil went up, not down, on that contradiction. At home, the fiscal aftershock of the Supreme Court's tariff ruling is now measurable: roughly \$100 billion refunded to importers. And markets are bracing for an 8:30 AM jobs print into a Fed that is openly discussing a *hike*.

## International

**Hormuz: framework close, reopening not.** Iran's deputy foreign minister said Tehran and Oman are in the "final stage" of a framework covering a safe commercial shipping corridor, with agreed coordinates and a proposed joint coordination center. Trump said Thursday that "a lot of progress" had been made but no deal is signed, and that the US maintains its blockade of Iranian ports. The important nuance, easy to lose in headlines: Iran describes this as a corridor arrangement, not a reopening, and says Washington must meet further conditions first. Meanwhile an Iranian parliamentary committee is reviewing a bill barring US and Israeli vessels outright, with penalties up to 20% of cargo value. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency separately logged a tanker master hearing two explosions near Kumzar, Oman; crew and vessel safe. *Developing; similar "imminent deal" framing has recurred since early July.* ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/iran/trump-iran-war-deal-strait-hormuz-deal-oman-rcna590920), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/6/iran-war-live-tehran-oman-close-in-on-hormuz-deal-houthis-attack-tankers), Aug 5-6)

**Yemen's deadliest day since 2022.** Houthi ballistic missiles and drones struck government military camps in Marib and Hadramout on Thursday, killing at least 58 Saudi-backed troops and wounding dozens. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the strikes answered a Saudi-backed buildup. Spillover reached Saudi territory: 11 civilians wounded in Najran, including a four-year-old with second-degree burns. *Caveat: the death toll is single-sourced to AFP citing local medical and military sources, and climbed from 38 to 45 to 58 across the news cycle. It may move again.* ([Arab News/AFP](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2653706/saudi-arabia), Aug 6-7)

**Ukraine is running out of interceptors, and Russia knows it.** Roughly 29 missiles (24 of them ballistic) and 115 drones hit overnight into Wednesday, killing 17 and wounding 44, mostly in Kyiv region including Brovary and Bucha. Zelenskyy said the targets were civilian: warehouses, retail, a brewery, logistics hubs, a rail station; Russia's MoD claimed military-linked transport nodes. The load-bearing detail is defensive, not offensive: CNN reported no Russian ballistic missiles were intercepted, and Zelenskyy put air-defense missile supply at roughly one-third of 2025 levels. Ukrainian authorities also reported cluster munition use. Well corroborated across five-plus outlets. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/russian-attacks-kill-17-exploiting-ukraines-lack-of-missile-interceptors), WaPo, NPR, Aug 5)

**A drone with a bomb on it, at a German airport.** An employee at Leipzig/Halle spotted a quadcopter near a runway overnight Tuesday carrying roughly 800 grams of explosive and a faulty detonator, reportedly near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft. A bomb-disposal robot removed the detonator; the runway closed about two hours. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt called it "a new quality of danger." Saxony prosecutors, who handle politically motivated crime, are leading. *No suspects, no attribution, and authorities have explicitly declined to link it to a second incident in which an object struck a DHL cargo plane.* ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/germany-investigates-explosive-device-attached-to-drone-at-leipzig-airport), NPR, Aug 5)

**Uganda signs on to the Gaza force.** Uganda's parliament approved deploying troops to the International Stabilization Force proposed by Trump's "Board of Peace" in February. Defense Minister Kiryowa Kiwanuka cited Uganda's Somalia peacekeeping record. The 20,000-strong force is to be commanded by US Maj. Gen. Jasper Jeffers and deploys only after a phased Israeli withdrawal. Reported contributors include Morocco, Indonesia, Kazakhstan, Kosovo and Albania, with Egypt and Jordan on police training. *Troop numbers unspecified; the wider contributor list is of mixed reliability, and no deployment has begun.* ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/uganda-approves-sending-troops-to-gaza-for-international-force-proposed-by-trumps-board-of-peace), Aug 6)

## United States

**The tariff refund is real money.** A Customs and Border Protection filing disclosed Wednesday shows roughly \$100 billion refunded of the approximately \$166 billion collected under the IEEPA "liberation day" tariffs the Supreme Court invalidated 6-3 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* in February. Another ~\$29 billion in claims has been accepted for processing; CBP counts 252,496 refund declarations covering more than 25 million import entries as of July 31. The political sting, noted by critics across coverage: the money returns to corporate importers, not to the households who absorbed the higher shelf prices. Cross-checked across three outlets. ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/trump-administration-refunded-100-billion-tariffs-struck-supreme-court-rcna591099), [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/08/tariff-refunds-reach-100-billion-mark/), Aug 5-6)

**Haitian TPS ends.** Judge Ana Reyes, who had blocked the termination in February, lifted her stay after the Supreme Court's June ruling in *Noem v. Doe* held that federal courts cannot review a DHS Secretary's decision to end Temporary Protected Status. Between 330,000 and 350,000 Haitians lose work authorization and deportation protection, effective this week. Reyes did deny the government's bid to halt discovery, so a separate racial-discrimination claim survives. *Litigation ongoing.* ([WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/immigration/2026/08/05/tps-haitians-officially-ends-putting-thousands-out-work-limbo/), [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/08/05/tps-deporation-haitians/4981785953505/), Aug 5)

**Farm Bill dies in committee, 10-11.** The Agricultural Act of 2026 failed to advance out of Senate Agriculture on Thursday, collapsing over SNAP. The specific fight: a provision requiring states with high SNAP payment-error rates to shoulder more program cost, with Democrats seeking a longer runway than Republicans would give. Amendments had already been adopted in markup, including reinstating mandatory country-of-origin labeling for beef. Chairman John Boozman says he will re-run the vote in September. ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6014898-senate-agriculture-committee-farm-bill-snap/), [Agri-Pulse](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/25091-senate-ag-committee-fails-to-advance-farm-bill-amid-snap-feud), Aug 6)

**Water systems under attack in at least 12 states.** Attackers are compromising internet-exposed Rockwell Automation MicroLogix PLCs, changing IP addresses and setting passwords to lock operators out. Confirmed impacts: 30-plus Minnesota community systems in late July, several Michigan communities, at least one South Dakota city, and Georgia's Clayton County Water Authority. The FBI reports pressure drops and localized flooding; no contamination or safety incidents. *Actively developing. The state count rose from 7 to 12 within days, and Iranian attribution is suspected by intelligence officials but NOT officially confirmed by the FBI.* ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/04/water-cyberattacks-us-iran), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/water-sector-cyberattacks-reportedly-hit-at-least-12-states/), Aug 4-5)

**First mRNA flu vaccine licensed in the US.** FDA approved mFlusiva, notable mostly for the political context: HHS under Secretary Kennedy canceled 22 mRNA vaccine projects worth roughly \$500 million in August 2025 and halted federal mRNA investment. FDA advisers voted unanimously in June that benefits outweigh risks for adults 50-64 and again for 65-plus. The Phase 3 trial enrolled 40,805 adults across 11 countries and reported 26.6% greater relative efficacy than a standard-dose comparator. *Single-source as retrieved (STAT); worth confirming the label details before quoting the efficacy figure clinically.* ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/06/mflusiva-fda-mrna-flu-vaccine-rfk-jr/), Aug 6)

**Courts, briefly.** Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong held DHS in contempt for failing to produce documents on the Los Angeles ICE surge; the administration paid a \$3,500 fine Aug 4. Judge Philip Brimmer dismissed DOJ's suit seeking unredacted Colorado voter rolls for want of justification. And Rhode Island Chief Judge John McConnell spoke publicly Aug 5 about credible death threats. *Each item is single-outlet as retrieved.* Separately, the House Oversight Epstein-files probe is stalling amid bipartisan frustration, more than a year in. ## Israel

**A new Sunni defense bloc.** Pakistani PM Shehbaz Sharif (with army chief Asim Munir), Turkish President Erdogan and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman signed a trilateral pact in Mecca today stipulating that an armed attack on any one of the three is an attack on all. Officials called it purely defensive, open to other regional states, and "not directed against any specific actor," which is diplomatic language nobody in the region will read literally. Reuters frames it as a response to Iranian and allied attacks on Gulf states and blockaded energy shipments since February. The strongest-sourced story of the cycle, and arguably the most consequential for Israel's medium-term strategic map: it is a Sunni security architecture with a nuclear-armed member, formed without Israel and without the US at the table. ([Euronews/Reuters](https://www.euronews.com/2026/08/07/saudi-arabia-turkiye-and-pakistan-to-sign-joint-defence-pact-amid-regional-escalation), Aug 7)

**Israel weighs open defiance of the Gaza roadmap.** Shin Bet chief David Zini reportedly told security discussions that Hamas's signing of the disarmament agreement is "a strategic ambush" meant to run the clock to Israeli elections; Minister Ze'ev Elkin urged resuming operations. The Trump "Board of Peace" roadmap announced July 30 would disarm Hamas in stages over eight months and dismantle the tunnel network; Netanyahu has said Israel did not agree to its terms. Confusingly, a separate ToI item the same morning quoted an unnamed official seeing "steps in the right direction" as Israel curbed strikes. *Unnamed-source leaks, internally contradictory, developing.* ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-7-2026/), Aug 7)

**Two reservists killed in Lebanon; escalation held back.** A squad entered a booby-trapped house during a search in Majdal Zoun; the blast killed Maj. (res.) Harel Birenstock, 34, and M.-Sgt. (res.) Tamir Vaknin, 33, and seriously wounded four. The IDF called it a blatant ceasefire violation and struck weapons stores and command centers in southern Lebanon with evacuation warnings for Mansouri. The US and France reportedly urged restraint; the government instructed the IDF against large-scale retaliation. *Important caveat: Israeli investigators reportedly now assess the device may have been emplaced before the ceasefire and simply gone undiscovered, which would mean it was not a violation at all. Outlets also differ on the date.* ([JPost](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/defense-news/article-904717), [ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-strikes-southern-lebanon-town-alleging-ceasefire-violation-by-hezbollah/), Aug 5-6)

**Lebanon talks stall on a verification committee.** Israel is reportedly refusing further withdrawals from southern Lebanon over disagreement about who sits on the committee verifying Hezbollah's disarmament. The State Department says the sides are "a lot closer" on expanding the pilot zone process; Rome talks reportedly snagged after the Majdal Zoun deaths. Israel's defense establishment also reports a marked rise in attempted explosive-drone attacks, including fiber-optic-guided models that resist jamming. *Mostly single-outlet with unnamed officials.* ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-06-2026/), Aug 6)

**The campaign is fragmenting.** IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir was reported Aug 5 to have called the Haredi draft law "warped" and accused the government of disregarding the army's position; the Knesset earlier passed a law suspending arrests of ultra-Orthodox draft evaders 58-54, with Netanyahu absent. Likud primaries are Aug 17. *Zamir's remarks are attributed to unnamed sources; party-formation reports are Israeli-media-only and fluid.* ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-05-2026/), [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bjlrj2tegg), Aug 5-7)

**Administrative detention of minors.** Data cited by ToI shows Palestinian minors held without charge under administrative detention rising from 103 to 191. **An ultrasound patch that buys you REM.** A soft bioelectronic device, NEUSLeeP, combines transcranial ultrasound stimulation with electrode monitoring. Across 28 participants it cut time-to-REM by 43 minutes and extended REM by about 16 minutes, published in *Nature Communications* out of UT Austin. Investigators flag PTSD, depression and chronic insomnia as targets. Small n, healthy-volunteer design, no clinical endpoints yet, but a genuinely novel modality. ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260806050709.htm), Aug 7)

**Earth's core changed direction and nobody can say why.** ESA satellite magnetic data show a vast region of molten iron under the Pacific flipped from westward to strongly eastward flow around 2010. Publishing in the *Journal of Studies of Earth's Deep Interior*, researchers cannot explain the cause; newer data hint the eastward flow is already weakening, suggesting a natural deep-interior cycle. ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260806050713.htm), Aug 7)

**Francesco Guccini, 1940-2026.** The Italian singer-songwriter died at 86 at home in Pàvana, Tuscany. Seventeen studio albums from the 1960s onward made him the defining literary voice of the cantautore tradition; he also wrote autobiographical novels and crime fiction with Loriano Macchiavelli. **UEFA holds the line on FIFA.** All 55 European federations will maintain their World Cup boycott despite Infantino abandoning and apologizing for a plan to spin off FIFA's commercial operations to financial investors. ## Worth reading

**["The Future, Made in China"](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china), Evan Osnos, The New Yorker, Aug 10 issue.** Osnos's argument is that the technology race has already tipped and Americans have not registered it. The evidence he stacks up is hard to wave off: China produces roughly 70% of the world's drones, EVs, lithium-ion batteries and solar cells, deploys more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined, has watched BYD pass Tesla in output, fields frontier AI models at near-parity, and now registers more clinical trials than the United States. That last one should land for you specifically. His framing is that decades of Chinese state investment and industrial policy are paying off at the exact moment the US is retreating from science funding, alliances, and open immigration, and that whoever owns the industries of the future writes the rules of the next order. Reported through good scenes: a Beijing AI expo where an "emotional X-ray" graded his face at 76.67% positive, a domestic auto market with a hundred-plus automakers competing on in-car karaoke.

**["What Makes an Adaptation Good?"](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/the-front-row/what-makes-an-adaptation-good), Richard Brody, The New Yorker.** Brody uses Nolan's *The Odyssey* to argue about fidelity generally, and his diagnosis is precise rather than curmudgeonly. The problem is not that Nolan cut material, since every adaptation cuts. It is that one specific thing is gone: the gods. Apart from Athena, the pantheon is essentially absent, even though divine will governs every moment of the poem, and what remains is a naturalistic human drama with an Odysseus who is burdened rather than mercurial. Brody's larger claim, which travels well beyond this film, is that good adaptation means finding a cinematic equivalent for the source's organizing sensibility, not its plot. Strip out the metaphysics and you lose the thing that made the original feel alive.
