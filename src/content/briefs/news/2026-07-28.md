## International

### A magnitude 7.1 quake hits Kyushu, and a mall roof comes down

The Japan Meteorological Agency recorded a magnitude 7.1 earthquake at about 10 km depth this afternoon local time, epicentered near Uto City in Kumamoto Prefecture, with maximum shaking of shindo 7, the top of Japan's intensity scale, registered in Uki City and Hikawa. USGS put it slightly lower at 6.8. A one-meter tsunami advisory was issued and later lifted. At least 50 people were injured, roughly 40,000 to 48,000 households lost power, JR Kyushu suspended service including the shinkansen, Kumamoto Airport closed its runway, and the stone walls of Kumamoto Castle collapsed again, a decade after the 2016 quake did the same. Around 6 PM local an explosion at the Aeon Mall in Kashima Town brought down part of the second floor and roof, with numerous people believed trapped; the cause is under investigation. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said the extent of casualties was still being assessed. No irregularities were reported at nearby nuclear plants, and TSMC, Sony and Fujifilm evacuated regional facilities. *This is hours old and actively developing, expect the casualty figures to move materially. One caution: a "32 dead" number circulating in search results belongs to the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake, not this one.* ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/07/28/japan/strong-earthquake-kyushu/), Reuters, CNN, Nikkei Asia)

### The guns stay quiet a third day, and Hormuz is the whole negotiation

After roughly thirteen days of intensifying US strikes, Washington paused its bombing campaign late last week and Iran reciprocally held fire through Monday. The Times of Israel reports that mediators from Pakistan, Egypt and Qatar, with Oman involved, have drafted a proposal to resolve the dispute that killed the June 17 memorandum of understanding: Iran read that document as granting it a degree of control over the Strait of Hormuz, and Washington rejects that reading. Oman has floated a joint strait-management mechanism with voluntary transit fees; Iran wants vessels routed closer to its coast paying mandatory tolls, while the US backs southern routes near Oman. Trump is reportedly holding his decision until after this morning's Netanyahu meeting. The rhetorical gap remains total and worth keeping in view: Trump says Iran "asked us very nicely" to stop and that talks are going well; Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei says there are "no negotiations with the United States at present" and that Tehran "will never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace." Hormuz transits fell to 57 over Friday through Sunday, a drop of more than half week on week. Iran reports roughly 3,528 killed and 27,170 injured since February. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/hopes-of-return-to-diplomacy-as-us-iran-hold-fire-for-third-day), CNN, [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/ahead-of-netanyahu-trump-meeting-mediators-believe-us-iran-breakthrough-near/), NPR)

### The Gironde fire is 15 km from Bordeaux, and the fourth heat wave is coming

The fire west of Bordeaux has burned roughly 42,000 hectares, about four times the area of Paris, and destroyed some 240 homes since starting Wednesday. Evacuations run to more than 250,000 in France, concentrated in Gironde and Landes, plus 63,000 to 75,000 in Spain across Madrid, Toledo and Ávila; combined totals are reported between 300,000 and 330,000 depending on the day and the count, and French officials describe it as the country's largest peacetime evacuation since the Second World War. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said the fires "had not progressed during the night." Bordeaux Mayor Thomas Cazenave says the fire is "at the gates of the metropolitan area," a city of about 850,000. France has deployed roughly 1,500 soldiers and 1,700 security personnel plus an A400M water-bomber; Spain declared a state of emergency and France requested EU assistance. The fires have generated pyrocumulonimbus clouds. Europe's fourth heat wave of the summer, near 40°C, arrives later this week. *I could not confirm a consolidated official death toll for the Gironde fire specifically; season-wide deaths include two firefighters in France, one in Italy and one civilian in Valencia.* ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/macron-calls-crisis-meeting-of-french-cabinet-as-blaze-approaches-bordeaux), AP, France 24, NPR)

### The DRC Ebola outbreak is the third-largest ever, and the vaccine does not fit

The Democratic Republic of Congo has reported 3,200 confirmed cases and 1,405 deaths as of July 25–26, an increase of 125 cases and 51 deaths in a single day. Ituri province dominates with 2,848 cases and 1,184 deaths across 28 of 36 health zones, followed by North Kivu at 306 cases and 196 deaths; 773 patients are hospitalized in isolation and 571 have recovered. The clinically decisive detail is the species: this is *Bundibugyo* virus, and the licensed Ervebo vaccine and the approved monoclonals (Inmazeb, Ebanga) target *Zaire ebolavirus*. There is no licensed countermeasure for this outbreak, candidates remain in testing. Uganda's linked outbreak stands at 20 cases and 2 deaths, with the last case June 21 and the 42-day countdown begun July 16. Three imported cases have been managed outside Africa, including two US citizens medevaced to Germany in May and July. MSF calls it the third-largest and fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record; ECDC rates EU/EEA risk very low but flags significant surveillance gaps. ([WHO DON 613](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON613), [ECDC](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-and-uganda), CDC HAN 530, MSF)

### Haitian TPS lapses, and ICE is reportedly ready

Temporary Protected Status for more than 300,000 Haitians, plus Syrians, ended Monday after a Supreme Court ruling cleared the administration to terminate the designations and the lower-court orders delaying it expired. CBS News, citing federal documents and two ICE sources, reports the agency has drawn up plans to arrest Haitian TPS holders and move them quickly onto removal flights, with Springfield, Ohio, home to an estimated 10,000 to 15,000 Haitians, and Florida named as focal points, potentially beginning this week. ### The mail-voting order goes to the Supreme Court on the emergency docket

The Justice Department filed an emergency application Monday asking the Supreme Court to let Trump's March executive order on mail voting take effect while litigation continues. The order directs the federal government to build a "state citizenship list" of eligible voters and to send mail ballots only to people on it. Democratic officials in 23 states and the District sued, arguing the Constitution assigns election rules to states and Congress, not the president. Judge Indira Talwani blocked it in June and the First Circuit upheld that block over the weekend. The Court has ordered a response by August 3. The practical objection is a data problem as much as a constitutional one: the DHS citizenship-matching data underlying the list produces false positives, which in this application means wrongly purging eligible voters months before November. ([PBS NewsHour/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/trump-administration-urges-supreme-court-to-allow-order-targeting-mail-in-voting-before-midterms), CNN, The Hill, CNBC)

### Health policy: the CPSC wants unredacted emergency department records

This is the item most likely to touch your working life. The Consumer Product Safety Commission, in the course of "modernizing" its NEISS injury surveillance system, is requiring at least 100 hospitals to send *unredacted* emergency department records, patient names, addresses, diagnoses and other identifiers, to a contractor, Konza Health, by year's end. Both the agency and the contractor have described participation as mandatory. The demand covers all ED visits, not merely consumer-product injuries: fractures, vaccine reactions, suicide attempts, everything. Hospital counsel and privacy experts question the agency's statutory authority, its capacity to safeguard PHI, and whether it skipped required notice-and-comment before expanding the system this way. Mass General Brigham, Henry Ford Health and Harborview Medical Center have declined or formally challenged participation. ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/cpsc-consumer-product-safety-commission-trump-er-injury-data-grab-neiss-konza/), CNN, US News)

## Israel

### Netanyahu at the White House this morning, for the first time since February

The meeting is scheduled for roughly 11 AM Eastern and has not happened as of this writing, so everything here is preview. It is the first Trump–Netanyahu meeting since the two launched the Iran campaign in late February. A White House official said the agenda covers the Iran war, the Israel–Lebanon peace framework and expanding the Abraham Accords, with Vice President Vance attending. The relationship is visibly strained: it hit a low in April during Iran ceasefire talks, Trump has called Netanyahu "a very difficult guy," and he publicly slapped down Netanyahu's objection to F-35 sales to Turkey with "nobody tells me what we should be selling." ### Tariffs, round three; and the yen at a 40-year low

### "How Netanyahu's Top General Became His Strongest Challenger", David Remnick, The New Yorker

Remnick embeds with Gadi Eisenkot, the retired IDF chief of staff who now looks like the likeliest person to unseat Netanyahu on October 27, and who, per a poll out today, is running neck and neck with him as preferred prime minister. The argument is that Eisenkot's appeal has almost nothing to do with ideology or policy and everything to do with biography and temperament: a Moroccan-immigrant, Tiberias-born Mizrahi outsider from the Golani Brigade whose son was killed in Gaza, running a deliberately dull "politics of service" under a new party called Yashar, meaning straight or upright. Remnick is clear-eyed about the limits. Eisenkot pledges to restore a "Jewish, democratic, progressive, and liberal state," but Gaza's devastation and West Bank settler violence go largely undiscussed on the campaign trail, and coalition arithmetic could leave him dominant in opposition and still unable to form a government. Worth it for the Ashkenazi/Mizrahi class politics Remnick draws out, and for the explicit parallel he makes between Netanyahu's survival strategy and MAGA-style demagoguery. ### "What Would It Mean to See a New Color?", Rivka Galchen, The New Yorker

The story of "olo," a color no camera can capture, and of the collaboration that produced it. Berkeley computer scientist Ren Ng spent three years cold-emailing vision scientist Austin Roorda, whose lab had built adaptive-optics technology capable of mapping and then laser-targeting individual cone cells in a living, constantly moving retina, technology originally developed for retinal disease work. Ng's question, which he called Oz Vision after the greenest grass in Oz, was what happens if you stimulate only the M cones, a pattern that never occurs in nature. The answer is a percept outside the normal human gamut. Galchen threads in Hume on the missing shade of blue and the question of whether you can imagine a color you have never seen, and she is careful about the L/M/S cone physiology rather than hand-waving it. The standout of the issue for anyone with a clinical eye. Published July 27, in the August 3 issue. ([Read it](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/03/what-would-it-mean-to-see-a-new-color))
