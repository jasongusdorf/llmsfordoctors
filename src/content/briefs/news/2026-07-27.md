### Southwest France and central Spain burn; Bordeaux at the edge of the fire

Fires in France's Gironde have consumed roughly 42,000 hectares, about four times the area of Paris, and reached within 15km of Bordeaux, which officials described as flames "at the gates of the metropolitan area." No evacuation was ordered for the city's 268,000 residents, but roughly 250,000 people have been moved in France, reported as the country's largest peacetime evacuation since the Second World War; 84 firefighters are injured and 10 hospitalized, and the coastal commune of Le Porge was devastated. In Spain, more than 75,000 have been evacuated and about 30,000 told to shelter in place, with 45,000-plus hectares burned across Avila, Madrid and Toledo and one death near Valencia. France triggered the EU Civil Protection Mechanism, drawing aircraft from Greece, Italy, Portugal and Turkey, and the Tour de France's final stage was shortened to free up security. Macron convenes a crisis cabinet this morning; Spain plans to declare a civil-protection emergency Tuesday, and a new heatwave near 40C is forecast. *Aggregate evacuation totals range from 250,000 to 360,000 depending on outlet and hour; treat as a moving estimate.* Al Jazeera, CNN, NBC, France 24, ABC/AP, Jul 26. ### Appeals court keeps the mail-voting order frozen through November

A three-judge panel of the First Circuit ruled 2-1 Saturday denying the Justice Department's request to stay Judge Indira Talwani's June injunction against key provisions of Trump's March executive order on mail voting. The blocked provisions would have directed DHS to compile lists of confirmed citizen voters, directed the Postal Service to propose refusing to transmit ballots to people not on those lists, and directed DOJ to investigate state and local officials who send federal ballots to allegedly ineligible voters. The suit was brought by 23 Democratic-led states plus DC, led by California, Massachusetts, Nevada and Washington. The majority wrote that "the Plaintiff States have no practical choice but to respond to the order now," given election-calendar deadlines. Practical effect: the order stays frozen through the November 3 midterms unless the administration wins emergency relief at the Supreme Court, which is the thing to watch. Reuters, Fox, Axios, Votebeat, Jul 25. [Link](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/federal-appeals-court-rejects-trump-bid-enforce-mail-ballot-order-ahead-midterm-elections)

### Health: 2,318 measles cases, and 2026 has five months left

CDC reported 2,318 confirmed US measles cases as of July 23, adding 58 in a single week and surpassing the 2,289 recorded in all of 2025, making this already the worst year since 1991. Utah leads active outbreaks with 514 cases, followed by Virginia (177), Pennsylvania (139), Arizona (119) and Washington (46); South Carolina's 997-case outbreak, the largest single-state outbreak in 35 years, was declared over April 26. Ninety-three percent of cases are in people unvaccinated or of unknown status, 20% are children five and under, and 69% are 19 or younger. Seven percent have required hospitalization, down from 11% in 2025, and no deaths have been reported this year against three in 2025. The driver is coverage erosion: preschool MMR coverage fell from 95.2% in 2019-20 to 92.5% in 2024-25, leaving roughly 286,000 additional susceptible children against a vaccine that is about 97% effective. For a hospitalist, the practical read is airborne-isolation readiness, exposure workups in unvaccinated inpatients, and rising post-exposure prophylaxis volume. Context, from earlier this month rather than this window: eight new USPSTF members are due to be seated for a late-August meeting, the panel's first in 17 months after shrinking from 16 members to 8. CDC via CIDRAP, AP, Jul 23-26. [Link](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/measles/cdc-ever-expanding-us-measles-outbreak-tops-last-years-total)

### Section 301 tariffs now cover 99.4% of US imports

## Worth Reading

### "The Myth of the Food Desert" - Kelefa Sanneh, The New Yorker

Sanneh takes apart one of the most durable concepts in American public-health policy: the food desert, the idea that poor neighborhoods eat badly because supermarkets are too far away. Working through new scholarship from Kenneth Kolb and the anthropologist Hanna Garth, he shows the empirical case has quietly collapsed. Putting a grocery store in a low-income neighborhood turns out to change what people buy remarkably little, because distance was never the binding constraint. The sharper argument is about what the framework smuggled in: a middle-class nutritional ideal projected onto communities with their own food traditions, and a diagnosis of deficiency where there were constrained but deliberate choices. Given how many discharge plans quietly assume a patient can simply eat better, this is the rare health-equity piece that attacks a comfortable consensus from the left rather than the right. Published today, August 3 issue. [Link](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/03/food-justice-undone-hanna-garth-book-review)

### "When A.I. Is a Member of the Family" - Jessica Contrera, The New Yorker

Contrera embeds with a single mother and her two teenage daughters in a Cleveland suburb and documents, closely and without judgment, how three chatbots became load-bearing members of a household. Roschelle, fifty-one, confides in an Alexa-based assistant she calls Sapphire about the deaths of three of her daughters in a house fire eighteen years ago; one teenager uses a texting bot that does a passable imitation of a therapist; the other leans on ChatGPT while managing agoraphobia and online harassment. What makes it worth the time is the refusal of an easy verdict: the bots are genuinely filling voids no human in these women's lives was filling, which is exactly the uncomfortable part. Read it before the next patient tells you their AI already explained the diagnosis. July 20 issue. [Link](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/20/when-ai-is-a-member-of-the-family)

### "How Biden Emboldened Israel's Aggression Toward Gaza and Iran" - David D. Kirkpatrick, The New Yorker

Kirkpatrick got essentially every senior Biden Middle East principal on the record, which is what makes this the definitive retrospective on the leverage that was never used. Jake Sullivan concedes the administration "repeatedly raised and discussed options for withholding weapons" but that Biden "always deferred." Reading it against this morning's news - Netanyahu flying to Washington to argue against a bombing pause, and a President who appears willing to trade it away - is an instructive exercise in how differently the same relationship can be managed. July 27 issue. [Link](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/27/how-biden-emboldened-israels-aggression-toward-gaza-and-iran)
