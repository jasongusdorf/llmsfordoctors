## International

### US–Iran war: 13th night of strikes, no ceasefire in sight

The American military campaign against Iran entered roughly its second week, with the US carrying out a 13th consecutive night of strikes and Iranian state media reporting at least four killed across several locations. President Trump made clear he is in no hurry to talk: "They need more of the same," he said, adding "We're doing very well against the Islamic Republic of Iran" and repeating that Tehran will not be allowed a nuclear weapon. June negotiations built on a US–Iran memorandum of understanding have collapsed into renewed bombing, with both sides signaling that fresh talks are unlikely near-term. The Strait of Hormuz remains the central flashpoint. Developing Sources: CNN live, ABC News, Bloomberg (Jul 22–24).

### Houthi escalation in the Red Sea

A Houthi attack in the Red Sea marked a new escalation on Wednesday, and Trump warned the US would hold Iran responsible for any future Houthi strikes on shipping, threatening "major military punishment." The combination of direct US–Iran exchanges and a reopened Red Sea front pushed crude past \$100 a barrel for the first time since May. Source: CNN live (Jul 23).

### Andy Burnham becomes UK prime minister

Andy Burnham, the former mayor of Greater Manchester, took office Monday as Britain's seventh prime minister in a decade, replacing the unpopular Keir Starmer, who resigned after just two years. Both are Labour. In his first Downing Street address Burnham cast his arrival as a "circuit-breaker," pledging a new political and economic model and a 10-year plan and insisting Britain must "regain our stability." A significant, still-settling transition worth watching for its posture toward the US and the Iran crisis. Sources: Washington Post, NPR, ABC News (Jul 19–20).

### France: 20,000+ evacuated as wildfire tears through the southwest

More than 20,000 people, most of them tourists, were evacuated as a fast-moving wildfire ravaged pine forest at Le Porge, near Bordeaux, burning roughly 2,400 hectares of coastline. Around 700 firefighters were deployed, some sent from the Paris region. France has endured three heatwaves since May; parched vegetation let the flames move with extreme speed. Sources: Al Jazeera, France 24, France info (Jul 23).

## US

### A new tariff wall goes up today

Beginning 12:01 a.m. today, dozens of US trading partners, from Europe to China to India, covering about 99.4% of imports, face new tariffs of 10% to 12.5%. The move is the administration's workaround after the Supreme Court's 6–3 ruling in *Learning Resources v. Trump* struck down Trump's earlier IEEPA-based tariffs. The new duties lean on Section 301 (unfair-trade / forced-labor grounds) to build a more durable, statute-based footing, though the Section 122 bridge and pending appeals leave meaningful legal uncertainty. Sources: Washington Post, PBS NewsHour, CFR (Jul 23).

### Severe weather across the South and Appalachia

Tropical Storm Bertha made landfall in Louisiana with heavy rain and flooding risk, while National Guard units deployed across West Virginia after rapid flash flooding inundated homes and infrastructure. Developing Source: wire round-ups (Jul 23).

## Israel

### Deadly West Bank clash near Havat Gilad

A group of dozens of Israelis, including children, hiked into Areas A and B near the village of Tell, zones normally off-limits to Israelis, and a violent confrontation erupted near a mosque. Palestinians reportedly seized a weapon from the settlement emergency-squad chief and opened fire. Benyahu Mellet, 32, of Gilad Farm's response squad, was killed; four Palestinians also died and several were wounded, three critically. Netanyahu convened security officials (Katz, IDF chief Zamir, Shin Bet's Zini) and vowed to "act with full force." Accounts of who fired first differ by outlet. Developing / disputed Sources: Times of Israel, Haaretz, Arab News (Jul 24).

### Gaza strikes continue

Israeli airstrikes destroyed homes in the Bureij and Jabalia camps and in the Zeitoun and Tal al-Hawa neighborhoods of Gaza City, killing at least two, per medical sources, part of a continued escalation despite the US-brokered ceasefire nominally in place since last year. Source: Al Jazeera (Jul 24).

## Economic & Markets

### Stocks slide on AI-spending shock and oil

US equities fell Thursday: the Dow lost 507 points (-0.97%) to 51,712, the S&P 500 dropped 1.21% to 7,408, and the Nasdaq sank 2.15% to 25,138. The tech rout was led by Alphabet (-7%) and Tesla (-14%). Alphabet beat on earnings but raised 2026 capex guidance to as much as \$205B (up from \$180–190B) and is reportedly eyeing \$80–85B in equity raises to fund AI buildout; Tesla's capex now tops \$25B. Both posted negative Q2 free cash flow, reigniting doubts about the sustainability of the AI infrastructure spend. Sources: CNBC, TheStreet, Yahoo Finance (Jul 23).

### Oil, inflation and the Fed

Crude's climb past \$100 revives inflation risk just as gasoline prices had been easing. The IMF warned renewed Middle East conflict "could extend commodity price volatility, further threaten supply chains, raise prices, and weigh on financial conditions." Roughly a fifth of global petroleum liquids moves through Hormuz, so any closure threat carries outsized macro weight, and complicates the Fed's rate path. Sources: NPR, Bloomberg, Stimson Center (Jul; updated Jul 23).
