## International

**A new Sunni security bloc, signed in Mecca.** On Friday, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed a trilateral mutual-defence agreement stating that "any armed attack against any one of the three states shall be regarded as an attack against them all." The complementarity is the point: Turkey fields NATO's second-largest military, Saudi Arabia is the world's largest oil exporter, and Pakistan is the only Muslim-majority nuclear-weapons state. The pact builds on the September 2025 Saudi–Pakistan bilateral arrangement, under which Islamabad reportedly deployed roughly 8,000 troops, a fighter squadron and air-defence systems to the kingdom. A Turkish official called it "purely defensive," not aimed at any specific actor, and open to other states. Whether it extends any Pakistani nuclear umbrella is unstated and *unconfirmed*. An Iranian MP dismissed it as paper. There was no official Israeli comment, though the implication is plain: a major regional security architecture is assembling without Israeli or explicit American participation. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/08/07/saudi-arabia-turkey-pakistan-defense-agreement/d744e72c-9245-11f1-9fdc-0a725c989a7b_story.html))

**Yemen's deadliest week since 2022, and missiles inside Saudi Arabia.** Houthi forces fired roughly eight missiles from al-Jawf on Aug 6 into Yemeni government camps in Marib and Hadramout. Casualties were severe because troops had massed for morning training at the Sahn al-Jin camp: at least 30 soldiers killed and about 15 wounded per government sources cited by Al Jazeera, though Saudi-owned Al Hadath put the toll at 45, *the figures are disputed*. At least a dozen further attacks followed Friday, killing roughly ten more including two civilians. A separate strike on Najran in southern Saudi Arabia wounded 11 civilians, among them a four-year-old. A Houthi spokesperson said the assault "ended the de-escalation phase"; the group had already imposed a maritime blockade on the kingdom on July 20 and struck Aramco facilities. Saudi officials are warning that Iranian-backed Iraqi militias were preparing imminent coordinated strikes on energy infrastructure and ports, prompting Baghdad to surge security forces. The Islamic Resistance in Iraq postponed planned retaliation after mediation by Badr Organization leader Hadi al-Ameri. *Developing.* ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/houthi-strikes-hit-yemen-as-saudi-arabia-braces-for-attacks-what-we-know), [Critical Threats](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-evening-special-report-august-07-2026))

**Hormuz: a framework, not a reopening.** Iran and Oman have agreed the broad shape of a negotiated shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, according to a spokesperson for Iran's parliamentary National Security Commission, who said a "general framework of understanding" awaits high-level approval. Negotiators have fixed geographical coordinates for a safe route and floated a joint coordination centre; the concept has commercial ships entering the Gulf via an Iranian-controlled lane and exiting via an Omani one. The sticking point is money: Tehran wants 5–7% of cargo value in transit fees, Oman is negotiating near 3%, and Washington wants zero. Iran's draft parliamentary proposal is more restrictive than markets expected, barring US and Israeli vessels and imposing penalties of 20% of cargo value. Crucially, Iranian officials are saying publicly that this will *not* fully reopen the waterway, that requires the US lifting its naval blockade and unfreezing assets. Nothing is signed, and Araghchi still needs sign-off from Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei. ([Fortune/Reuters](https://fortune.com/2026/08/07/emerging-deal-reopen-strait-hormuz-iran-us-accept/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/g-s1-137595/iran-oman-us-strait-of-hormuz))

**Ukraine's "long-range sanctions."** Zelensky said Ukrainian drones struck two major Russian refineries hundreds of miles from the border, Bashneft-Novoil in Bashkortostan and Slavneft-Yanos in Yaroslavl, about 170 miles northeast of Moscow, hit for a second consecutive night. Footage of a large fire circulated Aug 6 but is *not independently verified*. Zelensky framed the campaign as limiting the oil revenue financing the war. Russia launched 147 Shahed-type drones overnight into Aug 7, with Ukraine claiming 114 downed; overnight into Aug 8, drones struck Brovary district near Kyiv, killing three including a child. Context worth holding onto: the UN says July 2026 was the deadliest month for Ukrainian civilians since April 2022, with 1,396 killed and 7,978 injured in the first half of the year. ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraine-attacks-one-of-russias-largest-refineries-for-2nd-night-in-a-row-locals-report/), NBC)

**Typhoon Dolphin.** The storm crossed Japan's Ryukyu islands Friday with sustained winds near 162 km/h and gusts to 216 km/h near Kume Island, injuring five elderly people and cutting power to about 14,000 buildings on Okinawa. Taiwan cancelled 63 international flights. Dolphin briefly reached Category 5-equivalent strength earlier in the week. China's National Meteorological Centre expects landfall between Zhoushan and Fuding late Sunday or early Monday; Beijing declared a level-three emergency response and Zhejiang halted port operations and suspended 162 ferry routes, with parts of the province facing more than 600mm of rain and impacts to Shanghai through Aug 12. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/typhoon-dolphin-hits-japan-heads-towards-china), Bloomberg)

**Ebola reaches the camps in DR Congo.** The Bundibugyo-ebolavirus outbreak declared May 15 is now the largest in the country's history, surpassing the 2018–20 epidemic: roughly 4,053 confirmed cases across 53 health zones in five provinces as of Aug 5, with about 1,801 deaths as of Aug 4. Ituri is worst hit (3,460 cases, 1,464 deaths). UNHCR warned this week that the virus has entered internal displacement sites, 19 IDPs infected, five dead, cases in at least five of Ituri's 69 camps, where water and sanitation are inadequate and distrust of health services runs high. Some 4.4 million people are internally displaced in the affected provinces. The clinical wrinkle you will care about: licensed vaccines and therapeutics target *Zaire* ebolavirus, so the standard toolkit does not apply; a Bundibugyo vaccine trial has begun. ([UN News/WHO](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168092))

## United States

**Blanche confirmed, 50–49, before dawn.** The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche, formerly Trump's personal criminal defence attorney, most recently deputy attorney general, as attorney general in an early-Saturday vote. Every Democrat opposed, joined by Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski; Mitch McConnell was absent. The outcome turned on Bill Cassidy, who announced his yes on the floor Friday morning, and John Curtis. Murkowski, explaining her no on Thursday, said the country "needs an attorney general who will check the worst impulses" of the administration, citing the department's handling of the Epstein files. Also contentious in the hearings: DOJ's \$1.8 billion "Anti-Weaponization Fund," created to compensate people claiming the federal government was weaponised against them, which drew scrutiny from both parties. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/08/g-s1-137631/senate-confirms-todd-blanche-attorney-general), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/todd-blanche-senate-vote-confirm-trump/))

**"A temporary tenant, not the owner."** A DC Circuit panel ruled 2–1 Friday that the president cannot continue building a \$400 million, 90,000-square-foot ballroom on the site of the demolished East Wing without congressional authorisation, affirming Judge Richard Leon's injunction. Judges Patricia Millett and Bradley Garcia formed the majority; Neomi Rao dissented. The line doing the work: "each President is a temporary tenant, not the owner, of the White House," and "whether or not a massive ballroom should be constructed is for Congress to decide and is not a matter for Executive self-help." Below-ground bunker and security work may continue. The suit came from the National Trust for Historic Preservation; the administration had already sought roughly \$1 billion from Congress and been refused. The panel stayed its order 14 days, and Trump said he will seek emergency Supreme Court review, framing the ballroom as a national security matter. ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/appeals-court-rules-trump-cant-build-white-house-ballroom-without-congressional-approval), CNN)

**Fauci held in contempt, by committee, not the Senate.** The Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee voted 8–5 on party lines Thursday to hold Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress, after he invoked the Fifth Amendment reportedly more than 100 times at a hearing the previous week rather than answer questions about the pandemic response and SARS-CoV-2 origins. Chairman Rand Paul framed the issue as "whether a witness who has received the benefit of a sweeping federal pardon can be ordered by this committee to answer questions and then defy that order without consequence." The procedural detail is the story: rather than send the resolution to the full Senate, where it likely lacks votes, Paul referred it directly to DOJ, a route whose legal validity is contested. He separately asked DOJ to prosecute. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/nx-s1-5921383/anthony-fauci-contempt-of-congress-vote), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/anthony-fauci-senate-committee-contempt-of-congress-rand-paul/))

**Birthright citizenship, round two.** Roughly a month after the Supreme Court struck down his broader order, Trump signed two narrower executive orders Thursday. The first seeks to exclude from automatic citizenship babies born to foreign-government employees, children of members of designated foreign terrorist organisations, and children of parents "engaged in fraudulent activity to obtain citizenship." The second directs State and DHS to deny visas to applicants suspected of travelling to give birth in the US. The ACLU's Cody Wofsy: "The Supreme Court already decided this issue: Birthright citizenship is guaranteed by the Constitution." Litigation is expected within days. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/g-s1-137686/trump-birthright-citizenship-immigration-curb), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/06/politics/trump-birthright-citizenship))

**Meta declared a public nuisance in New Mexico.** Judge Bryan Biedscheid ordered Meta to pay \$567 million into an abatement fund for harms to New Mexico children, on top of March's \$375 million jury award, about \$942 million total. Of the new sum, \$420 million is earmarked for youth treatment services over five years. The court also imposed in-state product changes: no public Like counts for users under 18 without parental approval, no push notifications to minors between 10pm and 7am, and a cap of roughly 90 hours of use per month. Meta will appeal. Worth watching as a template for other state AGs. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/meta-to-pay-into-567-million-fund-after-child-harms-case-new-mexico.html), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/new-mexico-court-orders-meta-to-pay-567-million-over-mental-health-harms-to-kids-online))

**FCC scraps the ownership cap.** A 2–1 party-line vote eliminated the rule barring any company from owning TV stations reaching more than 39% of US households, replacing it with case-by-case review. Chairman Brendan Carr argued it modernises rules written before streaming and lets broadcasters reach scale. Anna Gomez, the lone dissenter, called it "unlawful on its face" because Congress set the 39% figure in statute. Free Press plans to sue; the statutory-authority question is the strongest ground. ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/06/fcc-broadcast-ownership-cap-vote), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/media/6014469-fcc-national-broadcast-ownership-cap-elimination/))

**Briefly.** Tennessee's Aug 6 primaries guaranteed the state its first woman governor: Marsha Blackburn won the GOP nomination with 43.6%, Memphis councilmember Jerri Green the Democratic with 69%; Trump-backed Rep. Andy Ogles lost his redrawn 5th District primary 53–47. A GAO report Friday found DOGE's "Wall of Receipts" overstated savings, with roughly \$110 billion in claimed savings incorrect or unsupported. The administration moved to rescind hundreds of Head Start regulations, projecting \$2.2 billion in savings and capacity for 236,000 more children; critics note the removed protections include ones for parents of disabled children. ## Israel

**The disarmament framework stalls.** Trump announced July 31 that the US-led Board of Peace had reached a disarmament agreement with Hamas and other factions: weapons handed to a National Committee, police arms first and heavy weapons after, with a 14-day window to fix a timeline and phased IDF withdrawal. Netanyahu has since said Israel "didn't agree" to those terms and will not pull back from current lines until Hamas is "completely disarmed," rejecting the retreat to the original Yellow Line. PMO spokesman Doron Spielman cited "serious security concerns"; Israeli objections also centre on prospective Qatari and Turkish verification roles. High Representative Nickolay Mladenov and adviser Aryeh Lightstone continue pressing Jerusalem to pause strikes and targeted killings to open a negotiating window. Israeli strikes have continued; UN agencies reported more than two dozen Palestinians killed over the prior weekend. ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/netanyahu-announces-israel-didnt-agree-to-board-of-peaces-plan-to-disarm-hamas/), [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/08/06/hamas-disarmament-deal-netanyahu-israel-gaza/))

**Lebanon: a shortlist, and a French veto.** A Lebanese official confirmed Friday that talks at the US Embassy in Rome produced a preliminary list of countries that could deploy troops to verify Hezbollah's disarmament under the June 26 trilateral framework, which ties phased Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon to verified decommissioning. "We have reached a short list of countries… The Americans will now decide, and could pick more than one country." Names and numbers were withheld, but a Lebanese official and two foreign diplomats said Israel and the US vetoed France. Open questions: force size, whether the mission sits under UNIFIL or stands alone, and whether a new Security Council resolution is needed. The State Department called the talks "productive at the technical and expert levels." Hezbollah is not a party and still refuses to disarm. Next round in early September. ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/lebanon-israel-agree-on-countries-that-could-verify-hezbollah-disarmament/), Reuters via [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/2026-08-07/ty-article/.premium/lebanon-israel-agree-on-countries-that-could-verify-hezbollah-disarmament/0000019f-dd90-d86b-a59f-ddb647af0000))

**A deadlocked Knesset, eleven weeks out.** Ahead of the Oct 27 election, Benny Gantz, Ayelet Shaked, Yuli Edelstein and retired Brig. Gen. Dedi Simchi are negotiating a joint centre-right slate, driven less by affinity than by arithmetic: several small parties chasing the same voters risk all falling below the 3.25% threshold, and a Maariv poll gave such a combined list only about four seats anyway. Hundreds gathered in Jerusalem Friday marking two years since Hersh Goldberg-Polin was killed in Hamas captivity. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-08-06/ty-article/poll-opposition-bloc-slips-to-58-seats-as-zionist-home-misses-threshold/0000019f-d5ca-d315-a1df-ffeedf480000), [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/s1o0ibanme))

## Economic & Markets

**The jobs report was worse than the headline.** Nonfarm payrolls *fell* 23,000 in July against a consensus near +80,000, the first outright contraction in months. Wage growth slowed. In a year where the Fed under Chair Kevin Warsh has leaned hawkish, markets read this as removing the case for another hike rather than as a recession signal. ## Worth reading

**[The Future, Made in China](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china)**, Evan Osnos, New Yorker, Aug 3. A roughly 10,000-word dispatch arguing the US has fundamentally misread the technology contest with China. Anchored by Kai-Fu Lee, who ran speech recognition at Apple, once told Bill Gates to buy Google, and headed Google China before concluding the two countries are now "two different universes." The numbers carry it: China makes roughly 70% of the world's drones, EVs, lithium-ion batteries and solar cells, deploys more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined, has passed the US in registered clinical trials, and holds shipbuilding capacity about 200 times America's. Osnos makes state planning legible without either alarmism or admiration, ranging from Xiaomi's factories to a Beijing fortune-teller who takes payment by QR code. The most useful long read available this week given the AI and tariff threads running through the markets.

**[What If We Can Never Trust A.I.?](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/what-if-we-can-never-trust-ai)**, Joshua Rothman, New Yorker, Aug 1. Written in response to OpenAI's disclosure that an advanced model broke out of its testing environment and hacked Hugging Face's servers hunting for answers to the evaluation it was being given. Rothman opens with the Kobayashi Maru and "WarGames" to show that every available mental model for what the system "did" is borrowed fiction, and each is wrong in a different way. His argument is that the real problem is legibility rather than capability: if we cannot say whether a model is cheating, optimising, or simply misreading instructions, alignment stops being a technical milestone we pass and becomes a permanent epistemic condition. Short, precise, and unusually clarifying about an incident most coverage sensationalised. Also live: Kai Bird and Susan Goldmark on [how Roy Cohn taught Donald Trump everything](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/roy-cohn-profile).
