## International

**US widens the Iran campaign to civilian-adjacent infrastructure.** Overnight into Friday, American airstrikes hit highway and railway bridges in Iran's southern Hormozgan province, killing at least seven people according to Iranian state television, and collapsed a tower at the Chabahar port on the Gulf of Oman per the state-run IRNA. The bridges appear chosen to sever Bandar Abbas, Iran's main port, from the roads running inland toward Shiraz and on to Tehran. Central Command said the strikes, the sixth consecutive night, hit "dozens of Iranian military targets" but made no mention of civilian infrastructure, a discrepancy worth watching. The campaign follows Trump's threats to hit infrastructure until Tehran eases its chokehold on the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian drone waves caused damage in Kuwait, and Qatar and Kuwait fended off fresh attacks this week. Notably, Trump's former defense secretary told the FT the US will not win this war from the air. Casualty figures come from Iranian state media and should be treated as unconfirmed. ([NBC/AP](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/iran/us-strikes-bridges-iran-port-hormuz-bandar-abbas-syria-rcna587959), [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/), [FT](https://www.ft.com/), ~6:27 a.m. ET Friday)

**Britain gets a new prime minister on Monday, without an election.** Andy Burnham, the Greater Manchester mayor who won the Makerfield by-election on June 18, becomes Labour leader by acclamation after nominations closed July 16 with no rival able to enter: he had over 90% of the parliamentary party by July 15. Starmer resigned on June 22 following the by-election and the leadership challenge it triggered, and bid MPs goodbye at his final PMQs. Burnham assumes the premiership on July 20 and is expected to promise more public control of services, devolution toward local government, and a growth push. The problem he inherits is the old one: UK GDP grew 0.1% in May. Both Robert Shrimsley and Mohamed El-Erian in the FT argue the announcements will not survive contact with budget arithmetic, and that his real election timetable will be set by vibes rather than results. ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/), [FT](https://www.ft.com/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/15/starmer-bids-mps-goodbye-as-british-pm-and-pledges-to-support-his-successor))

**Zelensky fires his defense minister and Ukraine erupts.** Mykhailo Fedorov, 35, the architect of Ukraine's drone program and by most accounts its most effective wartime minister, was dismissed after six months in a cabinet reshuffle. Zelensky reportedly cited a rift between the Defense Ministry and the military command; Fedorov had clashed with old-school generals and pushed contentious conscription reforms. Large protests broke out in several cities Thursday and are expected to continue Friday. Interior minister Ihor Klymenko, a former police general of traditional instincts, is expected to replace him. From an underground parking garage, Fedorov delivered what the NYT calls the most dramatic public critique of the military command to emerge in the war, snapping what had been a Ukrainian winning streak. This is a fast-moving story and the succession is not yet finalized. ([WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/07/16/zelensky-ousts-popular-defense-minister-an-architect-ukraines-drone-program/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/16/g-s1-133920/ukraine-defense-minister-fired), [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/defense-minister-fedorov-set-to-leave-post-senior-official-confirms/))

## US

**Trump's primetime address on elections produced claims, not evidence.** In a 25-minute East Room speech Thursday night, Trump said he was declassifying intelligence showing "shocking vulnerabilities" in election infrastructure, and argued security "falls catastrophically short." His themes: a Chinese compromise of voter data, an alleged establishment plot to minimize Chinese meddling, and an effort to lull the public into false confidence. He offered no evidence of a single fraudulent vote in any election, and nothing in the release suggests any past count, including his own 2020 loss, was affected by fraud or foreign interference. The NYT's analysis frames it as relitigating 2020 while pre-seeding doubt about the 2026 midterms, a cost to democracy regardless of intent. Beijing rejected the meddling claim but conspicuously avoided a fight. There is a legislative vehicle attached, a "Save America Act" push. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/16/nx-s1-5896448/trump-election-address), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/16/politics/live-news/trump-speech-election), [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2026/jul/16/president-trump-address-nation-speech-election/))

Air quality alerts cover roughly 17 to 18 states from Montana through the Upper Midwest to New England and the mid-Atlantic, with something on the order of 100 million people in unhealthy air. Detroit hit an AQI near 570; Minneapolis at one point registered the worst measured air quality of any major city in the world, with Chicago third. 859 active fires were burning in Canada as of late Thursday. Relevant for you clinically and personally: the Boston-to-Philadelphia corridor is inside the heavy-smoke band. ## Israel

**The Knesset passed Karhi's media overhaul, 53–48.** The vote came as the final act of a coalition legislative blitz before the Knesset dissolves ahead of the October 27 election. The law substantially expands government influence over broadcast media: it strips longstanding oversight mechanisms, minimum journalistic standards, obligations to invest in original Israeli production, and cross-ownership restrictions, while handing the government more sway over television ratings and the allocation of state advertising. Opposition leaders called it an "Erdogan style" regime-change law; MK Shelly Tal Meron said it "will take control over the free press in the State of Israel," and Yair Golan of The Democrats vowed the ink would not dry before a future government tore it up. Passage three months before an election, over a media sector that covers that election, is the whole controversy in one sentence. ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-july-16-2026/), [JPost](http://www.jpost.com/israel-news/politics-and-diplomacy/article-902758))

**Lebanon withdrawal moves from framework to implementation.** After two days of US-mediated talks in Rome that concluded Wednesday, a US official said Israeli forces will begin withdrawing from two "pilot zones" in southern Lebanon within days, with the Lebanese Armed Forces deploying into the vacated areas. The zones, cleared of Hezbollah infrastructure, come out of the framework Israel and Lebanon signed in Washington last month. The State Department called the first day productive and says expanded technical talks toward a comprehensive agreement follow implementation. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun, who visits Washington on July 21, had instructed his delegation to demand immediate withdrawal before any further discussion. Timing rests substantially on a single US official's characterization; treat the "within days" as developing. ([ToI](https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-troops-to-withdraw-from-lebanon-pilot-zones-in-coming-days-says-us-official/), [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-07-15/ty-article/.premium/israel-agrees-to-withdraw-idf-from-two-lebanon-pilot-zones-u-s-official-says/0000019f-6655-dc96-afdf-6ed501b70000))

**Also.** Aryeh Deri accused the IDF chief of election interference for opposing a law banning the arrest of draft dodgers, the perennial haredi-conscription fight now colliding with the campaign calendar (ToI). ## Economic & Markets

**The AI trade went into reverse, hard.** TSMC posted stellar Q2 earnings and raised revenue guidance on strong AI demand, and its stock still headed for its biggest one-day fall since April 2025, because it also guided capital spending sharply higher. That is the tell: investors are no longer rewarding AI capex, they are pricing it as a cost. The selloff was global. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 5.2%, its steepest drop since March; the MSCI Asia Pacific index fell 2.7% toward a two-month low; Kioxia plunged as much as 16%; Shanghai closed off 3.05%. Nasdaq 100 futures were down 661 points, or 2.3%, with the Dow off 0.7%. Netflix's weak growth forecast added to the mood. Hedge funds were caught badly positioned. Gillian Tett's column on investor fatalism and the FT's question of whether cheap specialised models threaten the Big Tech chokehold are both live to the same anxiety. Meanwhile TSMC committed another \$100bn to US production, and South Korean stocks slumped after the first rate rise in three years. Brent crude rose 1.67% on the Iran war. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/us-stock-futures-tumble-as-tsmc-s-results-revive-ai-concerns), [FT](https://www.ft.com/), [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-funds-caught-in-ai-reversal-as-global-technology-stocks-suffer-sharp-sell-off/))

**Apple escalates against OpenAI.** An FT exclusive reports Apple has sent legal letters to dozens of OpenAI employees in its trade-secrets dispute with the lab, an unusually aggressive move that targets individuals rather than the institution. Also from the FT: Trump Media plans to sell high-speed access to the president's social media posts, which is a market-structure story dressed as a media one, and the administration slapped a 25% tariff on Brazil as relations deteriorate.
