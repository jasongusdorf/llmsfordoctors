**Top of the morning.** Russia battered Kyiv again overnight, 12+ dead, hours before NATO leaders convene in Ankara, where Erdogan is the awkward host and Ukraine's air defenses are the central question. Iran buried its assassinated supreme leader before crowds chanting "revenge," with his presumed successor pointedly absent. And a weak US jobs report (57k) paradoxically pushed the Dow to a record as rate-hike fears eased.

## International

**Russia strikes Kyiv on the eve of the NATO summit.** Ballistic missiles and drones slammed into the Ukrainian capital overnight, killing at least 12 people in Russia's second major assault in under a week (NYT, ~2:30am ET; FT). The timing is pointed: NATO heads of state gather in Ankara tomorrow, and Zelensky is publicly pleading with allies for more Patriot interceptors. The NYT notes Ukraine has genuinely rewritten how militaries wield Patriot systems, but clever tactics can't offset a raw shortage of interceptors against saturation attacks. Meanwhile Ukraine is hitting back at unprecedented scale, with an intensified drone campaign against Russian refineries that the FT says is driving Russia's worst fuel crisis in decades. Cross-confirmed NYT + FT.

**NATO convenes in Ankara, with a difficult host.** The alliance summit runs July 7–8 in Ankara (not, as some trackers still say, The Hague). Secretary-General Rutte's stated priorities: higher defense spending, transatlantic industrial production, and Ukraine. But the run-up is ugly: Erdogan's government has detained journalists and a popular comedian who called him a "dictator," casting a pall over proceedings (FT). Turkey's foreign minister Hakan Fidan told the NYT the Erdogan–Trump rapport could actually ease alliance tensions. The FT's editorial board is more worried, arguing the real questions, the "Europeanisation" of NATO in the Trump era, won't even be on the Ankara agenda.

**Iran buries Khamenei; the succession stays murky.** On the second and third days of a six-day state funeral, millions filled Tehran for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, assassinated Feb. 28 in the joint US–Israeli strikes that opened the 2026 war. Mourners chanted "revenge" (NYT). The tell, flagged by both NYT and FT: his son and presumed successor, Mojtaba Khamenei, did *not* appear alongside the coffin with other top leaders, fueling speculation about an unsettled succession. The FT frames the mass turnout as a regime show of strength; the NYT's dispatch reads the same crowds as a symbol of Khamenei's failures. Both are worth holding in mind. Relatedly, an FT poll finds US voters now say Trump's Iran war wasn't worth the cost, dragging his approval ahead of the November midterms.

**China fires a ballistic missile into the Pacific.** A submarine-launched missile carrying a dummy warhead landed in a South Pacific nuclear-free zone, drawing formal condemnation from Japan, Australia and New Zealand (FT, ~4am ET; NYT). It's a deliberate signal of reach and a fresh irritant to a region already on edge over Chinese military assertiveness.

**Britain's next PM is coming into focus.** Two weeks after Keir Starmer's June 22 resignation, Greater Manchester mayor Andy Burnham is the clear favorite; Labour nominations open July 9 and close July 16 (NYT profiles; Al Jazeera). The NYT has run warm profiles of his Manchester roots; the FT's Martin Wolf is pointedly skeptical, arguing Burnham "needs to start by understanding how little he knows" and that even his full wish-list wouldn't fix UK growth. Separately, Trump escalated his feud with Italy's Meloni, once his most devoted European ally, with a post saying a "restraining order" is needed (FT).

## US

**Mass shooting at a Coney Island July 4 barbecue.** A masked gunman in all black approached a courtyard near West 30th and Surf Avenue around 10:37pm Saturday and opened fire with a TEC-9, wounding eight, four adults and four children ages 6, 7, 12 and 14 (CNN, NYPD). A 21-year-old woman is in critical condition; the others are expected to survive. No arrests; the suspect fled on foot, and detectives are probing a possible link to a gang homicide earlier that week on the same block. It was part of a bloody national Fourth, with at least six killed in shootings and blasts around the country. Worth flagging given your Brooklyn move, this is a couple of miles from the boardwalk.

**National Guard troops kill an armed man in Memphis.** Guard members fired their weapons in the early hours of July 5, killing a man police say was carrying a handgun (Memphis police via wires). Details are still thin and this is developing; expect scrutiny of the Guard's rules of engagement in the domestic-deployment debate.

**Heat and storms.** A heat wave from the Midwest to the East Coast is hitting up to 90 million people, with Washington forecast to top 100°F, while thunderstorms and high winds complicate efforts to contain a large wildfire in southern Colorado (wires). In the Pacific, Category 5 Typhoon Bavi pounded Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands, knocking out power across Saipan (NYT).

## Israel

**Netanyahu's cabinet defies the Supreme Court.** The Israeli cabinet formally rejected a Supreme Court ruling on broadcast/TV regulation, a move the FT says raises fears of a full constitutional crisis and sharpens the long-running Netanyahu–judiciary conflict (FT; NYT). A senior official later walked the threat back somewhat, per the NYT, so treat the final posture as still in flux. This is the confrontation to watch this week.

**Friction with Washington over Lebanon.** Netanyahu is expected at the White House in the coming days, but there's visible strain with Trump over Israel's military strikes in Lebanon (wires). Single-thread reporting on the friction's depth, worth watching how the visit is framed.

**Gaza ceasefire holds only loosely.** The November 2025 framework (UNSC Res. 2803) that freed the remaining hostages remains formally in place, but Al Jazeera and others report continued Israeli strikes and a dire humanitarian picture, with the IDF holding an expanded footprint inside the enclave (Al Jazeera). Along the northern border, the army detained settler activists who tried to breach the frontier with Syria (Times of Israel).

## Economic & Markets

**A weak jobs report the market loved.** The US added just 57,000 jobs in June, roughly half the ~113k consensus and the softest gain of the recovery, with unemployment ticking to 4.2% and April/May revised down a combined 74k (CNBC, July 2; FT). Counterintuitively, stocks rallied: a cooling labor market eases pressure on a Fed that (under Chair Warsh) had been flirting with a *hike*. Traders pulled a September move off the table. The Dow closed last week at a record, capping the S&P's strongest quarter in six years.

**but strategists are getting nervous.** Bank of America reaffirmed a 7,100 year-end S&P target, about 5% *below* Friday's close, warning its bear-market signposts show "speculation hitting extreme levels" (Fortune). The FT is running a cluster of caution: John Thornhill on why OpenAI and Anthropic may struggle to float, a Big Read on SoftBank's Son "betting the house on AI," and Joachim Klement warning the real 1990s echo is in capital expenditure. Samsung reports Tuesday (up 165% YTD) and SK Hynix eyes a \$29bn US listing, the AI-hardware trade remains the whole market's load-bearing wall.

**Deals.** EasyJet's board says it is minded to recommend a £5.5bn takeover by US private-credit group Castlelake (FT). And a Honeywell spin-off, Solstice, is in talks to merge with Element Solutions into a ~\$27bn materials group positioned as a key supplier of chipmaking inputs (FT).

## Also notable

Belgium is crying foul over the governing body's about-face (FT, NYT). The US had already beaten co-host Mexico., And Kelsey Pfendler, a Grand Canyon rafting guide, became the first American woman to row solo from California to Hawaii, covering 2,300+ miles in under 44 days (NYT).

## Worth reading, New Yorker & long-form

**"The Tick That Hunts Down Its Hosts, Including Us,"** by Burkhard Bilger. A genuinely good piece of science reporting on lone-star ticks and alpha-gal syndrome, the tick-borne allergy to red meat that is quietly upending diets and, Bilger argues, a whole way of life across expanding swaths of the country. Right in your wheelhouse clinically. (New Yorker, Body Issue)

**"Something Is Very Wrong with Modern Longevity Science,"** by Dhruv Khullar. The physician-writer reviews a new book arguing that many of the world's supposed supercentenarians simply aren't as old as claimed, bad records, not blue-zone magic, with real implications for how seriously to take the longevity-research industry. A sharp, skeptical read for anyone who follows the aging literature. (New Yorker) *Also in the issue: David Remnick on Haberman & Swan's Trump book "Regime Change."*
