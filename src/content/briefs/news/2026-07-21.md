**TL;DR , ** The US-Iran war is now the axis everything else turns on: a 10th straight night of strikes, ~100 US troops injured in two weeks, Iranian attacks on five Gulf states, tankers burning in Hormuz, and a Houthi Red Sea blockade threatening to widen the fight. Oil is back above \$90 and US pump prices over \$4. At home, Trump is opening a 50% tariff front on Canada and was revealed to have sought NYT reporters' phone records. And Britain woke up to its seventh prime minister in a decade, Andy Burnham.

## International

**US and Iran fight into a 10th night; the war spreads across the Gulf.** NYT (live, 7:00 a.m. ET Jul 21) / Al Jazeera / FT\
The conflict that reignited in early July after the Strait of Hormuz truce collapsed shows no sign of cooling. US forces struck Iranian command centers and missile sites for a 10th consecutive night, while Iran's IRGC says it launched ballistic missiles and drones at US positions across the region, Jordan's Aqaba airport, Kuwait's Ali Al Salem base and Al-Adiri camp, and radar and communications sites in Bahrain. The FT reports **nearly 100 US troops injured in the two weeks** since fighting resumed, alongside the three US service members killed that Trump has vowed Iran will "pay" for. In the strait itself, the IRGC claims it disabled two oil tankers it accused of taking an "unsafe route" and says Hormuz stays closed "until the end of US interference." Cross-checked across NYT, FT and Al Jazeera; casualty and tanker specifics come largely from IRGC/CENTCOM statements and remain hard to independently verify.

**Houthis declare a Red Sea blockade on Saudi Arabia.** NYT\
The Iran-backed Yemeni militia has declared a maritime navigation ban targeting Saudi Arabia, a move the NYT frames as the Houthis edging toward formally entering the US-Iran war and potentially opening a second front. Riyadh said it would take "all necessary measures" under international law to protect its vessels. Still developing; watch whether the blockade is enforced or remains rhetorical.

**Andy Burnham takes over as UK prime minister.** NYT / FT / NPR\
After Keir Starmer's resignation amid a Labour leadership crisis (poor May local elections, internal revolt), Andy Burnham, the former Manchester mayor, elected unopposed with 90%+ of Labour MPs behind him, became Britain's **seventh prime minister in roughly a decade**. He moved fast: ordering a cut to taxes on electricity (scrapping VAT on power bills, which immediately sparked a funding row), dropping the digital-ID scheme, and naming a cabinet that surprised MPs, John Healey as chancellor, Ed Miliband as foreign secretary, Wes Streeting at defense. The FT's editorial board flags "troubling signals" and notes his MPs have "handed him a blank cheque" without a clear program; Jamie Dimon warned of "consequences" if Burnham taxes banks. Gilts are under pressure as markets brace for higher spending.

**Ukraine hits Moscow with 400+ drones as Zelensky's political crisis deepens.** Fox/wires / NYT / FT\
Ukraine launched more than 400 drones toward the Moscow region in one of the war's largest strikes, hitting an oil depot and logistics sites well over 250 miles from the border. Domestically, Zelensky is under pressure: his firing of popular, drone-pioneering defense minister Mykhailo Fedorov has drawn sustained protests in Kyiv (crowds chanting "bring Fedorov back," waving EU flags), and the FT reports Fedorov has now rejected an offer to return, deepening the standoff. NYT's read: Zelensky's office is signaling openness to compromise in the face of public anger.

## US

**Trump to slap 50% tariffs on many Canadian goods.** NYT / FT\
The administration is set to impose 50% duties on a wide range of Canadian products, citing disputes over autos, alcohol and cheese, part of a broader tariff barrage as 10% reciprocal levies expire and Trump renews the trade war following a Supreme Court ruling that threw out earlier reciprocal duties. Ottawa is unmoved; the FT's Chrystia Freeland column captures Canada's mood ("not listening to American whining in the wine wars").

**Trump administration sought NYT journalists' phone records.** NYT / New Yorker\
The administration pursued the phone records of several New York Times reporters and their relatives, including one reporter's mother, in an effort to unmask confidential sources behind stories about the security capabilities of Trump's new Air Force One jet. The New Yorker's Ruth Marcus argues the subpoenas are designed to threaten the press, with few legal protections in place to stop it. (Relatedly, NYT reports the new Air Force One will be out of service for a month for "upgrades.")

**Republicans split over a \$95B war budget and the SAVE Act.** NYT\
GOP rifts are deepening over a \$95 billion budget package tied to funding the Iran war and the SAVE Act, a sign the fiscal and political costs of the conflict are starting to bite at home.

**Cyclospora outbreak from Taylor Farms lettuce spreads.** NYT / FDA\
The FDA says lettuce from Taylor Fresh Foods and Taylor Farms de Mexico remains linked to a multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak. NYT notes the case count implies fecal contamination entered the food system on a "surprising scale", a reminder of the fragility of fresh-produce safety. (Opinion companion: Timothy Lytton, "This Is Why Our Fresh Produce Keeps Making Us Sick.")

## Israel

**Lebanese army deploys south in a first post-withdrawal "pilot zone."** Times of Israel / Haaretz\
In a notable step for the US-brokered Lebanon track, the Lebanese Armed Forces are deploying into southern villages, Froun, Srifa, Zawtar al-Gharbiya, vacated by withdrawing Israeli troops, under a "pilot zone" arrangement. Secretary Rubio met Lebanon's president in Washington as the US pushes the deal forward. A concrete, if fragile, move toward de-escalation on Israel's northern border even as the Iran war rages.

**Far-right ministers turn Gaza resettlement into an election plank.** CNN / Times of Israel\
With Israeli elections set for October, Itamar Ben Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich led thousands of settlers and activists to the Gaza perimeter in a "March of Thousands," pushing to rebuild Jewish settlements in Gaza. Separately, Netanyahu, in a rare rebuke of his own base, called settler violence in the West Bank a "metastasizing cancer." The two stories together capture the widening fault line inside the Israeli right.

## Economic & Markets

**Oil above \$90, US gas back over \$4.** FT / Bloomberg / CNBC\
Brent touched \$90 (settling ~\$89) and WTI ~\$83 as Iran's tanker attacks and the Hormuz closure stoke supply fears; US petrol prices have climbed back above \$4 a gallon. NYT's lead story ties it together: the administration is pulling back on energy-efficiency rules just as the war, data-center demand and heat waves push gasoline and electricity costs up.

**Equities mixed; chips rebound.** CNBC / TheStreet / FT\
Monday closed mixed-to-higher, S&P +0.63%, Nasdaq +1.02%, Dow +0.23%, as chip names (SK Hynix, Micron, Western Digital, all +4%) recovered on hopes Iran might return to talks, offsetting war jitters. Futures early Tuesday were slightly soft. The tension of the tape: geopolitical risk vs. a heavy tech-earnings week and, per the FT's Robert Armstrong, worries about "broken valuation metrics" in an AI-inflated market. Consumer confidence, meanwhile, has slid to post-pandemic lows despite cooling headline inflation.

**Deals & corporates.** FT / NBC\
A US judge temporarily paused Paramount's \$110B acquisition of Warner Bros. Elsewhere: Segro rejected a £13.5B bid from Prologis; Mitie agreed a £3.1B takeover by OCS; Ryanair profits fell 34% on the Middle East war's hit to fares and fuel; and China's "national team" bought ~\$9B in shares to prop up its market.
