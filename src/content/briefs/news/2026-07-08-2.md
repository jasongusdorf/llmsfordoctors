## International

### US and Iran trade strikes; ceasefire declared "over"

The three-week-old US-Iran memorandum of understanding has effectively unraveled. Early Wednesday, US Central Command said it struck more than 80 Iranian targets, air-defense and coastal-radar sites, anti-ship missile batteries, drone-launch and port facilities, and more than 60 IRGC small boats in and around the Strait of Hormuz, in retaliation for Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels, including a laden Qatari LNG carrier, in the strait. The administration simultaneously revoked the waiver permitting Iranian oil sales and reimposed sanctions. CENTCOM framed the operation as imposing "heavy costs" for attacking civilian shipping in an international waterway. Iran's military vowed a "crushing response," and Trump, speaking in Ankara, said negotiations were "just a waste of time" and that the agreement is "over." Sources: CENTCOM via CNBC, Euronews, FT, ABC News (developing).

### Iran hits Kuwait; regional spillover widens

Kuwait's armed forces said they intercepted two ballistic missiles and 13 drones that entered its airspace at dawn on July 8, and Kuwait condemned the Iranian attacks on its soil. This follows a pattern of earlier Iranian strikes on Gulf states hosting US forces during the 2026 conflict. The renewed exchange raises the risk of a broader regional war centered on the world's most important oil chokepoint. Sources: WORLD, NPR, Al Jazeera. Casualty details still developing.

### NATO summit in Ankara: arms deals, F-35s, and Greenland again

With Trump in Ankara, NATO's Mark Rutte touted "tens of billions" in new defense contracts (no dollar figures given, and some projects long since agreed). The headline shift was Turkey: Trump said he will lift the CAATSA sanctions imposed after Ankara bought Russian S-400s and is prepared to consider selling Turkey F-35s, a big win for Erdogan and, per Israeli commentators, a sign of eroding Israeli clout. Trump also renewed his demand for US control of Greenland (conceding it "hurt my relationship with NATO"), threatened to pull US troops from Europe, and griped that allies wouldn't join the Iran campaign: "I just want loyalty." Sources: ABC7, CNBC, Times of Israel, Al Jazeera.

### Russia batters Kyiv; Zelensky pleads for interceptors

On the summit's eve, Russia launched roughly 68 missiles and 351 drones at Ukraine, killing about 19 in Kyiv and eight in surrounding regions. Zelensky said a shortage of interceptor missiles left Ukraine unable to down any ballistic missiles, and he used his face-to-face with Trump to press for more US-made Patriots while reiterating that "Ukraine belongs in NATO." Kyiv answered with deep strikes of its own: Zelensky claimed Ukrainian drones hit a Siberian oil refinery, asserting no major Russian refinery remains unstruck. Sources: Time, CNN, Al Jazeera.

Elsewhere: mourners thronged Najaf as Ayatollah Khamenei's funeral cortege passed through the Iraqi holy city ahead of Thursday's burial in Iran. In the UK, Nigel Farage's by-election gambit was undercut by rivals refusing to play along, and Prince Harry *lost* his phone-hacking claim against the Daily Mail's publisher. Sources: ABC News, FT.

## US

### Platner's Maine Senate campaign implodes

The Democratic bid to unseat Susan Collins is in crisis. After Politico published a detailed account from a Maine woman alleging Graham Platner sexually assaulted her in 2021, Democratic leaders moved fast: Schumer told him to "immediately" withdraw; Warren and Ruben Gallego rescinded endorsements; the state party and even longtime ally Bernie Sanders urged him to step aside. The DSCC says it will spend nothing on the race if he's the nominee, a seat considered pivotal to Senate control. Platner calls the allegation "categorically untrue" and is weighing his future as Democrats scramble for a replacement. Sources: Washington Post, CNN, Axios, NPR, Maine Public.

### ABC vs the FCC over "The View"

Disney-owned ABC urged the FCC to reject a proposal that would subject "The View" to federal equal-time rules for political candidates while, ABC argues, ignoring conservative talk radio, the latest flashpoint over the regulator's posture toward critical broadcasters. Sources: US News, Reuters. Separately, a new survey found GLP-1 weight-loss drug use at a record high even as US obesity shows a "meaningful" decline, one to watch clinically.

## Israel

### Hamas dissolves Gaza government

On July 6, the Hamas-led "Emergency Committee" that has administered Gaza announced its resignation, clearing the way to hand authority to the technocratic National Committee envisioned under the UN Resolution 2803 peace framework and the Board of Peace transitional administration. It's a notable procedural step in the fragile post-war governance plan, though disarmament remains the central unresolved sticking point. Source: Times of Israel.

### Ceasefire strains continue

Israeli strikes killed at least six Palestinians and wounded more than 20 this week; Gaza's health ministry counts 1,072 killed since the truce took hold. Nearly all of Gaza's 2.1 million residents remain displaced. Meanwhile, Israeli commentators note the Ankara summit, and US warmth toward Turkey, underscores a relative erosion of Israel's regional standing. Sources: Al Jazeera, Times of Israel (figures single-sourced to Gaza MoH; treat as contested).

## Economic & Markets

### Records despite the geopolitics

Wall Street largely shrugged: the Dow rose 0.3% to a record 53,055.91 and the Nasdaq gained 1.1% as the AI trade regained momentum on soft June payrolls that eased rate-hike worries, even as a gauge of semiconductor firms sank more than 4.5% on doubts that massive AI capex will pay off. The main event is today: FOMC minutes, with markets parsing whether the Fed (which held at 3.50-3.75% in June, nine officials still pencilling a 2026 hike) leans hawkish. Notably, June US unemployment ticked *down*, feeding the hawkish read. Sources: Globe & Mail, Bloomberg, CNBC, FT, 24/7 Wall St.

### Oil, chips, and a Japanese debt scare

Oil rose about 3% (Brent settling near \$74.16) on the Hormuz strikes and the revoked Iran waiver, though an OPEC+ supply bump and normalizing tanker traffic capped the move, a striking sign of how well-supplied the market is. Apple committed more than \$30bn to Broadcom for US-made radio-frequency chips (15B+ units, a \$1.5bn Fort Collins, Colorado expansion, running to 2031), the latest in its campaign to court Trump on domestic manufacturing. And in Tokyo, borrowing costs hit a 30-year high with bond yields at their loftiest since 1996, as fiscal-expansion fears box in the Bank of Japan. Sources: CNBC, FT, Bloomberg.
