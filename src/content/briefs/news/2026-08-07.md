## International

### Hormuz: a deal in principle, on Tehran's terms

Iran said this week it has agreed in principle with Oman on coordinates for a safe shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, closed since the US–Israel strikes on Iran began in late February. The framework would route inbound vessels through Iranian territorial waters and outbound traffic through an Omani route, managed by a joint coordination center. Iranian media report Tehran will seek to bar US- and Israeli-linked vessels and may impose penalties up to 20% of cargo value; a parliamentary committee is drafting legislation to that effect. Washington says it will not accept an arrangement leaving Iran in control of a waterway carrying roughly a fifth of global oil and LNG. Separately, the US push for a naval escort coalition is stalling, 30-plus states attended a technical meeting, but Britain and France, who said in April they would lead it, have not committed. Al Jazeera / Bloomberg / Reuters, Aug 5–6 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/hormuz-deal-close-whats-the-latest-on-each-sides-positions)

**Developing.** Specific terms (fees, vessel bans) come via Iranian state media and unnamed Iranian officials. Nothing is signed, and Trump has missed several self-imposed deadlines on this file.

### Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan sign a defense pact

Erdogan, Mohammed bin Salman and Shehbaz Sharif convened in Jeddah Friday to sign a trilateral defense agreement covering intelligence sharing, defense-industrial integration and joint technology development. The parties describe it as purely defensive. Its strategic content is hard to miss: it links a NATO member's defense industry, Saudi economic weight, and a nuclear-armed Pakistani military, at a moment when Gulf states are absorbing fire from Iran and Iran-aligned forces. ### Austria breaks its national heat record twice in two days

Austria hit 41.2C (106.2F) Wednesday at Bad Deutsch-Altenburg near the Slovak border, breaking a national record it had set only two days earlier at 40.8C. Italy placed all major cities under red heat alerts. The heat dome has shifted east across central and southeastern Europe after weeks over France and Spain. Danube levels are comparable to 2003 and 2018 lows; in Hungary the Paks nuclear plant, which draws river water for cooling, cut output to roughly 10% of normal and the government imposed power-use curbs. Albanian and Austrian firefighters are working active blazes. This is the third consecutive summer of record heat on the world's fastest-warming continent. Al Jazeera / Reuters / CBS, Aug 4–7 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/central-europe-sees-record-temperatures-as-heatwave-continues)

## United States

### Two new executive orders on birthright citizenship

Trump signed two immigration orders Thursday. The first declares children born to "alien enemies," members of designated foreign terrorist organizations, and people acting on behalf of foreign governments to be outside the scope of the 14th Amendment's Citizenship Clause. The second directs restrictions on visas and entry for people traveling to the US primarily to give birth. Both are markedly narrower than the January 2025 attempt, a direct response to the June 2026 Supreme Court ruling that rejected the broader effort and affirmed an expansive reading of birthright citizenship. The live legal question is whether the foreign-agent carve-out can be defended as a class distinguishable from what the Court already protected. Suits are expected within days. NPR / CNN, Aug 6 · [link](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/g-s1-137686/trump-birthright-citizenship-immigration-curb)

### Senate panel refers Fauci to DOJ for contempt

The Senate Homeland Security Committee voted 8–5 Thursday to hold Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress after he invoked the Fifth Amendment more than 100 times at a hearing last week on COVID origins and gain-of-function oversight. Chairman Rand Paul is bypassing a full floor vote and referred the matter straight to the Justice Department. That route is procedurally unusual and contested in its own right: the criminal contempt statute contemplates certification by the presiding officer of the chamber, so both DOJ's willingness to act and the validity of a committee-only referral are open. Fauci's attorney called the vote "a crude political stunt intended to punish Dr. Fauci for exercising his constitutional rights." It sets up a real test case on Fifth Amendment scope for congressional witnesses. NPR / CBS / WaPo, Aug 6 · [link](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/06/nx-s1-5921383/anthony-fauci-contempt-of-congress-vote)

The separate reporting that a GOP senator obtained a copy of Fauci's phone appeared primarily in the Washington Post and is less corroborated than the vote itself.

### FDA approves the first mRNA flu vaccine

The FDA on Wednesday licensed Moderna's mFLUSIVA (mRNA-1010) for adults 50 and older, standard approval for 50–64, accelerated approval for 65-plus contingent on a confirmatory postmarketing study. In pivotal trials it showed roughly 27% greater relative efficacy than a standard-dose comparator, a meaningful margin in a field where incremental gains are the norm. The approval closes an unusual regulatory saga: in February then-CBER director Vinay Prasad overrode career reviewers with a rare refusal-to-file, a decision reversed two weeks later after Moderna disclosed it publicly; Prasad left the agency in April. The bottleneck now is downstream. Approval does not produce an ACIP recommendation or insurance coverage, and the reconstituted advisory committee's posture toward mRNA platforms under Secretary Kennedy is the open question. It also unlocks Moderna's resubmission path for its flu/COVID combination. STAT / NBC / CNN, Aug 5 · [link](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/05/fda-approves-moderna-mflusiva-first-mrna-flu-vaccine/)

### HHS proposes stripping Head Start's federal standards

The administration proposed a rule Thursday, "Reducing Federal Burden for Head Start Programs," that would eliminate most of the program's 100-plus pages of federal regulation and defer to state and local child-care licensing instead. Gone would be requirements for low staff-to-student ratios, research-backed curricula, and the wraparound services that define the model, medical and dental screening, developmental screening, family services. Two things get added: an administrative-expense cap tightened from 15% to 5%, and new nutrition provisions championed by Secretary Kennedy. The clinically salient loss is the screening infrastructure, which for a great many low-income preschoolers is the only reliable point of contact with preventive care. Sixty-day comment period; litigation likely at the final-rule stage. PBS NewsHour / AP, Aug 6 · [link](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/education/trump-administration-moves-forward-with-head-start-overhaul-proposing-to-eliminate-regulations)

### Erica Schwartz confirmed to lead CDC

The Senate confirmed Dr. Erica Schwartz as CDC director Wednesday, 51–44, filling a post that has lacked Senate-confirmed leadership for about a year. A former deputy surgeon general and retired Coast Guard flag officer, she is the first Black woman to lead the agency. She succeeds NIH Director Jay Bhattacharya, who had been acting director since February, an arrangement criticized for concentrating two agency heads in one person. Tim Kaine was the only Democrat voting yes; others cited doubt she would hold her stated pro-vaccine line against Secretary Kennedy. She inherits workforce cuts, the ACIP overhaul, and heavy senior scientific attrition, and takes office in the same week as the mRNA flu approval, which makes her ACIP posture an immediate test rather than a theoretical one. NPR / CNN / CIDRAP, Aug 5 · [link](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/05/nx-s1-5920379/cdc-director-confirmed-erica-schwartz)

## Israel

### Israel reported to be weighing open defiance of the Gaza roadmap

This is the week's most consequential Israel story and also the least solid, so take the sourcing first: it rests on anonymous Israeli officials via Hebrew-language media, relayed by the Times of Israel liveblog. Per that reporting, ministers and security officials have discussed resuming significant military operations in Gaza specifically to signal that Israel does not accept the US-backed 15-point roadmap announced around July 30, under which Hamas would disarm in stages over roughly eight months. Shin Bet chief David Zini is quoted calling Hamas's signature "a strategic ambush" and "a process of strategic deception aimed at buying time", his read being that Hamas signed to prevent Israeli military action before the October 27 election. Minister Ze'ev Elkin is described as urging visible offensive action; Smotrich reportedly agreed Israel cannot quietly maneuver around a document it has not formally rejected. National Security Adviser Shmuel Ben Ezra has handed Washington a list of demanded corrections. Friction points include pressure to pull back to the original Yellow Line and a near-total 14-day freeze on operations. Herzog struck a softer note, calling the roadmap substantially positive while insisting Hamas must disarm. Times of Israel, Aug 7 · [link](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-7-2026/)

**Single-sourced and developing.** The Zini and Elkin quotes are secondhand and unconfirmed on the record. These are reported deliberations, not decided policy.

### Strikes continue in Gaza, straining the plan

Israeli strikes have continued and intensified in the week since the roadmap was announced, with Gaza health and civil defense authorities reporting daily fatalities, including 19 in a single day and 26 over one weekend. Hamas has told Egyptian, Qatari and Turkish mediators in Cairo that the strikes are derailing implementation. Israel says it is hitting Hamas weapons depots and personnel and cites serious security concerns about the roadmap; the IDF said one round destroyed four depots. Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir said the military "will not relent until we bring every person who played a part in the massacre to justice." Trump has publicly told Israel to stop bombing, arguing Hamas is ready for peace, and the Board of Peace's lead envoy has warned the strikes could undermine the effort. Netanyahu's office maintains there will be no withdrawal before full disarmament. Al Jazeera / Reuters / NBC, Aug 3–7 · [link](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/israel-defies-trumps-peace-plan-as-it-escalates-deadly-attacks-on-gaza)

Casualty counts come from Gaza's Hamas-linked health and civil defense authorities, which do not distinguish combatants from civilians; Israeli figures differ. Specific Aug 6–7 daily tolls could not be independently confirmed from the wires.

Tamir Vaknin, 33, both of the 55th Paratroopers Brigade, were killed and four soldiers seriously wounded Wednesday when a booby-trapped house detonated in Majdal Zoun during an operation to demolish Hezbollah infrastructure. ### July payrolls at 8:30, and the forecasts are all over the place

Consensus is roughly 80,000–83,000 nonfarm payrolls with unemployment holding at 4.2%, which would be a modest improvement on June's 57,000. The dispersion is the tell: FactSet had 97,500, Bank of America near 80,000, and Vanguard, using 401(k) plan data, flagged something as low as 18,000, a Street range of roughly 18K to 120K. Wednesday's ADP print made everyone more nervous, showing private payrolls up just 44,000, the weakest month of the year and well under the ~75,000 consensus, with leisure and hospitality down 11,000. Cutting the other way, Challenger reported the lowest monthly layoff total since July 2024 and the four-week average of initial claims fell to 198,750, the lowest since October 2022. Wage growth is expected to stay soft. CNBC / FactSet / ADP, Aug 5–6 · [link](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/the-july-jobs-numbers-are-due-out-friday-heres-what-to-expect.html)

### Cook: inflation is too high, and a hike is on the table

Governor Lisa Cook, in the week's only scheduled Fed speech, put 12-month PCE inflation at 3.7%, near double target, and signaled willingness to raise rates if disinflation doesn't materialize. She blamed conflict-driven energy costs and AI-infrastructure-driven semiconductor and tech prices, and described a "low-hire, low-fire" labor market. She flagged three offsetting disinflationary forces: waning tariff pass-through, expected oil declines, and AI supply-chain normalization. Worth noting she cited H1 GDP growth of 1.8% with business investment, heavily AI infrastructure, growing at a 10% annual rate. Context: the July 29 FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% by 9–3, with Logan, Hammack and Kashkari all dissenting for a 25bp *hike*, the first three-way same-direction dissent since September 2016, at Chair Warsh's second meeting. Federal Reserve / CNBC, Aug 5 · [link](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/cook20260805a.htm)

### Oil whipsaws on the Iranian draft

Crude reversed a week-long slide Thursday after Iranian state media published a draft Hormuz transit plan with restrictive conditions. Brent rose 3.8% to settle at \$82.49 and WTI gained about 2.8% to \$77.29; Friday morning Brent was near \$82.26. Prices had fallen roughly 8% earlier in the week after Treasury Secretary Bessent told CNBC a deal reopening the strait with freedom of movement could come "as soon as Wednesday." The published draft undercut that: it would bar US and Israeli vessels, bar ships from nations deemed to have harmed Iran until compensation is paid, and impose penalties equal to 20% of cargo value. Nothing is signed. This is now the largest single swing factor for near-term inflation expectations, Cook put oil explicitly on her disinflation checklist. CNBC / France 24, Aug 5–6 · [link](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/oil-price-iran-war-strait-hormuz-oman-deal.html)

### AI capex indigestion

The week's dominant equity theme was investors punishing AI-linked companies for spending rather than for missing. AMD beat on both lines Aug 4 but guided Q3 revenue to \$13.0B, short of hopes for data-center revenue roughly doubling, and fell 8–9%; capex more than doubled to \$808M. SpaceX, public since its June IPO, dropped more than 10% after its first quarterly report showed Q2 capex of \$18.4 billion, a sixfold increase mostly for AI infrastructure, despite revenue up 92% to roughly \$7.9B and a narrowed loss. Memory names sold off too. Then on Aug 6 SpaceX's insider lockup expired, 911.5 million shares, roughly \$100 billion, more than doubling the public float from 4.9% to 11.8% of shares outstanding, and the stock held steady, bucking higher after making new lows. A staggered release structure (only the first 20% eligible Thursday) helped, but demand for the name after a capex-driven selloff is the tell worth filing. Offsetting bright spots: Disney up 2%-plus on a fiscal Q3 beat, and Eli Lilly results helped push the S&P to a record intraday high Wednesday. **Marine One near-miss at Reagan National.** On Aug 4, Envoy Air Flight 3742 was cleared for takeoff from Runway 19 at DCA while Marine One, carrying the president, was airborne nearby; closest proximity was 0.8 miles laterally and 700 feet vertically. Controllers failed to pause commercial departures as required by rules imposed after the January 2025 DCA midair collision. NTSB and FAA have both opened investigations; both aircraft landed safely. CNN, Aug 5 · [link](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/05/us/trump-marine-one-reagan-safety-incident-hnk)

**Water-system cyberattacks now span 12 states.** Since July 27, hackers have hit municipal water and wastewater utilities in at least 12 states, up from seven earlier in the week, including 30-plus facilities in Minnesota and nine systems in Michigan. Attackers are exploiting internet-connected Rockwell Automation industrial control devices, changing IP addresses and passwords to lock out operators. Some incidents caused pressure drops and localized flooding; no drinking water has been declared unsafe. Iran is the leading suspect per intelligence officials, but the FBI has not formally attributed. Worth a thought for health-system infrastructure preparedness. Axios / NBC / WaPo, Aug 1–4 · [link](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/04/water-cyberattacks-us-iran)

WHO's Tedros visited Aug 5. Uganda declared its linked outbreak over July 28 after 20 cases and 2 deaths. ## Worth reading, New Yorker & long-form

### How Netanyahu's Top General Became His Strongest Challenger

David Remnick, *Letter from Israel*, New Yorker, Aug 3 issue. Remnick profiles Gadi Eisenkot, the retired IDF chief of staff who currently looks like the most plausible person to unseat Israel's longest-serving prime minister. The frame is Eisenkot as the deliberate anti-Bibi: a man whose view of war was reshaped by the death of his son, running on service, reform and national repair rather than fear. Remnick reports from campaign headquarters in Ramat HaSharon and from the road to Hebron with Breaking the Silence's Yehuda Shaul, and reconstructs Eisenkot's dissents inside the war cabinet, including his argument against opening a northern front before Hamas was dealt with in the south. The question it leaves open is whether Trump intervenes at the last moment to save Netanyahu. Given the poll arithmetic above, this is the single most useful thing you could read this weekend. [link](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/03/how-netanyahus-top-general-became-his-strongest-challenger)

### The Future, Made in China

Evan Osnos, *Letter from Beijing*, New Yorker, Aug 10 issue. Osnos's anchor essay on the technological race, built around Kai-Fu Lee and his forecast of a new world order tilting toward Beijing. The reporting contrasts China's state-directed industrial-policy approach to AI with the American decision to hand AI strategy largely to Silicon Valley, and marshals a striking set of comparisons: China produces at least 70% of the world's drones, EVs, lithium-ion batteries and solar cells, deploys more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined, and, the one that should stop you, has now surpassed the US in number of registered clinical trials. The news hook is Moonshot's July release of a frontier-comparable model at a fraction of US cost. The best piece here for the AI-in-medicine reader precisely because it treats capability as a question of state capacity rather than model benchmarks. [link](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china)

### The U.S. Relies on Family Caregivers. Millions of Them Are Kids.

Helen Ouyang, MD, MPH, *New York Times Magazine*, Aug 5. Ouyang, an emergency physician who also wrote the paper's piece on how AI might change the way doctors think, reports on the millions of American children serving as unpaid caregivers for parents, grandparents and siblings. Her argument is structural rather than sentimental: the health care system quietly assumes caregiving labor will fall to whoever else is in the household, and when no adult is available that means a 14-year-old managing a mother's metastatic breast cancer or an eighth grader caring for his 74-year-old grandmother. It centers the American Association of Caregiving Youth, one of the few organizations that even names this population. Read it as a critique of discharge planning, of work you sign orders for and never see performed. [link](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/05/magazine/child-family-caregivers-aacy.html)
