**TL;DR:** The renewed US–Israel war on Iran is the whole morning, an 11th consecutive night of American strikes, six Iranian missiles fired toward the Aqaba/Eilat area, Trump threatening the “Pickaxe Mountain” nuclear site, and oil up ~4% to six-week highs. Markets are jittery into today’s Alphabet and Tesla earnings. Elsewhere: the IDF made its first real pullback in south Lebanon, Britain has its 7th PM in a decade, and a US tariff cliff hits Friday.

## International

### The Iran war is back on, and escalating

The dominant story by a wide margin. The US carried out its **11th consecutive night of strikes** on Iran overnight, and on Wednesday morning Iran fired **six ballistic missiles toward the Aqaba region near Eilat**; Jordan’s military intercepted four, two hit uninhabited desert, and the IDF confirmed no Israeli territory was struck (explosions were visible from Eilat, no casualties reported). Trump is now openly threatening to hit **“Pickaxe Mountain”**, the fortified underground enrichment complex where Israel assesses Iran moved part of its ~440kg stockpile of 60%-enriched uranium after last year’s fighting, saying a strike will come “probably pretty soon.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Iran “is not meeting its commitments” and is “not serious about talks,” while insisting the US will keep protecting Gulf shipping. Mediators have reportedly floated a 10-day ceasefire; Trump has signaled he’ll decide soon between that and broader escalation. Sources: Al Jazeera & Times of Israel liveblogs, July 21–22. Fast-moving, treat casualty/strike specifics as provisional.

### Lebanon: the first real test of the disarmament deal

A genuinely notable, fresher thread. The IDF began withdrawing from **Zawtar al-Gharbiya**, one of two southern “pilot zones” under the Israel–Lebanon framework signed in Washington last month, and the **Lebanese Armed Forces deployed in**, the first on-the-ground test of a roadmap trading Israeli pullback for Hezbollah disarmament. Lebanese PM Nawaf Salam planted a flag and called it “the beginning of the Israeli withdrawal from our land.” It follows Tuesday’s White House meeting between Trump and President Joseph Aoun, where Trump restored direct US–Lebanon flights after 40+ years and Aoun pledged to end hostility with Israel “forever”, conditioned on a full IDF withdrawal. Israel says troops stay in a 10km security zone until Hezbollah disarms; Hezbollah rejects the deal outright. Source: Times of Israel, July 22.

### Britain’s 7th PM in 10 years

**Andy Burnham**, the Greater Manchester mayor, was confirmed as UK prime minister after **Keir Starmer resigned** (statement outside No. 10 on July 20) under a Labour rebellion driven by the rise of Reform UK and grim local-election results. Burnham met King Charles and was asked to form a government. Remarkable churn for a party that won a landslide barely two years ago. Source: NPR / Al Jazeera, July 19–20.

### Ukraine and Sudan, still grinding

Russia’s bombardment of Ukraine continues, two civilians killed and seven wounded in Donetsk and an Odesa residential building hit on July 21, as Ukraine keeps striking Russia’s oil infrastructure. And the UN’s fact-finding mission, having found “hallmarks of genocide” by the RSF in El-Fasher, warned that **El-Obeid** in North Kordofan is now being encircled the same way and could become “the next crime scene.” Sources: UN News; Al Jazeera. The Sudan inquiry itself dates to early July, included as an ongoing, worsening siege.

## US

### The war’s price tag hits Congress

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told the Senate Appropriations Committee the Iran conflict has now cost **\$37.5 billion**, up from \$29B in May, as senators pressed him over fresh funding requests, setting up an appropriations fight over an open-ended war. Source: CNN, July 21.

### A tariff cliff on Friday

The temporary **10% Section 122 tariff**, the Trump administration’s workaround after the Supreme Court struck down its sweeping IEEPA tariffs in February, expires at 5pm ET **Friday, July 24**, with Congress unlikely to act. USTR Jamieson Greer teased new trade action “soon,” and European officials reportedly expect to accept a fresh US tariff regime rather than fight it. Watch this one; it’s a real near-term market and trade-policy inflection. Sources: CNBC; Euronews, July 21–22.

### Netanyahu and the Mamdani flap

Trump declared that Netanyahu “will not be arrested, in any way, shape, or form” when he visits for the UN General Assembly in September, pushing back on NYC mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani’s stated intent to explore an arrest. A preview of a live tension between the incoming NYC administration and Washington. Source: Times of Israel, July 21.

## Israel

Beyond the war, Israeli politics is heating up into the **Oct 27 election**. The security cabinet held a six-hour meeting and emerged notably **wary of rejoining the Iran campaign**, with officials citing past experience of being pushed by Washington to stop after only a few days. President Herzog gave a televised address warning against political violence, “Elections are not a civil war”, and flagged threats to electoral integrity. Netanyahu drew fresh outrage over an **AI-generated attack ad** critics say mocked the slain son of former IDF chief Gadi Eisenkot. On the ground, the IDF reported killing Islamic Jihad operatives moving weapons in southern Gaza. And a US–Saudi civilian nuclear pact that Trump wants Congress to approve, reportedly without full safeguards, is alarming Israeli officials, one of whom vowed to block it. Source: Times of Israel liveblogs, July 21–22.

## Economic & Markets

**Oil is the tell.** Crude jumped roughly 4% early Wednesday to six-week highs, Brent around \$93–95, WTI up ~4% to the high-\$80s, after Rubio’s “not serious about talks” line and the 11th night of strikes revived Strait of Hormuz supply fears. US futures slipped (Nasdaq 100 −0.7%, S&P −0.3%) into the open, giving back some of Tuesday’s chip-led rally (Nasdaq +1.3%, S&P +0.9%, Dow +0.7%, snapping a three-day slide). The main event is tonight: **Alphabet and Tesla** report, the first two of the “Magnificent Seven”, with the market fixated on whether Google’s AI monetization justifies its surging capex. Earnings season is running hot so far, ~88% of reporters beating. Separately, and relevant to your world: **Anthropic reportedly agreed to a ~€1.3B copyright settlement**, described as the largest of its kind on record, a marker for how AI-training liability is starting to get priced. Sources: Yahoo Finance, TipRanks, SWI/Bloomberg, Euronews, July 21–22.
