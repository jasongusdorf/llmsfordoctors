## International

### UAE severs trade with Iran after alleged missile attack

The UAE Defence Ministry said its air defences detected two ballistic missiles launched from Iran on Tuesday, one landing inside Emirati territorial waters and one outside, both in the sea, and assessed they had targeted maritime traffic. The Foreign Ministry then suspended "all trade activities, commercial exchanges and financial transactions with Iran until further notice." Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the accusation baseless, with Iranian officials floating a false-flag theory. This matters more than another round of US sanctions would: Dubai is estimated to supply roughly a third of Iran's annual imports and has long been the main artery for sanctions evasion. Flag: the missile attribution rests entirely on UAE government statements; no independent verification. Al Jazeera, Reuters via Times of Israel, Bloomberg, Aug 18-19. [Open →](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/uae-imposes-indefinite-trade-embargo-on-iran-over-alleged-missile-attacks)

### The deadline lapses, and Trump reaches for the screws

The 60-day clock set by the Versailles agreement Trump signed in June expired Monday Aug 17 with no nuclear accord. The sticking points remain management of the Strait of Hormuz and the disposition of frozen Iranian funds. Trump has rejected an extension, told Tehran to "put up the white flag," and denied this week that any talks are underway; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says it is Washington that is "begging," and that Iran will accept a full end to the war but not a ceasefire. Treasury Secretary Bessent promised measures "like have never been seen in the history of economic isolation on a country," with rollout expected this week. As of writing the package has not been unveiled. The NYT's Steven Erlanger makes the obvious point that Iran has absorbed decades of sanctions and is likelier to escalate than fold. NBC, CNN, Al Jazeera, NYT, Aug 17-19. [Open →](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/iran/60-day-deadline-iran-peace-deal-expiring-s-things-stand-rcna592875)

### Hormuz still a trickle

Weekly confirmed transits fell 19.5% to 95; six vessels crossed on Tuesday, down from nine the day before, against 31 on a comparable weekend earlier in the year. Brent settled just above \$91 on Aug 18 and pushed toward \$92 Wednesday, a fourth straight gain, well off the wartime peak near \$126 but far above pre-war levels. Trump maintains the strait is open, mines cleared, and under US control; shipping and insurance behaviour says otherwise. Hegseth said last week the blockade can be sustained indefinitely through ship rotation. RTÉ, Al Jazeera, OilPrice, Aug 12-19. [Open →](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0819/1588442-middle-east/)

### FT: Iran has looked at European targets

The FT reported Wednesday that Iranian forces have assessed potential strikes on US assets in southeastern Europe if Trump escalates, naming Bulgaria's Bezmer air base (approved last month for US refuelling aircraft) and Cyprus, where a British base was hit by a drone in March. Tehran has also reportedly examined attacking subsea fibre-optic cables in the Strait. Flag hard: this rests on two unnamed people close to the regime, and every other outlet carrying it is citing the FT rather than reporting independently. The FT's own piece quotes analysts doubting Iran's medium-range accuracy against European targets. This is contingency planning, not imminent action. FT exclusive, Aug 19. ### Ukraine: Fedorov breaks with Zelensky

Mykhailo Fedorov, 35, sacked as defence minister in mid-July after roughly six months, released a video calling for elections despite martial law, the sharpest internal challenge to Zelensky since the invasion began. "Democracy cannot be held hostage by Russia," he said, arguing Ukraine must find a legal, safe and realistic mechanism to renew its mandate. His firing, attributed to a feud with commander-in-chief Oleksandr Syrskyi, set off weeks of street protests. Zelensky's office had not responded publicly. CBS, CNN, UPI, Aug 18-19. ### Trump cuts South Korean drills, citing Seoul's refusal to help on Iran

Trump ordered the Pentagon to scale back "inappropriate and hostile" exercises with Seoul, citing his relationship with Kim Jong Un and, pointedly, South Korea's refusal to assist in the war with Iran. ### Venezuela quietly abandons Maduro

The FT reports that across nearly two weeks of government-opposition negotiations this month, Venezuela's interim government made no demand for Maduro's release, the clearest signal yet that Caracas has written him off to the US justice system. He and Cilia Flores were seized in a January military raid and are held in Brooklyn on drug and weapons trafficking charges. It is a sharp turn for interim president Delcy Rodríguez, who in January called the capture "vile aggression." The first round of talks closed Aug 13 with agreements on judicial reform and frozen funds; 131 political prisoners were freed Aug 15. Flag: the central claim is single-outlet. ### China: a funeral, a comedian, and a mood

Zhu Rongji, the reformist premier of 1998-2003, was buried at Babaoshan on Tuesday, aged 98, with Xi and the full Politburo Standing Committee bowing three times and flags at half-mast from Tiananmen to Macau. Public mourning was censored and security was heavy around Zhongnanhai, Beijing remembers what happened after Zhou Enlai in 1976 and Hu Yaobang in 1989, and nostalgia for Zhu's reform era reads as an implicit verdict on the present. Separately, Guo Degang, the country's best-known crosstalk comedian, is under investigation by Wuhan's culture bureau for improvising a revolutionary song lyric, swapping "all is quiet on Weishan lake" for "all is quiet in the Forbidden City." Tour dates have been cancelled. Underneath both: July CPI rose just 0.5% year-on-year, a six-month low and a tenth straight quarter of deflationary pressure. ## United States

### Canada gets three more days

Hours before a midnight deadline, Trump paused threatened 50% tariffs on roughly \$20B of Canadian imports (Canadian outlets put it nearer C\$28B) through end of day Friday Aug 21, posting that the two countries, "subject to the finalization of documents, have a DEAL!" and that Keystone XL "may be awoken from the grave." The affected goods run from hockey sticks and honey to cement and medical supplies, about 5% of Canadian exports to the US. The legal basis is Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, a Depression-era provision never previously used. Carney, who spoke with Trump twice this week, was notably cooler: substantial progress, but "important work" remains. AP, NBC, NPR, CBC, Aug 18-19. ### Heidi Overton for FDA

Trump is nominating Dr. Heidi Overton, deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, to run the FDA, replacing Marty Makary after a volatile stretch of agency leadership. ## Israel

### A cafe in Gaza, and a peace plan going nowhere

An Israeli strike Tuesday hit a crowded cafe in the port area west of Gaza City, an area now serving as a displacement camp. Hamas-run civil defence and al-Shifa Hospital reported six killed and around fourteen wounded, including a child; those figures are not independently verified. The IDF said it struck a gathering of Hamas Nukhba commanders, a company commander, three platoon commanders and other operatives, one of whom it says took part in Oct 7; Al Jazeera notes no supporting evidence was provided. The timing is the story: it came a day after Jared Kushner ended an unsuccessful regional trip pressing Netanyahu to curb near-daily strikes, and after Trump said on Fox that Israel "should not be striking in Gaza right now." Netanyahu continues to insist Hamas disarm before any IDF withdrawal; Israel holds roughly 60% of the enclave. A separate strike Wednesday killed at least seven in Gaza City. Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, Aug 18-19. [Open →](https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-says-it-hit-meeting-of-elite-hamas-commanders-near-gaza-port-six-reported-dead/)

### Israel strikes Syria, Washington objects out loud

Israel hit the Abu al-Duhur airfield in Idlib province overnight into Tuesday, roughly eight strikes on the runway and storage facilities about 70km from the Turkish border. Netanyahu's office justified it publicly: Syria was on the verge of breaching an agreed security status quo by permitting Turkish troops to deploy at a base near Aleppo, and Israel had warned repeatedly. The strike followed a Turkish military delegation's visit to the site. US envoy Tom Barrack called it "an unnecessary escalation that does not advance regional stability," adding that Ahmed al-Sharaa's government "has neither adopted a predatory posture nor maintained proxy forces." Turkey denies having any presence there. No casualties reported. Public US criticism of an Israeli strike is itself the notable part. NBC, JPost, France 24, Aug 18. ### The West Bank chokehold, with a September deadline

This is structural rather than breaking, but it has a date on it. Israel Discount Bank has told Palestinian counterparts it will terminate correspondent banking services on Sept 1, with Bank Hapoalim following Oct 1, both citing the absence of a permanent state indemnity. Cutting those channels would sever Palestinian banks from the Israeli and international financial systems entirely. Smotrich signed a waiver in July extending indemnity to the end of 2026, but the banks want a binding legislative fix, and the Knesset is now dissolved ahead of the October election. Layered on top: over \$5 billion in clearance revenues withheld for more than a year, unemployment around 35% after 100,000-plus work permits were revoked, and public salaries paid partially and late. Separately, the government opened bidding Wednesday for 1,200 homes in the E1 settlement project. Times of Israel; International Crisis Group, "Chokehold," June 2026. ## Economic & Markets

### The bond market is the story

The S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,691.76, down 0.69%, a third consecutive losing session; the Nasdaq fell between 1.3% and 1.8%. The 30-year Treasury topped 5.33%, its highest since June 2007, before easing to about 5.285%; the 10-year sat near 4.70%. Semiconductors took the worst of it, with the PHLX index off more than 5% and Intel, Marvell, Arm, Coherent and Teradyne each down 7% or more. The mechanism is unglamorous and important: higher discount rates compress the present value of long-duration cash flows, and the AI trade is nothing if not long-duration. This is global, not American, Japan's 10-year hit roughly 2.95%, its highest since 1996, French yields are at 2008 levels, German yields at 2011 levels, and the UK 30-year gilt is near its highest since 1998. FOMC minutes were due at 2pm Wednesday; Chair Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole keynote Aug 27-29. CNBC, BNN Bloomberg, Nikkei Asia, Aug 18-19. [Open →](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/economics/2026/08/18/bond-markets-from-us-to-japan-whacked-as-inflation-and-fiscal-worries-take-hold/)

### Diesel is where the war reaches the American household

Retail diesel is averaging around \$5.40 a gallon, a record for mid-August, and gasoline has crossed \$4, the first time the national average has been above four dollars this late in any year on record. The US diesel crack spread hit an intraday record of \$102.20 a barrel, with futures up 7.4% on the week and inventories near 23-year lows. The drivers stack: Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure, a throttled Hormuz, and roughly 7.5m barrels a day of global refining capacity offline. Diesel is the input price for everything that moves, so this propagates. Brent above \$91. Sources vary between \$5.32 and \$5.40 on retail diesel depending on reference date; EIA's weekly series is the arbiter. FT, Bloomberg, Transport Topics, OilPrice, Aug 12-18. ### UK inflation reaccelerates to 2.9%

July CPI rose to 2.9% year-on-year from a fifteen-month low of 2.6% in June, driven by a 13% increase in the Ofgem energy price cap. Gas prices jumped 14.7% month-on-month, the largest monthly rise since October 2022; electricity rose 3.6%. Core held at 2.6% against 2.5% expected, while services inflation eased to 3.4% from 3.6%, the one encouraging line. The Bank had forecast 2.8% and sees a peak of 3.2% later this year. This July cap was the first to fully pass through wholesale prices hit by the Middle East conflict, which makes it the cleanest available read on how the war transmits into consumer prices in an advanced economy. RTÉ, ITV, Aug 19. [Open →](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0819/1588451-uk-inflation-figures/)

### Unitree's debut, and what it did not lift

Unitree Robotics opened Wednesday on the Shanghai STAR Market at ¥1,100, up 629% from its ¥150.80 IPO price, briefly valuing the humanoid robot maker around ¥445bn, or US\$66bn. ## Worth reading

### Rachel Aviv, "The Children a Scientist Took Home", The New Yorker

The one to read. Carleton Gajdusek won the 1976 Nobel for his work on kuru, the fatal spongiform encephalopathy confined to the eastern highlands of Papua New Guinea, work that opened the entire prion field. He also brought dozens of children from Papua New Guinea and Micronesia back to live with him in the United States, and was later convicted of child abuse. Aviv opens on Luwi Ikabala, fifty-seven, hemming sleeves at a market in Goroka on an overturned cooking-oil canister, reading Dostoyevsky between customers and keeping the journal Gajdusek told him to keep forty years ago. "I am the son of one of the world's greatest scientists," he says. The piece sits exactly on the fault line between what a scientific career produces and what it costs the people it is built on, a research-ethics case study with a human being at the center of it rather than a footnote. [Open →](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/24/the-scientist-who-lived-with-dozens-of-children-from-the-south-pacific)

### Eric Klinenberg, "The End of the European Summer", The New Yorker

Klinenberg wrote the definitive account of the 1995 Chicago heat wave, which established that heat deaths are a function of social infrastructure rather than temperature. Here he turns to European cities racing to retrofit themselves for a climate they were never designed to survive, the stone and glass of the continent's great capitals working as heat traps, and the political question of who gets cooled first. Pairs unhappily with the FT's item on Portuguese men o' war now plaguing warming European beaches. [Open →](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/24/the-end-of-the-european-summer)
