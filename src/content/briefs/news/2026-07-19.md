**TL;DR , ** The US-Iran war took its deadliest turn in weeks: an Iranian missile-and-drone strike on a base in Jordan killed two American troops (one still missing), pushing Washington and Tehran toward wider war as the June ceasefire collapses over the Strait of Hormuz. Russia hammered Kyiv overnight in one of the war’s biggest missile barrages amid a political crisis in Ukraine. At home: a court win for Trump on mail-in voting, storms and wildfire smoke blanketing the East, and markets carrying a growing war premium.

## International

### US and Iran edge toward a wider war after American troops are killed

The most consequential story of the weekend: an Iranian ballistic-missile and drone barrage struck Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan on Friday, killing two US service members, leaving one missing, and wounding four others (since discharged), U.S. Central Command announced Saturday. The deaths bring the American toll in the war with Iran to **16 killed and more than 400 wounded** since fighting began Feb. 28. The U.S. military said it responded by targeting the Iranian forces responsible. Sources: NYT live coverage; CENTCOM via Military Times, Reuters, AP, WashPost.

The strike lands as the interim ceasefire, a 14-point memorandum of understanding the two sides signed in June, unravels. After Iranian attacks on commercial ships, Trump reinstated a U.S. naval **blockade of Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz** on July 14 and threatened to toll passing ships; U.S. strikes this week hit Bandar Abbas, ports, bridges and other infrastructure. Iran calls Hormuz an “inviolable red line” and warns it will strike infrastructure across the Gulf if the campaign continues. Iran’s new supreme leader, **Mojtaba Khamenei**, said the repeated U.S. breaches make Trump’s signature “worthless.” Developing, fast-moving; casualty and strike details still being confirmed. Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera.

### Russia unleashes one of the war’s largest missile assaults on Kyiv

Overnight into Sunday, Russian missiles battered Kyiv, killing at least one person and injuring nine as fires broke out at a dormitory, a residential block and a supermarket, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said. It followed Ukrainian long-range drone strikes on Russian oil depots and warehouses that killed at least eight or nine people. The exchange comes amid a genuine **political crisis in Kyiv**: mass protests erupted after President Zelensky dismissed 35-year-old defense minister Mykhailo Fedorov, a symbol of Ukraine’s drone campaign, and reshuffled his government. Separately, Ukraine says it has effectively cut Crimea’s fuel and power by striking Russia’s “shadow fleet” tankers. NYT Russia-Ukraine coverage; Reuters; Al Jazeera; SBS.

## US

### Court win for Trump on mail-in voting; election-security push continues

The administration won a temporary victory in a court battle over mail-in voting, per network reporting, coming just after Trump’s prime-time address to the nation alleging foreign (Chinese) interference in U.S. elections. Fact-checkers and analysts note the documents released to support the President’s claims did not back his most aggressive statements. ABC/CBS evening broadcasts; NYT.

### NYT: Trump gutted a civil-rights office that scrutinized contractor bias

A New York Times investigation reports that a little-known federal office that spent decades investigating potential workplace discrimination by government contractors was effectively shut down by the Trump administration, sharply curtailing federal scrutiny of employment bias. NYT (10-min read).

### Immigration cases falter in court

The Times reports that criminal cases the administration ordered against ICE protesters and immigrants are “crumbling” in court, with prosecutors fleeing the U.S. attorney’s office in Chicago over the cases they’ve been told to bring. A parallel piece notes many “sanctuary” states still cooperate with deportations despite Trump’s attacks. NYT.

### Severe weather and wildfire smoke across the East

Roughly 100 million Americans were under alert for severe storms and flash flooding, with water rescues underway, even as smoke from Canadian wildfires choked Northeast cities. Forecasters expected incoming storms to bring some relief from the smoke. ABC World News Tonight; CBS.

## Israel

### Netanyahu: Gaza no longer a military threat, but Hamas rule not yet dismantled

Netanyahu said Gaza no longer poses a military threat to Israel while conceding Hamas’s civilian rule remains in place, under the second phase of Trump’s 20-point plan (Hamas to cede power; Gaza to be disarmed with an International Stabilization Force). Israel controls roughly 70% of the Strip; U.S.-brokered talks remain deadlocked, and Netanyahu has warned there will be no reconstruction if Hamas does not disarm. JNS; BBC; Jerusalem Post; The Hill.

### Israel heads to Oct. 27 elections

Israel’s next national election is set for October 27, the latest legally permissible date and the first vote since the Oct. 7, 2023 attacks and the ensuing wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. Polling suggests Netanyahu’s coalition would fall short, though rivals lack a clear path to a majority. Times of Israel via ABC (Australia).

## Economic & Markets

### War premium: oil elevated, equities soft

The Hormuz blockade and widening strikes keep a risk premium in crude, WTI has traded in the low-\$80s and is sharply higher on the year. U.S. equities slipped Friday: the S&P 500 closed around **7,458 (-1.0%)** and the Nasdaq was off about 1.4%. The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, core PCE, remains elevated at **3.4%**, above the 2% target, complicating the rate-cut debate. Note: intraday oil quotes across data providers were inconsistent this morning; treat exact levels as approximate. MarketWatch; WSJ market data; Barchart.
