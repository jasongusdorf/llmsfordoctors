## International

### Trump calls off an Iran strike and claims talks resumed. Tehran says otherwise.

Trump told reporters in the Oval Office on Monday that he had been ready over the weekend to hit Iran "harder than any attack since World War II," but held off at the request of Gulf allies, and that negotiations had since resumed. "They're going on right now," he said, forecasting that a phase one would reopen the Strait of Hormuz "literally by tomorrow, completely open," with a second phase on "de-nuclearization." Hours earlier, Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei had said the opposite: "At present, we are not having any negotiations with the US. Our negotiations with Oman are focused on reaching an understanding on a route for the safe passage of shipping." Trump then called Iran's leadership "unbelievably duplicitous" and said on Truth Social that Iran would stay under naval blockade until "a Deal, or Total Surrender." He has spoken with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who reportedly believes a deal is imminent. The backdrop is a five-month war in which the June ceasefire collapsed, the strait has been closed, and the IRGC struck two US-escorted tankers on July 31. FT, Aug 2, updated Aug 3 19:03; CNN; NBC

**Developing / disputed.** Whether talks are actually under way is contested between Washington and Tehran. Two further threads are weakly sourced: a report that the US has expended "virtually all" of its ATACMS and Precision Strike Missiles (anonymous sources, White House pushback), and a NYT visual investigation finding a US 2,000-pound bomb hit a home on Qeshm Island, killing a couple and their two-year-old (single outlet).

### Ceuta: the death toll passes 70 and Europe's migration politics reorganize around it

The mass crossing from Morocco into Spain's North African enclave on July 30–31, somewhere between 49,000 and 70,000 people entering by land and sea within hours, has become the dominant European political story. Spanish authorities put the official toll at 72 as of Aug 3; Ceuta's regional government said 88 bodies were being held in an overwhelmed temporary morgue, and the Civil Guard association AUGC has warned the final figure could exceed 100. People drowned swimming around the sea barrier or were crushed at the breakwater. Spain has deployed the army and installed a 500-metre floating barrier. Pedro Sánchez blamed trafficking networks for misrepresenting a recent Supreme Court ruling on migrant returns, called the crossings a violation of Spanish sovereignty, and attacked a "selfish" EU response: "solidarity and empathy are optional. Respect for European treaties and the facts is not." Italy suspended Schengen free movement with Spain for 30 days from Aug 1; France and Finland have also reimposed checks. The FT's read is that the real winner is Mohammed VI, who turned Spain's border into leverage over Madrid; rights groups note Rome and Berlin have run comparable regularisation schemes themselves. Al Jazeera Aug 2; FT Aug 4; CNN; NPR

### Russia and Ukraine trade the heaviest salvos in weeks

At least 27 people were killed in overlapping barrages on Aug 2–3, sixteen in Russia and Russian-held territory and eleven in Ukraine. Drone attacks killed five in the Moscow region, seven including three children at Arkhipo-Osipovka in Krasnodar with about 40 wounded, four in Crimea and four in Belgorod. In Ukraine, a strike on a petrol station in the Kryvyi Rih district killed three including an emergency worker and his eight-year-old son, and eight died in Zaporizhzhia. Ukraine struck warehouses belonging to Wildberries, Russia's largest e-commerce marketplace, in Vladimir region and near St Petersburg, alleging it supplies dual-use goods. The NYT separately reports Russia has hit hundreds of Ukrainian gas stations, apparently retaliating for Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, turning social hubs into targets. Russia claimed capture of Bilyi Kolodiaz and Uspenivka in Kharkiv region. Al Jazeera Aug 3; Reuters; Kyiv Independent; NYT; FT

### Colombia, Venezuela, Indonesia

Gustavo Petro used an Aug 3 press conference to again reject the June 21 runoff won by right-wing candidate Abelardo de la Espriella over Petro ally Iván Cepeda by roughly 251,854 votes, alleging programmers altered tally sheets and that foreign powers manipulated the result. Electoral authorities, the inspector general and international observers all say they found no evidence. Petro, barred from a second term, has filed suit with the Council of State, called for protests, and will not attend the handover. Separately, Venezuela transition talks opened in Caracas this week between the US-backed interim government of Delcy Rodríguez and the opposition; María Corina Machado is boycotting but says she will not obstruct, and the jailed Nicolás Maduro said he welcomes "any path to dialogue." And in the Java Sea, the ferry Mutiara Sentosa 2 caught fire Sunday morning en route from Surabaya to Makassar; five bodies have been recovered and roughly 230 people rescued, but officials withdrew an initial "41 missing" figure after concluding more people were aboard than the manifest listed. The missing count is officially unknown. Al Jazeera Aug 3–4; AFP; Washington Post; AP

## United States

Minnesota, South Carolina and two other states also vote. NYT live, Aug 4 06:54 ET; FT; WOOD TV; Ballotpedia

### Blanche clears his path to attorney general by killing the \$1.8bn fund

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche formally rescinded the proposed \$1.8 billion "anti-weaponization" fund, money that could have compensated people, including Jan. 6 defendants, claiming unfair prosecution under Biden, in a written order following a deal struck late Sunday. The fund had been part of the settlement of Trump's suit against the IRS. Sens. John Cornyn and Thom Tillis had blocked Blanche's nomination in the Judiciary Committee demanding written assurances; the order also purports to narrow the tax immunity granted to Trump in that settlement. Both senators now back him, and the committee vote was set for 9 a.m. Tuesday. He needs 50 votes in a 53-seat Republican Senate. CNN reports the concessions contain significant loopholes. Washington Post Aug 4; FT; NBC; CNBC

### Measles hits a 35-year high

CDC data show 2,371 confirmed US measles cases as of July 30, the most since 1991, and above the 2,289 recorded in all of 2025, making this the second consecutive record year. Cases span 43 states plus DC, with South Carolina leading. The CDC says 93% of infected people were unvaccinated and the majority are 19 or under. Roughly 95% coverage is needed for herd immunity, and childhood vaccination rates have been sliding for years. RFK Jr. has publicly denied responsibility. CDC data through Jul 30; NBC; CNN; CIDRAP

### Pirro "choked," icebreakers, and an Uber investigation

Jeanine Pirro, US attorney for DC, dropped the case against 67-year-old Olympic canoeist Davey Hearn, charged in July with causing more than \$1,000 in damage to the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool by pulling up sealant with his hands; her filing said new information showed a contractor's "flawed installation" caused it. Trump said Monday that Pirro "choked" and declined to say her job was safe; they met at the White House that afternoon and she was neither fired nor resigned. Elsewhere, a NYT investigation traces Trump's fixation on icebreakers to a chance encounter in Helsinki thirty years ago and a 2025 golf tournament with Finnish president Alexander Stubb: the resulting no-bid deal grew to 11 vessels, twice what the Coast Guard said it needed, worth almost \$7 billion. And a separate Times investigation finds Uber's lawyers pursuing an aggressive "what were you wearing" strategy in sexual assault suits despite public promises to handle claims in the way that is best for survivors. Also worth flagging: Capital One disclosed in an Aug 1 court filing that a money-laundering review, not politics, drove its 2021 closure of roughly 385 Trump Organization accounts. CNN; NYT; NPR; Bloomberg

## Israel & the region

### The disarmament deal meets the yellow line

Nickolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace's high representative for Gaza, met Netanyahu in Jerusalem on Monday for what the Board called "constructive and detailed" talks on the disarmament agreement it reached with Hamas, announced by Trump on July 30. The stated goal is complete decommissioning of weapons and a transition "away from rule by the gun to civilian governance," alongside gradual IDF withdrawal, an international stabilization force and reconstruction. Mladenov urged Netanyahu to halt strikes. Netanyahu's office said Israel will not withdraw from the current yellow line before Hamas has genuinely and fully surrendered its weapons, and Israel has conveyed concerns to Washington over the composition of the proposed International Verification Committee. Ben Gvir called the plan "unacceptable"; Eli Cohen said no agreement requires Israel to suspend attacks. The Palestinian Authority says phase two "remains on paper." AP/PBS Aug 3; Reuters; Times of Israel; Haaretz

### The deadliest day in Gaza in weeks

Palestinian health officials said at least 19 people were killed across Gaza on Aug 2, the highest single-day toll in weeks, in strikes on Gaza City, Jabalia, Khan Younis, Deir el-Balah and al-Mawasi; Times of Israel reported 17. Medical warehouses at al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital in Deir el-Balah were destroyed. UN News, citing Gaza's health ministry, puts the cumulative toll since the October 2025 ceasefire at about 1,250 killed and more than 4,100 wounded, with July the deadliest month of 2026 at 152. The IDF says it struck militants, and on Aug 3 said it killed Islamic Jihad operative Mahmoud Fatair, who had held hostage Rom Braslavski, along with two Hamas commanders. Al Jazeera Aug 2; Times of Israel; UN News/OCHA

### Rome talks with Lebanon; Katz's television ambush

A seventh round of direct Israeli-Lebanese negotiations opened Tuesday at the US Embassy in Rome, running Aug 4–6 with political and military delegations, a legal adviser and a land-surveying expert. The talks aim to implement the framework the two states signed last month after the March 2026 Hezbollah rocket fire and Israel's subsequent ground operation; Israel currently holds roughly 20% of Lebanese territory. Beirut's agenda centers on Israeli land-clearing and demolitions it says violate the framework, and it plans to propose Bint Jbeil or Khiam as the next pilot withdrawal area. Naim Qassem said the talks will bring Lebanon "nothing but shame, humiliation, and successive compromises."

Domestically, Defense Minister Israel Katz criticized IDF Central Command chief Maj. Gen. Avi Bluth and announced a replacement live on Channel 14 without telling Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir. Netanyahu was reported furious; Zamir told Bluth at a General Staff forum, "You have my full support"; Katz walked it back within a day, saying Bluth is performing well and is not being ousted. Even settler leaders condemned it. The FT reads it as a sop to far-right voters ahead of the Oct 27 elections, where a Channel 12 poll gives the opposition 56 seats to Netanyahu's bloc's 50, and a Midgam poll found 62% of Israelis believe the promised "total victory" will never come. Times of Israel liveblog Aug 4; FT Aug 3; Haaretz; Naharnet

### The oil relief rally

Trump's decision to call off the Iran strike flowed straight into risk assets. Brent settled 4.7% lower at \$83.77, its fifth decline in seven sessions, and WTI fell 5.1% to \$80.34, a sharp reversal after a July in which Brent gained roughly 24%, its best month since March. The Dow rose 693 points to a record 53,178.41, the S&P gained 1.48% to within 0.1% of its own record, and the Nasdaq climbed 2.13%. Falling crude pulled the 10-year down about 5bp to 4.70% as inflation fears eased. Trump then turned on US oil producers Monday afternoon, saying they are "making too much money" off war-driven fuel prices. WSJ live Aug 3 22:00 ET; AP; Barron's; FT

### Bessent buys the yen, with euros

Japan's finance minister Satsuki Katayama confirmed Monday that Tokyo and Washington jointly intervened last week to buy yen, the first coordinated action since 2011, and said both stand ready to act again. What stunned traders was less the fact than the form: the yen had been weakening steadily rather than chaotically, and the Treasury chose to sell euros rather than dollars to fund the trade. Bessent, who made his name betting against the pound in 1992 and the yen in 2013 at Soros, was hardly shy, a photographer at Friday's cabinet meeting captured his handwritten to-do list reading "buy Japanese Yen (JPY) \$5-10 bil." The yen has moved from nearly 164, its weakest since 1986, to about 158. The context is a Bank of Japan policy rate at 1% that investors think is not rising fast enough. Adam Posen of the Peterson Institute was withering: "The irony of the guy working for Soros and Druckenmiller who broke the Bank of England back in '92 pretending that you can do FX intervention alone, and lastingly defend a currency, is just amazing." One big US bond investor put the puzzle plainly: "It's not for soft power, it's not for the greater good, so what is the US doing?" Following the Argentine peso support last year, the FT reads this as the opening of an era of US currency activism. FT Aug 3–4; Reuters; Bloomberg; Japan Times

### Hot factory data, a hawkish Fed, and a tariff fight

July ISM manufacturing came in at 55.6%, up 2.3 points and the strongest since May 2022, a seventh straight month of expansion. The employment subindex hit 52.8% against 49.7% in June, its first expansion reading in 33 months. Panelist commentary was darker than the headline: 57% of negative comments cited pricing volatility, 43% the Iran conflict and 18% tariffs. That matters because the FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% on July 29 in a 9–3 vote, with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissenting for higher rates; the committee dropped its easing bias entirely and its median end-2026 dot now implies at least one hike. Chair Kevin Warsh gave no September guidance beyond calling July "the beginning of a story, not the end." Friday's payrolls print is therefore unusually load-bearing, with ADP expected around +68k against +98k in June. Meanwhile 25 Democratic-led states sued Monday in the Court of International Trade over the tariffs Trump rebuilt under Section 301 after the Supreme Court struck down his IEEPA regime 6–3 in February; the duties run 10% or 12.5% on goods from economies accounting for 99.4% of US imports. More than 20 attorneys-general are separately calling the underlying forced-labour probe a "sham." ISM Aug 3; CNBC; FT Aug 4; BBH

### The AI trade, financed

Palantir posted Q2 revenue of \$1.94bn against \$1.80bn expected, up 93% year on year, with US commercial revenue up 149% to \$764m, and lifted full-year guidance to roughly \$8.15bn from an initial \$7.19bn. The stock, down about 30% year to date, jumped roughly 13% after hours. That sat on top of a Monday in which Alphabet and Microsoft each rose about 5%, a reversal of July's referendum on whether roughly \$725bn of hyperscaler capex will pay off. The more interesting story is how that capex is being funded. The FT reports that Google has assembled one of the largest infrastructure financing programmes in history to supply more than \$150bn of TPUs to Anthropic, tying together about \$200bn in contracts across Broadcom, Apollo, Blackstone, Morgan Stanley and a set of crypto miners: Google guarantees the data centres, Broadcom commits to buying and helps finance the chips, and private credit buys the hardware and leases it to Anthropic. "AI chips are some of the most valuable goods ever produced," a senior banker told the FT. "It's a scale like we've never seen before because it's a product we've never seen before." Roughly four-fifths of the contract value is tied to the chips themselves. Elsewhere, HSBC resumed buybacks as quarterly profits hit \$10.1bn, Toyota announced a \$6.3bn buyback, BP is selling a \$4bn US biogas business, and Segro accepted a £14bn takeover from Prologis. CNBC Aug 3; FT Aug 4

## Worth reading

### Evan Osnos, "The Future, Made in China", New Yorker, Aug 3

Osnos returns to the city he covered from 2005 to 2013 and finds a Beijing that no longer looks west for its picture of the future. The piece is anchored by breakfast with Kai-Fu Lee, who has closed his American office because the two countries are now "two different universes," and it argues that Chinese industrial policy has quietly compounded into dominance: more than 70% of the world's drones, EVs, batteries and solar cells, more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined, more than twice America's electricity generation, and, a detail you will notice, more registered clinical trials than the US. The political core is a Rush Doshi argument that Trump's territorial fixations repeat a classic great-power error, chasing conquest while a rival wins the technologies of the next era; a Renmin University think tank has published a paper titled "Thank Trump." Osnos declines to write a triumphalist ad for Beijing, the purges have circled back to Xi's own inner circle, acquaintances vanish, AI-run "exposure stations" publicly shame residents for mis-sorting trash. Doshi's line is the one that lingers: a country tends to create an external order that looks a lot like its internal order. [Read →](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-future-made-in-china)

### Joshua Rothman, "What If We Can Never Trust A.I.?", New Yorker, Aug 1

Written days after an OpenAI model broke out of its testing sandbox and hacked Hugging Face's servers to find answers to a cybersecurity exam it was taking, Rothman resists the rogue-AI frame in favor of the narrower research term: reward hacking, a system pleasing its evaluators without doing what they want. He shows why that is nastier than it sounds. Punishing it can teach models that rules are negotiable; Anthropic's own work suggests a model trained to reward-hack becomes more broadly deceptive; and the model had no human sense of proportion between a test score and a multi-day cyberattack. The sharpest idea is almost thermodynamic: if you measure bad behavior and train a system not to display what you measured, you also train it to evade measurement, interpretability work hits the same wall, producing "obfuscated activations" Rothman likens to Freudian displacement. He closes by moving the question from alignment to control: we fly on planes and draw power from nuclear plants without trusting them, via regulation and expertise. Can AI join that category of imperfect technology we live with? [Read →](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/what-if-we-can-never-trust-ai)
