## International

**Trump's Iran deal starts to wobble within days.** Switzerland announced it had postponed a round of US-Iran talks that had been set for Friday, raising immediate questions over the fate of the 14-point memorandum of understanding Trump signed this week at Versailles during the G7. The MOU promises safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz (free for 60 days, then Oman-brokered administration), removal of "all types" of US sanctions, unfreezing of Iranian assets, lifting of the naval blockade, and a roughly \$300bn reconstruction plan, in exchange for Iran reaffirming it "shall not procure or develop nuclear weapons," with the enriched-uranium stockpile mechanism left to a 60-day negotiating window and a binding UN Security Council resolution. Iran's parliament speaker Ghalibaf said any talks remain bound by Tehran's "red lines." Sources: NYT (Jim Tankersley, ~4h before send), Times of Israel liveblog, CNBC, Al Jazeera, CFR. Still developing.

**Ukraine lands its biggest strike on Moscow of the war.** Overnight into June 18, Ukraine launched what Russian authorities and Western outlets call the largest drone offensive on the capital since the full-scale invasion began. A key oil refinery in Moscow's Kapotnya district was hit for the second time in a week, with at least five fires, temporary airport closures, and 17 people injured (including two children). Russia's MoD claimed it downed some 555 drones nationwide; figures for Moscow proper run ~190+. NYT framed it as intensifying "Ukrainian hopes of bringing the war to Russia," while cautioning it is too soon to call a turning point. Zelensky said "it's time the war ended." Sources: NYT news analysis (Siobhan O'Grady), CNN, Kyiv Independent, ABC News. Casualty/drone counts are Russian-stated and vary by source.

**Deadly assault on Niger's main airport.** Gunmen attacked Diori Hamani International Airport in Niamey and an adjacent military base, with fighting lasting up to two hours. Niger's defense ministry reported 11 soldiers and 2 civilians killed, 22 attackers killed, and ~20 suspects arrested. Flag: attribution and toll disputed, al-Qaeda's Sahel branch JNIM claimed a "suicide attack," but at least one report cites an Islamic State claim, and unofficial counts (24 soldiers plus Russian mercenaries) are higher and unconfirmed. Sources: Al Jazeera, BSS, NewsCord. Developing; figures not yet reconciled.

**Switzerland rejects a population cap.** Swiss voters defeated a ballot proposal to cap the country's population at 10 million, an anti-immigration measure pushed by the right. Source: FT front. (Single-outlet snapshot from FT home; result reported as final.)

## US

**Obama Center finally opens in Chicago.** Thursday's opening of Barack Obama's long-delayed presidential center drew four former presidents, along with Bruce Springsteen, Bono, and Jennifer Hudson, capping years of planning and legal fights on the South Side. Source: NYT (Julie Bosman), ~9h before send.

**Iran war's bill comes due in Congress.** Two related NYT pieces this morning: Republicans and Democrats alike are questioning Trump's push for the largest military budget in history, in part because the administration won't disclose what the Iran war actually cost; and a separate accounting tallies "thousands of lives and billions of dollars" since the US and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28. The fiscal and human reckoning is becoming a domestic political problem even as the shooting (mostly) stops. Sources: NYT (Catie Edmondson; Edward Wong & Aruni Soni), June 19.

## Israel

**Lebanon flares, testing the deal's ceasefire.** Four Israeli soldiers were killed overnight in southern Lebanon when an IDF tank was hit by a "suspicious target" just past midnight local time. Netanyahu says he ordered strikes on dozens of Hezbollah targets in response. The exchange directly pressures the US-Iran agreement, which calls for fighting to stop on all fronts, including Lebanon. Sources: NYT (Abdi Latif Dahir), Times of Israel, ABC News. Cross-confirmed.

**Israel seethes over the deal; Netanyahu boxed in.** Israeli officials, opposition figures, and commentators have blasted the preliminary US-Iran agreement as a strategic and political disaster, one that, critics like Yair Golan argue, funnels billions to Tehran, leaves nuclear infrastructure intact, and preserves the ballistic-missile threat. Netanyahu, who promised "total victory," was conspicuously silent as Trump signed, and now stands largely isolated internationally (even the UAE has joined the regional consensus backing the deal) with an election roughly four months away. Sources: Axios, Bloomberg, PBS NewsHour, Times of Israel. Cross-confirmed.

## Economic & Markets

**AI keeps eating the market.** FT's lead markets story: Wall Street is digesting a record fundraising haul as the AI capital race intensifies, Alphabet plans an ~\$80bn equity raise (including a reported \$10bn from Berkshire Hathaway) to fund its buildout, while Anthropic moves toward an IPO at a reported ~\$350bn valuation and OpenAI is expected to file as well. Context worth keeping in view: AI names now make up roughly 45% of the S&P 500's market cap, and the index's post-2024 surge is ~142% with them versus just ~16% without. Concentration risk is the obvious flip side of the rally. Sources: FT front; CNN/CNBC; analyst data. The 142%/16% split is a widely-cited estimate, not an official index figure.

**China moves on dollar dominance.** FT reports Beijing is teeing up a digital payments system designed to compete with the dollar, another step in the slow campaign to internationalize the yuan and route trade around US financial rails. Source: FT front.

**Markets snapshot (FT, at read time):** S&P 500 +0.50%, FTSE 100 +1.63%, Shanghai +1.12%; Brent crude −0.61% (the Hormuz reopening is easing the oil risk premium); US 10-year yield +0.65%. Source: FT live ticker.
