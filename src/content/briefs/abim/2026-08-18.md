Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. Constriction vs restriction: constriction has ventricular interdependence, respirophasic septal shift/bounce, discordant RV-LV systolic pressures on cath, expiratory hepatic vein diastolic flow reversal, and *annulus reversus* (medial e' ≥ lateral e'). BNP is usually only mildly elevated in constriction and markedly high in restriction. A normal-thickness pericardium does not exclude constriction, up to ~20% have no thickening. Constriction is surgically curable; that's why the distinction is tested.
2. **Pulmonary**. ABPA in an asthmatic or CF patient: total IgE >1000 IU/mL, positive Aspergillus fumigatus–specific IgE or skin test, peripheral eosinophilia, and central/proximal bronchiectasis with mucus plugging. Treat with systemic glucocorticoids ± itraconazole (check levels and interactions), antifungal monotherapy and inhaled steroids alone are the wrong answers. Follow total IgE to gauge response.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Eosinophilic esophagitis: dysphagia to solids and food impaction in a young atopic man; diagnosis is ≥15 eosinophils/hpf on biopsies from proximal AND distal esophagus. A PPI trial is no longer required to exclude it, PPI responsiveness is a treatment, not a diagnostic criterion. Options are PPI, swallowed topical steroids (budesonide/fluticasone), elimination diet, or dupilumab; dilate strictures only after inflammation is treated.
4. **Infectious disease**. Screen for Strongyloides (serology) before starting glucocorticoids or other immunosuppression in anyone from an endemic area, Latin America, Southeast Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, Appalachia, even decades after exposure. Unrecognized infection plus steroids gives hyperinfection with gram-negative bacteremia and meningitis. Unexplained eosinophilia is the clue; treat with ivermectin.
5. **Rheumatology**. IgG4-related disease: autoimmune pancreatitis with a sausage-shaped pancreas, salivary/lacrimal enlargement, retroperitoneal fibrosis, or a "mass" that mimics malignancy. Serum IgG4 is normal in a substantial minority and is not specific, biopsy showing storiform fibrosis, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, and obliterative phlebitis is what makes it. Dramatic steroid response is characteristic; rituximab for relapse or steroid sparing.
6. **Endocrinology**. Adrenal incidentaloma: every one gets a 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test (cortisol ≤1.8 µg/dL excludes autonomous secretion) and plasma free or 24-h urine metanephrines; add aldosterone/renin only if hypertensive or hypokalemic. Unenhanced attenuation ≤10 HU with a homogeneous appearance is a lipid-rich adenoma and needs no further imaging. Never biopsy an adrenal mass until pheochromocytoma is excluded, and biopsy only changes management if you suspect metastasis.
7. **Hematology**. MGUS risk stratification (Mayo): non-IgG isotype, M-protein ≥1.5 g/dL, and an abnormal serum free light chain ratio. Zero factors → very low risk, and you can follow with periodic labs without a marrow biopsy or skeletal survey. Any risk factor, or IgM MGUS (which progresses to Waldenström/lymphoma, not myeloma), warrants marrow evaluation and imaging.
8. **Neurology**. Triptans are contraindicated in CAD, prior stroke, uncontrolled hypertension, and hemiplegic/basilar migraine, gepants (ubrogepant, rimegepant) are the vascular-safe acute option. Medication-overuse headache: ≥10 days/month for triptans, ergots, opioids, or combination analgesics, ≥15 days/month for simple analgesics, over >3 months. Start prophylaxis around ≥4 headache days/month or disabling attacks.
9. **Women's health**. Combined hormonal contraception is a hard no (CDC MEC category 4) in migraine WITH aura at any age, <21 days postpartum, age ≥35 smoking ≥15 cigarettes/day, prior VTE, known thrombophilia, active breast cancer, and uncontrolled hypertension. Progestin-only pills, implant, and IUDs remain options in nearly all of those. Migraine *without* aura is not an absolute contraindication.
10. **General IM (periop)**. BRIDGE: in AF patients on warfarin undergoing elective procedures, forgoing heparin bridging was noninferior for arterial thromboembolism and halved major bleeding, so most AF patients should NOT be bridged. Bridging is still reasonable for mechanical mitral valves, older caged-ball/tilting-disc aortic valves, recent stroke/TIA, or VTE within the last 3 months. DOACs are simply held by renal function and bleeding risk, bridging a DOAC is always wrong.
