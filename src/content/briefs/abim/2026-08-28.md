Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. AF with preexcitation (WPW): irregular, wide, very fast. AV nodal blockers, adenosine, verapamil/diltiazem, beta-blockers, digoxin, are contraindicated; they accelerate conduction down the accessory pathway and can precipitate VF. Use procainamide, or cardiovert if unstable. Definitive therapy is catheter ablation.
2. **Pulmonary**. Blood eosinophils guide inhaled steroids in COPD: ≥300 cells/µL predicts benefit from adding ICS, <100 predicts little. Escalate to LABA/LAMA/ICS triple therapy in exacerbators with high eosinophils; for the chronic-bronchitis phenotype with FEV1 <50% and recurrent exacerbations despite triple therapy, roflumilast (or azithromycin) is the add-on.
3. **Gastroenterology**. SBP is ascitic PMN ≥250/µL, start cefotaxime/ceftriaxone before culture returns. Add **IV albumin 1.5 g/kg on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3** if creatinine >1 mg/dL, BUN >30, or bilirubin >4, it reduces hepatorenal syndrome and mortality. After one episode, indefinite secondary prophylaxis. Nonselective beta-blockers should be held in refractory ascites or SBP with hypotension.
4. **Infectious disease**. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is treated in exactly two groups: **pregnancy** and **before urologic procedures with anticipated mucosal bleeding**. Not in diabetes, not in the elderly, not in nursing-home residents, not with an indwelling catheter, not in renal transplant beyond the early postoperative period. Pyuria alone is never an indication.
5. **Rheumatology**. Axial spondyloarthritis: inflammatory back pain (age <45 at onset, insidious, morning stiffness >30 min, improves with exercise not rest, night pain). Plain films are normal for years, **MRI of the sacroiliac joints** is the early test. NSAIDs first, then a TNF inhibitor; if there is concurrent uveitis or IBD, choose a monoclonal TNF inhibitor over an IL-17 inhibitor (IL-17 blockade can worsen IBD).
6. **Endocrinology**. Screen for primary aldosteronism in resistant hypertension, hypertension with hypokalemia (spontaneous or diuretic-induced), an adrenal incidentaloma, or hypertension with OSA or early-onset stroke. Use the aldosterone-to-renin ratio, but **correct hypokalemia first** and stop MRAs (spironolactone, eplerenone) for 4–6 weeks, they invalidate the test. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and CCBs can usually stay on.
7. **Hematology**. HIT: platelets fall **>50% from baseline, typically days 5–10** after heparin exposure, and it is a *thrombotic* disorder, bleeding is unusual. Use the 4Ts score, stop all heparin including flushes, and start a non-heparin anticoagulant (argatroban, bivalirudin, or a DOAC). Do not start warfarin until platelets recover above ~150k (venous limb gangrene), and do not transfuse platelets prophylactically.
8. **Oncology**. Universal mismatch-repair IHC or MSI testing on **every** colorectal and endometrial cancer, regardless of age or family history, Amsterdam/Bethesda criteria miss too many. Loss of MLH1 requires BRAF V600F or MLH1 promoter methylation testing to separate sporadic from Lynch. Confirmed Lynch: colonoscopy every 1–2 years beginning age 20–25.
9. **Nephrology**. Chronic hyponatremia: correct no more than ~8 mEq/L in 24 hours (slower with alcohol use, malnutrition, hypokalemia, advanced liver disease). SIADH is euvolemic with urine osmolality >100 mOsm/kg and urine sodium >30 mEq/L, normal thyroid and adrenal function. If you overcorrect, **relowering with D5W ± desmopressin is the right move**, not observation.
10. **Neurology**. Acute ischemic stroke: IV thrombolysis within 4.5 hours; BP must be below 185/110 before giving it and below 180/105 for the next 24 hours. Mechanical thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion out to **24 hours** in selected patients by perfusion or clinical-core mismatch (DAWN, DEFUSE-3). Do not give antiplatelets or anticoagulants for 24 hours after lysis.
