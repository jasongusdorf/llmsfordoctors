1. **Cardiology**. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: ICD is reasonable with any major SCD risk marker: family history of HCM sudden death, unexplained syncope, max LV wall thickness ≥30 mm, NSVT, LV apical aneurysm, EF <50%, or extensive LGE. Avoid dehydration, vasodilators, and aggressive diuretics in obstructive disease; nonvasodilating beta-blocker or verapamil first. Mavacamten (EXPLORER-HCM) for symptomatic obstructive HCM despite that, under REMS with serial echo because it can reversibly drop EF.

2. **Pulmonary**. Parapneumonic effusion needs a chest tube, not just antibiotics, when it is frank pus, Gram stain/culture positive, pH <7.20, glucose <60 mg/dL, or large/loculated. Loculated empyema that will not drain: intrapleural tPA plus DNase together (MIST2): either agent alone does not work. Surgery (VATS decortication) if that fails.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Acetaminophen liver injury: give NAC to anyone with acetaminophen-related hepatotoxicity or acute liver failure regardless of time since ingestion: the Rumack-Matthew nomogram only applies to a single ingestion with a known time, 4–24 h out. King's College criteria for transplant listing: arterial pH <7.3 after resuscitation, or all of INR >6.5 + creatinine >3.4 mg/dL + grade III–IV encephalopathy. Any ALF → early transplant-center transfer.

4. **Infectious disease**. Endemic mycoses in one line each. Histoplasma: Ohio/Mississippi valleys, caves and bird/bat droppings; disseminated disease in AIDS with pancytopenia, hepatosplenomegaly, adrenal involvement; urine/serum Histoplasma antigen is the best test. Blastomyces: Great Lakes/Ohio valley, broad-based budding yeast, lung + verrucous skin + bone. Coccidioides: desert Southwest, erythema nodosum, eosinophilia, serology titers. Mild-moderate → itraconazole (fluconazole for cocci); severe or CNS → liposomal amphotericin. Self-limited acute pulmonary histo needs no treatment.

5. **Rheumatology**. Behçet: recurrent painful oral aphthae plus genital ulcers, papulopustular or erythema nodosum-like lesions, panuveitis/retinal vasculitis, and positive pathergy; Silk Road ancestry, HLA-B51. The trap: its venous thrombosis (DVT, Budd-Chiari, cerebral venous sinus) is vessel-wall inflammation: treat with glucocorticoids plus immunosuppression (azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, anti-TNF); anticoagulation is not the primary answer and is dangerous if pulmonary artery aneurysms are present. Colchicine for mucocutaneous disease; apremilast for oral ulcers.

6. **Endocrinology**. Paget disease of bone: isolated high alkaline phosphatase with normal calcium, phosphate, and GGT (a normal GGT separates it from a hepatobiliary source; bone-specific ALP if unsure). Plain films confirm; bone scan maps extent. Treat with a single IV zoledronic acid infusion (replete vitamin D first to avoid hypocalcemia) when symptomatic, at weight-bearing or periarticular sites, skull/spine involvement, or before orthopedic surgery at the site. Complications: hearing loss, high-output heart failure, rare osteosarcoma.

7. **Hematology**. Hereditary spherocytosis vs warm AIHA: both show spherocytes and reticulocytosis; HS is DAT-negative, warm AIHA is DAT-positive. HS also has a high MCHC, often a family history (autosomal dominant), and is confirmed by EMA-binding flow cytometry (osmotic fragility is the older test). Complications: pigment gallstones, parvovirus B19 aplastic crisis, folate deficiency. Splenectomy for moderate-severe disease, with pneumococcal/meningococcal/Hib vaccination beforehand.

8. **Nephrology**. IgA nephropathy vs post-streptococcal GN by timing and complement: IgAN: gross hematuria within 1–3 days of a URI ("synpharyngitic"), normal C3. PSGN, 1–3 weeks after pharyngitis (longer after skin infection), low C3 that normalizes within ~8 weeks, positive ASO/anti-DNase B. IgAN treatment is maximal RAAS blockade plus an SGLT2 inhibitor; persistent proteinuria >1 g/day after optimized supportive care → consider glucocorticoids (TESTING: reduced-dose regimen works with fewer serious adverse events). Newer: targeted-release budesonide, sparsentan.

9. **Psychiatry**. Bupropion is the switch when an SSRI causes sexual dysfunction or weight gain, and it doubles as smoking-cessation therapy. Contraindicated in seizure disorders, current or prior bulimia or anorexia nervosa, and abrupt alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal: all because seizure risk is dose-dependent. It can raise blood pressure; it has no serotonergic activity.

10. **Dermatology**. Dermatitis herpetiformis: intensely pruritic, symmetric grouped papulovesicles on extensor elbows, knees, buttocks, and scalp, usually excoriated so intact vesicles are absent. Diagnosis is direct immunofluorescence of perilesional skin showing granular IgA in the dermal papillae; tTG-IgA is usually positive because nearly all patients have celiac enteropathy even without GI symptoms. Treatment: gluten-free diet (definitive but takes months) plus dapsone for rapid relief: check G6PD first. Linear IgA on DIF is a different disease (linear IgA bullous dermatosis, classically vancomycin-induced).
