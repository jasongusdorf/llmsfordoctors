Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. Cardiac amyloidosis: LVH on echo with LOW voltage on ECG, apical sparing on strain. A grade 2–3 bone scintigraphy (PYP) scan with a NEGATIVE monoclonal screen (SPEP/UPEP with immunofixation + serum free light chains) diagnoses ATTR without biopsy, but you must exclude AL first, since a positive light chain screen mandates biopsy. Avoid digoxin, CCBs, and aggressive afterload reduction.
2. **Pulmonary**. Pulmonary hypertension is defined hemodynamically by right heart cath, not echo: mPAP >20 mmHg, and precapillary disease requires PCWP ≤15 with PVR >2 Wood units. PAH-specific therapy in group 2 (left heart) or group 3 (lung disease) PH is a classic wrong answer, it can worsen pulmonary edema and V/Q matching.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Acute severe UC: IV steroids, and reassess at day 3. Persistent high stool frequency/CRP → rescue infliximab or cyclosporine, not more steroid days. Give VTE prophylaxis despite the bloody diarrhea (IBD flare is a hypercoagulable state), and check C. diff and CMV. Antidiarrheals and opioids risk toxic megacolon.
4. **Infectious disease**. Candidemia: echinocandin first-line (not fluconazole), remove the central line, repeat blood cultures daily until clearance, treat 14 days from the FIRST negative culture, and get a dilated ophthalmologic exam. Step down to fluconazole only once susceptible and the patient is stable.
5. **Rheumatology**. Any lupus patient with proteinuria ≥0.5 g/day (or an active urine sediment) needs a renal biopsy, class drives therapy and you cannot predict it clinically. Proliferative (III/IV) disease: glucocorticoids plus MMF or low-dose IV cyclophosphamide, increasingly with belimumab or voclosporin added. Hydroxychloroquine goes to essentially every lupus patient, including in pregnancy.
6. **Endocrinology**. Prolactinoma: cabergoline is first-line even for a macroadenoma with chiasmal compression, surgery is for dopamine-agonist failure or intolerance, or apoplexy. Exclude drugs (antipsychotics, metoclopramide), hypothyroidism, renal failure, and stalk effect first. If a huge tumor comes with only a modestly elevated prolactin, suspect the hook effect and ask for a diluted specimen.
7. **Hematology**. von Willebrand disease is the most common inherited bleeding disorder: mucocutaneous bleeding, menorrhagia, normal platelet count, and a PTT that is often NORMAL, a normal screen does not exclude it. DDAVP works for type 1 and is contraindicated in type 2B, where it worsens thrombocytopenia; those patients need VWF concentrate.
8. **Psychiatry**. Clozapine is the only agent proven for treatment-resistant schizophrenia and for reducing suicidality, and it carries the tested toxicities: agranulocytosis requiring serial ANC monitoring, myocarditis in the first ~8 weeks (unexplained tachycardia, fever, eosinophilia, check troponin/CRP), dose-dependent seizures, and severe constipation/ileus that can be fatal. Smoking cessation raises clozapine levels.
9. **Dermatology**. Melanoma prognosis is driven by Breslow depth and ulceration, not diameter. Excisional/full-thickness biopsy, a shave or punch through the middle of a pigmented lesion can under-stage it. Sentinel node biopsy is offered around ≥0.8 mm (or thinner if ulcerated) and recommended for ≥1 mm. Amelanotic and acral lesions are the missed ones.
10. **General IM / Prevention**. Vaccine updates worth knowing: ACIP lowered routine pneumococcal vaccination to age 50 (2024), PCV20 alone, or PCV15 followed by PPSV23. Recombinant zoster (Shingrix) is given regardless of prior shingles or prior live vaccine, and is the immunocompromised-safe option. RSV vaccination is recommended for adults 75+, and 50–74 with risk factors.
