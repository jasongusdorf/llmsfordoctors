Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. Constrictive pericarditis vs restrictive cardiomyopathy: constriction shows respirophasic ventricular interdependence, a septal bounce, expiratory hepatic vein diastolic flow reversal, and *annulus reversus* (medial e' ≥ lateral e'). BNP is usually only modestly elevated in constriction and markedly elevated in restrictive disease. Constriction is surgically curable, pericardiectomy.
2. **Pulmonary**. Nodular-bronchiectatic MAC ("Lady Windermere"): right middle lobe/lingular tree-in-bud and bronchiectasis in a thin older woman. Diagnosis needs compatible imaging plus **two** separate positive sputum cultures (or one bronchoscopic wash). Treat with azithromycin + rifampin + ethambutol **three times weekly**, macrolide monotherapy is the trap, it breeds resistance.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Cholestatic LFTs in a middle-aged woman with fatigue and pruritus: PBC, antimitochondrial antibody, ursodeoxycholic acid 13–15 mg/kg/d first-line. Contrast with autoimmune hepatitis: hepatocellular pattern, ANA/anti-smooth muscle, elevated IgG, interface hepatitis on biopsy, treated with glucocorticoids plus azathioprine.
4. **Infectious disease**. Febrile neutropenia is ANC <500/µL (or expected to fall below) with a single temp ≥38.3°C or ≥38.0°C sustained an hour. Empiric antipseudomonal β-lactam **monotherapy**, cefepime, pip-tazo, or a carbapenem. Adding vancomycin routinely is the wrong answer: reserve it for catheter infection, skin/soft tissue, hemodynamic instability, pneumonia, or known MRSA colonization.
5. **Rheumatology**. Drug-induced lupus: anti-histone antibodies, anti-dsDNA typically **negative**, complements normal, renal and CNS disease rare. Classic culprits: procainamide, hydralazine, minocycline, isoniazid, quinidine. Exception worth knowing, TNF-inhibitor-induced lupus often *does* have anti-dsDNA. Treatment is stopping the drug.
6. **Endocrinology**. Suspected pheochromocytoma: plasma free metanephrines (or 24-h urine fractionated metanephrines), catecholamine levels alone are the weaker test. Then α-blockade (phenoxybenzamine or doxazosin) for ~7–14 days with liberal salt/volume **before** any β-blocker; β-blockade first causes unopposed α-stimulation and hypertensive crisis.
7. **Nephrology**. Recurrent stones: get a 24-hour urine. Thiazide for hypercalciuria, potassium citrate for hypocitraturia, allopurinol for hyperuricosuric calcium oxalate. Uric acid stones are radiolucent on plain film (visible on CT) and can be **dissolved** by alkalinizing urine to pH >6.0–6.5. Restrict sodium and animal protein, do NOT restrict dietary calcium, which increases oxalate absorption.
8. **Oncology**. Carcinoma of unknown primary has curable presentations you must not miss: isolated axillary adenocarcinoma in a woman → treat as stage II breast cancer; papillary peritoneal carcinomatosis in a woman → treat as ovarian; midline poorly differentiated carcinoma in a young man with elevated AFP/β-hCG → treat as extragonadal germ cell tumor with platinum-based therapy.
9. **Women's health**. PCOS is a Rotterdam diagnosis (2 of 3: oligo-ovulation, clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries) *after* excluding thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia, and nonclassic CAH (17-OHP). Screen for glucose intolerance with a 2-hour OGTT. Unopposed estrogen raises endometrial cancer risk, give a COC or cyclic progestin. For ovulation induction, **letrozole beats clomiphene**.
10. **Psychiatry**. The somatic differential in one line each: somatic symptom disorder = real or unreal symptoms with disproportionate thoughts/anxiety/time devoted to them; illness anxiety = preoccupation with *having* a disease, minimal actual symptoms; functional neurological disorder = internal inconsistency on exam (Hoover sign); factitious = deliberate deception with **no** external incentive; malingering = deliberate, with an obvious external incentive.
