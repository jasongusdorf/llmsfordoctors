1. **Cardiology**. Brugada syndrome is a sodium-channel disorder whose diagnostic type 1 pattern is coved ST elevation of at least 2 mm followed by a negative T wave in one or more right precordial leads (V1 to V2). The pattern is dynamic: fever and sodium-channel-blocking drugs (class IC antiarrhythmics, tricyclics, some anesthetics) unmask it and can trigger ventricular fibrillation, so treat fever aggressively and avoid those drugs. An ICD is indicated after aborted cardiac arrest or with arrhythmic syncope; an asymptomatic drug-induced pattern alone is not an ICD indication.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. In massive hemoptysis, the airway kills before blood loss does. Position the patient bleeding side down, secure the airway with a large endotracheal tube, and localize with CT angiography or bronchoscopy. The usual culprit is the high-pressure bronchial arterial circulation rather than the pulmonary arteries, which is why bronchial artery embolization is the first-line definitive intervention, with surgery reserved for failures or specific lesions.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Acute mesenteric ischemia is pain out of proportion to the abdominal exam, classically in an older patient with atrial fibrillation (embolic) or diffuse atherosclerosis and postprandial pain (thrombotic). A normal lactate does not exclude it early; lactate rises once bowel has infarcted. Get CT angiography promptly rather than plain films, start heparin, and involve vascular surgery; peritoneal signs mean laparotomy.

4. **Infectious disease**. A patient with cirrhosis or iron overload who eats raw oysters or gets a wound in warm seawater and then develops septic shock with hemorrhagic bullae has *Vibrio vulnificus* until proven otherwise. Mortality is high and rises with each hour of delay. Treat with doxycycline plus a third-generation cephalosporin such as ceftazidime, and get early surgical debridement of necrotizing soft-tissue infection.

5. **Rheumatology**. Psoriatic arthritis is the seronegative arthritis that hits the distal interphalangeal joints, and it can precede the skin disease, so look in the scalp, umbilicus, gluteal cleft, and nails (pitting, onycholysis). Dactylitis, enthesitis, and asymmetric involvement point away from rheumatoid arthritis, and pencil-in-cup erosions with new bone formation are the radiographic signature. Oral glucocorticoids are generally avoided because tapering can flare the psoriasis.

6. **Endocrinology**. Levothyroxine requirements rise early in pregnancy, so a woman with treated hypothyroidism should increase her dose as soon as pregnancy is confirmed rather than waiting for the next TSH. The American Thyroid Association approach is an increase of roughly 20 to 30%, conveniently done by taking two extra daily doses per week, then checking TSH about every 4 weeks through mid-pregnancy. Unexplained TSH rises outside pregnancy are often absorption: separate levothyroxine from calcium, iron, and PPIs, and take it consistently on an empty stomach.

7. **Hematology**. G6PD deficiency causes episodic intravascular hemolysis after an oxidant stress: infection, fava beans, or drugs such as dapsone, primaquine, and rasburicase. The smear shows bite cells and, with supravital stain, Heinz bodies. A G6PD level measured during or just after a hemolytic episode can be falsely normal because the surviving young red cells and reticulocytes have more enzyme, so repeat it after recovery (roughly 3 months) if the first result is normal.

8. **Oncology**. A painless solid testicular mass in a young man is germ cell cancer until proven otherwise. Confirm with scrotal ultrasound, draw AFP, beta-hCG, and LDH before surgery, and proceed to radical inguinal orchiectomy. Never biopsy or operate through the scrotum, which violates the lymphatic drainage. An elevated AFP excludes pure seminoma, so a "seminoma" with a high AFP is managed as nonseminoma.

9. **Nephrology**. Hypokalemia that will not correct despite adequate potassium replacement usually means concurrent hypomagnesemia, because low intracellular magnesium removes the brake on renal outer medullary potassium (ROMK) secretion. Check and replete magnesium first. Common culprits are diuretics, alcohol use, diarrhea, and long-term proton pump inhibitor therapy, which causes hypomagnesemia through impaired intestinal absorption.

10. **Neurology**. After a nondisabling stroke or TIA from ipsilateral carotid stenosis of 70 to 99%, carotid endarterectomy is recommended and the benefit is time dependent, so it should ideally be done within 2 weeks of the event. For 50 to 69% stenosis the benefit is smaller and depends on patient factors; below 50%, revascularization is not indicated. Near-occlusion and asymptomatic stenosis are separate, more contested questions.
