Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. Acute pericarditis: NSAID/aspirin **plus colchicine for 3 months** (6 for recurrence), colchicine roughly halves recurrence (ICAP/CORP). Glucocorticoids are the trap: they relieve symptoms but increase recurrence, so reserve them for NSAID failure, uremic, or autoimmune cases.

2. **Pulmonary**. Use an **age-adjusted D-dimer** (age × 10 ng/mL FEU for patients over 50) when pretest probability for PE is low or intermediate, it safely excludes PE in more patients without missing events (ADJUST-PE). Do not apply it to high pretest probability; that goes straight to CT.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Celiac serology only works **on a gluten-containing diet**: IgA tissue transglutaminase plus a **total IgA** (selective IgA deficiency is overrepresented in celiac and causes false negatives, then use IgG DGP/tTG). Confirm with duodenal biopsy before committing someone to lifelong gluten avoidance. HLA-DQ2/DQ8 is useful only to *rule out*.

4. **Infectious disease**. C. difficile: **fidaxomicin is preferred over oral vancomycin**, and metronidazole is no longer a first-line option except when neither is available. First recurrence → fidaxomicin or a vancomycin taper/pulse; multiply recurrent → fecal microbiota transplant. Do not test asymptomatic patients or repeat testing to prove cure.

5. **Rheumatology**. Scleroderma renal crisis: abrupt severe hypertension + AKI + MAHA in diffuse cutaneous disease, classically anti-RNA polymerase III. Treat with an **ACE inhibitor (captopril), and keep it going even as creatinine rises**, this is the one setting where you don't stop the ACEi. Prednisone ≥15 mg/day is a precipitant, so avoid steroid doses in that range in diffuse scleroderma.

6. **Endocrinology**. Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis splits two ways: **type 1** = iodine load on preexisting nodular goiter/Graves, increased vascularity on Doppler → thionamide; **type 2** = destructive thyroiditis, *decreased* vascularity → glucocorticoids. Mixed cases get both. Amiodarone-induced *hypo*thyroidism, by contrast, is common and simply gets levothyroxine, you can continue the drug.

7. **Hematology**. A "low-normal" B12 does not exclude deficiency: check **methylmalonic acid** (elevated in B12 deficiency, normal in folate deficiency) and homocysteine (elevated in both). Giving folate alone corrects the anemia while the neurologic disease progresses, that's the classic board harm.

8. **Oncology**. Lung cancer screening (USPSTF 2021): annual low-dose CT for ages **50–80 with ≥20 pack-years**, currently smoking or quit within the past 15 years. Stop once 15 years have passed since quitting, or when a health problem substantially limits life expectancy or the ability to have curative surgery.

9. **Nephrology**. Nephrotic syndrome in an adult: **anti-PLA2R antibody** identifies primary membranous nephropathy in roughly 70–80% and correlates with disease activity. Antibody-negative membranous still demands a secondary hunt, malignancy (age-appropriate screening), hepatitis B, lupus, NSAIDs.

10. **Geriatrics**. Antipsychotics do **not** prevent delirium and do not shorten it; the evidence supports the nonpharmacologic bundle (reorientation, sleep protocol, early mobility, sensory aids, hydration). Reserve antipsychotics for dangerous agitation, and avoid benzodiazepines entirely unless the cause is alcohol or sedative withdrawal.
