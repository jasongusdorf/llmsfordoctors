1. **Cardiology**. Cardiac myxoma is the most common primary cardiac tumour in adults, although metastases to the heart are far more common than any primary tumour. About three-quarters arise in the left atrium, typically on a stalk attached to the interatrial septum near the fossa ovalis. The presentation is a triad: obstruction that mimics mitral stenosis (positional dyspnoea or syncope, an early diastolic "tumour plop"), systemic embolism, and constitutional symptoms with raised inflammatory markers driven by interleukin-6. Echocardiography makes the diagnosis, and prompt surgical excision is indicated because of the embolic risk. Multiple, recurrent, or non-atrial myxomas in a young patient, especially with spotty skin pigmentation or endocrine overactivity, suggest Carney complex (*PRKAR1A*) and warrant family screening.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Central sleep apnea with Cheyne-Stokes respiration, a crescendo-decrescendo breathing pattern alternating with central apneas, is common in heart failure and after stroke; opioids and high altitude are other causes. The first step is optimising heart failure therapy. Do not use adaptive servo-ventilation in heart failure with an ejection fraction of 45% or less and predominantly central apnea: SERVE-HF (2015) found higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality with ASV even though it controlled the apneas, and the AASM recommends against it in that group. CPAP is a reasonable trial, and opioid-induced central apnea often improves with dose reduction.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Most incidental pancreatic cysts are benign, but mucinous cysts (intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm and mucinous cystic neoplasm) have malignant potential, and main-duct IPMN carries a much higher cancer risk than branch-duct IPMN. In the 2017 international (Fukuoka) consensus, high-risk stigmata that warrant surgical evaluation are obstructive jaundice with a cyst in the pancreatic head, an enhancing mural nodule of 5 mm or more, and a main pancreatic duct of 10 mm or more. Worrisome features that prompt endoscopic ultrasound include a cyst of 3 cm or more, a smaller enhancing nodule, a main duct of 5 to 9 mm, and rapid growth. In cyst fluid, a high CEA and low glucose favour a mucinous cyst, and a high amylase favours a pseudocyst or a cyst communicating with the duct. Serous cystadenoma (microcystic, honeycomb, central scar) is benign. Surveillance intervals differ between society guidelines.

4. **Infectious disease**. Uncomplicated gonorrhea of the cervix, urethra, rectum, or pharynx is treated with a single dose of ceftriaxone 500 mg IM (1 g if weight is 150 kg or more), per the CDC 2020 update; azithromycin is no longer routinely added. If chlamydia has not been excluded, add doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days. Pharyngeal gonorrhea needs a test of cure 7 to 14 days after treatment because it is harder to eradicate, and everyone treated should be retested at about 3 months because reinfection is common. Separately, CDC 2024 guidance supports doxycycline post-exposure prophylaxis (200 mg once within 72 hours of condomless sex) for men who have sex with men and transgender women with a bacterial STI in the past year; it reduces syphilis and chlamydia substantially and gonorrhea less, owing to tetracycline resistance.

5. **Rheumatology**. Mixed connective tissue disease is an overlap of lupus, systemic sclerosis, and myositis features defined by high-titre anti-U1 RNP antibodies. The early picture is Raynaud phenomenon with diffusely puffy, swollen hands, plus inflammatory arthritis, myositis, and esophageal dysmotility. A high-titre speckled ANA with anti-U1 RNP but no anti-dsDNA or anti-Sm points here. Severe lupus nephritis and CNS lupus are uncommon. Pulmonary arterial hypertension is a leading cause of death and interstitial lung disease is common, so screen with echocardiography and pulmonary function tests including DLCO. Many patients evolve over years into a more defined disease, usually systemic sclerosis or lupus.

6. **Endocrinology**. For most hospitalised patients, critically ill or not, start insulin for persistent glucose of 180 mg/dL or above and target 140 to 180 mg/dL. Tighter control harms: NICE-SUGAR (2009) found higher 90-day mortality and more severe hypoglycemia in ICU patients targeted to 81 to 108 mg/dL than to 180 or below. In noncritically ill patients who are eating, a basal-plus-prandial regimen with correction doses beats sliding-scale insulin alone (RABBIT 2, 2007); correction-only insulin is acceptable only for mild hyperglycemia. Hold SGLT2 inhibitors at least 3 days before scheduled surgery (4 days for ertugliflozin) to reduce the risk of euglycemic ketoacidosis.

7. **Hematology**. For most stable hospitalised adults, including the critically ill, the 2023 AABB guideline recommends a restrictive red-cell transfusion threshold of 7 g/dL, with 7.5 g/dL for cardiac surgery and 8 g/dL for orthopaedic surgery or preexisting cardiovascular disease. Acute myocardial infarction is the contested exception: MINT (2023) compared a restrictive strategy (threshold 7 to 8) with a liberal one (threshold under 10), and death or recurrent infarction at 30 days was numerically higher with the restrictive strategy without reaching statistical significance, so many clinicians now transfuse more liberally in acute MI. Transfuse one unit at a time and reassess, and treat symptoms and active bleeding rather than the number alone.

8. **Oncology**. People with HIV have a markedly increased risk of anal cancer. The ANCHOR trial (2022) showed that treating biopsy-proven anal high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions in people with HIV reduced progression to anal cancer by 57% compared with active monitoring, which established screening as worthwhile. The 2024 International Anal Neoplasia Society consensus recommends screening men who have sex with men and transgender women with HIV from age 35, and other people with HIV from age 45, with anal cytology and/or high-risk HPV testing; abnormal results go to high-resolution anoscopy with biopsy. Other high-risk groups include solid-organ transplant recipients and women with a history of vulvar precancer or cancer.

9. **Nephrology**. BK polyomavirus nephropathy causes a rising creatinine in a kidney transplant recipient, usually within the first year or two, and closely mimics acute rejection. It reflects the overall intensity of immunosuppression, typically tacrolimus plus mycophenolate. Recipients are routinely screened with plasma BK PCR in the early post-transplant years. Biopsy shows intranuclear viral inclusions confirmed by SV40 large T antigen staining, with tubulointerstitial inflammation that can be mistaken for rejection; giving more immunosuppression for presumed rejection makes it worse. Treatment is reduction of immunosuppression, because no antiviral (cidofovir, leflunomide, IVIG, fluoroquinolones) has proven benefit. BK can also cause ureteral stenosis, and in stem-cell transplant recipients it causes hemorrhagic cystitis instead.

10. **Neurology**. Degenerative cervical myelopathy (cervical spondylotic myelopathy) is the most common cause of spinal cord dysfunction in older adults. Look for clumsy hands with trouble buttoning, a broad-based unsteady gait, Lhermitte sign, hyperreflexia below the level with a Hoffmann sign, and bladder involvement late; neck pain may be absent. An inverted brachioradialis reflex localises to C5-C6. MRI confirms cord compression, and surgical decompression is recommended for moderate, severe, or progressive myelopathy; it mainly halts decline rather than reversing deficits. Because cervical spondylosis is common on imaging, check B12 and copper before attributing a myelopathy to it; copper deficiency after bariatric surgery or from excess zinc (including zinc-containing denture cream) produces a dorsal-column myelopathy that mimics B12 deficiency.
