1. **Cardiology**. Syncope red flags that rule out vasovagal: exertional syncope, syncope while supine, no prodrome, or a family history of sudden death under 40. Get an ECG on everyone, Brugada type 1 (coved ST elevation ≥2 mm in V1–V2), QTc >500 ms, delta wave, or epsilon wave/T-wave inversions in V1–V3 (ARVC) change the whole disposition. "Reassure and discharge" is the trap.
2. **Pulmonary**. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis: take the exposure history (birds, molds, hot tubs, humidifiers, metalworking fluid). CT shows upper/mid-lobe centrilobular ground-glass nodules with mosaic attenuation and air trapping on expiration; BAL shows marked lymphocytosis. Fibrotic HP mimics IPF, the discriminators are upper-lobe predominance and air trapping. Antigen avoidance is the intervention that matters most.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Screen HBsAg, anti-HBc, and anti-HBs before immunosuppression. Anyone HBsAg-positive gets prophylactic entecavir or tenofovir; so does an HBsAg-negative/anti-HBc-positive patient receiving anti-CD20 (rituximab) or a stem cell transplant, because reactivation there can be fulminant. Monitoring LFTs instead of prophylaxis is the wrong answer in the high-risk groups.
4. **Infectious disease**. Bacterial meningitis: dexamethasone before or with the first antibiotic dose (benefit is in pneumococcal disease; no benefit if given after). Add ampicillin for Listeria if age >50, pregnant, or immunocompromised. Do not delay antibiotics for the CT, CT before LP only for immunocompromise, known CNS disease, seizure, papilledema, focal deficit, or depressed consciousness; draw cultures and treat first.
5. **Rheumatology**. Giant cell arteritis: start high-dose glucocorticoids on suspicion, do not wait for the biopsy, temporal artery biopsy stays positive for roughly 2 weeks on steroids. Vision loss gets IV methylprednisolone. Jaw claudication is the single most useful positive feature; a normal ESR and CRP make it unlikely but do not exclude it. Tocilizumab (GiACTA) is the steroid-sparing add-on.
6. **Endocrinology**. Cushing syndrome workup: first exclude exogenous glucocorticoids (including inhaled, topical, and joint injections). Then screen with any two of, 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression, late-night salivary cortisol, or 24-hour urine free cortisol. Only after biochemical confirmation check ACTH: suppressed = adrenal (image the adrenals), normal or high = ACTH-dependent (pituitary MRI, and inferior petrosal sinus sampling to separate pituitary from ectopic). Imaging first is the classic error.
7. **Hematology**. Cancer-associated VTE: DOACs are now acceptable first-line, but not universally. Rivaroxaban and edoxaban caused more major bleeding in patients with luminal GI and GU malignancies, LMWH (or apixaban) is preferred there. Anticoagulate at least 6 months and continue while the cancer is active or on treatment. Also check for drug interactions with the anticancer regimen.
8. **Oncology**. PSA screening (USPSTF): shared decision-making for men **55–69**, and do **not** routinely screen at **70 and older**. The exam wants the conversation about overdiagnosis and treatment harms, not a reflex PSA. An elevated PSA gets confirmed and worked up with MRI-informed biopsy, not immediate treatment.
9. **Nephrology**. Hyperkalemia with ECG changes: IV calcium first for membrane stabilization, it does not lower potassium. Insulin with glucose and high-dose nebulized albuterol shift it intracellularly; only dialysis, loop diuretics, and GI binders actually remove it. Sodium polystyrene is not acute therapy. And before treating a surprising value, consider pseudohyperkalemia from fist clenching, hemolysis, or marked leukocytosis/thrombocytosis.
10. **Obstetrics / women's health**. Preeclampsia does not require proteinuria: BP ≥140/90 on two occasions ≥4 hours apart after 20 weeks plus **either** proteinuria **or** end-organ features (platelets <100,000, creatinine >1.1 mg/dL or doubled, transaminases twice normal, pulmonary edema, new headache or visual symptoms). Magnesium is for seizure prophylaxis, not blood pressure. Low-dose aspirin from 12 weeks (ideally before 16) in high-risk women. Delivery is the definitive treatment.
