1. **Cardiology**. Endocarditis surgery indications: HF from valve dysfunction, uncontrolled infection/abscess/heart block, and recurrent emboli or persistent large vegetation. POET: after ≥10 days IV and stabilization, step-down to oral antibiotics was noninferior.
2. **Oncology**. Tamoxifen: endometrial cancer + VTE risk, needs an intact CYP2D6, avoid paroxetine/fluoxetine for hot flashes, use venlafaxine or citalopram. Aromatase inhibitors: bone loss and arthralgias, postmenopausal only.
3. **Neurology**. IIH: obese woman, headache + papilledema, opening pressure >25 cm H2O with normal CSF composition. MRI **with MRV** to exclude venous sinus thrombosis first; acetazolamide + weight loss; vision loss → shunt/fenestration.
4. **Psychiatry**. AUD: naltrexone is first-line (contraindicated with opioid use), acamprosate when renal function permits and hepatic disease limits naltrexone (avoid CrCl <30). Benzodiazepines/gabapentin are not maintenance therapy; disulfiram only with supervision.
5. **Dermatology**. Pyoderma gangrenosum: violaceous undermined border, pathergy, **do not debride**, that worsens it. Diagnosis of exclusion (culture/biopsy to rule out infection). Look for IBD, RA, or hematologic malignancy/IgA gammopathy.
6. **Allergy/Immunology**. Hereditary angioedema: no urticaria, no pruritus, no response to epinephrine/antihistamines/steroids. Screen with **C4** (low), then C1-INH level and function. Acute: C1-INH concentrate, icatibant, or ecallantide. ACE-inhibitor angioedema is the look-alike, stop the drug permanently.
7. **Women's health**. Aspirin 81 mg daily from 12–16 weeks for preeclampsia risk. Severe features: BP ≥160/110, thrombocytopenia, transaminitis, Cr >1.1 or doubling, pulmonary edema, neuro/visual symptoms. Magnesium for seizure prophylaxis, delivery is the cure, and it can present up to 6 weeks postpartum.
8. **Hematology**. Smoldering myeloma becomes myeloma without CRAB via SLiM: clonal marrow plasma cells ≥60%, involved:uninvolved free light chain ratio ≥100 (involved FLC ≥100 mg/L), or >1 focal lesion ≥5 mm on MRI. These are treated as active disease.
9. **Gastroenterology**. Microscopic colitis: chronic watery nonbloody diarrhea in an older woman with a **normal-appearing colonoscopy**, diagnosis requires random biopsies of the right and left colon. Budesonide is first-line; stop the culprits (NSAIDs, PPIs, SSRIs).
10. **Rheumatology**. ANCA vasculitis: rituximab or cyclophosphamide + glucocorticoids for organ-threatening disease. PEXIVAS changed two things, plasma exchange is no longer routine (no reduction in death/ESKD, even in severe renal disease or alveolar hemorrhage), and a reduced-dose steroid taper was noninferior with fewer serious infections.
