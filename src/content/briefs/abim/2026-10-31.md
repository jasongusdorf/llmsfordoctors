1. **Cardiology**. Drug-induced torsades de pointes rarely comes from one drug alone. It usually needs a combination of risk factors: female sex, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, bradycardia or pauses (including just after conversion from atrial fibrillation), structural heart disease, older age, and two QT-prolonging drugs or one drug plus a CYP3A4 inhibitor that raises its level. A QTc above 500 ms, or a rise of more than 60 ms from baseline, is the usual signal to stop or swap the drug. Bazett's formula overcorrects at fast heart rates, so a tachycardic patient's QTc can look worse than it really is.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Think of chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension when dyspnea or reduced exercise tolerance persists after at least 3 months of effective anticoagulation for pulmonary embolism. The screening test is a ventilation-perfusion scan, which is more sensitive than CT pulmonary angiography for chronic disease; a normal V/Q scan essentially rules it out. Echocardiography suggests the diagnosis and right heart catheterization confirms it. Pulmonary endarterectomy is potentially curative, so every patient should be assessed for operability at an expert center before medical therapy is chosen. Riociguat and balloon pulmonary angioplasty are for inoperable or residual disease, and anticoagulation is lifelong. Do not screen asymptomatic patients after PE.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Lynch syndrome changes treatment as well as surveillance. Mismatch-repair-deficient (MSI-high) metastatic colorectal cancer responds to checkpoint inhibitors, and first-line pembrolizumab beat chemotherapy for progression-free survival in KEYNOTE-177. Long-term follow-up of the CAPP2 trial showed that aspirin lowers colorectal cancer incidence in Lynch carriers. Endometrial cancer is the most common extracolonic cancer in women with Lynch, so after childbearing is complete, offer risk-reducing hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. Sebaceous adenomas or carcinomas of the skin point to the Muir-Torre variant.

4. **Infectious disease**. Bartonella causes three different board pictures. *B. henselae* cat-scratch disease is tender regional lymphadenopathy appearing 1 to 3 weeks after a kitten scratch, usually near a small papule at the inoculation site. It is mostly self-limited, and azithromycin is reserved for extensive lymphadenopathy. Neuroretinitis (a macular star) and Parinaud oculoglandular syndrome are the classic complications. In advanced HIV, bacillary angiomatosis gives vascular papules that can be mistaken for Kaposi sarcoma, and it responds to doxycycline or a macrolide. *B. quintana* is carried by body lice and causes trench fever and culture-negative endocarditis in people experiencing homelessness. Make the diagnosis by serology or PCR.

5. **Rheumatology**. Crowned dens syndrome is calcium pyrophosphate deposition around the odontoid. It causes acute severe neck pain and stiffness with fever and a high CRP in an older patient, and it gets mistaken for meningitis, polymyalgia rheumatica, or giant cell arteritis. CT of C1-C2 shows the calcification. Acute CPP crystal arthritis is treated much like gout, with NSAIDs, colchicine, or glucocorticoids; aspiration plus intra-articular steroid works well for a single joint. Unlike gout, no therapy dissolves the deposits; low-dose colchicine can reduce recurrent attacks. In patients younger than about 60, check calcium, PTH, magnesium, iron studies, and alkaline phosphatase, since a low value suggests hypophosphatasia.

6. **Endocrinology**. The 2023 European Society of Endocrinology adrenal incidentaloma guideline puts unenhanced CT attenuation at the center of the workup. A homogeneous mass of 10 Hounsfield units or less is a benign adenoma and needs no further imaging, whatever its size. Every patient gets a 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test. A morning cortisol of 1.8 µg/dL (50 nmol/L) or less excludes autonomous secretion. A higher value without overt Cushing features is "mild autonomous cortisol secretion," which calls for screening for hypertension, diabetes, and osteoporosis rather than automatic surgery. Screen for aldosteronism if there is hypertension or hypokalemia. Measure metanephrines when the mass is not clearly benign on imaging.

7. **Hematology**. In acquired hemophilia A, the mixing study can mislead. The factor VIII autoantibody is time- and temperature-dependent, so the aPTT may correct on an immediate mix and prolong again after 1 to 2 hours of incubation at 37°C. A lupus anticoagulant, by contrast, usually fails to correct right away and causes thrombosis, not bleeding. Confirm with a low factor VIII level and a Bethesda inhibitor titer. Patients are mostly older adults or postpartum women, and about half have an associated autoimmune disease, cancer, or drug exposure. The bleeding is into skin and muscle, not joints. Avoid unnecessary procedures and intramuscular injections until the inhibitor is controlled.

8. **Oncology**. Endocrine toxicities from immune checkpoint inhibitors break the usual rule for immune-related adverse events. They are generally permanent and are treated with hormone replacement, not high-dose glucocorticoids, and immunotherapy can usually continue. Thyroiditis (most often with anti-PD-1/PD-L1) is a painless thyrotoxic phase, treated with a beta-blocker and not thionamides, followed by lasting hypothyroidism. Hypophysitis is classically seen with ipilimumab and causes headache with central adrenal insufficiency. Start hydrocortisone before levothyroxine. Checkpoint-inhibitor diabetes comes on abruptly, often as DKA with a low C-peptide, and needs insulin for life.

9. **Nephrology**. Treat hyperkalemia in three steps. First stabilize, then shift, then remove. IV calcium is for ECG changes: it works within minutes and lasts about 30 to 60 minutes, and it does not lower potassium, so repeat it if the ECG changes persist. Shift potassium with 10 units of regular insulin IV plus dextrose (unless glucose is already high), and add nebulized albuterol at 10 to 20 mg, several times the bronchodilator dose. Hypoglycemia can occur hours later, especially in kidney failure, and some centers use 5 units to reduce that risk (evidence mixed). Bicarbonate helps mainly when there is metabolic acidosis. Removal (loop diuretics, sodium zirconium cyclosilicate or patiromer, or dialysis) is the step that actually fixes the problem. Avoid sodium polystyrene sulfonate when possible because of colonic necrosis.

10. **Neurology**. Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome is diagnosed on nerve conduction testing. Resting compound muscle action potentials are small, and they increase sharply, by 100% or more, after 10 seconds of maximal voluntary exercise or with high-frequency repetitive stimulation. Low-frequency stimulation gives a decrement, as in myasthenia gravis. P/Q-type voltage-gated calcium channel antibodies are present in most patients. Because the syndrome can come before small cell lung cancer is found, a negative initial CT should lead to PET, and smokers should be rescreened periodically for about 2 years. Magnesium, aminoglycosides, and neuromuscular blockers can worsen weakness.
