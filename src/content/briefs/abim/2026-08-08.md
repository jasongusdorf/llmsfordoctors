1. **Cardiology**. HFpEF: SGLT2 inhibitors are the one class with randomized benefit above EF 40% (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER), driven by HF hospitalization, not mortality. TOPCAT's spironolactone signal was regional/post hoc; MRAs are reasonable but weaker. "Nothing works in HFpEF" is now the wrong answer.
2. **Pulmonary**. ARDS: 6 mL/kg *predicted* body weight (height/sex, not actual weight) and plateau pressure ≤30. Prone positioning ≥16 h/day for P/F <150 is the mortality-reducing intervention (PROSEVA). Routine PA catheter, high tidal volumes, and liberal fluids are the distractors.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Barrett's: nondysplastic → surveillance every 3–5 years. Confirmed low-grade dysplasia → endoscopic eradication is preferred over surveillance. High-grade or intramucosal cancer → EMR of visible lesions plus ablation, not esophagectomy. All dysplasia should be confirmed by a second GI pathologist.
4. **Infectious disease**. C. difficile: fidaxomicin is preferred first-line over oral vancomycin (IDSA 2021); metronidazole only when neither is available. No test of cure and never test formed stool, PCR detects colonization. Recurrences: fidaxomicin, tapered vanc, ± bezlotoxumab; FMT after multiple recurrences.
5. **Rheumatology**. Scleroderma renal crisis: start an ACE inhibitor (captopril, titrated fast) and *continue it even as creatinine rises*, dialysis may be temporary. Prednisone ≥15 mg/day precipitates it, so avoid steroids in diffuse disease. Anti-RNA polymerase III is the antibody; anti-centromere is the low-risk one.
6. **Oncology**. Checkpoint-inhibitor toxicity: grade 2 → hold drug + prednisone ~0.5–1 mg/kg; grade 3–4 → 1–2 mg/kg with slow taper over ≥4 weeks; steroid-refractory colitis → infliximab or vedolizumab. Endocrine toxicities (hypophysitis with CTLA-4, thyroiditis with PD-1) are the exception: replace hormones and usually continue the drug.
7. **Neurology**. Thrombolysis ≤4.5 h with BP <185/110 before the bolus and <180/105 for 24 h after. Thrombectomy extends to 24 h for large-vessel occlusion with favorable perfusion/core mismatch (DAWN, DEFUSE-3). In a non-lysed stroke, do not lower BP unless >220/120, permissive hypertension.
8. **Nephrology**. Membranous nephropathy is the classic adult nephrotic pattern; anti-PLA2R is positive in most primary cases and can support the diagnosis without biopsy in the right setting. Antibody-negative disease should push you to look for secondary causes: malignancy, hepatitis B, lupus, NSAIDs.
9. **General IM / Prevention**. Colorectal cancer screening now starts at **45** (USPSTF 2021): colonoscopy q10y, annual FIT, or stool DNA q3y. Screen through 75; 76–85 is individualized; stop at 86. Any positive noninvasive test requires colonoscopy, repeating the stool test is the trap.
10. **Endocrinology**. Thyroid nodule workup starts with **TSH, not ultrasound alone**. Suppressed TSH → radionuclide scan; a hot nodule is essentially never malignant and does not need FNA. Normal or high TSH → ultrasound, and FNA by size plus sonographic risk pattern, not size alone.
