1. **Cardiology**. In a patient aged 60 or younger with an embolic-appearing ischemic stroke, a patent foramen ovale, and no other mechanism after a full workup (vessel imaging, echocardiography, and prolonged rhythm monitoring for atrial fibrillation), percutaneous PFO closure plus antiplatelet therapy reduces recurrent stroke compared with antiplatelet therapy alone. That was the result of CLOSE, REDUCE, and long-term RESPECT (all published in 2017), and the AAN 2020 practice advisory says closure may be recommended to such patients after shared decision-making. Benefit is greatest with a large shunt or an atrial septal aneurysm. Closure is not indicated for an incidental PFO, in older patients with vascular risk factors that offer another explanation, or for migraine. Expect a small excess of new atrial fibrillation after the procedure, mostly early and transient.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis is a disease of young adult smokers, almost all of whom smoke. High-resolution CT shows upper- and mid-zone nodules, some cavitating, that evolve into thick- and thin-walled cysts of bizarre, irregular shape, with relative sparing of the costophrenic angles. Spontaneous pneumothorax can be the first presentation, and pituitary involvement can cause central diabetes insipidus. Contrast this with lymphangioleiomyomatosis, which affects women and produces uniform, round, thin-walled cysts distributed diffusely, including the bases. The cornerstone of treatment is smoking cessation, which alone stabilises or improves many patients.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Classify suspected drug-induced liver injury by the R ratio: (ALT ÷ ALT upper limit of normal) divided by (alkaline phosphatase ÷ ALP upper limit of normal). An R of 5 or more is hepatocellular, 2 or less is cholestatic, and between 2 and 5 is mixed; the pattern directs the search for alternative causes (viral and autoimmune workup for hepatocellular, imaging of the biliary tree for cholestatic). Hy's law, hepatocellular injury with ALT above 3 times the upper limit plus total bilirubin above 2 times the upper limit, without another explanation, marks a high risk of liver failure, death, or transplant. Amoxicillin-clavulanate is the most common single cause in US and European registries; it usually causes cholestatic or mixed injury, often in older patients, and can begin days to weeks after the course has ended. Stop the suspect drug first.

4. **Infectious disease**. In suspected herpes simplex encephalitis (fever, altered mental status, behavioural change, focal seizures, aphasia), start intravenous acyclovir 10 mg/kg every 8 hours, adjusted for renal function, immediately; do not wait for the lumbar puncture or imaging. MRI typically shows asymmetric T2/FLAIR signal in the medial temporal lobes and insula. CSF HSV PCR can be falsely negative in the first few days, so if suspicion remains, continue acyclovir and repeat the PCR in 3 to 7 days. Treat for 14 to 21 days, and hydrate to prevent acyclovir crystal nephropathy. Weeks after apparent recovery, new psychiatric symptoms, movement disorder, or encephalopathy can be autoimmune anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis triggered by the infection, not viral relapse; test for the antibody before simply restarting antivirals.

5. **Rheumatology**. Acute parvovirus B19 infection in an adult can look exactly like new rheumatoid arthritis: a sudden, symmetric polyarthritis of the small joints of the hands, wrists, and knees, more common in women and in people with exposure to young children (parents, teachers, daycare workers). Adults usually lack the "slapped cheek" rash, though a faint lacy rash may appear on the trunk or limbs. Rheumatoid factor and other autoantibodies can be transiently positive, which is the trap. Confirm with parvovirus B19 IgM. The arthritis is non-erosive and usually resolves within weeks with NSAIDs, though a minority persists for months. Do not start a disease-modifying drug for a few weeks of polyarthritis without considering it, and counsel exposed pregnant contacts because of the risk of fetal hydrops.

6. **Endocrinology**. High-dose biotin, common in hair, skin, and nail supplements, interferes with streptavidin-biotin immunoassays. In competitive assays (free T4, T3, TSH-receptor antibody) it causes falsely high results, and in sandwich assays (TSH) falsely low ones, so the laboratory picture can perfectly mimic Graves disease, positive antibody included, in a patient who is clinically euthyroid. The FDA warned in 2017 that the same interference can produce a falsely low troponin and a missed myocardial infarction. When thyroid tests do not fit the patient, ask about supplements, stop biotin, and repeat the tests after a washout of a few days (longer after very high doses), or ask the lab to use a platform not affected by biotin.

7. **Hematology**. Suspect myelodysplastic syndrome in an older adult with unexplained cytopenias, often a macrocytic anemia. First exclude the mimics that also cause dysplasia: vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, copper deficiency (after bariatric surgery or with excess zinc), alcohol, HIV, and drugs such as methotrexate. Diagnosis needs a bone marrow showing dysplasia in at least 10% of cells of a lineage, defining cytogenetics or mutations, and fewer than 20% blasts (20% or more is AML). A clonal mutation without cytopenia is CHIP; with cytopenia but without MDS criteria, it is CCUS. Risk is stratified with IPSS-R or the molecular IPSS-M. Lower-risk disease is treated for anemia: lenalidomide for isolated del(5q), and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents or luspatercept (MEDALIST, COMMANDS). Higher-risk disease is treated with a hypomethylating agent such as azacitidine, and allogeneic transplant is the only potentially curative option.

8. **Oncology**. The USPSTF 2024 recommendation is biennial screening mammography for women aged 40 to 74 at average risk (grade B), moving the start from 50 to 40. Evidence is insufficient (grade I) for screening at 75 and older and for supplemental ultrasound or MRI in women with dense breasts on an otherwise negative mammogram. Since September 2024, an FDA rule requires mammography facilities across the US to tell every patient whether her breasts are dense, so expect patients to ask about supplemental imaging. These recommendations do not apply to high-risk women (a known BRCA variant, prior chest radiation at a young age, or a high calculated lifetime risk), who need risk-based MRI surveillance.

9. **Nephrology**. Pyroglutamic acidosis (5-oxoproline) is an underrecognised cause of high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis. The classic patient is a malnourished, septic, or chronically ill woman, often with kidney or liver disease or alcohol use, taking regular therapeutic doses of acetaminophen, which depletes glutathione and disrupts the gamma-glutamyl cycle. Lactate, ketones, and salicylate are unremarkable, the osmolal gap is normal, and the acetaminophen level is often therapeutic or low, so it is missed. Flucloxacillin is another reported trigger. Confirm with urine organic acids showing 5-oxoproline. Treatment is stopping acetaminophen and correcting the underlying illness; N-acetylcysteine is often given to replenish glutathione, though evidence for it is limited.

10. **Neurology**. Separate neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder from multiple sclerosis. NMOSD causes severe, often bilateral or chiasmal optic neuritis; longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis spanning three or more contiguous vertebral segments; and the area postrema syndrome of intractable hiccups, nausea, and vomiting. Brain MRI is often normal or not typical of MS. Test serum AQP4-IgG with a cell-based assay, and MOG-IgG if negative, since MOG antibody disease is a separate entity. NMOSD is strongly female-predominant and associated with SLE and Sjögren disease. Treat attacks with high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone and early plasma exchange. Some MS drugs, including interferon-beta, natalizumab, and fingolimod, can worsen NMOSD. For AQP4-positive disease, approved maintenance options include eculizumab, ravulizumab, inebilizumab, and satralizumab.
