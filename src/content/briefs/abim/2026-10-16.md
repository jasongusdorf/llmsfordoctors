1. **Cardiology**. Suspect transthyretin (ATTR) cardiac amyloidosis in an older man with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and a thick-walled left ventricle whose ECG voltage is low or merely normal; that mismatch argues against hypertensive hypertrophy. Extracardiac clues often precede the heart failure by years: bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome, lumbar spinal stenosis, and spontaneous biceps-tendon rupture. Bone-tracer scintigraphy (technetium pyrophosphate or a related agent) can diagnose ATTR without a biopsy, but only after AL amyloidosis has been excluded with serum free light chains and serum and urine immunofixation; a monoclonal protein means tissue is still needed. The hereditary V122I (Val142Ile) variant is carried by roughly 3 to 4% of Black Americans. Tafamidis reduced all-cause mortality and cardiovascular hospitalization in the ATTR-ACT trial (2018).

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Varenicline is the most effective single drug for tobacco cessation. In EAGLES (2016), which enrolled smokers with and without psychiatric illness, varenicline beat bupropion, the nicotine patch, and placebo for abstinence without a significant increase in neuropsychiatric adverse events, and the FDA removed the boxed warnings on varenicline and bupropion later that year. The 2020 American Thoracic Society guideline prefers varenicline over the patch or bupropion, and recommends starting it even in smokers not yet ready to quit rather than waiting for readiness. Combination nicotine replacement (a patch plus a short-acting gum, lozenge, or inhaler) beats either form alone. Offer every smoker pharmacotherapy plus counseling.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Not every case of acute uncomplicated diverticulitis needs antibiotics. In immunocompetent patients without systemic illness, randomized trials (AVOD, DIABOLO) found no difference in recovery or complications with observation alone, and the 2021 AGA clinical practice update endorses using antibiotics selectively. Get CT when the diagnosis is uncertain or a complication (abscess, perforation, fistula, obstruction) is suspected. After a CT-confirmed episode, colonoscopy is advised if the colon has not been evaluated recently, especially after complicated diverticulitis, because a perforated colon cancer can look the same. Elective colectomy is an individualized decision based on complications, symptoms, and quality of life, not an automatic step after a set number of episodes.

4. **Infectious disease**. *Streptococcus gallolyticus* (formerly *S. bovis* biotype I) bacteremia or endocarditis mandates colonoscopy, because of its strong association with colorectal adenomas and carcinoma; it is also linked to chronic liver disease. *Clostridium septicum* bacteremia carries the same message: it points to occult colorectal cancer or hematologic malignancy, often with neutropenia, and can cause spontaneous, rapidly progressive gas gangrene with no wound. Curing the infection is not the end of the workup; find the colon lesion.

5. **Rheumatology**. Olecranon and prepatellar bursitis are superficial, and a substantial minority are septic, usually from *Staphylococcus aureus* entering through skin breaks from leaning or kneeling. Warmth, redness, and tenderness occur in septic and aseptic (traumatic or crystal) bursitis alike, and fever is often absent with infection, so aspirate the bursa for Gram stain, culture, cell count, and crystals whenever infection is possible. Unlike septic arthritis, joint motion is largely preserved, because the joint space is not involved; pain through the full range of motion points to the joint itself. Never inject a glucocorticoid into a bursa until infection is excluded.

6. **Endocrinology**. In Graves disease, radioactive iodine can provoke or worsen thyroid eye disease, and smoking is the strongest modifiable risk factor. The 2016 American Thyroid Association guideline advises a thionamide or thyroidectomy, not radioiodine, for patients with active moderate-to-severe or sight-threatening orbitopathy. With mild active orbitopathy, radioiodine can be given with prophylactic oral glucocorticoids, particularly in smokers. Radioiodine is contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding, and pregnancy should be deferred for at least 6 months afterward. Urge smoking cessation in every patient with Graves disease.

7. **Hematology**. Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (classically O157:H7) causes bloody diarrhea, often with little fever, and in a minority hemolytic uremic syndrome follows about 5 to 10 days after the diarrhea begins, often as it is resolving: microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, and acute kidney injury. Avoid antibiotics and antimotility agents in suspected STEC infection; antibiotics have been associated with a higher risk of HUS. Treatment is supportive, with early volume expansion and dialysis when needed. ADAMTS13 activity is not severely reduced, which separates it from TTP. HUS without a diarrheal prodrome, or with recurrence or a family history, suggests complement-mediated (atypical) HUS, which is treated with a C5 inhibitor such as eculizumab.

8. **Oncology**. USPSTF (2021) recommends colorectal cancer screening for average-risk adults from age 45, not 50, through 75, with individualized decisions from 76 to 85. A positive stool test (FIT or multitarget stool DNA) must be followed by colonoscopy; repeating the stool test is the wrong answer. Family history changes the start date: with colorectal cancer or an advanced adenoma in one first-degree relative diagnosed before 60, or in two first-degree relatives at any age, the US Multi-Society Task Force (2017) advises colonoscopy every 5 years starting at age 40 or 10 years before the youngest relative's diagnosis, whichever comes first.

9. **Nephrology**. In a normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis, the urine anion gap (urine Na + K − Cl) is a surrogate for urinary ammonium, the kidney's main acid-excretion response. A negative gap means ammonium excretion is appropriately high, pointing to extrarenal bicarbonate loss such as diarrhea ("neGUTive"). A positive gap means impaired ammonium excretion, as in distal renal tubular acidosis. The calculation fails when the urine contains unmeasured anions such as ketoacids or hippurate from toluene, where the urine osmolal gap is the better estimate, and it is less reliable when volume depletion drives urine sodium very low.

10. **Neurology**. Foot drop from common peroneal (fibular) neuropathy at the fibular head is a compression palsy after habitual leg crossing, rapid weight loss, prolonged squatting, a cast, or prolonged bed rest. It weakens ankle dorsiflexion and eversion but spares inversion (tibial nerve) and plantar flexion, with sensory loss over the dorsum of the foot and lateral shin and normal reflexes. An L5 radiculopathy also causes foot drop, but it weakens inversion and hip abduction as well and usually comes with back pain radiating down the leg. Most compressive peroneal palsies recover once the cause is removed; nerve conduction studies and EMG localize the lesion when the picture is unclear.
