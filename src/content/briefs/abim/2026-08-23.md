1. **Cardiology**. Iron deficiency in HFrEF: treat when ferritin <100 ng/mL, or 100–299 with TSAT <20%, even without anemia. IV ferric carboxymaltose improves symptoms and cuts HF hospitalizations; oral iron does not work (IRONOUT, no change in peak VO2). Don't wait for anemia.
2. **Pulmonary**. Fleischner 2017: an incidental solid nodule <6 mm in a low-risk patient needs no routine follow-up. Applies only to age ≥35, no cancer history, no immunosuppression, and NOT to lung cancer screening CTs. Part-solid nodules are the worry: a solid component ≥6 mm is suspicious.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Variceal bleed: octreotide plus prophylactic ceftriaxone (antibiotics reduce mortality, that's the tested part), EGD within 12h, restrictive transfusion at Hgb 7 g/dL (Villanueva, liberal transfusion was worse). Child-Pugh C or B with active bleeding at endoscopy → consider early TIPS within 72h.
4. **Infectious disease**. Endocarditis surgical indications: heart failure from valve dysfunction, uncontrolled infection (abscess, new heart block, fungal/resistant organism), and recurrent emboli or a mobile vegetation >10 mm with an embolic event. Also know POET: in stabilized left-sided IE, oral step-down was noninferior to full-course IV.
5. **Rheumatology**. Septic arthritis: synovial WBC is typically >50,000/µL with >90% neutrophils, but the trap is that crystals do NOT exclude infection, gout and septic arthritis coexist. Gram stain is negative in about half. Arthrocentesis before antibiotics, then drainage plus antibiotics.
6. **Endocrinology**. Suspected adrenal crisis: give **dexamethasone**, not hydrocortisone, if you need to treat before the cosyntropin test, dexamethasone doesn't cross-react with the cortisol assay. Primary AI has hyperkalemia, hyperpigmentation, and needs fludrocortisone; secondary AI has neither and doesn't.
7. **Hematology**. Anticoagulant reversal, one line each: warfarin → 4-factor PCC **plus IV vitamin K** (FFP is the wrong answer for major bleeding); dabigatran → idarucizumab; apixaban/rivaroxaban → andexanet alfa, or 4F-PCC if unavailable. Dabigatran is also the only one meaningfully dialyzable.
8. **Oncology**. Checkpoint-inhibitor toxicity: high-grade irAEs (colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis) get high-dose glucocorticoids and drug hold, infliximab for steroid-refractory colitis. The exception is endocrine irAEs: hypophysitis and thyroiditis are managed with hormone replacement, and the drug can often continue. Check TSH/cortisol, don't reflex to steroids.
9. **General IM / Prevention**. One-time screening abdominal ultrasound for AAA in men 65–75 who have ever smoked (USPSTF B). Never-smoker men 65–75 is selective; women who never smoked, do not screen. Repair threshold is ≥5.5 cm in men, or rapid growth >0.5 cm in 6 months.
10. **Dermatology**. SJS/TEN is defined by BSA of epidermal detachment: SJS <10%, overlap 10–30%, TEN >30%, with mucosal involvement at two or more sites and a positive Nikolsky sign. Stop the culprit drug immediately (that single act improves survival) and transfer to a burn unit. Systemic steroids remain contested; supportive care is the answer.
