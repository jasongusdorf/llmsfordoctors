1. **Cardiology**. Severe AS = valve area <1.0 cm², mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, peak velocity ≥4 m/s. AVR is Class I for symptomatic severe AS *and* for asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%. When the gradient is low but the area is small with reduced EF, do a **dobutamine stress echo** to separate true-severe from pseudo-severe (afterload mismatch), pseudo-severe does not benefit from valve replacement.

2. **Pulmonary**. ARDS: tidal volume **6 mL/kg predicted** body weight (not actual weight, use height and sex) with plateau pressure ≤30. **Prone positioning** for ≥12–16 h/day when P/F <150 (PROSEVA reduced mortality). Routine steroids, inhaled nitric oxide, and liberal fluids are the wrong answers; conservative fluid strategy improves ventilator-free days.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Eosinophilic esophagitis: young atopic patient, solid-food dysphagia, food impaction, rings/furrows on endoscopy, **≥15 eosinophils/hpf** on biopsy. A PPI trial is no longer required to exclude it, PPI responsiveness is now a *treatment*, not a diagnostic criterion. Options: PPI, swallowed topical steroids, elimination diet, dupilumab.

4. **Infectious disease**. Native vertebral osteomyelitis: blood cultures ×2 plus **MRI**. In a hemodynamically stable patient without neuro deficits, **hold antibiotics** until you have an organism, image-guided biopsy if cultures are negative. Empiric therapy before sampling is the classic wrong move. ESR/CRP track response; repeat MRI early does not.

5. **Rheumatology**. PMR: age >50, shoulder/hip girdle pain and morning stiffness, high ESR/CRP, **normal CK** (pain limits effort, it is not true myopathy). Prednisone 12.5–25 mg/day gives a dramatic response within days; if it doesn't, question the diagnosis. Always screen for GCA symptoms.

6. **Endocrinology**. Hypercalcemia with a **suppressed PTH**: next tests are PTHrP (solid tumors), 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (lymphoma, granulomatous disease), and SPEP/light chains (myeloma). Treat with aggressive IV saline plus **zoledronic acid**; use **denosumab** when GFR is too low for a bisphosphonate. Calcitonin buys ~48 hours only (tachyphylaxis). Loop diuretics are not routine therapy.

7. **Nephrology**. Normal-anion-gap acidosis, RTA in three lines: **Type 1 (distal)**, urine pH stays >5.5, hypokalemia, calcium phosphate stones/nephrocalcinosis. **Type 2 (proximal)**, Fanconi features (glycosuria with normal serum glucose, phosphaturia, aminoaciduria), urine pH can drop below 5.5 once the filtered load falls. **Type 4**, the hyperkalemic one, hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism, classically diabetic nephropathy.

8. **Hematology**. TTP: MAHA + thrombocytopenia, often without significant renal failure or DIC labs (normal PT/PTT/fibrinogen). Use the **PLASMIC score**, send ADAMTS13 (<10% confirms), and start **plasma exchange emergently**, do not wait for the assay. Add glucocorticoids ± caplacizumab. Platelet transfusion is avoided unless there is life-threatening bleeding.

9. **Oncology**. Myeloma no longer requires end-organ damage to treat. The **SLiM** criteria make it symptomatic disease: clonal marrow plasma cells **≥60%**, involved:uninvolved serum free light chain ratio **≥100** (with involved FLC ≥100 mg/L), or **>1 focal lesion on MRI**. Any one of those, or classic CRAB, means treat now.

10. **Obstetrics / Women's health**. Low-dose **aspirin 81 mg** daily from 12–28 weeks (ideally before 16 weeks) for preeclampsia prevention in high-risk pregnancy (prior preeclampsia, chronic hypertension, pregestational diabetes, CKD, autoimmune disease, multifetal gestation). Preeclampsia with severe features gets **magnesium sulfate** for seizure prophylaxis; delivery is the only definitive treatment.
