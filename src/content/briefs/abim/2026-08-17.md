1. **Cardiology**. Asymptomatic severe *primary* MR gets surgery at LVEF ≤60% or LVESD ≥40 mm, the thresholds are earlier than they feel, because a "normal" EF is already abnormal in a volume-loaded ventricle. *Secondary* MR is different: optimize GDMT and CRT first, then TEER (COAPT). MITRA-FR was negative largely because MR was proportionate to a very dilated ventricle.
2. **Pulmonary**. IPF: nintedanib or pirfenidone slow FVC decline. Immunosuppression is the trap, PANTHER-IPF stopped early because prednisone + azathioprine + NAC increased death and hospitalization. Also treat the comorbidities (GERD, OSA), refer early for transplant, and remember acute exacerbation carries very high mortality.
3. **Gastroenterology**. HRS-AKI is a diagnosis of exclusion: hold diuretics and give an albumin challenge 1 g/kg/day (max 100 g) × 2 days; if creatinine doesn't improve and there's no shock, nephrotoxin, or structural disease, it's HRS. Treat with terlipressin + albumin (US-approved 2022) or, off ICU, midodrine/octreotide + albumin. Terlipressin causes respiratory failure, avoid it in hypoxemia or ACLF grade 3. TIPS and transplant are the definitive options.
4. **Infectious disease**. Vertebral osteomyelitis: MRI plus two sets of blood cultures. If the patient is hemodynamically stable with no neuro deficit, **hold antibiotics until you have a microbiologic diagnosis**, image-guided biopsy if blood cultures are negative. Empiric coverage in a stable patient is the wrong answer. Typically 6 weeks of therapy; follow the clinical course, not serial MRIs, which lag.
5. **Rheumatology**. RA: methotrexate is first-line (with folic acid), and before any biologic or JAK inhibitor you must screen for latent TB and hepatitis B. Tested pearls: JAK inhibitors carry an FDA boxed warning for MACE, malignancy, thrombosis, and death after ORAL Surveillance; RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia = Felty syndrome; and cervical spine instability matters before intubation.
6. **Endocrinology**. Asymptomatic primary hyperparathyroidism goes to surgery for any of: age <50, serum calcium >1.0 mg/dL above the upper limit, T-score ≤−2.5 or vertebral fracture, CrCl <60, 24-h urine calcium >400 mg, or nephrolithiasis/nephrocalcinosis. Before you commit, exclude familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia, 24-h urine calcium/creatinine clearance ratio <0.01, and those patients get no surgery.
7. **Hematology**. Sickle cell: hydroxyurea for ≥3 vaso-occlusive crises a year, prior acute chest, severe anemia, or chronic pain. In acute chest syndrome give antibiotics covering atypicals, incentive spirometry, and simple transfusion, exchange transfusion for severe or progressive disease. Do not push hemoglobin above ~10 g/dL; hyperviscosity worsens it. Opioids do not cause acute chest, but under-treated pain with splinting does.
8. **Oncology**. Early-stage asymptomatic CLL is observed, treating early does not improve survival. Indications to treat: progressive marrow failure, bulky or symptomatic nodes, massive splenomegaly, rapid lymphocyte doubling, or autoimmune cytopenia refractory to steroids. Lymphocyte count alone is never an indication. A rapidly enlarging single node with B symptoms and a high LDH = Richter transformation, biopsy it.
9. **Nephrology**. Albuminuric CKD now has a stack: ACEi/ARB, then an SGLT2 inhibitor (DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY, benefit extends to nondiabetic CKD and down to low eGFR), then finerenone in type 2 diabetes with albuminuria (FIDELIO/FIGARO). Expect an acute eGFR dip after starting an SGLT2 inhibitor; it is hemodynamic and not a reason to stop.
10. **Dermatology**. DRESS: rash 2–8 weeks after the culprit (allopurinol, aromatic anticonvulsants, sulfonamides, vancomycin), with facial edema, fever, eosinophilia or atypical lymphocytes, and organ involvement, hepatitis most often. The long latency is the discriminator from a simple morbilliform drug rash. Stop the drug, systemic steroids with a taper over weeks to months, and check thyroid function months later for late autoimmune thyroiditis.
