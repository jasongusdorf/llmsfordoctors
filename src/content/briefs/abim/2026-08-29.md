1. **Cardiology**. Acute aortic dissection: rate before pressure. Give an IV beta-blocker (esmolol/labetalol) to a heart rate ~60 FIRST, then add a vasodilator for SBP 100–120. Nitroprusside alone is the trap, reflex tachycardia raises dP/dt and propagates the dissection.
2. **Pulmonary**. Light's criteria over-call exudates in diuresed heart failure. When the fluid looks exudative but the story is CHF, calculate the serum-to-pleural **albumin gradient**: >1.2 g/dL means transudate regardless of Light's.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Wilson disease: young patient with hepatitis plus neuropsychiatric or movement symptoms. Low ceruloplasmin, Kayser-Fleischer rings on slit lamp, elevated 24-hour urinary copper. In fulminant Wilson the giveaway is a **low alkaline phosphatase** with Coombs-negative hemolysis and an AST:ALT ratio >2.
4. **Infectious disease**. Endocarditis prophylaxis is now a short list: prosthetic valve or prosthetic valve material, prior infective endocarditis, unrepaired cyanotic CHD (or repaired with residual defect/within 6 months), and transplant valvulopathy, and only for dental procedures manipulating gingiva or the periapical region. Mitral valve prolapse, bicuspid aortic valve, and HCM are **not** indications; neither are GI or GU procedures.
5. **Rheumatology**. Adult-onset Still disease: quotidian spiking fevers, evanescent salmon-colored rash that appears with the fever, arthritis, sore throat, leukocytosis with neutrophilia, very high ferritin, and characteristically **negative ANA and RF**. Diagnosis of exclusion; watch for macrophage activation syndrome (falling counts, falling ESR with high ferritin).
6. **Endocrinology**. Before diagnosing primary hyperparathyroidism, exclude familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia: check the 24-hour **calcium-to-creatinine clearance ratio**. <0.01 favors FHH (do not operate); >0.02 favors PHPT. Lithium and thiazides also raise calcium and PTH.
7. **Hematology**. Isolated prolonged aPTT: run a mixing study. **Corrects** → factor deficiency. **Does not correct** → inhibitor. The board discriminator: a lupus anticoagulant prolongs the aPTT in vitro but causes *thrombosis*, not bleeding; a factor VIII inhibitor causes bleeding and shows time/temperature-dependent incomplete correction.
8. **Oncology**. Adjuvant endocrine therapy in HR-positive breast cancer: **tamoxifen** if premenopausal, **aromatase inhibitor** if postmenopausal (an AI alone is wrong in a premenopausal woman without ovarian suppression). Toxicity split: tamoxifen → endometrial cancer, VTE, hot flashes; AI → arthralgias and bone loss, so monitor DXA.
9. **Nephrology**. ADPKD: tolvaptan slows eGFR decline in rapid progressors (requires LFT monitoring for hepatotoxicity). Do **not** screen everyone for intracranial aneurysm, MRA only with a family history of aneurysm/SAH, prior rupture, or a high-stakes occupation. Aggressive BP control (~110/75) benefits young patients with preserved eGFR.
10. **Neurology**. Idiopathic intracranial hypertension: obese young woman, headache, pulsatile tinnitus, papilledema, possible sixth-nerve palsy. Image first, MRI with **venography** to exclude cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, then LP showing opening pressure >25 cm H2O with normal CSF composition. Acetazolamide plus weight loss; the thing you are protecting is vision, so serial formal visual fields, not symptoms, drive escalation.
