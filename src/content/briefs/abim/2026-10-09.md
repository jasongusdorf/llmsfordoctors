1. **Cardiology**. Fixed splitting of S2, unchanged with respiration, is the classic finding of an atrial septal defect, often with a pulmonic flow murmur from increased right-sided flow. On the ECG, a secundum defect typically shows incomplete right bundle branch block with right axis deviation, whereas a primum defect shows left axis deviation. Watch for paradoxical embolism and atrial arrhythmias in adults; once Eisenmenger physiology with shunt reversal has developed, closure is contraindicated.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Separate primary spontaneous pneumothorax, in a young patient without known lung disease, from secondary pneumothorax in someone with COPD or other lung disease. A clinically stable primary pneumothorax can often be managed conservatively or with simple aspiration, and the 2023 British Thoracic Society guideline steers management by symptoms and patient preference rather than radiographic size alone. Secondary pneumothorax has less physiologic reserve and usually warrants admission and drainage. Smoking cessation is the main modifiable factor for recurrence.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Acute colonic pseudo-obstruction (Ogilvie syndrome) is massive colonic dilation without a mechanical cause, typically in a hospitalized, postoperative, or critically ill patient. Exclude mechanical obstruction and toxic megacolon first, then manage conservatively: bowel rest, decompression, correction of potassium and magnesium, and withdrawal of opioids and anticholinergics. If that fails or the cecum is markedly dilated, give intravenous neostigmine with cardiac monitoring and atropine at the bedside for bradycardia; colonoscopic decompression is the next step. Neostigmine is contraindicated if mechanical obstruction or perforation has not been excluded.

4. **Infectious disease**. In suspected infectious mononucleosis, a negative heterophile (Monospot) test in the first week of illness does not exclude EBV; repeat it or send EBV viral capsid antigen IgM. Amoxicillin or ampicillin given for presumed streptococcal pharyngitis commonly produces a diffuse maculopapular rash, which is not a true penicillin allergy. Advise avoiding contact sports for at least 3 weeks from symptom onset because of splenic rupture risk. Reserve corticosteroids for complications such as impending airway obstruction or severe autoimmune hemolytic anemia or thrombocytopenia.

5. **Rheumatology**. Fibromyalgia is a clinical diagnosis of chronic widespread pain with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive symptoms; current American College of Rheumatology criteria no longer require a tender-point examination. Inflammatory markers and the examination should be normal, so abnormal results point toward another diagnosis rather than "fibromyalgia plus." Aerobic exercise is the best-supported first-line therapy, alongside duloxetine, milnacipran, pregabalin, or low-dose amitriptyline. Opioids have no established benefit and should be avoided.

6. **Endocrinology**. Gynecomastia is palpable glandular breast tissue beneath the areola, distinct from the fatty tissue of pseudogynecomastia. Review medications first: spironolactone, antiandrogens, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors, ketoconazole, cimetidine, and anabolic steroids are common culprits. Laboratory evaluation includes testosterone, LH, estradiol, and hCG; an elevated hCG or estradiol, or rapid painful enlargement, warrants testicular ultrasound for a germ-cell or Leydig-cell tumor. A hard, eccentric, or fixed mass is a male breast cancer workup, not gynecomastia.

7. **Hematology**. Von Willebrand disease, the most common inherited bleeding disorder, causes mucocutaneous bleeding: heavy menstrual bleeding, epistaxis, easy bruising, and bleeding after dental work. The aPTT and platelet count are often normal, so test VWF antigen, VWF activity, and factor VIII directly. VWF rises with estrogen, pregnancy, stress, exercise, and inflammation, so one normal panel does not exclude it. Desmopressin works in most type 1 disease, ideally confirmed with a trial; it can worsen thrombocytopenia in type 2B, and fluid restriction is needed to avoid hyponatremia.

8. **Oncology**. Survivors of Hodgkin lymphoma who received chest or mantle radiation carry a long-term excess risk of breast cancer, coronary and valvular heart disease, hypothyroidism, and lung cancer. Women irradiated to the chest at a young age (generally before 30) qualify for high-risk breast screening with annual MRI plus mammography, starting at age 25 or 8 years after radiation, whichever is later. Ask every lymphoma survivor about prior radiation fields; it changes screening more than the original stage does.

9. **Nephrology**. Fibromuscular dysplasia is the classic cause of renovascular hypertension in a young or middle-aged woman, with a "string of beads" appearance in the mid-to-distal renal artery, unlike ostial atherosclerotic stenosis. It can involve the carotid and vertebral arteries, so image the cervicocephalic vessels once the diagnosis is made because of dissection and aneurysm risk. Unlike atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis, hypertension from fibromuscular dysplasia often responds to percutaneous balloon angioplasty, usually without a stent.

10. **Neurology**. Trigeminal neuralgia causes brief, electric, unilateral paroxysms in a trigeminal distribution, triggered by light touch, chewing, or talking, with a normal neurologic examination between attacks. Carbamazepine is first-line, with oxcarbazepine as an alternative; screen for HLA-B*15:02 before carbamazepine in patients of Asian ancestry because of the risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis. Obtain MRI to exclude a secondary cause, especially in a young patient or with bilateral symptoms or sensory loss, which suggest multiple sclerosis or a tumor. Microvascular decompression is an option for refractory classic disease.
