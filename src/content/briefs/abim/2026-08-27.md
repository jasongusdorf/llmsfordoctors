1. **Cardiology**. Preoperative cardiac evaluation: if the patient can do ≥4 METs (climb a flight of stairs, brisk walk) and has no unstable cardiac condition, proceed to surgery, stress testing is the wrong answer unless the result would change management. After a drug-eluting stent, delay elective noncardiac surgery 6 months (3 may be acceptable if delay risk outweighs ischemic risk); 30 days after a bare-metal stent. Continue aspirin when possible.
2. **Pulmonary**. Löfgren syndrome (erythema nodosum + bilateral hilar adenopathy + ankle arthritis/periarthritis ± fever) is a clinical diagnosis of acute sarcoidosis, **no biopsy needed**, and most remit spontaneously. Sarcoid hypercalcemia comes from macrophage 1-α-hydroxylase, so the abnormal value is **1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D**, not 25-OH.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Screen for hereditary hemochromatosis with a **fasting transferrin saturation ≥45%** plus ferritin, then HFE genotyping (C282Y homozygote). Treat with phlebotomy to a ferritin of ~50–100 ng/mL. Ferritin >1000 ng/mL, elevated AST, or platelets <200k flags advanced fibrosis, image or biopsy. Phlebotomy does not reverse arthropathy or hypogonadism.
4. **Infectious disease**. HIV opportunistic-infection prophylaxis by CD4: **<200** → TMP-SMX for PJP (also if oropharyngeal candidiasis or CD4% <14); **<100 with positive Toxoplasma IgG** → TMP-SMX covers both. Primary **MAC prophylaxis is no longer recommended** for patients starting effective ART. Start ART immediately at diagnosis regardless of CD4.
5. **Rheumatology**. Statin-associated **immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy**: proximal weakness with CK often >2000 that keeps progressing after the statin is stopped, that persistence is the discriminator from ordinary statin myalgia. Anti-HMGCR antibody positive; requires immunosuppression, not just drug withdrawal.
6. **Endocrinology**. Thyroid nodule workup starts with **TSH**, not FNA. Low TSH → radionuclide scan; a *hot* nodule is essentially never malignant and is not biopsied. Normal or high TSH → ultrasound, and FNA by sonographic pattern plus size (highly suspicious nodules at ≥1 cm; purely cystic nodules never).
7. **Hematology**. Polycythemia vera: **JAK2 V617F** positive with a **low** erythropoietin (a high EPO points to secondary causes). Treat everyone with phlebotomy to **hematocrit <45%** (CYTO-PV showed fewer thrombotic deaths vs 45–50%) plus low-dose aspirin; add hydroxyurea for high-risk disease, age >60 or prior thrombosis.
8. **Oncology**. Suspected malignant epidural spinal cord compression: give **dexamethasone immediately**, before imaging, do not wait for the MRI. Then MRI of the **entire** spine (multilevel disease is common), and urgent radiation oncology/neurosurgery. Pretreatment ambulatory status is the strongest predictor of walking afterward.
9. **Neurology**. Myasthenia gravis: fatigable weakness with ptosis and diplopia; acetylcholine-receptor antibody first, MuSK if negative, and **CT chest in everyone** to look for thymoma. Board trap is the drug list that unmasks or worsens it, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, aminoglycosides, IV magnesium, beta-blockers.
10. **Obstetrics / Women's health**. Hypertension in pregnancy: **labetalol, nifedipine, or methyldopa**; ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and direct renin inhibitors are contraindicated. Severe-range BP (≥160/110) needs treatment within about an hour. Preeclampsia with severe features gets magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis, and delivery is the only cure. High-risk patients start low-dose aspirin at 12–28 weeks (ideally before 16).
