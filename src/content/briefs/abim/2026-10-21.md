1. **Cardiology**. When the aortic valve area is 1.0 cm² or less but the mean gradient is under 40 mm Hg and the ejection fraction is reduced, the question is whether the stenosis is truly severe or only looks that way because a weak ventricle cannot open a moderately stenotic valve. Low-dose dobutamine stress echocardiography settles it. True severe stenosis shows the gradient climbing to at least 40 mm Hg (or a peak velocity of at least 4 m/s) while the valve area stays at 1.0 cm² or less; pseudo-severe stenosis shows the valve area opening up beyond 1.0 cm² as flow increases. If stroke volume fails to rise by at least 20%, there is no contractile reserve and the test is indeterminate, and CT aortic valve calcium scoring helps. Calcium scoring is also the tiebreaker in paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient stenosis, where the ejection fraction is preserved but a small, hypertrophied ventricle delivers a low stroke volume.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. In ARDS, set tidal volume by predicted body weight, which is calculated from height and sex, not by actual weight. The ARDS Network trial (2000) showed lower mortality with 6 mL/kg predicted body weight than with 12 mL/kg, with plateau pressure kept at or below 30 cm H2O. For moderate to severe disease, PROSEVA (2013) showed that early prone positioning for at least 16 hours a day roughly halved 28-day mortality in patients with a PaO2/FiO2 ratio below 150 mm Hg on FiO2 of at least 0.6 and PEEP of at least 5 cm H2O. Once shock has resolved, a conservative fluid strategy shortens time on the ventilator.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Screen for hereditary hemochromatosis with a fasting transferrin saturation and ferritin; a transferrin saturation of 45% or more with a high ferritin warrants *HFE* genotyping, and C282Y homozygosity is the classic genotype, though penetrance is incomplete. The key management threshold is a ferritin above 1000 ng/mL or abnormal aminotransferases in a C282Y homozygote, which signals a meaningful risk of cirrhosis and calls for fibrosis staging. Treat with phlebotomy to a ferritin of roughly 50 to 100 ng/mL, advise against iron and vitamin C supplements and raw shellfish, and keep cirrhotic patients in hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance even after iron is depleted. Arthropathy of the second and third metacarpophalangeal joints is a board clue and often does not improve with phlebotomy.

4. **Infectious disease**. *Strongyloides stercoralis* can persist for decades through autoinfection, long after a patient has left an endemic area. Corticosteroids, even short courses, and HTLV-1 coinfection can trigger hyperinfection or disseminated disease, in which larvae carry enteric gram-negative bacteria across the gut wall and cause polymicrobial bacteremia or gram-negative meningitis. Eosinophilia is often absent in hyperinfection, partly because steroids suppress it, so its absence is not reassuring. Before starting steroids or other immunosuppression in someone from an endemic region, send *Strongyloides* serology or treat empirically with ivermectin.

5. **Rheumatology**. In suspected giant cell arteritis (age 50 or older, new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, polymyalgia symptoms, high ESR or CRP), start high-dose glucocorticoids immediately and do not wait for the biopsy, because vision loss is usually permanent. Biopsy findings persist for at least the first couple of weeks of treatment. Visual symptoms call for IV pulse methylprednisolone. Guidelines differ on diagnosis: the American College of Rheumatology (2021) favors temporal artery biopsy, while EULAR favors vascular ultrasound first. Tocilizumab, which reduced glucocorticoid exposure and relapses in the GiACTA trial (2017), is the established steroid-sparing agent.

6. **Endocrinology**. Primary aldosteronism is the commonest curable cause of hypertension, and most patients have a normal potassium, so hypokalemia is not required to look for it. The 2025 Endocrine Society guideline recommends screening everyone with hypertension, a broader approach than older guidance that targeted high-risk groups. Screen with the aldosterone-to-renin ratio after correcting hypokalemia; remember that ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and diuretics raise renin and can produce a false negative, while beta-blockers lower renin and can produce a false positive. CT cannot reliably tell an aldosterone-producing adenoma from a nonfunctioning incidentaloma, so a surgical candidate needs adrenal venous sampling to confirm lateralization before adrenalectomy. Bilateral disease is treated with a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.

7. **Hematology**. Before diagnosing immune thrombocytopenia, look at the smear to exclude pseudothrombocytopenia from EDTA-induced platelet clumping (repeat the count in a citrate tube), and test for HIV and hepatitis C. ITP remains a diagnosis of exclusion. The ASH 2019 guideline suggests treating newly diagnosed adults when the platelet count is below 30 × 10⁹/L, with a short corticosteroid course (prednisone for no more than about 6 weeks, or pulsed dexamethasone) rather than prolonged steroids. IVIG is for when a rapid rise is needed, such as bleeding or before a procedure. Platelet transfusion is reserved for life-threatening bleeding. Second-line options are thrombopoietin receptor agonists, rituximab, or splenectomy, which is generally deferred until at least 12 months after diagnosis.

8. **Oncology**. New or worsening back pain in a patient with cancer is metastatic epidural spinal cord compression until proven otherwise, and pain usually precedes weakness, sensory level, or sphincter dysfunction by weeks. Image the entire spine with MRI urgently, because multilevel disease is common, and give dexamethasone as soon as compression is suspected. Definitive treatment is radiotherapy, with decompressive surgery followed by radiotherapy for selected patients; Patchell's randomized trial (2005) showed that this combination preserved ambulation better than radiotherapy alone in patients with a single area of compression. Ambulatory status at diagnosis is the strongest predictor of ambulatory status afterward, which is the argument for speed.

9. **Nephrology**. Membranous nephropathy is the classic cause of nephrotic syndrome in a nondiabetic adult. Anti-PLA2R antibodies are present in most primary cases, and KDIGO 2021 allows a positive anti-PLA2R test in a patient with typical nephrotic syndrome and preserved kidney function to establish the diagnosis without a biopsy. A negative or atypical picture should send you looking for secondary causes: malignancy in older patients, hepatitis B, lupus (class V), and drugs such as NSAIDs. Membranous has the highest venous thrombosis risk of the nephrotic lesions, including renal vein thrombosis, and the risk climbs as serum albumin falls, which is why prophylactic anticoagulation is considered at very low albumin levels. Rituximab was noninferior to cyclosporine for remission at 12 months and superior for sustained remission at 24 months in the MENTOR trial (2019).

10. **Neurology**. Myasthenia gravis causes fatigable ptosis, diplopia, and proximal or bulbar weakness, with normal reflexes and sensation. Acetylcholine receptor antibodies are positive in most generalized cases; MuSK antibodies identify a subgroup with prominent bulbar involvement and a poorer response to pyridostigmine. Every new diagnosis needs chest CT or MRI for thymoma. The MGTX trial (2016) showed that thymectomy improves outcomes in acetylcholine-receptor-positive generalized disease without thymoma in patients aged 18 to 65. Avoid aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, macrolides, IV magnesium, and beta-blockers where possible, and remember that starting high-dose prednisone can transiently worsen weakness. In crisis, follow forced vital capacity and negative inspiratory force rather than oxygen saturation, which falls late, and treat with IVIG or plasma exchange.
