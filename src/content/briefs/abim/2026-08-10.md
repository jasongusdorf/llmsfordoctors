1. **Cardiology**. Aortic dissection: type A → emergency surgery. Uncomplicated type B → medical therapy, and the sequence is tested, IV beta-blocker (esmolol/labetalol) FIRST to HR <60, then a vasodilator. Nitroprusside before rate control causes reflex tachycardia and raises dP/dt.
2. **Pulmonary**. Löfgren syndrome (erythema nodosum + bilateral hilar adenopathy + fever/arthritis) is a clinical diagnosis with excellent prognosis: no biopsy, no steroids. Treat sarcoid for organ threat only, cardiac, neuro, ocular, hypercalcemia, progressive lung disease. Hypercalcemia is from macrophage 1-alpha-hydroxylase, so avoid vitamin D.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Cholestatic LFTs: PBC = AMA positive, ursodiol 13–15 mg/kg/day, obeticholic acid or a fibrate second-line. PSC = beaded ducts on MRCP, associated with UC, and ursodiol does NOT help (high-dose was harmful). PSC needs annual colonoscopy and cholangiocarcinoma surveillance; PBC does not.
4. **Infectious disease**. Erythema migrans is a clinical diagnosis, do not serologize, early serology is insensitive and a negative test misleads. Doxycycline 10 days. Lyme carditis with high-grade AV block gets IV ceftriaxone and telemetry; the block resolves, so a permanent pacemaker is the wrong answer.
5. **Rheumatology**. Dermatomyositis warrants age-appropriate malignancy screening. Anti-TIF1-gamma carries the highest cancer association; anti-MDA5 marks clinically amyopathic disease with rapidly progressive ILD and normal-ish CK, a normal CK does not reassure you.
6. **Endocrinology**. Hypoglycemia workup is one table: insulinoma = high insulin, high C-peptide, high proinsulin, negative sulfonylurea screen. Exogenous insulin = high insulin, **suppressed C-peptide**. Sulfonylurea = high insulin, high C-peptide, positive screen. Check beta-hydroxybutyrate too, it's suppressed in all insulin-mediated causes.
7. **Hematology**. Polycythemia vera: JAK2 V617F in ~95%, and **erythropoietin is low** (high EPO points to secondary/hypoxic causes or a tumor). Phlebotomize to Hct <45% (CYTO-PV showed higher thrombosis above that) plus low-dose aspirin; add hydroxyurea if age >60 or prior thrombosis.
8. **Nephrology**. ADPKD: tolvaptan slows eGFR decline in rapidly progressive disease but requires scheduled LFT monitoring for hepatotoxicity. Screen for intracranial aneurysm only with a family history of aneurysm/SAH or a high-risk occupation, universal MRA screening is the trap.
9. **Oncology**. SVC syndrome is usually NOT an emergency: get tissue diagnosis before steroids or radiation, since empiric steroids can obscure lymphoma histology. Exceptions are stridor or cerebral edema. Contrast with cord compression, there you give dexamethasone immediately, then urgent MRI of the entire spine.
10. **Neurology**. Before starting an MS disease-modifying therapy, exclude NMOSD with aquaporin-4 IgG (and MOG): longitudinally extensive cord lesions, optic neuritis, area postrema syndrome. Interferon-beta and natalizumab worsen NMOSD. Acute relapse steroids speed recovery but do not change long-term disability.
