1. **Cardiology**. Unexplained Mobitz II or complete heart block in a patient younger than 60 should prompt evaluation for cardiac sarcoidosis, as should unexplained sustained ventricular tachycardia; a 2014 Heart Rhythm Society consensus statement made exactly that recommendation. The workup is cardiac MRI with late gadolinium enhancement and FDG-PET, which needs a high-fat, very-low-carbohydrate preparation to suppress normal myocardial glucose uptake. Isolated cardiac disease is common, so a normal chest radiograph does not exclude it. Treatment combines immunosuppression, usually glucocorticoids first, with device therapy; many of these patients need an ICD rather than a pacemaker alone.

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Asbestos-related findings mean very different things. Pleural plaques, often calcified along the diaphragm, are the commonest finding and are markers of exposure, not premalignant lesions, so they need no treatment. Asbestosis is lower-lobe-predominant interstitial fibrosis appearing decades after heavy exposure. Mesothelioma risk is unrelated to smoking, whereas lung cancer risk from asbestos and smoking is multiplicative, which makes smoking cessation the most useful intervention in an exposed worker. Rounded atelectasis next to thickened pleura can mimic a lung mass.

3. **Gastroenterology**. Suspect Zollinger-Ellison syndrome with multiple, refractory, or postbulbar duodenal ulcers, ulcers accompanied by chronic diarrhea, or prominent gastric folds. Proton-pump inhibitors raise gastrin themselves, so fasting gastrin is interpreted alongside gastric pH: a gastrin above 10 times the upper limit of normal with gastric pH below 2 is diagnostic, and equivocal results go to a secretin stimulation test. Stopping a PPI for testing can precipitate complications in true gastrinoma, so do it cautiously and with specialist input. Check calcium and PTH, because roughly a quarter of gastrinomas occur in MEN1.

4. **Infectious disease**. HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis now comes in four FDA-approved forms: daily oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine, daily oral tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine, cabotegravir injection every 2 months, and lenacapavir injection every 6 months. Document a negative HIV test first, and use an HIV RNA assay with the injectables, because starting PrEP during unrecognized acute infection invites resistance. Oral TDF/FTC requires creatinine clearance of at least 60 mL/min (TAF/FTC, at least 30), and TAF/FTC is not approved for people at risk through receptive vaginal sex. Test for hepatitis B, since stopping tenofovir can trigger a flare.

5. **Rheumatology**. Polyarteritis nodosa is a necrotizing medium-vessel vasculitis that is ANCA-negative and spares the lungs and the glomeruli. The kidney is injured through renal artery microaneurysms and infarcts, producing hypertension and renal impairment without an active glomerulonephritic sediment. Other clues are mononeuritis multiplex, livedo or skin nodules, postprandial abdominal pain from mesenteric involvement, and testicular pain. Always test for hepatitis B; HBV-associated disease is treated with antiviral therapy plus short-course glucocorticoids, with or without plasma exchange, rather than prolonged immunosuppression. Diagnosis is by biopsy or angiography showing microaneurysms.

6. **Endocrinology**. Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY) is the commonest genetic cause of primary hypogonadism and is underdiagnosed. Look for small, firm testes with elevated FSH and LH, low or low-normal testosterone, azoospermia, and often gynecomastia and tall stature; a karyotype confirms it. Testosterone therapy treats the androgen deficiency but suppresses spermatogenesis and does not restore fertility, so discuss sperm retrieval (microdissection testicular sperm extraction) before starting it if fertility matters. Affected men also carry higher risks of male breast cancer, venous thromboembolism, osteoporosis, and metabolic syndrome.

7. **Hematology**. Transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease occurs when donor T lymphocytes engraft in a recipient who cannot reject them. It appears 1 to 2 weeks after transfusion with fever, rash, diarrhea, liver dysfunction, and, unlike graft-versus-host disease after stem-cell transplantation, marrow aplasia with pancytopenia; it is almost always fatal. Leukoreduction does not reliably prevent it. Irradiated (or pathogen-reduced) cellular products are required for stem-cell transplant recipients, Hodgkin lymphoma, purine-analog therapy such as fludarabine, congenital T-cell immunodeficiency, and directed donations from blood relatives or HLA-matched platelets.

8. **Oncology**. Anthracycline and trastuzumab cardiotoxicity behave differently. Anthracycline injury is cumulative-dose related, involves myocyte loss, is largely irreversible, and can surface years after treatment; doxorubicin risk climbs steeply beyond a cumulative dose of about 400 mg/m², and dexrazoxane is cardioprotective. Trastuzumab dysfunction is not dose-related and usually recovers when the drug is held and standard heart failure therapy is given, after which rechallenge is often possible. Check LVEF before trastuzumab and periodically during treatment.

9. **Nephrology**. In metabolic acidosis, check respiratory compensation with Winter's formula: expected PaCO2 = 1.5 × HCO3 + 8 ± 2. A PaCO2 above that range means a concurrent respiratory acidosis; below it, a concurrent respiratory alkalosis, as in salicylate poisoning. In an anion-gap acidosis, compare the rise in the anion gap with the fall in bicarbonate (the delta-delta): a ratio below 1 suggests an added normal-anion-gap acidosis, and above 2 an added metabolic alkalosis. Correct the anion gap for hypoalbuminemia, adding about 2.5 for each 1 g/dL fall in albumin below 4.

10. **Neurology**. Cluster headache is strictly unilateral orbital or temporal pain lasting 15 to 180 minutes, often recurring daily at the same hour, with ipsilateral tearing, rhinorrhea, ptosis, or miosis. The patient paces and rocks, unlike a migraineur who lies still in the dark. Abort attacks with 100% oxygen at 12 to 15 L/min by non-rebreather mask or subcutaneous sumatriptan 6 mg. Verapamil is the standard preventive and needs ECG monitoring for PR prolongation and heart block; a short glucocorticoid course or occipital nerve block can bridge until it works. Image the brain at least once, because pituitary and other structural lesions can mimic it.
