1. **Cardiology**. In heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, guideline-directed therapy rests on four drug classes started together and titrated, not sequentially over months: an ARNI (or ACE inhibitor or ARB if an ARNI is not feasible), an evidence-based beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol), a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor, which reduces heart failure hospitalization and cardiovascular death whether or not the patient has diabetes (DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced). When switching from an ACE inhibitor to sacubitril-valsartan, allow a 36-hour washout to avoid angioedema. Add-ons with their own selection rules: ivabradine for sinus rhythm with a resting heart rate of 70 or more despite a maximally tolerated beta-blocker (SHIFT), and hydralazine plus isosorbide dinitrate for self-identified Black patients with persistent NYHA class III to IV symptoms on optimal therapy (A-HeFT).

2. **Pulmonary medicine**. Obstructive sleep apnea is diagnosed by an apnea-hypopnea index of 5 or more with symptoms or comorbidity, or 15 or more regardless of symptoms. A home sleep apnea test is appropriate for uncomplicated adults with a high pretest probability, but in-lab polysomnography is needed when there is significant cardiopulmonary disease, neuromuscular weakness, chronic opioid use, suspected hypoventilation, or a negative home test with persistent suspicion. CPAP improves sleepiness and quality of life and lowers blood pressure modestly, but in established cardiovascular disease it did not prevent cardiovascular events (SAVE). Weight loss is treatment, not just advice: tirzepatide is FDA-approved for moderate to severe OSA with obesity (SURMOUNT-OSA).

3. **Gastroenterology**. For *Helicobacter pylori*, avoid empiric clarithromycin-based triple therapy unless clarithromycin susceptibility is known, because resistance is now common in the United States. The 2024 ACG guideline prefers 14 days of optimized bismuth quadruple therapy (PPI, bismuth, tetracycline, metronidazole) for treatment-naive patients, with rifabutin-based triple therapy or vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy as alternatives when susceptibility is unknown. Every treated patient needs a test of cure by urea breath test, stool antigen, or biopsy at least 4 weeks after antibiotics, with the PPI held for about 2 weeks beforehand to avoid a false negative. Serology cannot confirm eradication.

4. **Infectious disease**. Asymptomatic bacteriuria should be screened for and treated in only two settings: pregnancy, and before an endoscopic urologic procedure that breaches the mucosa (IDSA 2019). Do not treat it in older adults with falls, delirium, or nonspecific decline without urinary or systemic signs of infection; in diabetes; in spinal cord injury without symptoms; or in catheterized patients, and pyuria alone does not change that. Cloudy or foul-smelling urine is not an indication. Treating asymptomatic bacteriuria buys resistance and *C. difficile* without improving outcomes, and on the exam, "observe, no antibiotics" is often the correct answer.

5. **Rheumatology**. In rheumatoid arthritis, anti-CCP antibodies are far more specific than rheumatoid factor and predict erosive disease. Methotrexate is the anchor DMARD and should be started early, with folic acid to reduce mucositis, cytopenias, and transaminase elevation. Stop it well before a planned conception because it is teratogenic. Before starting a biologic or JAK inhibitor, screen for latent tuberculosis and hepatitis B (and hepatitis C), and update vaccines. JAK inhibitors carry a boxed warning for major cardiovascular events, malignancy, and thrombosis after ORAL Surveillance (tofacitinib vs TNF inhibitors in patients 50 or older with a cardiovascular risk factor), so they are generally used after a TNF inhibitor in such patients.

6. **Endocrinology**. With a thyroid nodule, check the TSH first. A low TSH calls for a radionuclide uptake scan, and a hyperfunctioning ("hot") nodule is rarely malignant and does not need fine-needle aspiration. With a normal or high TSH, ultrasound pattern plus size decides biopsy under the 2015 ATA guideline: high or intermediate suspicion at 1 cm or more, low suspicion at 1.5 cm or more, and very low suspicion (spongiform) at 2 cm or more or observation. Purely cystic nodules are not aspirated for diagnosis. Indeterminate cytology (Bethesda III or IV) is where molecular testing or diagnostic lobectomy comes in.

7. **Hematology**. Polycythemia vera is a JAK2-mutated myeloproliferative neoplasm (V617F in about 95%, exon 12 in most of the rest); a low serum erythropoietin supports the diagnosis, whereas a high EPO points toward secondary erythrocytosis from hypoxia, sleep apnea, testosterone, or an EPO-secreting tumor. Every patient gets phlebotomy to a hematocrit below 45%, which lowered cardiovascular death and major thrombosis compared with a 45 to 50% target (CYTO-PV), plus low-dose aspirin unless contraindicated. High-risk disease, defined as age 60 or older or a prior thrombosis, adds cytoreduction, usually hydroxyurea. Aquagenic pruritus and erythromelalgia are classic clues.

8. **Oncology**. Adjuvant endocrine therapy for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer splits on menopausal status. Aromatase inhibitors work only when ovarian estrogen production is absent, so in premenopausal women they require ovarian suppression; tamoxifen works in either. Learn the toxicity trade: tamoxifen raises the risk of venous thromboembolism and endometrial cancer (so new postmenopausal bleeding needs evaluation), while aromatase inhibitors cause bone loss and arthralgias (so get a baseline DXA and treat osteoporosis). Tamoxifen should be avoided in patients with a history of VTE.

9. **Nephrology**. In CKD, the kidney-protective layer now goes beyond an ACE inhibitor or ARB. KDIGO 2024 recommends an SGLT2 inhibitor for most patients with an eGFR of 20 or more, with or without diabetes (DAPA-CKD, EMPA-KIDNEY), continued once started even as eGFR falls, until dialysis or transplant. In type 2 diabetes with albuminuria, the nonsteroidal MRA finerenone adds further benefit (FIDELIO-DKD, FIGARO-DKD), with potassium monitoring. An early creatinine rise of up to 30% after starting an ACE inhibitor, ARB, or SGLT2 inhibitor is expected hemodynamics, not injury, and is not a reason to stop.

10. **Neurology**. In acute ischemic stroke, thrombolysis is given within 4.5 hours of last known well, and blood pressure must be below 185/110 before the drug and kept below 180/105 for 24 hours afterward. Check a fingerstick glucose first, because hypoglycemia mimics stroke. Tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg, maximum 25 mg) as a single bolus is now an accepted alternative to alteplase. Large-vessel occlusion gets mechanical thrombectomy, extended to 24 hours from last known well with perfusion-imaging selection (DAWN, DEFUSE 3), and lytic eligibility should never delay the thrombectomy pathway. Hold antiplatelets and anticoagulants for 24 hours after alteplase.
