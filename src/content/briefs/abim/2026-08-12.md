Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. ISCHEMIA: in stable CAD with moderate-severe ischemia, an initial invasive strategy did not reduce death or MI vs optimal medical therapy. Revascularize for refractory symptoms, left main disease, or ACS, not for an abnormal stress test alone. Note the trial excluded EF <35%, left main, and advanced CKD.
2. **Nephrology**. Atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis: CORAL and ASTRAL both showed stenting adds nothing to medical therapy. Reserve intervention for flash pulmonary edema, refractory HTN on maximal therapy, or rapidly declining function. A creatinine rise up to ~30% after starting an ACEi/ARB is expected and is not a reason to stop.
3. **Pulmonary**. COPD exacerbation: prednisone 40 mg × 5 days (REDUCE, noninferior to 14 days). NIV is the mortality- and intubation-reducing intervention for respiratory acidosis (pH <7.35 with hypercapnia); delaying it for "a trial of medical therapy" is the wrong answer.
4. **Gastroenterology**. MASLD: risk-stratify with FIB-4 first, <1.3 is low risk and needs no elastography. Resmetirom (2024) is approved for biopsy-confirmed MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis; GLP-1s and weight loss ≥10% remain the backbone. Fibrosis stage, not steatosis, drives outcomes.
5. **Infectious disease**. PJP prophylaxis is indicated at prednisone ≥20 mg/day for ≥1 month (and CD4 <200). Treatment is TMP-SMX, with **adjunctive steroids only if PaO2 <70 mmHg on room air or A-a gradient >35**, and steroids must start with, not after, antibiotics. LDH is supportive, never diagnostic.
6. **Rheumatology**. Axial spondyloarthritis: inflammatory back pain (age <45 at onset, morning stiffness >30 min, better with exercise/worse with rest, night pain). If plain films are normal, MRI of the SI joints finds non-radiographic disease. NSAIDs first-line; TNF or IL-17 inhibitors next. HLA-B27 is neither necessary nor sufficient, it is a likelihood ratio, not a diagnosis.
7. **Endocrinology**. Thyroid storm sequencing is the tested part: beta-blocker, then a thionamide (PTU preferred, it also blocks peripheral T4→T3), then **iodine at least 1 hour AFTER the thionamide**, plus hydrocortisone. Give iodine first and you fuel hormone synthesis.
8. **Hematology**. VTE duration: provoked by a major transient risk factor (surgery, trauma) → 3 months, stop. Unprovoked or persistent risk → extended anticoagulation, and after the first 6 months you can drop to reduced-dose apixaban 2.5 mg BID or rivaroxaban 10 mg daily (AMPLIFY-EXT, EINSTEIN-CHOICE). Aspirin is markedly inferior to a DOAC for secondary prevention.
9. **Neurology**. Red flags that a "Parkinson disease" patient has an atypical syndrome: poor or absent levodopa response, early falls with vertical gaze palsy (PSP), early autonomic failure or cerebellar signs (MSA), early dementia/visual hallucinations (DLB, where antipsychotics can cause severe sensitivity reactions). Symmetric parkinsonism plus a dopamine-blocking drug is drug-induced until proven otherwise.
10. **Palliative / Geriatrics**. Avoid morphine in renal impairment: the active metabolite accumulates and causes myoclonus and delirium, use hydromorphone or fentanyl. When rotating opioids, calculate the equianalgesic dose then reduce it 25–50% for incomplete cross-tolerance. Methadone prolongs QT and has an unpredictably long half-life; benzodiazepines are not the answer for dyspnea, low-dose opioids are.

Subspecialties covered: Cardiology, Nephrology, Pulmonary, GI, ID, Rheumatology, Endocrinology, Hematology, Neurology, Palliative/Geriatrics.
