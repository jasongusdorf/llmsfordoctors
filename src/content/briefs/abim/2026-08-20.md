Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. Obstructive HCM: anything that shrinks the ventricle worsens the gradient, avoid diuretics, nitrates, ACEi/ARBs, and digoxin. Beta-blocker first, then verapamil or disopyramide; mavacamten (EXPLORER-HCM) for persistent symptoms. ICD risk factors: family history of SCD, unexplained syncope, LV wall ≥30 mm, apical aneurysm, EF <50%, NSVT, extensive LGE.
2. **Pulmonary**. SABA-only asthma treatment is now the wrong answer at every step (GINA). Even mild intermittent asthma gets as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol; at moderate severity the same inhaler serves as both maintenance and reliever (MART). Reliance on albuterol alone is a marker of risk, not a benign finding.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Acute pancreatitis: lactated Ringer's over normal saline, and moderate rather than aggressive fluids (WATERFALL, aggressive resuscitation caused more volume overload without benefit). Feed orally early as tolerated; no prophylactic antibiotics even for necrosis. Mild gallstone pancreatitis gets cholecystectomy on the *same admission* (PONCHO).
4. **Infectious disease**. Syphilis: benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM ×1 for primary, secondary, and early latent; three weekly doses for late latent or unknown duration; IV aqueous penicillin for neurosyphilis. In pregnancy there is no acceptable alternative, desensitize. Fever and myalgias within hours of the first dose is Jarisch-Herxheimer, not an allergy.
5. **Rheumatology**. Gout: treat to a serum urate <6 mg/dL, and titrate, a fixed 300 mg allopurinol dose that leaves urate at 7 is a common trap. Screen HLA-B*58:01 before allopurinol in patients of Southeast Asian, Han Chinese, or Korean descent (the last with CKD). Never stop urate-lowering therapy during a flare, and starting it during a flare is acceptable if anti-inflammatory prophylaxis is on board.
6. **Endocrinology**. SGLT2 inhibitors cause euglycemic DKA: ketoacidosis with glucose under 250, so a normal fingerstick does not exclude it. Check a beta-hydroxybutyrate in any acidotic patient on one. Hold the drug 3–4 days before elective surgery or during acute illness/fasting.
7. **Hematology**. DOACs fail in two specific settings: antiphospholipid syndrome (TRAPS, rivaroxaban had more arterial thrombosis in triple-positive patients) and mechanical heart valves (RE-ALIGN, dabigatran caused more thrombosis and bleeding). Both get warfarin.
8. **Oncology**. Tumor lysis: allopurinol prevents new uric acid but cannot clear what is already there, established hyperuricemia needs rasburicase. Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (hemolysis, methemoglobinemia). Do not routinely alkalinize the urine; it promotes calcium-phosphate precipitation.
9. **Neurology**. Status epilepticus: the classic error is underdosing the benzodiazepine, lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV, or IM midazolam 10 mg if there is no line. For the second-line agent, ESETT showed levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, and valproate stopped seizures at essentially identical rates, so the choice is driven by comorbidity, not efficacy.
10. **Allergy/Immunology**. Anaphylaxis: IM epinephrine into the anterolateral thigh, and there is no absolute contraindication. Antihistamines and glucocorticoids treat neither the airway nor the hypotension and do not prevent biphasic reactions, giving them instead of epinephrine is the tested mistake. For refractory hypotension on a beta-blocker, add glucagon. Serum tryptase must be drawn within a few hours.
