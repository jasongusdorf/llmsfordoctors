1. **Cardiology**. Low-flow/low-gradient AS: AVA <1.0 cm² but mean gradient <40 mmHg with EF <50% → dobutamine stress echo. If the gradient rises above 40 with a still-small valve area, it's true severe AS (operate); if the area opens up as flow improves, it's pseudosevere (treat the cardiomyopathy). No contractile reserve is a poor prognostic sign but does not by itself exclude benefit from AVR.
2. **Pulmonary**. Obesity hypoventilation: BMI ≥30 plus awake PaCO2 >45 with no other cause. Serum bicarbonate is the screen, a level <27 mEq/L makes OHS very unlikely and spares you an ABG. Treat with PAP (CPAP is adequate for the majority who also have severe OSA) plus real weight management; avoid supplemental O2 alone, which worsens hypercapnia.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Achalasia: dysphagia to solids **and** liquids from the start (mechanical obstruction is solids first). Barium shows a bird's-beak; high-resolution manometry is the diagnostic standard. Endoscopy is mandatory to exclude pseudoachalasia from a GE-junction tumor, suspect it with age >60, rapid onset, and weight loss out of proportion.
4. **Infectious disease**. Febrile neutropenia: empiric antipseudomonal monotherapy (cefepime, pip-tazo, or a carbapenem). Vancomycin is **not** routine, add it only for hemodynamic instability, suspected catheter infection, skin/soft-tissue infection, pneumonia, known MRSA colonization, or a gram-positive blood culture pending speciation. Don't stop antibiotics for count recovery alone if the patient is still febrile.
5. **Rheumatology**. PMR: prednisone 12.5–25 mg/day, and the response should be dramatic within days. Failure to respond means rethink the diagnosis (RA, myositis, malignancy, or occult GCA). ESR is normal in a meaningful minority, so a normal ESR does not exclude it. Methotrexate is the steroid-sparing add-on for relapsers; check for GCA symptoms at every visit.
6. **Endocrinology**. Male hypogonadism: confirm with a repeat **early-morning fasting** total testosterone before treating. Then LH/FSH separates primary from secondary. Low/inappropriately normal gonadotropins → prolactin, iron studies (hemochromatosis), and pituitary MRI if severe. Exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis, the wrong answer in a man seeking fertility.
7. **Hematology**. TTP: MAHA plus thrombocytopenia; the pentad is rarely complete and waiting for it is the trap. Use PLASMIC scoring, send ADAMTS13 (<10% confirms), and start plasma exchange plus glucocorticoids immediately, don't wait for the level. Caplacizumab is added in acute episodes. **Platelet transfusion is contraindicated** absent life-threatening bleeding.
8. **Oncology**. Metastatic non-squamous NSCLC: get molecular testing (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS G12C, RET, MET, NTRK) **plus** PD-L1 before starting therapy. A driver mutation means targeted therapy first, not chemo-immunotherapy, and giving a checkpoint inhibitor shortly before osimertinib raises severe pneumonitis risk. Never-smoker with adenocarcinoma is the highest-yield phenotype.
9. **Nephrology**. Normal-anion-gap acidosis, RTA in one line each: type 1 distal, urine pH >5.5, hypokalemia, nephrocalcinosis/calcium phosphate stones (Sjögren, amphotericin). Type 2 proximal, Fanconi picture with glycosuria, phosphaturia, hypouricemia (myeloma, tenofovir, acetazolamide); urine pH is variable and falls below 5.5 once filtered bicarb is depleted. Type 4, hyperkalemic, mild acidosis, hypoaldosteronism of diabetic CKD.
10. **Psychiatry**. Serotonin syndrome vs NMS: serotonin syndrome comes on within hours, with **clonus, hyperreflexia, mydriasis, and hyperactive bowel sounds**, treat with benzodiazepines and cyproheptadine. NMS evolves over days on a dopamine blocker, with **lead-pipe rigidity, hyporeflexia, and very high CK**, stop the agent, cool, and consider dantrolene or bromocriptine.
