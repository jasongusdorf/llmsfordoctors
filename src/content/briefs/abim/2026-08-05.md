Ten facts a day, written for recall rather than for reading. Specialty in bold, then the thing worth remembering.

1. **Cardiology**. EAST-AFNET 4 shifted AF management: rhythm control started within 12 months of diagnosis reduced CV death/stroke/hospitalization vs usual care. "Rate control is always fine" is now the trap in a newly diagnosed patient.
2. **Pulmonary**. SABA-only asthma is no longer acceptable at any severity step. Even mild intermittent disease gets as-needed ICS-formoterol; a patient on albuterol alone with good control is still a wrong answer.
3. **Gastroenterology**. Acute variceal bleed: octreotide + ceftriaxone (prophylactic antibiotics are mortality-reducing, up to 7 days) + EVL within 12h. Early pre-emptive TIPS for Child-Pugh C (<14) or Child B with active bleeding at endoscopy.
4. **Infectious disease**. Latent TB: rifamycin short-course regimens are now preferred, 3HP (weekly INH/rifapentine × 12) or 4R (rifampin × 4 months), over 9 months of INH, on completion rates and hepatotoxicity. Rule out active disease first.
5. **Rheumatology**. Statin-associated autoimmune myopathy: CK stays high and weakness progresses *after* the statin is stopped, anti-HMGCR positive, necrotizing biopsy. Needs immunosuppression, not just drug withdrawal, unlike ordinary statin myalgia.
6. **Endocrinology**. Subclinical hypothyroidism: treat if TSH >10, or persistently 7–10 with symptoms/positive TPO/pregnancy plans. TRUST showed no symptom or fatigue benefit from levothyroxine in older adults with mild elevation, observe instead.
7. **Hematology**. TTP: don't wait for ADAMTS13 (<10% confirms). High PLASMIC score → urgent plasma exchange plus steroids, add caplacizumab. Platelet transfusion is contraindicated absent life-threatening bleeding.
8. **Oncology**. Tumor lysis: rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (hemolysis, methemoglobinemia). Send the uric acid on ice, rasburicase keeps degrading urate in the tube and falsely reassures you.
9. **Nephrology**. PRESERVE: neither IV sodium bicarbonate nor oral N-acetylcysteine beat isotonic saline/placebo for contrast-associated AKI. Isotonic volume and holding nephrotoxins is the answer; NAC is the distractor.
10. **Psychiatry**. Lithium toxicity is usually precipitated by a drug or volume change: NSAIDs, thiazides, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, dehydration. Hemodialysis for level >4, or >2.5 with severe neuro symptoms or renal failure. Levels can rebound after dialysis, recheck.

Subspecialties covered: Cardiology, Pulmonary, GI, ID, Rheumatology, Endocrinology, Hematology, Oncology, Nephrology, Psychiatry.
