1. **Cardiology**. Acute pericarditis: NSAID/aspirin **plus colchicine ×3 months** (0.5 mg BID, 0.5 mg daily if <70 kg), colchicine roughly halves recurrence (ICAP/CORP). Corticosteroids relieve pain but *increase* recurrence; reserve for failed or contraindicated NSAIDs.
2. **Nephrology**. Stone prevention by type: hypercalciuria → thiazide + normal dietary calcium (low-calcium diets worsen oxalate stones). Uric acid stones are radiolucent on plain film, visible on CT, and are the only ones that dissolve, alkalinize urine to pH 6.5–7 with potassium citrate.
3. **Dermatology**. Bullous pemphigoid: elderly, **tense** bullae, mucosa spared, DIF linear IgG/C3 along the basement membrane. Pemphigus vulgaris: **flaccid** bullae, oral erosions usually first, Nikolsky positive, intercellular "chicken-wire" IgG (desmoglein 3). Tense = pemphigoid.
4. **Psychiatry**. Bipolar depression: antidepressant **monotherapy is the wrong answer** (switch/cycling risk). Use quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, or lamotrigine; lamotrigine requires slow titration for SJS/TEN risk and its dose is halved with valproate, doubled with carbamazepine.
5. **Women's health**. Systemic estrogen for vasomotor symptoms is reasonable if age <60 **or** within 10 years of menopause; add a progestin whenever the uterus is intact. Contraindicated with prior breast cancer, VTE, CAD, or stroke. Isolated genitourinary symptoms → low-dose **vaginal** estrogen, not systemic.
6. **Geriatrics**. Nothing pharmacologic prevents delirium. Multicomponent nonpharmacologic protocols (orientation, sleep, mobility, vision/hearing, hydration) are the evidence-based prevention; antipsychotics do not reduce incidence, duration, or mortality and are for dangerous agitation only. Benzodiazepines are the classic wrong choice except in alcohol/benzo withdrawal.
7. **Hematology**. Ferritin <30 ng/mL is diagnostic of iron deficiency. With inflammation, CKD, or heart failure ferritin is falsely normal, use ferritin <100 (or 100–300 with **TSAT <20%**). IV iron is preferred in HFrEF and in inflammatory bowel disease, where oral absorption fails.
8. **Pulmonary**. Fleischner 2017 for incidental **solid** nodules: <6 mm in a low-risk patient needs no follow-up at all. 6–8 mm → CT at 6–12 months. Subsolid nodules follow separate, longer rules, and these criteria do not apply to lung cancer screening, known cancer, or age <35.
9. **Infectious disease**. Start ART immediately in new HIV regardless of CD4, with two exceptions. **Cryptococcal meningitis: delay ART 4–6 weeks** (early ART increased mortality in COAT); TB meningitis also warrants delay. Other opportunistic infections: start within ~2 weeks.
10. **Endocrinology**. Adrenal incidentaloma: every one gets a 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test and plasma/urine metanephrines; add aldosterone-renin ratio only if hypertensive or hypokalemic. Noncontrast attenuation **<10 HU = benign adenoma**. Resect if functional (other than mild autonomous cortisol) or if imaging is indeterminate and >4 cm. Never biopsy before excluding pheochromocytoma.
